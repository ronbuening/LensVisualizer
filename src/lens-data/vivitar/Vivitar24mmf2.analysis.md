# Vivitar 24mm f/2

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [US 4,258,980 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4258980)
**Application Number:** US 97,543
**Priority:** continuation-in-part of US Ser. No. 941,524 (filed September 11, 1978, abandoned)
**Filed:** November 26, 1979
**Granted:** March 31, 1981
**Inventor:** Jacob Moskovich
**Assignee:** Vivitar Corporation
**Title:** Retrofocus Lens
**Example details:** Table 1 and FIG. 1, repeated value for value as claim 5; the patent normalizes the focal length to 1.0 and the model scales every length by 24

US 4,258,980 was granted to Jacob Moskovich of Cincinnati, Ohio, and assigned to Vivitar Corporation of Santa Monica, California. It describes a fast retrofocus lens for the 24 × 36 mm frame that is smaller and cheaper than the Vivitar Series 1 wide angle of US 4,099,849, covers a field of up to 84° and focuses to a magnification of about 1:10. Six prescriptions are given. Tables 1, 5 and 6 cover 84°, the field of a 24 mm lens; Tables 2, 3 and 4 cover 72°.

The model is Table 1. Its association with the Vivitar 24mm f/2 rests on construction, not on a maker's drawing:

1. Eight air-spaced elements in eight groups, the count listed for the Vivitar 24mm f/2 in both its 52 mm and 55 mm filter versions.
2. Relative aperture f/2 and an 84° field, the diagonal field of a 24 mm lens on the 24 × 36 mm frame.
3. The patent's close-focus target of 1:10 corresponds to the production lens's 0.3 m minimum focus.
4. Moskovich's 1978 SPIE paper on a family of wide-angle lenses with shared parts uses the 24mm f/2.0 and 28mm f/2.0 Vivitar lenses as its examples, and the patent's parent application was filed the same year.

The Vivitar 24mm f/2 was supplied by more than one contract manufacturer. No published cross-section was found, so which production version carries this design, and whether Table 1 is the exact production prescription, is not established. The catalog mounts follow a lens-database listing for the Vivitar 24mm f/2 and are not taken from the patent.

## Optical Architecture

Eight spherical, air-spaced elements form a negative front group G1 (L1–L3) and a positive rear group G2 (L4–L8), with the iris between L4 and L5. Table 1 gives the group focal lengths as −0.79 and +0.99 of the system focal length; the scaled prescription computes to −19.05 mm and +23.75 mm. The arrangement yields a back focus of 38.2 mm, 1.59 times the 24.01 mm focal length, which clears an SLR mirror.

The patent sets four conditions. The front group focal length lies between 0.7 and 1.0 of the system focal length in magnitude, so that the rear group can correct the front group's aberrations without growing complex while the lens stays compact. The rear group focal length stays below 1.1, which shortens the back focus and the overall length. The mean Abbe number of L1–L3 lies between 52 and 70 (57.2 here) and that of L4 between 23 and 33 (29.5 here); the patent ties these two to lateral colour and to the chromatic variation of distortion. The facing surfaces of L4 and L5 are nearly flat (R8 = −238.7 mm, R9 plano), which claim 1 states as radii of at least three focal lengths.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1: Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.697, νd = 55.5, LAC14 (HOYA catalog equivalent). f = +127.3 mm.

A weak positive meniscus, convex to the object, placed ahead of the two negative menisci. A positive front element in a retrofocus front group offsets part of the barrel distortion the negative elements introduce (an interpretation; the patent does not assign the role).

### L2: Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.697, νd = 55.5, LAC14 (HOYA catalog equivalent). f = −30.2 mm.

The stronger of the two negative menisci. Its rear surface, R4 = 12.72 mm, is the steepest in the lens and supplies most of the front group's divergence.

### L3: Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.603, νd = 60.7, BACD14 (HOYA catalog equivalent). f = −42.0 mm.

The second negative meniscus, separated from L2 by a 9.3 mm air space. It completes the negative front group.

### L4: Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.717, νd = 29.5, SF1 (SCHOTT catalog equivalent). f = +31.6 mm.

A thick dense-flint positive element in front of the iris with a nearly flat rear surface. It is the high-dispersion element of patent condition (4), set against the three crowns of the front group to correct lateral colour.

### L5: Plano-Convex Positive

nd = 1.603, νd = 60.7, BACD14 (HOYA catalog equivalent). f = +31.8 mm.

A plano-convex crown directly behind the iris, flat side to the stop. It is the first of the four rear elements that move ahead of the front four in close focusing.

### L6: Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.805, νd = 25.5, SF6 (SCHOTT catalog equivalent). f = −17.3 mm.

The only negative element of the rear group and the strongest element in the lens. Its dense flint corrects the axial colour of the surrounding crowns, and its surfaces carry the overcorrecting spherical aberration that balances the positive elements.

### L7: Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.640, νd = 60.2, LACL60 (HOYA catalog equivalent). f = +38.5 mm.

A positive meniscus, concave to the object, that shares the converging power of the rear group with L8.

### L8: Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.640, νd = 60.2, LACL60 (HOYA catalog equivalent). f = +39.9 mm.

The biconvex final element. With L7 it brings the beam to focus over the long back focal distance.

## Glass Identification

The patent prints refractive index and Abbe number to three and one decimals and names no glass. Five distinct pairs serve the eight elements, and each agrees with a catalog glass at the printed precision:

| Elements | nd / νd | Catalog equivalent | Catalog nd / νd |
| -------- | ------- | ------------------ | --------------- |
| L1, L2 | 1.697 / 55.5 | HOYA LAC14 | 1.69680 / 55.46 |
| L3, L5 | 1.603 / 60.7 | HOYA BACD14 | 1.60311 / 60.69 |
| L4 | 1.717 / 29.5 | SF1 | 1.71736 / 29.51 |
| L6 | 1.805 / 25.5 | SF6 | 1.80518 / 25.43 |
| L7, L8 | 1.640 / 60.2 | HOYA LACL60 | 1.64000 / 60.20 |

Each of these types has equivalents in other makers' catalogs (for example Schott LaK14 and SK14, Ohara LAL14 and BSM14). The 1.640 / 60.2 pair is the exception: the nearest Schott type, LaK21, is 1.6405 / 60.1, so that pair points to a Japanese catalog. The labels are catalog equivalents chosen by the printed values; the supplier of the production glass is not known. All are ordinary crowns and flints, and no anomalous-dispersion glass is used.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent describes focusing in which all elements move forward while the four rear elements L5–L8 move faster than the front four, so that the air space S4 between L4 and L5 closes. Table 7 gives S4 at a magnification of 1:10 as 0.135 of the focal length, against 0.155 at infinity: 3.24 mm against 3.72 mm at this scale. The patent adds that the differential movement may be omitted when close focusing is not required.

The model holds the infinity state only; the 1:10 state is not modeled. The production lens focuses to 0.3 m.

## Stop and Clear Apertures

FIG. 1 draws the iris between L4 and L5 without giving its position. The model places it at the middle of the 3.72 mm air space. The patent prints no clear apertures, so every semi-diameter is read from FIG. 1, which is drawn to scale within about 10 %. L2's rear surface is drawn with a flat land outside its curved cap, and the model follows that: the curve ends at 11.4 mm and a land continues to the 14 mm rim.

L6 and L7 are drawn square, about 10.4 mm and 10.6 mm in semi-diameter, and meeting at the rim. The prescription confirms the contact: the rear surface of L6 and the front surface of L7 curve toward each other across a 1.66 mm air space and touch at a height of 9.74 mm. The model ends both curved faces at 9.6 mm and continues them as flat lands to the element rims. The full f/2 marginal ray would cross these two surfaces at 9.76 mm, so the edge contact of L6 and L7 is itself a limit on the aperture of the printed prescription.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The scaled prescription has a paraxial focal length of 24.008 mm. The image plane is at the printed back focal length, 1.59 × 24 = 38.16 mm, which lies 0.166 mm inside the paraxial focus of 38.326 mm. The printed prescription has undercorrected spherical aberration of −0.18 to −0.21 mm between the half-aperture and 0.85 zones, so the printed plane sits at the zonal best focus and is kept. The printed value also equals the paraxial back focus cut to two decimals (1.5969 against 1.59).

The stop radius, 8.66 mm, is the paraxial value for f/2. The prescription's spherical aberration grows steeply at the edge of the pupil: the full f/2 marginal ray would reach 9.83 mm at the stop and focus 1.42 mm short. The real ray that just passes the modeled stop enters at a height of 5.48 mm, so the model admits about f/2.19 of real beam on axis. This is a property of the prescription as printed to three decimals, not of the scaling.

The rims of L6 and L7 cut part of the oblique beam. Wide open, the geometric illumination of the model falls to 70 % at a half-field of 28°, 37 % at 39.5° and 19 % at the 42.4° corner of the 24 × 36 mm frame. These figures depend on rims read from the figure, not on patent data.
