## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [US 3,942,875](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3942875A/en)
**Application Number:** US 468,472
**Priority:** continuation-in-part of US Ser. No. 286,920 (filed 7 September 1972), itself a continuation-in-part of Ser. No. 179,304 (filed 10 September 1971)
**Filed:** May 9, 1974
**Granted:** March 9, 1976
**Inventor:** Ellis I. Betensky
**Assignee:** Ponder & Best, Inc.
**Title:** Wide Aperture Long Focal Length Lens
**Example details:** Table VII and FIG. 10; repeated as claim 5 — the last of five embodiments; the patent does not number its examples

US 3,942,875 was granted to Ellis I. Betensky of Toronto and assigned to Ponder & Best, Inc. of Santa Monica, California, the company behind the Vivitar brand. It shares its 1971–72 parent applications with GB 1,408,910 and is a companion of US 3,942,876, granted the same day; those two supply the Vivitar Series 1 135mm f/2.3 and 200mm f/3. The patent addresses one problem: when a long, fast lens is focused by moving its objective, axial imagery holds up but off-axis imagery degrades at close distances. Its answer is a rear corrector group of nearly zero power that stays fixed to the film plane while the objective moves. FIG. 10 is described as "a 90mm EFL f/1.9 macro lens scaled for a 24 x 36mm image frame", focusing to 404.6 mm at a magnification of 0.5.

Table VII is identified with the **Vivitar Series 1 90mm f/2.5 VMC Macro**, made for Vivitar by Tokina and introduced about 1975–77 (sources differ on the year). The evidence:

1. A collector's list of Series 1 patents names US 3,942,875 for the 90/2.5 and states that the production lens is the FIG. 10 design, which the patent gives at f/1.9.
2. Table VII has eight elements, with L4/L5 the only cemented pair: 8 elements in 7 groups, the production count.
3. The patent states 90 mm EFL and 0.5× magnification; the production lens is a 90 mm macro reaching 1:2 at about 0.39 m without an adapter.
4. The patent's objective moves for focus while the corrector stays fixed; the production lens focuses by extending its front unit.
5. The application was filed in 1974 by Vivitar's parent company, shortly before the lens appeared.

**Key marketed specifications:** 90 mm, f/2.5–22, 8 elements in 7 groups, 8 aperture blades, minimum focus 0.39 m (1:2), 1:1 with the dedicated three-element Macro Adapter; mounts include Canon FD, Nikon F, Minolta SR, Olympus OM, Pentax K and M42.

## Optical Architecture

The patent calls all its embodiments Sonnar-type lenses and groups FIG. 10 as follows: Group I (L1, L2) positive; Group II (L3) negative, with most of its power on the image side; the aperture stop; Group III (L4, L5, L6) positive; and the corrector (L7, L8). L1–L6 and the stop form the focusing front unit (paraxial f = +89.5 mm); the air-spaced corrector L7/L8 adds almost no power, so the complete lens computes to f = 90.013 mm.

The power is concentrated around the stop. L3's rear surface (R6 = 28.95 mm) and L4's front surface (R7 = −28.07 mm) are strongly concave toward the stop from either side. This near-symmetric pair of negative surfaces helps control coma and distortion and keeps the Petzval sum small, 0.00095 mm⁻¹ (a Petzval radius of about 1.06 m, roughly twelve focal lengths). The patent's general conditions are met: the EFL is 2.08 times the 43.3 mm frame diagonal (range 1.5–3.0), and the 40.50 mm back focus is 0.94 times the diagonal (minimum 0.90).

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Plano-Convex Positive

nd = 1.67800, νd = 55.5. Glass: LAC12 (HOYA catalog equivalent). f = +126.6 mm.

Front element of Group I, with a convex front (R1 = 85.85 mm) and a flat rear. It and L2 share the positive power of the front group.

### L2 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.67800, νd = 55.5. Glass: LAC12 (HOYA catalog equivalent). f = +100.0 mm.

A thick (14.85 mm) meniscus convex to the object and the stronger of the two Group I elements. Its rear surface sits 1.40 mm in front of L3 on axis and nearly touches L3 at a height of about 18 mm, which limits how large L3's front clear aperture can be.

### L3 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.67300, νd = 32.2. Glass: SF5 (SCHOTT catalog equivalent). f = −46.5 mm.

The Group II negative element. Its front (R5 = 406.87 mm) is almost flat and its rear (R6 = 28.95 mm) carries nearly all its power, as the patent's description of Group II specifies. Together, Groups I and II are only weakly positive (f = +305 mm), so the converging power of the lens lies mostly behind the stop.

### L4 / L5 — Cemented Doublet

- **L4 (Biconcave Negative):** nd = 1.57500, νd = 41.5. Glass: S-TIL27 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = −35.1 mm.
- **L5 (Biconvex Positive):** nd = 1.69100, νd = 54.7. Glass: N-LAK9 (SCHOTT catalog equivalent). f = +38.6 mm.

The only cemented pair in the lens, first element behind the stop. As a whole it is almost afocal (f ≈ +2,200 mm): its strongly concave front (R7) mirrors L3's rear across the stop, and the cemented interface (R8 = 74.74 mm, a 0.116 index step) and the convex rear (R9 = −39.95 mm) help correct spherical aberration and color without adding net power.

### L6 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.67800, νd = 55.5. Glass: LAC12 (HOYA catalog equivalent). f = +88.2 mm.

The main positive power of Group III (Group III as a whole: f = +76.3 mm) and the last element of the focusing front unit. The variable gap D11 follows it.

### L7 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.51700, νd = 64.2. Glass: N-BK7 (SCHOTT catalog equivalent). f = +149.9 mm.

A thick (15.19 mm) meniscus concave to the object, first element of the fixed corrector.

### L8 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.51700, νd = 64.2. Glass: N-BK7 (SCHOTT catalog equivalent). f = −130.4 mm.

A meniscus concave to the object, 7.95 mm behind L7. The two corrector elements are of the same glass and nearly cancel in power, so the corrector changes focal length and color very little; its job is to rebalance off-axis aberrations as the front unit moves away from it.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent prints Nd and Vd to three decimals and one decimal and gives no partial dispersions.

| Elements   | nd    | νd   | Catalog label                      | Δnd / Δνd (catalog − printed) |
| ---------- | ----- | ---- | ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| L1, L2, L6 | 1.678 | 55.5 | LAC12 (HOYA catalog equivalent)    | −0.0001 / +0.02               |
| L3         | 1.673 | 32.2 | SF5 (SCHOTT catalog equivalent)    | −0.0003 / +0.01               |
| L4         | 1.575 | 41.5 | S-TIL27 (OHARA catalog equivalent) | 0.0000 / +0.01                |
| L5         | 1.691 | 54.7 | N-LAK9 (SCHOTT catalog equivalent) | 0.0000 / +0.01                |
| L7, L8     | 1.517 | 64.2 | N-BK7 (SCHOTT catalog equivalent)  | −0.0002 / −0.03               |

The positive elements in front of and behind the stop use lanthanum crowns; the negative elements of the front unit (L3, L4) use flints. Every printed pair rounds from a catalog glass at the patent's three-decimal precision, so all eight elements trace on catalog dispersion curves. The design uses no anomalous-dispersion glass; it is an ordinary achromat.

## Focus Mechanism

Focusing is by unit movement of the front unit: L1–L6 and the stop move objectward while the corrector L7/L8 stays fixed to the film plane. Only D11, the gap between L6 and L7, changes.

| State                 | D11 (mm) | Object to film | Magnification | EFL (mm) |
| --------------------- | -------- | -------------- | ------------- | -------- |
| Infinity              | 0.30     | ∞              | 0             | 90.01    |
| Patent close state    | 44.76    | 404.9 mm       | −0.499        | 86.98    |

The patent gives the close state as 404.6 mm ("4.5 EFL") and 0.5×; the 404.9 mm and −0.499 above are calculated from the printed gap and back focus, and the difference is rounding. Because the corrector is weakly negative, the focal length falls slightly as the front unit moves away from it. The production lens is specified at 0.39 m (1:2), close to the patent state. At 1:2 the working aperture of the f/2.5 lens is about f/3.8 (paraxial). The patent gives no intermediate focus rows; intermediate positions in the model interpolate D11 linearly.

## Design Aperture and Model Scope

**F/1.9 versus f/2.5.** The patent's design aperture is f/1.9; its title and stated object are a wide-aperture long-focus lens, and all its embodiments are f/2.0 or faster. The production lens opens to f/2.5. The patent gives no clear apertures, so every semi-diameter in the data file is inferred from FIG. 10's proportions and the first-order beam. With those rims the full f/1.9 axial beam is clipped at L3's front surface and at the L4/L5 doublet (surfaces 8 and 9). L3 cannot be enlarged, because L2's concave rear and L3's front leave only about 0.16 mm of air at its present 17.5 mm rim, and enlarging the doublet enough would thin L5's edge below 0.5 mm. The model therefore sets its stop from the production f/2.5, at which the axial beam clears every surface; `catalog.fNumber.design` keeps the patent's f/1.9. Why Vivitar marketed the lens at f/2.5 is not documented.

**Stop.** The patent marks the stop A in the 12.635 mm air space between L3 and L4 without a station. It is placed at the midpoint, consistent with FIG. 10, and moves with the front unit.

**Scale and back focus.** Table VII is already at production scale and is stored unscaled. The printed 40.50 mm back focus is kept as the last gap. The paraxial back focus of the stored prescription is 40.54 mm, a 0.04 mm residual.

**Corrector power.** The patent text gives the corrector's focal length as +1250 mm (13.89 times the lens EFL). The printed L7/L8 data compute to −1283 mm. The magnitude agrees and the overall focal length and back focus reproduce the patent, so the sign in the text is treated as a source inconsistency and no radius is changed.

## Design Heritage and Context

Ponder & Best launched the Vivitar Series 1 line in the early 1970s as its premium range, with designs it commissioned built by Japanese makers: Kino for the first 70–210 mm zoom, Komine for the 200 mm, and Tokina for this 90 mm macro. Betensky's corrector principle runs through the three related patents: the 135 mm and 200 mm use a single fixed rear element, while the macro uses an air-spaced pair to hold off-axis correction down to half life size.

Tokina later sold its own AT-X 90mm f/2.5 Macro, which reviewers widely describe as a re-release of the Series 1 design. Tokina lists 9 elements in 7 groups for its AT-X M90, against 8 in 7 here, so the two are not established as optically identical.
