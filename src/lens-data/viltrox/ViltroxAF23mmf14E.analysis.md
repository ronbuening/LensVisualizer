## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** CN 211826700 U  
**Application Number:** 202020728236.8  
**Filed:** 7 May 2020  
**Granted:** 30 October 2020  
**Inventors:** Liu Ruijun; Chen Baofeng  
**Assignee:** Shenzhen Leiying Photoelectric Technology Co., Ltd.  
**Title:** 内合焦式成像镜头 (Internal-focusing imaging lens)  
**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 1, Tables 1–2, Figure 1

The patent's assignee is the former registered name of Shenzhen Viltrox Technology. Example 1 is correlated with
the Viltrox AF 23mm F1.4, an APS-C lens sold in Sony E, Fujifilm X, Canon EF-M and Nikon Z mounts with one optical
construction. The correlation rests on several convergent features:

1. Viltrox lists 11 elements in 10 groups; Example 1 has 11 elements in 10 groups, with one cemented pair, and a
   printed infinity focal length of 23.44 mm at f/1.4.
2. The product's 63.4° full angle of view agrees with the patent's 31.7° half-field in ¶0086.
3. Viltrox specifies internal focusing and a 0.3 m minimum focus distance (0.1× maximum magnification). The patent
   focuses by moving one negative element and tabulates a 0.3 m close-focus state.
4. Viltrox lists two ED elements. The patent names the fourth and fifth front elements as ultra-low-dispersion
   material (¶0028).

The manufacturer does not confirm the patent attribution. Examples 2–4 of the same patent are closely related
23 mm designs; Example 1 is the first worked example. The same-disclosure invention application CN 111474691 A and
its grant CN 111474691 B repeat the Example 1 tables.[1–4]

## Optical Architecture

The source describes an all-spherical, four-functional-group imaging lens with positive–positive–negative–positive
group power. G1 contains L11–L15; G2 contains L21–L23; G3 consists of L31; G4 contains L41–L42. There are 11 lens
elements in 10 air-separated groups because L21/L22 form a cemented pair. The source's rear plane-parallel GL plate
is additional sensor/filter optics and is retained in the traced model, without being counted as a twelfth lens element.

The printed stop lies between G1 and G2. L11 is a negative front meniscus. Three positive elements and the final
negative L15 form the rest of G1. G2 combines the negative net cemented pair with positive L23. Negative L31 is the
focusing element. The positive/negative L41/L42 combination forms G4.

Table 1 prints nd 1.85 for L12; this model carries 1.83 as a source correction (see Glass Identification). With
it, the computed infinity EFL is 23.350 mm against the printed 23.44 mm (−0.38%). The four groups have EFLs
+45.953, +33.783, −41.312 and +38.267 mm. The first vertex lies 85.78 mm from the printed image plane, and the
infinity paraxial focus falls 0.125 mm in front of that plane. The patent prints indices to two decimals only;
substituting plausible six-digit catalog indices for the same glasses reduces the residuals to about −0.1% and
0.04 mm, but the printed indices are kept.[1]

## Element-by-Element Analysis

All element focal lengths below are thick-element values in air. The patent prints nd to only two decimals; the
five-decimal display below does not imply additional source precision.

### L11 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.88000, νd = 40.79. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.88, νd 40.79; coarse patent coordinates). f = −32.52 mm.

L11 begins G1 with negative power. Its rear curvature is substantially stronger than its front curvature.
Paragraphs 0065–0066 credit the high-index front element with reducing the ray-bundle diameter presented to later
optics, which keeps the rear elements small.

### L12 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.83000, νd = 42.72. Glass: Unmatched (printed nd 1.85 corrected to 1.83, νd 42.72; coarse patent
coordinates). f = +39.16 mm.

L12 follows the large front air gap and supplies positive power within G1. Its material is one of the two negative
terms in the patent's front-group Abbe-number difference. Its index is the one corrected value in this model:
Table 1 prints 1.85, and the model carries 1.83 for the 1.83481/42.72 glass class (see Glass Identification).

### L13 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.85000, νd = 36.59. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.85, νd 36.59; coarse patent coordinates). f = +65.28 mm.

L13 adds positive power after L12. Its meniscus form, convex to the object, keeps the angles of incidence on the
strongly converging front group moderate.

### L14 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.51000, νd = 81.59. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.51, νd 81.59; coarse patent coordinates). f = +157.76 mm.

L14 has weak positive power and is one of the two low-dispersion front elements identified by ¶0028 and ¶0065;
its νd fits a fluorophosphate ED glass, and it is tagged as an inferred ED position. Its printed index differs
from common catalog glasses with a closely similar Abbe number (the 1.497/81.6 class); the printed value is kept.

### L15 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.48000, νd = 70.44. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.48, νd 70.44; coarse patent coordinates). f = −70.03 mm.

L15 closes G1 immediately before the stop. Although its material has a high Abbe number, its power is negative.
Paragraph 0028 states that L14 and L15 compensate the chromatic aberration of G1's strong positive power. The
nd/νd pairs alone do not establish anomalous partial dispersion or apochromatic correction.

### L21 — Biconcave Negative, Cemented to L22

nd = 1.85000, νd = 23.78. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.85, νd 23.78; coarse patent coordinates). f = −10.75 mm.

L21 is the negative member of the design's only cemented pair. Surface 13 is its rear interface and L22's entry
interface.

### L22 — Biconvex Positive, Cemented to L21

nd = 1.73000, νd = 54.67. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.73, νd 54.67; coarse patent coordinates). f = +16.41 mm.

L22 is the positive member of that pair. Its higher Abbe number against L21's dense flint gives the pair the usual
achromatizing contrast; the cemented pair has a net EFL of −64.11 mm. G2 as a whole is positive once L23 is
included.

### L23 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.93000, νd = 20.88. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.93, νd 20.88; coarse patent coordinates). f = +28.99 mm.

L23 supplies most of G2's positive power. Its index is the highest among the eleven lens elements, and its Abbe
number is the lowest. The air gap behind it is the variable D1.

### L31 — Negative Meniscus, Focusing Element

nd = 1.75000, νd = 28.30. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.75, νd 28.30; coarse patent coordinates). f = −41.31 mm.

L31 alone forms G3. Paragraphs 0060 and 0073 describe it as the negative focusing element that moves toward the
image. Condition (4) keeps its index between 1.70 and 1.85 so that it compensates the aberrations the other groups
introduce while focusing (¶0075). A single light element also eases the load on the focusing motor (¶0028).

### L41 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.73000, νd = 54.67. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.73, νd 54.67; coarse patent coordinates). f = +20.99 mm.

L41 begins G4 after the D2 gap. It has the same printed nd/νd as L22. Its strong positive power combines with L42
to give G4 a net EFL of +38.27 mm.

### L42 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.85000, νd = 23.78. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.85, νd 23.78; coarse patent coordinates). f = −39.76 mm.

L42 ends the lens and shares its printed nd/νd with L21. Behind it the patent prints 14.29 mm of air, a 2.00 mm
GL filter plate (¶0076), then 1.00 mm of air to the image; the model traces that chain unchanged.

## Glass Identification and Selection

Example 1 prints nine distinct lens-glass pairs, to two decimals in nd. L22/L41 and L21/L42 are repeated pairs.
The GL plate adds nd = 1.52, νd = 64.20. No manufacturer or spectral lines are given, so the elements carry
`Unmatched` labels and dispersion is modeled from the Abbe numbers.

**Source correction, L12.** Table 1 prints L12 as 1.85/42.72. νd 42.72 belongs to the 1.83481 class, and Example 3
of the same patent prints that glass as 1.83/42.72. The printed 1.85 gives F1/F = 1.879, outside claim 1's range of
1.9–2.2 (Table 9 lists 1.93), and places the infinity paraxial focus 0.873 mm in front of the printed image plane,
far outside the ±0.2 mm scale of the infinity spherical-aberration plot in Fig. 2. With 1.83, F1/F = 1.968, the
infinity focus offset falls to 0.125 mm, and the printed 0.3 m spacings image an object 0.3 m from the image plane
exactly onto the printed image plane at β = −0.10. The model therefore carries nd 1.83 for L12.

Across the patent's four examples (Tables 1, 3, 5 and 7, PDF pp. 7–14), 1.85/23.78 and 1.93/20.88 appear in every
example, 1.83/42.72 returns in Example 3, and the L14 glass returns as 1.51/81.60 in Examples 2–4. L11's
1.88/40.79, L13's 1.85/36.59, L15's 1.48/70.44 and the 1.73/54.67 pair appear only in Example 1. L31 keeps nd 1.75
in every example while its Abbe number changes (28.30, 27.55, 25.46, 26.20).

OHARA S- and L-prefix families are not interchanged. The patent's low-dispersion description and Abbe-number
condition explain its material-selection strategy, but neither establishes anomalous partial dispersion or
apochromatic correction.[1,5–9]

## Focus Mechanism

G3 (L31) alone moves toward the image while G1, G2 and G4 stay fixed relative to the image (¶0060). Table 2 gives:

| State | D1 (mm) | D2 (mm) | D1 + D2 (mm) | Printed EFL (mm) | Model EFL (mm) |
| ----- | ------: | ------: | -----------: | ---------------: | -------------: |
| ∞     |    1.00 |    4.43 |         5.43 |            23.44 |         23.350 |
| 0.3 m |    3.76 |    1.88 |         5.64 |            22.95 |         22.907 |

The model uses both printed states. Measured from the image plane, the 0.3 m state focuses onto the printed image
plane at a magnification of −0.10, matching Viltrox's 0.3 m minimum focus distance and 0.1× maximum magnification.
D1 grows by 2.76 mm while D2 shrinks by 2.55 mm, so the printed D1 + D2 changes by 0.21 mm even though only G3
moves; no single source value isolates the cause, and the printed spacings are kept. The invention application
and its grant repeat these numbers.[1–4]

## Model Scope and Limitations

The patent publishes no clear apertures. Element semi-diameters follow Fig. 1, which is drawn to scale, and are
checked against real rays at infinity and 0.3 m; the stop radius is derived from the design f/1.4. Neither is a
measurement of the production lens. Fig. 1 draws several elements, including L11, L15 and L21, with a flat
mounting land outside a smaller curved face. The model cannot draw that step: it carries L11's concave face out to
the full rim, and joins the two face rims of L15 and L21 with a straight edge. The model is all-spherical, as the
patent is, and traces at the d-line with Abbe-number dispersion because no vendor glass is identified. At full
aperture the off-axis bundles vignette at the front elements, which is expected for a compact f/1.4 design but is
set here by figure-measured rather than published apertures.

The model's distortion (about −1.5% barrel) and spherical aberration follow the patent's Figs. 2 and 4, but its
tangential field curve does not follow Fig. 3. The patent keeps the tangential focus within about 0.1 mm of the
axial focus out to 31.7°, while the printed table, traced with L12 at 1.83, puts it 0.75 mm in front of the axial
focus at the field edge; Fig. 6 shows the same gap at 0.3 m. Single changes to one of several G1 or G4 spacings or radii flatten the curve,
but the best one depends on index decimals the patent does not print, and index shifts of 0.01 or less reproduce
Fig. 3 on their own. No single printed value is isolated, so the table is kept as printed and the mismatch is
recorded as an unresolved source erratum. The sagittal curve has the patent's shape but dips about 1.7 times as
deep.
