**DIAGNOSTIC ONLY.** This is a literal-source optical diagnostic associated provisionally with the Viltrox AF 23mm
F1.4 E. It is not a verified prescription of the production lens. The source's radii, axial gaps, coarse refractive
indices and Abbe numbers are retained without optical correction. Several source claims are not reproduced by
those numbers. The application model is static at the source-labeled infinity/base state; its authored image
plane is retained even though it is not the computed infinity focal plane.

The production correlation has several convergent features:

1. Viltrox's official product information describes a Sony E, APS-C 23 mm f/1.4 lens with 11 elements in 10 groups;
   Example 1 has the same lens-element and air-separated-group counts and a printed infinity EFL of 23.44 mm.
2. The product's 63.4° full viewing angle agrees numerically with the patent's 31.7° half-field label in ¶0086.
   That agreement is a source-label comparison, not a validation of the diagnostic model's full-field performance.
3. The product specifies internal focusing and a 0.3 m closest distance. The patent also proposes internal focusing
   and labels its closest row 0.3 m, but its tabulated spacings conflict with the stated single-moving-group mechanism.
4. Viltrox describes two ED elements and two high-index elements. The patent describes the fourth and fifth front
   elements as low-dispersion materials, but no production glass supplier or unique material identity is established.

These similarities do not constitute manufacturer confirmation of the selected patent/example attribution. No
primary launch-date announcement was verified. The selected patent's date is not used as an asserted product
release date. The same-disclosure invention application CN111474691A and later grant CN111474691B repeat the inspected
Example 1 tables and supply no correction to the discrepancies discussed below.[1–4]

## Optical Architecture

The source describes an all-spherical, four-functional-group imaging lens with positive–positive–negative–positive
group power. G1 contains L11–L15; G2 contains L21–L23; G3 consists of L31; G4 contains L41–L42. There are 11 lens
elements in 10 air-separated groups because L21/L22 form a cemented pair. The source's rear plane-parallel GL plate
is additional sensor/filter optics and is retained in the traced model, without being counted as a twelfth lens element.

The printed stop lies between G1 and G2. L11 is a negative front meniscus. Three positive elements and the final
negative L15 form the rest of G1. G2 combines the negative net cemented pair with positive L23. Negative L31 is the
source's proposed focusing element. The positive/negative L41/L42 combination forms G4. These power statements come
from the source and reproduced paraxial calculations; they do not independently identify each element's aberration contribution.

For the literal base prescription, the computed EFL is 23.377873 mm. The four isolated functional groups have EFLs
+43.938312, +33.782911, −41.312250 and +38.267442 mm. The first vertex to the authored physical image plane is
85.780000 mm. The air-equivalent paraxial BFD from the last lens vertex, surface 22, is 15.733217 mm, whereas the
printed rear chain has reduced length 16.605789 mm. An axial parallel bundle therefore has paraxial best focus
0.872572 mm before the authored image plane. The published EFL is 23.44 mm and is not silently substituted for the
computed value. These quantities describe the literal diagnostic model, not a measured production lens.[1]

## Element-by-Element Analysis

All element focal lengths below are standalone thick-element values in air, calculated from the actual model's
radii, center thickness and index. They are distinct from net cemented-group power and in-situ behavior. The patent
prints nd to only two decimals; the five-decimal display below does not imply additional source precision. All
`Unmatched` descriptions are explicit non-identifications rather than claims that a particular catalog glass is proprietary.

### L11 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.88000, νd = 40.79. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.88, νd 40.79; coarse patent coordinates). f = −32.52 mm.

L11 begins G1 with negative power. Its rear curvature is substantially stronger than its front curvature.
Paragraphs 0065–0066 associate the front element with reduction of the ray-bundle diameter presented to later optics.
That stated design rationale is retained as a patent assertion; the present diagnostic does not isolate an aberration
budget or verify the claimed system optimization.

### L12 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.85000, νd = 42.72. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.85, νd 42.72; coarse patent coordinates). f = +38.24 mm.

L12 follows the large front air gap and supplies positive power within G1. Its material is one of the two negative
terms in the patent's front-group Abbe-number difference. A coordinate-close modern HOYA glass is not proof of
historical identity; the relevant chronology is addressed in the glass section.

### L13 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.85000, νd = 36.59. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.85, νd 36.59; coarse patent coordinates). f = +65.28 mm.

L13 adds positive standalone power after L12. It shares the printed nd value with L12 but has a different Abbe
number, so treating the two as one identified material would discard source information. Its front-to-rear shape
is preserved directly from Table 1, without an inferred asphere.

### L14 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.51000, νd = 81.59. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.51, νd 81.59; coarse patent coordinates). f = +157.76 mm.

L14 has comparatively weak positive standalone power and is one of the two low-dispersion front elements identified
by ¶0065. Its printed index differs materially from common catalog glasses with a closely similar Abbe number.
The index is retained rather than replaced to obtain a preferred EFL or focus result.

### L15 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.48000, νd = 70.44. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.48, νd 70.44; coarse patent coordinates). f = −70.03 mm.

L15 closes G1 immediately before the stop region. Although its material has a high Abbe number, the element's
standalone power is negative. The patent's use of low-dispersion materials in this group does not establish APO
performance or a unique secondary-spectrum correction from the available nd/νd pair alone.

### L21 — Biconcave Negative, Cemented to L22

nd = 1.85000, νd = 23.78. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.85, νd 23.78; coarse patent coordinates). f = −10.75 mm.

L21 is the negative member of the source's sole cemented pair. Surface 13 is its rear interface and L22's entry
interface; the modeled medium changes directly from nd 1.85 to 1.73, with no synthetic air or cement layer.
The standalone focal length here is calculated with air on both sides solely to make element powers comparable.

### L22 — Biconvex Positive, Cemented to L21

nd = 1.73000, νd = 54.67. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.73, νd 54.67; coarse patent coordinates). f = +16.41 mm.

L22 is the positive member of that pair. Its higher Abbe number contrasts with L21's lower value, but this material
contrast alone is insufficient to certify an achromatic correction. The actual cemented pair has net EFL
−64.111835 mm in air. The complete G2 nevertheless has positive net power once L23 and the source spacings are
included. Net group power is not obtained by assigning the cemented interface independently to two air-spaced elements.

### L23 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.93000, νd = 20.88. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.93, νd 20.88; coarse patent coordinates). f = +28.99 mm.

L23 supplies positive power at the end of G2. Its printed index is the highest among the eleven lens elements,
and its Abbe number is the lowest. The rear air gap is D1 in Table 2. That source variable remains visible in the
raw diagnostic record, but the application candidate fixes it at the literal base value rather than implementing
an inconsistent physical focus movement.

### L31 — Negative Meniscus, Proposed Focus Element

nd = 1.75000, νd = 28.30. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.75, νd 28.30; coarse patent coordinates). f = −41.31 mm.

L31 alone forms G3. Paragraphs 0060 and 0073 describe it as the translating negative focus element.
The diagnostic preserves its literal shape, index and base station. It does not animate that proposed mechanism,
because the two tabulated neighboring-gap changes are incompatible with fixed G2 and G4.

### L41 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.73000, νd = 54.67. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.73, νd 54.67; coarse patent coordinates). f = +20.99 mm.

L41 begins G4 after the D2 gap. It has the same printed material coordinates as L22, which supports sharing a
coordinate class but not a supplier identification. Its standalone positive power combines with L42 to give
G4's separately calculated net positive power.

### L42 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.85000, νd = 23.78. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.85, νd 23.78; coarse patent coordinates). f = −39.76 mm.

L42 ends the lens group and shares its printed material coordinates with L21. The source's physical rear chain
begins at its exit surface: 14.29 mm of air, the 2.00 mm GL plate, then 1.00 mm of air to the source image.
The model preserves that chain; the computed best-focus plane is reported separately rather than moving the
source image plane to conceal defocus.

## Focus Mechanism

The source proposes movement of G3/L31 toward the image while G1, G2 and G4 remain fixed relative to the image
(¶0060). Table 2 instead gives:

| Raw source state       | D1 (mm) | D2 (mm) | D1 + D2 (mm) | Printed EFL (mm) |
| ---------------------- | ------: | ------: | -----------: | ---------------: |
| Infinity label         |    1.00 |    4.43 |         5.43 |            23.44 |
| Closest, labeled 0.3 m |    3.76 |    1.88 |         5.64 |            22.95 |

D1 increases by 2.76 mm while D2 decreases by 2.55 mm. Their 0.21 mm sum change exceeds the 0.02 mm maximum
combined rounding allowance for four gaps printed to 0.01 mm, if nearest rounding is assumed. The source does
not specify its quantization rule. Neither changing D1 nor changing D2 to enforce
conservation is justified by the source. The parallel application and later grant repeat these numbers.

The closest row's literal EFL computes to 22.905770 mm. At its printed image plane, its paraxial conjugate is
160.954974 mm object-to-first-vertex, or 246.944974 mm object-to-image. Because the source does not identify which
reference plane its 0.3 m label uses, neither interpretation is certified. These closest-row computations belong
to the raw-source diagnostic branch and are not application focus states.

The implementation status is **NO_INTERNAL_RECONSTRUCTION**. Operational focus is not modeled: `var` and
`varLabels` are empty, and `closeFocusM = 1e15` is an infinity-only UI sentinel, not the production minimum focus
distance. The application preserves only the literal source-labeled base prescription, including its defocus.
The actual control-availability calculation disables focus and presents “Not modeled”. The raw closest row is
retained here and in the evidence without creating a false physical focus law. Viltrox's documented STM/lead-screw
internal-focus mechanism is a production specification; it does not supply the missing correction to these tables.[1–4]

## Diagnostic Geometry and Limitations

All clear semi-diameters are modeled rather than published. Figure 1 provides qualitative proportions, and exact
ray/material checks constrain the apertures. The physical iris radius of 8.73515648 mm is inferred by the real runtime
from the f/1.4 target. The author's earlier paraxial iris estimate was not used to transfer clearance claims to the
runtime. The final authored and runtime iris radii agree within 2×10⁻¹² mm; this agreement is calibration, not a
measurement of a production diaphragm.

The actual runtime validator accepts the data and the production element-geometry diagnostics report zero hidden
trim. Five nominal control positions confirm unchanged static geometry and disabled focus. Exact 3D probes sample
25 circular-pupil positions per field at 0°, ±19.02° and ±31.7°, repeated at those five static control positions.
All 25 on-axis samples pass; 15/25 pass at each ±19.02° field and 5/25 at each ±31.7° field. These are finite-grid
counts, not transmission percentages. The remaining samples vignette at exposed entry surfaces; they do not
establish full-pupil edge-field clearance or measured production illumination.

A separate portable exact meridional calculation samples 21 pupil fractions at the same five fields. It confirms
the on-axis bundle and the modeled iris, retaining off-axis clipping rather than treating it as full-field success.
Both methods use the literal geometry and inferred apertures. They do not establish a continuum proof, a physical
focus mechanism, corrected aberration performance, or a unique production lens prescription. No aspherical
surface or polynomial departure is present.

A separate source-first audit also tested a denser 161-point circular-pupil grid per field using freshly written
3D intersection/Snell code. The real engine was run on the same grid: both returned 161/161 clear on axis,
98/161 at each ±19.02° field and 47/161 at each ±31.7° field, with no nonphysical failure or internal cemented-group
clipping. The denser grid additionally detects clipping at exposed surface 4 at ±19.02°; the smaller-grid entry-surface
statement above is limited to its stated samples. Exact spherical shared-band geometry gives minimum element
thickness 0.124407 mm and minimum gap-policy margin 0.090000 mm. Actual runtime d-line indices remain the literal
patent values, with Abbe fallback and no selected vendor glass. These independent checks support the bounded static
diagnostic only; they do not resolve the failed source EFL, conjugate, group-condition or focus-conservation checks.
