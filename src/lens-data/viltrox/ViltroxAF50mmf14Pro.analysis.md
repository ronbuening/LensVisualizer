# VILTROX AF 50mm f/1.4 PRO

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** CN 120871380 A\
**Application Number:** 202410457423.X\
**Filed:** 2024-04-16\
**Published:** 2025-10-31\
**Inventors:** Liu Ruijun; Chen Baofeng\
**Applicant:** Shenzhen Viltrox Technology Co., Ltd.\
**Title:** 一种大光圈内合焦式标准摄影镜头及摄影装置\
**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 1, as a fitted prescription

This model is **not a literal transcription of the patent table, an established correction of it, or a factory prescription**. Example 1's printed numbers are internally inconsistent, so the radii, aspheric coefficients and several spacings were fitted to the patent's own stated focal lengths and aberration curves while its topology and glasses were kept. It covers infinity focus only.

The product correlation is supported by convergent construction evidence:

1. Example 1 and the manufacturer manual both show 15 elements in 11 air-separated groups, excluding the camera-side plate.
2. The manual's three ED-marked positions correspond to L06, L08, and L11. Its sole aspherical element corresponds to L10, with the two aspheric surfaces S17/S18 in the patent.
3. The manual distinguishes eight high-index elements from the aspheric element. Counting numeric nd ≥ 1.8 gives nine elements including L10, consistent with that separate category convention, but not proof of manufacturer glass identities.
4. The patent's 51.1 mm infinity design and f/1.47 plot labels are compatible with a marketed 50 mm f/1.4 lens.
5. Both sources describe internal focus. The patent moves G2 only; the manual identifies a VCM drive and 0.45 m minimum focus.
6. Viltrox sells the lens as the AF 50mm F1.4 Pro in Sony E (FE) and Nikon Z mounts. The Nikon Z product page states the same 15 elements in 11 groups, with 1 ultra-precision aspherical, 3 ED and 8 high-refractive elements. It lists the Z version at Φ84.5 × 113 mm and about 830 g, so the barrel differs between mounts while the stated optical construction does not.

These similarities associate the construction with the product. They do not resolve the numerical discrepancies in the printed table. Traced paraxially as printed, Table 1 gives an infinity EFL of 50.55 mm against the stated 51.1 mm and brings focus 2.87 mm behind the printed image plane; with the printed near gaps the EFL is 45.74 mm against the stated 45.9 mm. Aspheric terms cannot change these first-order values. The printed coefficients (for example S17 A8 = −2.598 × 10⁻² and S18 A4 = +2.934 × 10⁻²) give sags of metres at the element rim under the standard dimensional equation. No single changed digit, sign or transposed pair in Table 1 restores both stated focal lengths and the printed focus position. Examples 2 and 3 are also first-order inconsistent (paraxial EFL about 38.6 mm and 80.1 mm against stated 50.5 mm). The patent's equation and coefficient normalization are not printed, so these remain unexplained source inconsistencies rather than identified misprints.

## Optical Architecture

The source has three functional groups with positive-positive-negative power. G1 comprises L01–L07, G2 comprises L08–L11, and G3 comprises L12–L15. The stop is S13 between G1 and G2. Four cemented pairs are L02/L03, L06/L07, L08/L09, and L13/L14.

This is an internally focusing fast standard-lens architecture with an asymmetric front collector, a positive moving middle unit, and a negative rear unit. Calling it a simple double-Gauss would obscure its strongly asymmetric element and power distribution. The general roles below are optical interpretations of the patent construction and the fitted prescription, not manufacturer design statements.

The camera-side GL plate is 2.5 mm thick, with nd = 1.5168 and νd = 64.198. It is traced as a physical plate and excluded from the 15-element drawing and count. The printed final air interval is 1.000 mm; the model uses 0.996 mm, placing the image plane 0.004 mm closer.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Element focal lengths below are isolated thick-element values in air, calculated from the fitted radii and center thicknesses. For cemented elements they are descriptive values, not in-situ group powers. Numeric nd/νd are preserved from the source. Thirteen elements use a catalog glass whose nd/νd match the source as a spectral proxy for chromatic tracing; Viltrox's actual glass identities and suppliers remain unresolved.

### L01 — Meniscus Negative

nd = 1.54810, νd = 45.82. Glass: Hoya E-FEL1 as a catalog-equivalent spectral proxy; supplier unresolved. f = -112.434 mm.

The very weakly curved front face and stronger rear face produce negative isolated power. It precedes the first positive cemented combination and helps distribute the large entrance bundle.

### L02 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.92290, νd = 20.88. Glass: Schott N-SF66 as a catalog-equivalent spectral proxy; supplier unresolved. f = -27.860 mm.

This high-index, low-Abbe negative element shares S4 with L03. The low Abbe number makes it a dispersive partner rather than an ED element.

### L03 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.83480, νd = 42.725. Glass: Ohara S-LAH55V as a catalog-equivalent spectral proxy; supplier unresolved. f = +38.885 mm.

The positive high-index, higher-Abbe member completes the front cemented doublet. The pairing offers opposing chromatic contributions without requiring a large air separation.

### L04 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.94900, νd = 17.942. Glass: unmatched (no catalog glass within source precision); Abbe surrogate; supplier unresolved. f = +62.458 mm.

The high index supports substantial positive power with less curvature than a lower-index material would require. The distinct source nd = 1.949 is retained; it is not silently changed to the repeated 1.946 elsewhere. Example 3 prints the same 1.949/17.942 for its L04, while every 1.946 element in the three examples carries the same νd = 17.942, which is CDGM H-ZF88's catalog value (nd 1.94595). That makes a 6-for-9 misprint of 1.946 likely, but the patent does not confirm it.

### L05 — Meniscus Positive

nd = 1.92290, νd = 20.88. Glass: Schott N-SF66 as a catalog-equivalent spectral proxy; supplier unresolved. f = +76.277 mm.

This positive meniscus continues the fixed front power distribution. Both of its radii are fitted values.

### L06 — Plano-Convex Positive

nd = 1.59280, νd = 68.342. Glass: CDGM H-ZPK5 as a catalog-equivalent spectral proxy; supplier unresolved. f = +50.920 mm.

The manufacturer diagram marks this position as ED. Its nd/νd match CDGM's H-ZPK5 phosphate crown, whose catalog partial dispersion lies about +0.016 above the normal line; that curve is a proxy, not an identification of the production glass.

### L07 — Plano-Concave Negative

nd = 1.84670, νd = 23.791. Glass: CDGM H-ZF52 as a catalog-equivalent spectral proxy; supplier unresolved. f = -26.318 mm.

The plano-concave negative partner shares the plane S11 interface with L06. Its low Abbe number supports achromatizing action immediately ahead of the stop.

### L08 — Meniscus Positive

nd = 1.60300, νd = 65.46. Glass: Ohara S-PHM53 as a catalog-equivalent spectral proxy; supplier unresolved. f = +55.607 mm.

The manufacturer diagram marks this position as ED. Its nd/νd match Ohara's S-PHM53 phosphate crown (catalog ΔPgF about +0.007), used as the spectral proxy. It is the positive front member of the G2 cemented pair and moves with the rest of G2 in the source mechanism.

### L09 — Meniscus Negative

nd = 1.64770, νd = 33.84. Glass: CDGM H-ZF1 as a catalog-equivalent spectral proxy; supplier unresolved. f = -26.527 mm.

The low-Abbe negative member completes that doublet. Its strong concave front interface and much weaker rear face split power and dispersion within the moving group.

### L10 — Biconvex Positive (2× Asph)

nd = 1.80610, νd = 40.731. Glass: Hoya M-NBFD130 as a catalog-equivalent spectral proxy; supplier unresolved. f = +46.367 mm.

This is the only double-aspherical element. Its two fitted even-power profiles provide higher-order correction in the model, but are not the literal printed coefficients. The matching catalog glass, Hoya M-NBFD130, is a precision-moulding type, which fits a moulded asphere, but the manufacturing process is not established.

### L11 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.59280, νd = 68.342. Glass: CDGM H-ZPK5 as a catalog-equivalent spectral proxy; supplier unresolved. f = +67.309 mm.

The manufacturer diagram marks this position as ED; like L06 it uses the H-ZPK5 proxy. It ends G2 ahead of the variable D2 gap. Its front radius R19 keeps the printed +367.935 mm; its rear radius and centre thickness are fitted.

### L12 — Meniscus Positive

nd = 1.72920, νd = 56.685. Glass: unmatched (no catalog glass within source precision); Abbe surrogate; supplier unresolved. f = +143.482 mm.

Example 3 prints the same 1.7292/56.685 for its L12, so the pair is a consistent design material rather than a one-off value; the nearest catalog glasses (nd 1.72916) have νd about 54.7. The positive meniscus begins the fixed rear unit. Its position contributes to off-axis correction and pupil mapping, although the complete group is negative.

### L13 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.94600, νd = 17.942. Glass: CDGM H-ZF88 as a catalog-equivalent spectral proxy; supplier unresolved. f = +69.117 mm.

This high-index positive element is the front member of the rear cemented doublet. Its material retains the source nd = 1.946 and νd = 17.942.

### L14 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.80520, νd = 25.456. Glass: CDGM H-ZF7LA as a catalog-equivalent spectral proxy; supplier unresolved. f = -57.034 mm.

The negative member of the rear doublet uses a different low-Abbe high-index material. It shares S24 with L13; the following air space is a fitted value.

### L15 — Meniscus Negative

nd = 1.94600, νd = 17.942. Glass: CDGM H-ZF88 as a catalog-equivalent spectral proxy; supplier unresolved. f = -98.297 mm.

The final negative meniscus supplies rear negative power. Its front rim meets L14's rear rim at 16.6 mm, as Figure 1 draws it; the corner bundle is limited mainly by L01 and the G2 rims, and none of these is factory vignetting data.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The source supplies refractive indices and Abbe numbers rather than verified supplier names. Thirteen elements are assigned catalog glasses whose nd and νd match the source (nd within 5 × 10⁻⁵, νd within 0.02). These serve as spectral proxies, so the chromatic trace follows their catalog dispersion curves. They do not identify Viltrox's suppliers. L04 and L12 have no catalog glass within source precision and keep an Abbe-number surrogate. ΔPgF below is relative to the normal line 0.6438 − 0.001682 νd.

The patent text requires νd ≥ 60 for L06, L08 and L11 but names no special or anomalous-dispersion glass; the ED marking comes from the manufacturer's diagram. The ED-marked L06, L08 and L11 share correction duties with more dispersive partners. Their proxies are phosphate crowns with only moderate positive ΔPgF, while the dense flints (N-SF66, H-ZF88) carry large positive ΔPgF, so the proxy set leaves an ordinary secondary spectrum. In a paraxial trace at infinity, the C and F foci fall 41 µm and 54 µm behind the d focus, 14 µm apart, and the g focus falls 0.28 mm behind it. With an Abbe-only surrogate for all fifteen elements, the same prescription gives 17, 30 and 114 µm. These figures describe the proxies; the production glasses and their partial dispersions are unpublished, and no apochromatic or factory polychromatic-performance claim is made.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent moves G2, L08–L11, toward the object by 7.859 mm. G1 and G3 remain fixed relative to the image. The source settings are:

| Source state | D1 after stop (mm) | D2 after S20 (mm) | EFL label (mm) | Object-distance label |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Infinity | 13.125 | 3.000 | 51.1 | Infinity |
| Near | 5.266 | 10.859 | 45.9 | 0.32 m |

The manufacturer manual separately gives 0.45 m minimum focus, 0.145× maximum magnification, and internal focus with a VCM motor. The patent's 0.32 m reference plane remains unknown. Those two labels are not silently equated.

The fitted prescription also changes the infinity D2 gap (3.000 → 2.347 mm), so the printed travel does not reproduce the printed near state. Applying the 7.859 mm travel to the fitted radii gives EFL 45.90 mm with focus at an object about 342 mm before the first vertex (467 mm before the image), neither of which matches the 0.32 m label under an obvious reference. Ray-traced spot sizes at that conjugate are also about seven times larger than at infinity.

Accordingly, **close focus is not modelled**: the focus control does not move any group, and the 0.45 m figure is product metadata only.

## Aspherical Surfaces

S17 and S18 bound L10. The numerical reconstruction assumes the standard dimensional sag

$z(h)=h^2/[R(1+\sqrt{1-(1+K)h^2/R^2})]+A_4h^4+A_6h^6+A_8h^8+A_{10}h^{10}$,

with h, R, and z in millimetres and K = 0 on both surfaces. K = 0 is explicitly printed in the source tables. The source's coefficient normalization and precise equation have not been established. The fitted coefficients below must therefore be distinguished from the source numbers.

| Surface | Term | Printed source | Fitted dimensional coefficient |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| S17 | A4 | -1.2354297270e-03 | -1.3167749515e-06 |
| S17 | A6 | 5.0279448160e-05 | 6.6077781238e-09 |
| S17 | A8 | -2.5978067700e-02 | -1.3000806190e-11 |
| S17 | A10 | 1.4040232890e-19 | 9.9473429815e-15 |
| S18 | A4 | 2.9336044030e-02 | 2.7285451880e-06 |
| S18 | A6 | 1.6022580370e-05 | 3.8036869577e-09 |
| S18 | A8 | -1.0576774830e-19 | -2.6343496588e-12 |
| S18 | A10 | 1.2669782850e-11 | 1.1594375528e-18 |

The eight fitted terms retain the printed signs but not their magnitudes. Each was bounded during fitting to contribute at most 3 mm of sag at a 16 mm height. They are neither established exponent corrections nor an identified normalization. At higher ray heights the terms work together; the sign of one coefficient alone is not a complete aberration diagnosis. The manual identifies an ultra-high-precision aspherical element, but does not establish a specific molding or polishing process here.

## Fitted Prescription and Aperture

The fit varied 23 of the 25 finite radii (each within ±10 % of its printed value), the eight aspheric coefficients and four spacings, against the patent's stated 51.1 mm and 45.9 mm focal lengths and readings of its spherical-aberration and field-curvature plots (Figs. 2–3). The largest radius changes are R12 +10.0 % (20.258 → 22.284 mm), R24 +8.5 %, R18 +8.4 %, R27 −8.0 % and R2 +7.4 %; most others move by less than 4 %. The spacing changes alter seven printed intervals, including the L08 and L11 centre thicknesses (+0.09 and +0.56 mm); the 125.3 mm track is kept. Printed nd/νd, topology, the flat surfaces, K = 0, R7 = −150.275 mm and R19 = +367.935 mm are unchanged. The fitted system has EFL 51.10 mm, and its four spot-checked spherical-aberration readings fall within 9 µm of the patent plot.

Clear semi-diameters are estimated from the patent's Figure 1, not measured. The figure draws flat-topped rims. L01–L05 sit near 23 mm and the elements behind the stop between 14 and 17.4 mm. Every rim clears the axial f/1.48 marginal ray, and L04's rim sits on it. The figure draws L01 and the L02–L03 doublet as one square-cut block, with L01 meeting L02 at the rim. S1–S5 therefore share 22.0 mm, about 0.9 mm below the drawn block, which takes a 98 % cross-gap sag allowance; the fitted S2 and S3 would cross at 22.25 mm. S25/S26 share 16.6 mm, where the figure draws them meeting, at 97 % of their gap; the fitted faces would cross near 16.8 mm. Straight tapered edges are drawn, not barrel dimensions.

The patent's f/1.47 is a paraxial entrance-pupil value. The model's iris radius of 14.52 mm gives f/1.48 by exact marginal-ray tracing; the catalog keeps the f/1.47 design and f/1.4 marketed labels. Only the wide-open aperture is modelled.

## Model Scope and Limitations

- The prescription is a fit, not the printed table. It reproduces the patent's stated infinity focal length and approximately reproduces its aberration curves, but other prescriptions could match the same curves, so individual radii, thicknesses and aspheric profiles should not be read as Viltrox's production values.
- At infinity the traced RMS spot is about 3–8 µm at the d line and 10–14 µm with C, d and F weighted equally, across the full-frame field. Because the drawn rims sit close to the axial beam, off-axis clipping starts within the first few degrees. Geometric transmission is about 89 % at 5.8°, 75 % at 11.6° and about 35 % at the corner. These are geometric estimates, not measured illumination.
- Close focus, stopped-down apertures, factory clear apertures, the true stop diameter and Viltrox's actual glasses are not modelled. Chromatic views use catalog proxy dispersion for 13 elements and an Abbe-number approximation for L04 and L12.
- The patent's aberration plots do not state whether their ordinate is normalized to the iris or the entrance pupil; the fit assumes the iris.
