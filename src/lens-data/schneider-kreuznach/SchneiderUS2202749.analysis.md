# SCHNEIDER-KREUZNACH 100mm f/3.5 (US 2,202,749 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,202,749 A — Asymmetric Objective

**Inventors:** Albrecht Wilhelm Tronnier

**Assignee:** Jos. Schneider & Co., Optotechnische Gesellschaft

**Published:** 28 May 1940

**Filed:** 9 August 1938; German priority 4 October 1935

**Example details:** Example II, the lower table on PDF page 2, specifies f = 100 mm and f/3.5.

The [patent facsimile](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/2202749)
gives two numerical examples. Example I uses violet-ray indices and f/5;
Example II uses yellow-ray indices and f/3.5. Figure 2 depicts the Example I
form, with curved R2 and R5. The text describes the tabulated designs as
analogous to Figure 2; Example II has planar R2 and R5.

No production lens is documented for this prescription.

## Optical Architecture

Five elements form two air-spaced cemented groups: a front triplet and a rear
doublet. R2 and R3 join the triplet, and R6 joins the doublet. The front group
has a calculated focal length of −646.35 mm in air; the rear group is strongly
positive at +69.25 mm.

The printed axial intervals are 7.6, 6.1, 1.8, 10.9, 1.8 and 14.1 mm, for
42.3 mm from the first to the last optical vertex. The 10.9 mm interval is
the diaphragm space. The model divides it into 7.6 mm after R4 and 3.3 mm
before R5; that stop position is inferred.

The patent's index-difference condition gives
ΔN = (n1 − n2) + (n2 − n3) − (n4 − n5) = 0.1231,
within its stated range of greater than 0.005 and at most 0.185.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below describe each element in air, rather than its power
inside the cemented assembly.

### L1 — Plano-Convex Positive

nd = 1.7015, νd = 41.2. Glass: BAFD7 (Hoya), an optical equivalent.
f = +41.63 mm.

The +29.2 mm front sphere and planar cemented rear surface enclose 7.6 mm.
L1 forms the positive front element of the triplet. Its inferred clear
semi-diameters are 17.5 mm on both faces, with a minimum edge thickness of
about 1.775 mm.

### L2 — Plano-Convex Positive

nd = 1.4875, νd = 70.0. Glass: S-FSL5 (Ohara), spectral proxy. f = +84.31 mm.

The plane front face and −41.1 mm rear face enclose 6.1 mm. This low-index
central element is cemented to both neighbours. Its inferred clear
semi-diameters are 17.5 and 18 mm; the minimum edge thickness is about 2.188 mm.

### L3 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.6545, νd = 33.8. Glass: SF9 (Hikari), an optical equivalent.
f = −22.03 mm.

The −41.1 and +22.6 mm faces enclose 1.8 mm. L3 supplies negative power at the
rear of the triplet and exits into the diaphragm space. Its inferred clear
semi-diameters are 18 and 14 mm.

### L4 — Plano-Concave Negative

nd = 1.6254, νd = 35.6. Glass: F7 (Hoya), an optical equivalent.
f = −34.70 mm.

The planar R5 and +21.7 mm cemented R6 enclose 1.8 mm. L4 is the negative
front element of the rear doublet. Both inferred clear semi-diameters are 14.5 mm.

### L5 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.7015, νd = 41.2. Glass: BAFD7 (Hoya), an optical equivalent.
f = +24.65 mm.

The +21.7 and −62.3 mm faces enclose 14.1 mm. L5 supplies the rear group's
positive collecting power. Both inferred clear semi-diameters are 14.5 mm;
the working-edge thickness is about 6.83 mm.

## Glass Identification and Index Reference

The patent says only “yellow ray.” Four of its five index/Abbe pairs match
helium d catalogue values, while none matches the D line (taken as sodium,
589.3 nm). The model therefore uses helium d, 587.6 nm, with a note identifying
the inference. The printed indices and Abbe numbers remain unchanged.

At that inferred line, L1/L5 match BAFD7 (Δn = +0.000043, Δν = −0.05)
and L4 matches F7 (Δn = −0.000037, Δν = −0.02) from the
[Hoya catalogue](https://www.hoya-opticalworld.com/english/datadownload/index.html).
L3 matches SF9 (Δn = −0.000040, Δν = +0.06) from the
[Hikari catalogue](https://www.hikari-g.co.jp/optical_glass/catalog/).
These pairs meet the catalogue matching tolerances of 0.0001 in index
and 0.15 in Abbe number.

L2 uses [Ohara's S-FSL5](https://www.ohara-inc.co.jp/en/product/catalog/)
as a spectral proxy (Δn = −0.00001, Δν = +0.24), within the site's
spectral-proxy tolerance of 0.002 in index and 0.25 in Abbe number.
Differences are catalogue minus printed values at the inferred helium-d
line. The model retains L2's printed index and Abbe number and uses the
catalogue curve for wavelength dependence.
All five elements use catalogue curves; none remains unmatched. These rows
do not identify historical suppliers or melts. No partial-dispersion data or
measured MTF curves are published.

## Focus Mechanism and First-Order Values

The model is focused at infinity. The patent supplies no close-focus
spacing, focus travel, minimum focus distance or mount.

The calculated EFL is 99.936 mm, 0.064 mm below the printed 100 mm. The
paraxial back focus is 77.95 mm, giving a first-vertex-to-image distance of
120.25 mm. No back focus is printed; the last gap places the image at the
computed paraxial plane.

## Aperture, Field and Model Limits

The inferred stop lies 23.1 mm behind the first vertex. Its 11.18 mm radius
gives paraxial f/3.50; exact rays reach an entrance-pupil radius of 14.20 mm,
equivalent to about f/3.52. The stop clears R4's inferred rim by about
2.74 mm, and the minimum air clearance between the groups is about 6.04 mm.

The patent describes the design class as retaining a useful angle of about
60°, without assigning that field to Example II. The model retains an
illustrative ±12.0° field. Its chief ray reaches 21.09 mm image height there.
Wide-open geometric transmission is about 99.8% at 12°. Chief rays also
reach 20°, with increasing vignetting at the front and rear rims.

All clear semi-diameters are estimated. Figure 2 supplies proportions only:
the triplet's rear face and the rear doublet are about 79% and 82% of the
front group's body radius. The retained 17.5–18 mm front body, 14 mm R4
and square-cut 14.5 mm rear doublet follow those proportions while clearing
the f/3.5 axial beam and 12° chief ray. The drawing's curved R2/R5 belong
to Example I, so neither its absolute rim dimensions nor those curvatures
establish Example II's construction.
