# Schneider-Kreuznach US 2,202,749 A — Example II audit

## Source

US 2,202,749 A, “Asymmetric Objective,” was published on 28 May 1940.
Albrecht Wilhelm Tronnier assigned it to Jos. Schneider & Co.,
Optotechnische Gesellschaft. The US filing date is 9 August 1938 and the
German priority date is 4 October 1935.

## Transcription

Two independent readings of the patent page images agree on all seven
radii, five element thicknesses, the 10.9 mm diaphragm space, five indices
and five Abbe numbers in Example II. Every radius sign is printed; R2 and
R5 are planes. The cemented interfaces are R2, R3 and R6. No numerical
transcription correction is needed.

Example I is f/5 with violet-ray indices. Example II is f/3.5 with yellow-ray
indices. Figure 2 depicts the Example I form; Example II is described as
analogous to it. The earlier attribution of the drawing directly to Example
II is closed by this distinction.

## First-Order Values

The printed focal length is 100 mm at f/3.5. The calculated EFL is
99.936000 mm, back focus 77.948626 mm and total track 120.248626 mm.
The optical vertex track is 42.3 mm. The stored image gap is the paraxial
back focus, with zero paraxial defocus. Exact rays at the inferred paraxial
iris give an entrance-equivalent aperture of about f/3.520.

The inferred stop radius is 11.182944 mm. A 14.276571 mm entrance marginal
corresponding to nominal f/3.5 reaches 11.247923 mm at the stop, slightly
outside that opening. The optical rims admit that marginal; the small
aperture difference is due to the iris convention.

## Values Not Printed

The 7.6 / 3.3 mm division of the diaphragm space, stop size, all clear
semi-diameters and the image distance are inferred. The minimum air
clearance is about 3.744 mm; no optical rim crosses the stop plane.

The ±12° field is illustrative. The text's
useful angle of about 60° applies to the design class, not specifically to
Example II. No format, mount or close-focus prescription is given.

## Product Identification

No production lens is documented for this five-element, two-group asymmetric
prescription. The patent establishes the design and its Schneider
assignment, without a commercial model name.

## Corrections and Remaining Questions

The earlier D-line convention is superseded by the helium d interpretation:
four of five printed index/Abbe pairs match d-line catalogue values and none
matches the D line (taken as sodium, 589.3 nm). The patent itself says only
“yellow ray.” L2 remains Unmatched because the nearest FK5-family Abbe
numbers exceed the printed 70.0.

Open: Example II's clear rims remain unmeasured; Figure 2 depicts the other
example's form. The original glass supplier and melts, the exact historical
iris station and diameter, and any production association remain unknown.
