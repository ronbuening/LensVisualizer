# SCHNEIDER-KREUZNACH 100mm f/3.5 (US 2,202,749 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,202,749 A, “Asymmetric Objective”](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2202749A/en)
names Albrecht Wilhelm Tronnier and the assignee Jos. Schneider & Co.,
Optotechnische Gesellschaft. The US application was filed on 9 August 1938,
with German priority dated 4 October 1935; publication was on 28 May 1940.

The [patent facsimile](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/2202749)
prints Example II in the lower table on PDF page 2, at f = 100 mm and f/3.5.
The drawing sheet is PDF page 1. Figure 2 shows the f/5 Example I form;
Example II is described as analogous to it, with planar R2 and R5.

## Transcription Check

All seven radii, five element thicknesses, the 10.9 mm diaphragm space,
five refractive indices and five Abbe numbers agree with the page images.
Every finite radius has its sign printed; R2 and R5 are planes. R2, R3 and
R6 are cemented interfaces. No numerical transcription error was found,
and the dimensions retain the printed millimetre scale.

Example I uses violet-ray indices; Example II uses yellow-ray indices.
The patent does not identify a wavelength or spectral line for either.
The Example II indices give ΔN = 0.1231 in the patent's expression,
within its stated range greater than 0.005 and at most 0.185.

## First-Order Check

The calculated EFL is 99.936 mm, 0.064 mm below the printed 100 mm.
The paraxial aperture is f/3.50, agreeing with the printed f/3.5.
Calculated back focus is 77.95 mm and the first-vertex-to-image distance is
120.25 mm. The optical vertex track is 42.3 mm. No back focus is printed;
the final air gap places the image at the calculated paraxial plane.

The inferred stop radius is 11.183 mm. Exact rays give an entrance-pupil
radius of 14.196 mm, equivalent to f/3.52. A nominal f/3.5 entrance marginal
at 14.277 mm radius reaches the stop at 11.248 mm, slightly beyond the
opening. The optical rims admit that marginal; the iris accounts for the
small difference between the paraxial and finite-aperture f-numbers.

The surfaces stay clear of each other. The 12.0° chief ray reaches
21.09 mm image height without blockage. Chief rays can reach about 20°,
where the marginal bundle has about 14% vignetting at R1.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The 10.9 mm diaphragm space is divided into 7.6 mm after R4 and 3.3 mm
before R5, placing the stop 23.1 mm behind the first vertex. This station,
the iris diameter and every clear semi-diameter are inferred. The retained
clear radii at R1–R7 are 17.5, 17.5, 18, 16.5, 17, 17 and 17 mm.
Minimum air clearance is about 3.74 mm; R4's rim clears the stop plane
by about 0.44 mm. Figure 2 supports the topology, but its different R2/R5
curvatures do not establish Example II's rim sizes.

The ±12.0° field is illustrative. The text's useful angle of about 60°
describes the design class without specifying Example II's useful field.
No image-circle diameter or image format is established for this example.

`focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. The patent gives
no close-focus spacings, travel or minimum focus distance. The selectable
f-stops extend from f/3.5 to f/22, but the source supplies no minimum iris
opening or production stop range.

Helium d at 587.6 nm is inferred from the catalogue agreement of four
element index/Abbe pairs; none of those pairs agrees at sodium D, 589.3 nm.
The patent itself says only “yellow ray.” The named glasses supply
approximate dispersion curves without establishing a historical supplier
or melt. L2 remains Unmatched: FK5-family indices are close to 1.4875,
but their nearest Abbe number is about 70.24 rather than the printed 70.0.
L2 therefore uses an Abbe-based dispersion estimate. No measured partial
dispersion or MTF curves are supplied.

## Product Identification

No production lens is documented for this five-element, two-group
asymmetric prescription. The patent establishes the design and Schneider
assignment without a commercial model name. Marketing specifications,
mounts, a production image format and diaphragm blade details are not
assigned.

## Corrections, 2026-11-21

- The key and title were replaced by the maker and patent form, and the
  maker was added as Schneider-Kreuznach to reflect the named assignee.
  The title is “SCHNEIDER-KREUZNACH 100mm f/3.5 (US 2,202,749 Design)”,
  and the design is listed in the catalogue.
- The patent number gained kind code A and publication year 1940.
  The abbreviated Schneider assignee became Jos. Schneider & Co.,
  Optotechnische Gesellschaft, following the patent's named party.
  Albrecht Wilhelm Tronnier and the Example II designation were retained.
- Example II was distinguished from the f/5 form shown in Figure 2.
  Its planar R2 and R5 follow the numerical table.
- The subtitle identifies “US 2,202,749 A, Example II — Schneider / Tronnier”.
  The specifications give “5 ELEMENTS / 2 GROUPS”, “ALL SPHERICAL”,
  “DESIGN f = 100 mm” and “DESIGN F/3.5”, preserving the printed scale
  without implying a production lens. The focus description identifies
  infinity focus and the absence of close-focus data.
- The catalogue design focal length changed from 100 to 99.94 mm to
  describe the calculated EFL; the printed design focal length remains
  100 mm.
- All five index references changed from sodium D to helium d, with an
  explanatory note about the unnamed yellow ray and catalogue agreement.
  Every printed index and Abbe number was retained.
- The former unidentified labels for L1/L5 became BAFD7 (Hoya),
  with Δn = +0.000043 and Δν = −0.05; L3 became SF9 (Hikari),
  with Δn = −0.000040 and Δν = +0.06; L4 became F7 (Hoya),
  with Δn = −0.000037 and Δν = −0.02. Differences are catalogue minus
  printed values. These optical equivalents supply dispersion estimates;
  L2 remains Unmatched. No anomalous-dispersion designation or additional
  spectral-line indices were added.
- Generic element shapes became plano-convex positive for L1/L2,
  biconcave negative for L3, plano-concave negative for L4 and biconvex
  positive for L5. Labels became Element 1–5, with L1–L5 on the diagram.
  Optical roles and calculated focal lengths in air were added:
  +41.63, +84.31, −22.03, −34.70 and +24.65 mm.
  C1 identifies the front triplet and C2 the rear doublet, with annotations
  spanning each complete cemented group.
- The inferred 42.19 mm image-circle diameter was removed because it came
  from the illustrative ±12.0° field rather than a published format or
  Example II field angle.

The resulting model retains EFL 99.94 mm, paraxial f/3.50, back focus
77.95 mm, first-vertex-to-image distance 120.25 mm, stop radius 11.183 mm
and half-field 12.0°. The finite-aperture result remains f/3.52.
Radii, thicknesses, spaces, indices, Abbe numbers, stop position,
clear rims, field and image plane retain their original numerical values.

## Remaining Questions

- The exact spectral line intended by “yellow ray” remains unidentified;
  helium d is an inference.
- Original glass suppliers and melts, including L2's material identity,
  are unknown.
- Example II's historical iris station, diameter, blade count, blade shape
  and stop range are not given.
- Example II's clear rims, useful field and image format are not specified.
- A production lens association, marketed focal length and aperture, mount,
  and minimum focus distance remain undocumented.
- Measured partial dispersion and imaging performance are not available
  from the cited source.
