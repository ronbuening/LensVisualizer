# SCHNEIDER-KREUZNACH XENAR 100mm f/3.5 (US 2,812,689 Design)

## Patent and Design Identification

**Patent:** [US 2,812,689 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2812689A/en), _Unsymmetrical Photographic Objective with Three Air-Spaced Members_

**Inventors:** Wolfram W. Albrecht

**Assignee:** Jos. Schneider & Co., Optische Werke

**Filed:** 10 March 1955

**Published:** 12 November 1957

**Priority:** Germany, 13 March 1954

**Example details:** Sole numerical example; the table in column 2 is repeated in the claim in column 3.

The patent prints nominal focal length 100 and aperture f/3.5 without specifying a physical length unit. The model represents these dimensions in millimeters. Both printed tables give the same seven signed radii, six thicknesses and spaces, and four glass index/Abbe pairs, including the front index 1.69347. Related publications are GB 769,241 A and FR 1,120,790 A; their numerical tables have not been compared here.

## Optical Architecture

This is an all-spherical Tessar-type objective: a positive front singlet (member I), a negative middle singlet (II), and a positive rear cemented doublet (III). Four elements form three air-spaced groups, with seven refracting surfaces and the L3/L4 cemented interface at R6. The cemented pair has a computed focal length of +59.74 mm. The patent cites Paul Rudolph's US 721,240, the original Tessar patent.

The patent places the diaphragm in the 7.06 mm space after R4 but gives no exact stop plane. The model divides that space into 4.50 mm before and 2.56 mm after the inferred stop, placing it 21.90 mm behind the first vertex. No filter or sensor plate is specified or modeled.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.69347, νd = 53.5. Glass: H-LaK6A (CDGM), catalog match. Standalone in-air f = +55.12 mm.

The front element supplies positive power. Its +37.3 mm front radius is much stronger than the +1373 mm rear radius, giving the nearly plano-convex appearance of the drawing while retaining a positive-meniscus shape.

### L2 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.62004, νd = 36.3. Glass: F2 (Schott), a catalog equivalent. Standalone in-air f = −35.44 mm.

The negative middle singlet balances the positive front and rear groups. Its −70.6 mm and +32.4 mm surfaces enclose the most dispersive glass in the prescription.

### L3 and L4 — Rear Cemented Doublet C1

L3: nd = 1.62364, νd = 36.7. Glass: Unmatched. Standalone in-air f = −49.62 mm. L4: nd = 1.74472, νd = 44.7. Glass: S-LAM2 (Ohara), spectral proxy. Standalone in-air f = +28.09 mm.

L3 is biconcave and negative; L4 is biconvex and positive. They share the +33.5 mm cemented surface. L3 provides the more dispersive negative member, while L4 supplies the rear component's converging power. Individual in-air focal lengths describe the separated elements; the cemented assembly is positive overall.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent defines nd at the yellow helium d line, 587.6 nm, and prints νd. All four printed pairs are retained. L1 matches CDGM H-LaK6A at nd 1.69350 / νd 53.38; L2 matches Schott F2 at nd 1.62004 / νd 36.37. Their catalog-minus-patent differences at the d line are Δn = +0.00003 / Δν = −0.12 and Δn = 0 / Δν = +0.07, respectively, within the match limits of 0.0001 in index and 0.15 in Abbe number. These row names do not identify the historical supplier or melt.

L4 uses Ohara S-LAM2 as a spectral proxy, with Δn = −0.00072 / Δν = +0.09 at the d line, within the proxy limits of 0.002 in index and 0.25 in Abbe number. The catalog curve supplies only the dispersion shape while the printed index and Abbe number are kept, so colour analysis follows a real glass curve rather than an Abbe-based estimate. It does not identify Schneider's historical glass. L3 remains Unmatched because no catalog row lies within those proxy limits; it retains an Abbe-based dispersion estimate. The patent prints no separate C-, F- or g-line indices or partial dispersions. No measured broadband or apochromatic performance is established.

## Focus Mechanism and Aperture

The model is focused at infinity; the patent gives no close-focus data or group movement law. The selectable f-stops span f/3.5 to f/32; the source documents only the full aperture.

The paraxial f-number is 3.50 with a stop radius of 11.51 mm. Finite-aperture estimates are f/3.55 from the entrance pupil and f/3.56 from the image-side sine angle. An f/3.5 axial marginal ray requires about 11.69 mm at the stop. The inferred opening differs from nominal by less than 3%; its axial position is also inferred.

## First-Order Properties and Model Limits

At the helium d line, the model computes EFL 100.13 mm against the printed 100, a difference of +0.1%. The paraxial back focal distance is 83.21 mm and sets the image plane; no back focal distance is printed. The optical track is 33.83 mm, matching the printed total, and the first-vertex-to-image distance is 117.04 mm.

The patent's dimensional conditions are satisfied: the first air space is 67.6% of the diaphragm space, the front thickness is 9.8% of nominal focal length, and R6 is 99.0% of optical track. The printed values retain their small focal-length residual.

The unprinted ±5.0° field is illustrative. The patent specifies no image format or image circle, so neither is assigned.

Clear semi-diameters are estimated at 18 / 18 / 12.5 / 11.7 / 12.6 / 13.7 / 13.7 mm from the sole figure's approximate proportions. Its major axial spans and front-surface curvature support a scale of about 32 pixels/mm at 600 dpi, but the first air gap is drawn about 11% long. The front element's 18 mm rims lie within about 5% of the drawing. The smaller rear face of L2 and front face of the rear doublet follow the drawn bevels; the L4 faces share one height. The minimum glass edge is about 2.38 mm, the R4–R5 common-radius gap is about 4.71 mm, and the maximum rim angle is about 28.9°. All rims clear the axial f/3.5 beam and the 5.0° chief ray; wide-open geometric transmission is about 99.9% at that illustrative field edge. These estimated optical boundaries do not describe a measured barrel or baffle.

## Design Heritage and Product Association

The Xenar name expresses a family association. The four-element Tessar-type architecture and Schneider attribution support it; the [Tessar reference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tessar#Tessar-derived_lenses) lists the Schneider Xenar among Tessar-derived lenses, and Franke & Heidecke's [Rolleicord V instruction manual](https://www.cameramanuals.org/rolleiflex/rolleicord_v.pdf) documents a Schneider Xenar f/3.5 taking lens. No source ties the prescription of US 2,812,689 A to that camera or to a specific production Xenar. Tronnier's US 2,084,714 Xenar and DE 753,329 describe different prescriptions.
