# LAOWA 10mm f/2.8 Zero-D FF

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** CN 221303696 U, Chinese utility model\
**Filed:** 2023-12-13\
**Published:** 2024-07-09 (grant)\
**Inventors:** Dayong Li (李大勇)\
**Assignee:** Anhui Changgeng Optics Technology Co., Ltd. (安徽长庚光学科技有限公司)\
**Title:** 小型化超级广角镜头 (Compact Ultra-Wide-Angle Lens)\
**Example details:** 第一实施例, ¶0056–0064, Figs. 1–2

Laowa’s construction drawing matches Example 1 element for element. Both have 15 elements in nine air-separated groups, with four cemented doublets and one cemented triplet. The second and last elements are aspherical, and the maker’s three ED markings fall on elements 5, 10 and 12, all of which have the same low-dispersion coordinates in the patent. The drawing also shows the two doublets between the front asphere and the stop that form the patent’s moving group G2. It shows a single position, so the motion and travel come from the patent’s description and spacing table. Example 2 uses 16 elements and has a focal length of 12.35 mm. Example 3 has a focal length of 14.3 mm and places aspheres on both front elements. The match identifies Example 1 as the published design of the 10 mm lens. The production prescription and tolerances are unpublished.

The patent prints 10.2855 mm, f/2.87 and a 129.2° full field. Laowa specifies 10 mm, f/2.8 and a 130.4° angle of view, with a 77 mm filter thread. Its specified aperture range is f/2.8–f/22. The five-blade version has autofocus on Sony E and Nikon Z, and manual focus on Canon RF and L-mount. The 14-blade version is manual focus on all four mounts. Laowa specifies a stepping motor for autofocus. [Laowa product page][laowa-en], [Laowa specifications][laowa-cn], [Laowa construction drawing][diagram].

## Optical Architecture

The lens is rectilinear and uses a retrofocus layout. The patent divides its nine air-separated groups into three optical assemblies. Fixed G1 consists of two negative menisci (elements 1–2), which spread the broad field into the lens. Positive G2 consists of the two cemented pairs immediately ahead of the stop (elements 3–6). It moves toward the image for closer focus. The stop lies at surface 11. Fixed positive G3 (elements 7–15) contains two doublets, a triplet and two singlets. The strongly negative front assembly and positive rear assemblies give a 13.182 mm paraxial back focus for a 10.285 mm effective focal length.

The model uses the patent’s original scale, printed glass coordinates, two focus spacings and 13.143 mm image gap. The paraxial infinity focus is 0.039 mm behind that plane, which lies near the focus of the 0.7 pupil zone. The 5.23 mm stop radius is calculated from f/2.87. The clear radii are based on Fig. 1, with allowances for adjacent surfaces, edge thickness and the rear aspheric profile. At infinity the chief ray reaches the 21.60 mm image height marked in Fig. 2. About half the geometrically sampled pupil remains clear at the edge at full aperture.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are paraxial values for each element in air. The cemented assemblies share refracting surfaces.

### L1 — Negative Meniscus

$n_d = 1.88300$, $\nu_d = 45.00$. Glass unmatched. $f = -31.37$ mm.

The large front meniscus receives the steep off-axis bundles and supplies the first negative power of G1.

### L2 — Negative Meniscus, Two Aspherical Surfaces

$n_d = 1.81667$, $\nu_d = 45.18$. Glass unmatched. $f = -15.14$ mm.

L2 supplies stronger negative power than L1. Its aspheres let the front assembly carry strong power within a compact diameter, the design aim described in ¶0009–0025.

### L3 and L4 — First Focusing Doublet

- **L3, negative meniscus.** $n_d = 1.92286$, $\nu_d = 20.88$. E-FDS1 (Hoya) equivalent. $f = -23.76$ mm. The dense flint provides negative power and a large dispersion difference relative to its positive partner.
- **L4, biconvex positive.** $n_d = 1.64769$, $\nu_d = 33.84$. H-ZF1 (CDGM) equivalent. $f = +13.45$ mm. It supplies the dominant positive power in the first moving doublet.

### L5 and L6 — Second Focusing Doublet

- **L5, biconcave negative.** $n_d = 1.49700$, $\nu_d = 81.61$. H-FK61 (CDGM) equivalent, marked ED by Laowa. $f = -12.56$ mm. Its low dispersion accompanies the negative power in this pair.
- **L6, biconvex positive.** $n_d = 1.52306$, $\nu_d = 60.80$. Glass unmatched. $f = +13.84$ mm. It balances L5 to make a weakly positive doublet next to the stop.

### L7 and L8 — First Rear Doublet

- **L7, negative meniscus.** $n_d = 1.83437$, $\nu_d = 41.98$. Glass unmatched. $f = -12.06$ mm. It provides the negative entrance member of the rear assembly.
- **L8, positive meniscus.** $n_d = 1.92286$, $\nu_d = 20.88$. E-FDS1 (Hoya) equivalent. $f = +14.50$ mm. Its dense-flint positive power offsets part of L7’s negative power in the doublet.

### L9, L10 and L11 — Rear Triplet

- **L9, negative meniscus.** $n_d = 1.92286$, $\nu_d = 20.88$. E-FDS1 (Hoya) equivalent. $f = -39.38$ mm. The first dense flint forms the negative front flank of the triplet.
- **L10, biconvex positive.** $n_d = 1.49700$, $\nu_d = 81.61$. H-FK61 (CDGM) equivalent, marked ED by Laowa. $f = +10.55$ mm. The thick ED core supplies most of the triplet’s positive power.
- **L11, negative meniscus.** $n_d = 1.90325$, $\nu_d = 27.29$. Glass unmatched. $f = -26.25$ mm. This highly dispersive rear flank pairs with L10 across the second cemented interface.

### L12 — Biconvex Positive

$n_d = 1.49700$, $\nu_d = 81.61$. H-FK61 (CDGM) equivalent, marked ED by Laowa. $f = +20.17$ mm.

The air-spaced ED singlet adds converging power immediately after the triplet.

### L13 and L14 — Final Cemented Doublet

- **L13, biconcave negative.** $n_d = 1.89249$, $\nu_d = 33.04$. Glass unmatched. $f = -7.82$ mm. It supplies strong negative power ahead of the final asphere.
- **L14, biconvex positive.** $n_d = 1.61202$, $\nu_d = 39.42$. Glass unmatched. $f = +21.97$ mm. The gently curved rear face and positive power balance part of L13’s negative power.

### L15 — Positive Meniscus, Two Aspherical Surfaces

$n_d = 1.72665$, $\nu_d = 48.06$. Glass unmatched. $f = +25.73$ mm.

The last element provides convergence and shapes the outer bundles through two aspherical surfaces close to the image.

The patent prints helium d-line indices and Abbe numbers (¶0062–0063). The named glasses are catalog equivalents to those coordinates. The supplier and melt used in the lens are unknown.

The three ED positions distribute low-dispersion glass across the moving group and the fixed rear assembly. The negative ED member in G2 is paired with a more dispersive positive member. In G3 a positive ED core is flanked by two negative flints, and a separate positive ED singlet follows. The range of powers and dispersions gives the designer several ways to balance chromatic error. The ED designation comes from [Laowa’s construction drawing][diagram].

## Focus Mechanism

Only G2 (surfaces 5–10) moves. From infinity to the printed near setting it travels 0.157 mm toward the image, with G1, the stop and G3 fixed. The two changing gaps always sum to 10.091 mm.

| Patent setting | D4, after surface 4A | D10, before the stop |
| -------------- | -------------------: | -------------------: |
| Infinity       |             6.484 mm |             3.607 mm |
| 500 mm         |             6.641 mm |             3.450 mm |

The 500 mm row fits a distance from the front vertex better than one from the image plane. It corresponds to 0.590 m object to image and about 0.020× paraxial magnification. The model’s focus range ends there. Laowa specifies 0.12 m and 0.24× for the production lens. At the printed near setting the chief ray clears the rims at 64.6° and reaches 21.40 mm, or 99.1% of the 21.60 mm patent image height. [Laowa specifications][laowa-cn].

## Aspherical Surfaces

Both surfaces of L2 and L15 are aspherical. The sag equation in ¶0027 contains $1+K$. The table gives that quantity as $\varepsilon$, so $K=\varepsilon-1$. Examples 2 and 3 give $\varepsilon=1$ for sphere-based aspheres. At f/2.87, the d-line longitudinal spherical aberration is −0.009, −0.038, −0.038 and −0.024 mm at pupil fractions 1, 0.85, 0.7 and 0.5. Distortion is approximately +0.53% at 40° and −0.15% at 64.6°. Both agree with Fig. 2. Substituting the table’s $\varepsilon$ directly for $K$ gives +0.565 mm at the full pupil and about −20% distortion at 40°. It also ends the mathematical profile of surface 4 at roughly 6.0 mm, short of the 9.75 mm rim in Fig. 1.

Surface 3A uses a steeper conic base than the vertex sphere, with a negative leading polynomial term. Surface 4A uses a flatter conic base and a stronger negative fourth-order term on the more curved face of L2. The two rear conics flatten away from their vertex spheres. Surface 24A adds aspheric departure to a shallow base, and 25A has the largest fourth-order coefficient in magnitude, reshaping the final refraction toward the image.
