**Title:** 一种超微距镜头 (A super macro lens)  
**Embodiment analyzed:** Native Example 3, original millimetres, published infinity / nominal 1x / nominal 2x spacing states

This prescription is a qualified patent-example model associated with the LAOWA 60mm f/2.8 2X Ultra-Macro. The selected numerical example is not manufacturer-confirmed as the exact production prescription. The grant specifies a 61.0269 mm, FNO 2.90 design with a 13.07° half-field (¶0067–0069), distinct from the manufacturer's nominal 60 mm, f/2.8 lens. The original radii, thicknesses, d-line indices and focus gaps are retained without scaling or fitting.

The association has several useful limits:

1. The manufacturer's nine-element/seven-component count agrees with this example, as does a main and an auxiliary moving focus group.
2. The manufacturer associates the product with application 201310608351.6, but does not select a numerical example.
3. The final element's front boundary in the supplied sectional drawing is nearly planar and possibly weakly concave, compatible with Example 3's R16 = −138.9230 mm. The small raster departure and surface correspondence are weak evidence only. The drawing has no scale or focus-state label, and other proportions do not establish identity.
4. At the native nominal 2x state, object–image distance is 200.020210 mm, exceeding the manual's explicit 185 mm object–image minimum by 15.020210 mm. That discrepancy remains unresolved.

The approximate 50 mm subject-to-front-end clearance in the manual has a mechanical reference. It is not a subject-to-first-vertex or principal-plane distance. Nor does the first-vertex/image distance of 135.0403 mm prove the physical front lies at that vertex. Mount-dependent barrel length, crop equivalence and drawing scale do not remove an object–image discrepancy.

## Optical Architecture

The optical sequence consists of nine spherical or planar glass elements in seven air-separated components. Two cemented pairs, L3–L4 and L5–L6, lie within the seven-element positive main assembly G1. The diaphragm is source surface 13, immediately behind G1. A negative plano-concave singlet L8 forms G2, followed by a stationary positive meniscus L9, G3 (¶0059–0071; Figure 5).

The isolated functional-group focal lengths are G1 +40.459360 mm, G2 -53.246626 mm and G3 +141.610704 mm. The first cemented pair has net focal length -20.758666 mm; the second has +177.651664 mm. These are sequential powers of each isolated assembly in air, not sums of the listed standalone element powers.

At infinity the complete native prescription has EFL 61.026446 mm and Gaussian back focal distance 39.274089 mm from S17. The authored image plane remains at the printed 39.2744 mm gap. First-to-last-vertex length is 32.9002 mm, while first-vertex-to-authored-image length is 72.1746 mm. With BFD/EFL = 0.643559 and total authored track/EFL = 1.182677, neither a retrofocus classification by BFD > EFL nor a telephoto classification by track < EFL applies to this infinity state.

The main architecture is a positive image-forming assembly followed by a negative moving auxiliary group and a positive fixed rear element. Descriptions of individual aberration balancing below are deliberately limited: power signs, glass coordinates and positions alone do not determine a unique aberration contribution or prove the inventor's optimization intent.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.77965, νd = 46.35. Glass: Unmatched (high-index moderate-dispersion crown/flint boundary; supplier and melt unproven). f = +48.34 mm.

This front biconvex singlet contributes positive standalone power at the entrance to G1. Its relatively high index accompanies a moderate Abbe number. The patent does not identify its supplier, melt or an individual aberration assignment. Source: Example 3, prescription rows 1–2 (¶0070–0071).

### L2 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.49787, νd = 81.39. Glass: K-PFK80 (Sumita spectral proxy; source 1.49787/81.39 retained; supplier and melt unproven). f = +58.08 mm.

The second element is a positive meniscus convex toward the object. Its lower-index, high-Abbe coordinates differ strongly from the neighboring high-index glass. That contrast is relevant to chromatic balancing, but the published d-line pair alone does not determine partial dispersion or establish an ED trade name. Source: Example 3, prescription rows 3–4 (¶0070–0071).

### L3 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.83868, νd = 26.75. Glass: Unmatched (dense flint class; supplier and melt unproven). f = -11.59 mm.

The front member of L3–L4 is a biconcave negative element. Its short negative standalone focal length must not be confused with the net power of the cemented pair. The following shared surface refracts directly into L4; no artificial air film or generic cement is inserted. Source: Example 3, prescription rows 5–6 (¶0070–0071).

### L4 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.91782, νd = 20.98. Glass: Unmatched (very-high-index dense flint class; supplier and melt unproven). f = +23.29 mm.

The rear member of L3–L4 is a positive meniscus in isolation. At the cemented front interface the incident medium is L3 glass rather than air, so its in-situ refracting power differs from the standalone number. Together the pair remains net negative. The inferred rear clear radius is slightly smaller than the shared front radius; this is an aperture-model assumption rather than a proven factory bevel. Source: Example 3, prescription rows 6–7 (¶0070–0071).

### L5 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.63170, νd = 34.87. Glass: Unmatched (flint class; supplier and melt unproven). f = -26.69 mm.

The negative front member of L5–L6 is biconcave. It has greater dispersion than its positive partner. The cemented interface and the distinct indices are retained exactly, without substitution from a catalog comparator. Source: Example 3, prescription rows 8–9 (¶0070–0071).

### L6 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.72325, νd = 54.99. Glass: Unmatched (high-index crown class; supplier and melt unproven). f = +24.54 mm.

The positive rear member of L5–L6 is biconvex. Its standalone positive power nearly balances the negative partner, leaving a weak positive net cemented component. The sign and size of that net power are directly calculated; an apochromatic or secondary-spectrum claim would require additional spectral evidence. Source: Example 3, prescription rows 9–10 (¶0070–0071).

### L7 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.87481, νd = 41.28. Glass: Unmatched (very-high-index moderate-dispersion glass; supplier and melt unproven). f = +39.61 mm.

This biconvex positive element closes the main assembly before the diaphragm. It travels with the other G1 elements and the stop. Its high-index, moderate-dispersion pair does not uniquely resolve to a catalog material in the source audit. Source: Example 3, prescription rows 11–12 (¶0070–0071).

### L8 — Plano-Concave

nd = 1.49861, νd = 79.98. Glass: Unmatched (low-index low-dispersion fluor-crown class; supplier and melt unproven). f = -53.25 mm.

This plano-concave negative singlet is the auxiliary moving group. The front surface is planar and the rear has positive radius under the object-to-image convention. The growing D13 and D15 spacings move it objectward relative to the stationary rear element while changing its separation from G1. Source: Example 3, prescription rows 14–15 (¶0070–0071).

### L9 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.88768, νd = 36.55. Glass: Unmatched (very-high-index flint class; supplier and melt unproven). f = +141.61 mm.

The final element is a positive meniscus with a concave object-side surface. Both radii are negative, with the rear curvature stronger in magnitude. Its D17 image gap is invariant; it is stationary relative to the image. This explicit fixed rear element is compatible with a mechanism containing two moving groups. Source: Example 3, prescription rows 16–17 (¶0070–0071).

## Focus Mechanism

The focus disposition is PUBLISHED for the three tabulated configurations. G1 comprises L1–L7 plus the diaphragm; G2 is L8; G3 is L9. Both G1 and G2 move toward the object relative to the fixed image, while G3 stays stationary. This is two moving groups plus a stationary rear group, not two groups total (Figure 5; ¶0060–0071).

| Source state | D13 (mm) | D15 (mm) | D17 (mm) | G1 objectward travel (mm) | G2 objectward travel (mm) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Infinity | 1.0000 | 2.3259 | 39.2744 | 0 | 0 |
| Nominal 1x | 23.1591 | 12.9182 | 39.2744 | 32.7514 | 10.5923 |
| Nominal 2x | 40.9245 | 25.2671 | 39.2744 | 62.8657 | 22.9412 |

At the authored image plane, the calculated finite magnifications are -0.997928863 and -1.998543143. Their small departures from the nominal state labels are reported rather than forced to exactly −1 or −2. Object-to-S1 distances are 95.118364 and 64.979910 mm; adding each current first-vertex/image length gives 200.044364 and 200.020210 mm. The native state EFLs are 48.015325 and 44.553373 mm. These optical-state quantities are distinct from the nominal marketed focal length.

The data preserves all three states with focus coordinates 0, 0.9998792550876074, and 1. The finite coordinates follow the schema's closest object–image distance divided by the current source-state distance. Published-station markers identify both finite rows; finiteConjugates supplies only those exact source spacing configurations and their calculated object–image distances. The near coincidence of the two finite coordinates is real for these native conjugates and compresses their slider interval. Source digit perturbations are not used to conceal it.

Intermediate spacings are piecewise-linear visualization, not a source-published or validated cam law. The inverse-distance readout is unreliable between source stations. Halfway from infinity to nominal 1x, the interpolated prescription conjugates at 250.137185 mm but the label is 400.088728 mm, a +149.951543 mm discrepancy. Halfway from nominal 1x to nominal 2x, the actual conjugate is 195.589253 mm while the label is 200.032286 mm, a +4.443034 mm discrepancy. The physical conjugate is non-monotonic in the latter interval. Geometry/ray checks on intermediate spacings establish only that those sampled shapes can be traced; they do not certify the focus-distance law or production mechanics.

The production lens is manually focused. The manual's 185 mm closest object–image distance and approximately 50 mm subject-to-front-end clearance remain manufacturer facts, not substituted source-model constraints.

## Verification Summary

The native first-order prescription is evaluated with both reduced-angle ABCD matrices and separate scalar height/slope recurrence. Exact spherical vector-Snell tracing approaches the same paraxial result near the axis. Source-digit interval propagation accounts for the small infinity EFL/BFD residuals; the authored image plane remains unchanged.

The surface-by-surface Petzval sum is 0.002570780 mm⁻¹. At infinity the paraxial entrance/exit pupil diameter ratio, expressed as exit divided by entrance, is 0.727330. Those first-order pupil quantities are distinguished from the finite-aperture stop calibration. The modeled physical iris radius is 7.733738 mm, found by tracing the axial entrance radius EFL/(2 × 2.90) to the published stop position. This is calibration to FNO 2.90, not independent diaphragm metrology. Using the computed EFL, a purely paraxial FNO 2.90 calibration would instead give a 7.549612 mm stop radius. The chosen finite-aperture calibration corresponds to paraxial equivalent f/2.830956. The two conventions are not interchangeable: the runtime aperture label follows its exact-ray calibration, while the pupil ratios above remain first-order quantities.

All clear radii are inferred. The L4 rear radius of 8.2 mm retains the stated axial beam and keeps the S7/S8 shared-band intrusion at 87.893402%, below the default 90% allowance. A 9.0 mm cemented-front radius and 8.2 mm rear radius describe a modeled clear aperture, not a known manufactured bevel. The maximum actual spherical rim slope is 49.276710°; the minimum sampled glass thickness is 0.500000 mm. No gap-sag allowance override or hidden render trim is required.

Exact source-state tests cover 81 axial stop radii, 21 chief field heights and 12 × 48 two-dimensional stop-area samples at five fields and three aperture settings. Additional full three-dimensional containment sampling covers six intermediate spacing configurations. Clipping and failed aiming are separate outcomes. At infinity and source f/2.90, the full 13.07°-field stop-grid transmission fraction is 64.93%; 10.42% of attempts have unresolved physical aiming, so the corresponding upper bound is 75.35%. These are finite geometrical sampling bounds, not measured relative illumination or a radiometric throughput claim. The finite-state grids also expose any modeled edge vignetting rather than hiding it with enlarged apertures.

The real pinned LensVisualizer main source validates and builds the candidate, reports zero render trim at the sampled focus states, and independently retraces 28,450 successfully aimed rays with identical clip dispositions and image coordinates agreeing within 0.000001 mm. Runtime format bounds can be narrower than the source angle: at infinity the APS-C analysis half-field is approximately 12.7986° while the preserved source half-field is 13.07°. Full compiler/corpus/build and live browser acceptance are outside these targeted checks. No integrated or production-performance claim follows from them.
