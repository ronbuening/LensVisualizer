**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 1, paragraphs 0037–0050, prescription on PDF pages 5–6, Figure 1 on PDF page 10.

The selected example is associated with the **LAOWA 90mm f/2.8 2× Ultra Macro APO** by convergent construction evidence, not by a manufacturer statement that the published prescription was manufactured unchanged:

1. Both source and official construction have **13 elements in 10 air-separated components**. Three cemented pairs occur at L4/L5, L6/L7 and L10/L11; the widely separated weak front singlet and final negative/positive rear pair also agree.
2. The three source media at L2, L3 and L7 have **nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.61**, matching the positions of the manufacturer's three ED highlights. This is a coordinate/position correlation, not supplier identification.
3. The source's infinity summary is **87.0609 mm, F/2.9, 13.5° half-field**, compared with marketed **90 mm, f/2.8, 27° full field**. The prose separately rounds the focal length to 87.1 mm.
4. The source supplies infinity, 1.0x and 2.0x spacing columns and coordinated internal motion, consistent with the marketed two-times macro function. Example 2 is a distinct 59.21 mm, 14-element design and is not mixed into this model.

The [official product page](https://www.laowalens.com/camera-lens-52) lists Sony E, Nikon Z, Canon RF, L-Mount and Micro Four Thirds variants, with full-frame optical coverage, manual focus and 13 diaphragm blades. The [official manual](https://www.laowalens.com/Public/Uploads/uploadfile/files/20260114/FF90mmF2.8CA-DreamerMacro2Xshuomingshu.pdf) gives conflicting close-distance wording: 18.3 cm in the usage prose and 205 mm in the specification table; the current product page gives 20.4 cm. None of these marketing distances replaces a patent spacing or certifies a patent conjugate. The model represents the common optical association, not the variant-specific electronics.

## Optical Architecture

This is a **three-group floating-focus macro architecture with positive–positive–negative group powers**. The weak positive front group G1 is the isolated L1. G2 contains L2–L8 and carries the stop; G3 contains L9–L13 and provides the auxiliary rear motion described in ¶0038.

| Functional group | Source surfaces | Standalone group EFL, mm | Construction |
|---|---|---:|---|
| G1 | 1–2 | +624.743156 | Weak positive front meniscus |
| G2 | 3–15, including flat stop | +43.617127 | Seven elements; two cemented pairs |
| G3 | 16–24 | -47.019146 | Five elements; one cemented pair |

All powers in this analysis are executed paraxial d-line calculations from the unchanged native prescription. The infinity model gives **EFL 87.057580 mm**, Gaussian **BFD 19.663324 mm** from the final optical vertex, and a source first-vertex-to-image track of **128.8533 mm**. The rear and front principal planes lie -67.394256 mm from the last vertex and 15.025541 mm from the first vertex, respectively; positive is imageward. Track/EFL is 1.480093, and BFD/EFL is 0.225866. These reference-plane results do not support a short-track telephoto or long-back-focus retrofocus classification.

The surface-by-surface Petzval sum is **0.002617297 mm⁻¹**, including the actual glass-to-glass transitions. It is a paraxial curvature indicator, not a traced field-curvature or flat-field-performance certification. No cover plate or aspherical surface is prescribed.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are **isolated thick-element powers in air**, with each element's own front and rear curvature. They must not be added directly to reconstruct the cemented component or entire system. The qualitative roles describe power, placement and dispersion balance; they do not attribute a separately measured aberration correction to an individual element.

### L1 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.88403, νd = 43.00. Glass: Unmatched (native nd=1.88403, vd=43.00; no tight six-vendor coordinate match). f = +624.743156 mm.

This weak positive meniscus is the fixed G1. Its long focal length distinguishes its contribution from the much stronger main focusing group; the large following airspace provides G2 travel.

### L2 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.61. Glass: H-FK61 (CDGM coordinate equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = +87.904584 mm.

The first G2 element is a positive ED biconvex singlet. It shares the same low-dispersion coordinate as L3 and L7, spreading positive power across the group rather than concentrating it at one surface.

### L3 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.61. Glass: H-FK61 (CDGM coordinate equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = +79.204698 mm.

This positive ED meniscus follows L2. Its separated placement and lower dispersion distinguish it from the subsequent negative flint element; both singlets move with G2.

### L4 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.71736, νd = 29.50. Glass: 717295 — coordinate-equivalent class; supplier unconfirmed. f = -16.403576 mm.

The strongly negative biconcave element begins D1. Its relatively low Abbe number supplies a high-dispersion negative contribution beside the positive high-index partner L5.

### L5 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.92286, νd = 20.88. Glass: 923209 — coordinate-equivalent class; supplier unconfirmed. f = +53.813483 mm.

L5 is the positive meniscus partner in D1. The actual cemented interface must be traced as a transition between its index and L4’s index, rather than as two air interfaces.

### L6 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.88300, νd = 43.00. Glass: Unmatched (native nd=1.88300, vd=43.00; no tight six-vendor coordinate match). f = -121.766145 mm.

This negative meniscus begins D2. Its standalone power is much weaker than the strongly positive ED partner L7, so the cemented component remains positive.

### L7 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.61. Glass: H-FK61 (CDGM coordinate equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = +28.845177 mm.

L7 is the strongest isolated positive element in G2 and the third ED-coordinate element. The high Abbe number complements the more dispersive negative partner L6; it does not independently establish secondary-spectrum correction.

### L8 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.90366, νd = 31.31. Glass: 904313 — coordinate-equivalent class; supplier unconfirmed. f = +43.332874 mm.

The final G2 singlet is positive and precedes the stop. Its high-index, moderately dispersive coordinate is compatible with several named catalog families, but no vendor is identified by the source.

### L9 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.88177, νd = 40.87. Glass: Unmatched (native nd=1.88177, vd=40.87; no tight six-vendor coordinate match). f = -35.407551 mm.

This negative meniscus is the first G3 element, immediately behind the variable stop-to-rear-group gap. Its inferred rim is constrained by the following air-gap geometry; aperture and vignetting limits are documented separately.

### L10 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.62004, νd = 36.30. Glass: 620363 — coordinate-equivalent class; supplier unconfirmed. f = -22.990758 mm.

The negative biconcave element begins the rear D3 pair. Its higher Abbe number (36.30 versus L11’s 29.88) makes L10 less dispersive by this measure; its negative power is distinct from the positive partner’s contribution.

### L11 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.89786, νd = 29.88. Glass: Unmatched (native nd=1.89786, vd=29.88; no tight six-vendor coordinate match). f = +17.949753 mm.

The positive biconvex partner makes D3 net positive even though G3 as a whole is negative. Cemented net power and standalone power are separate calculations.

### L12 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.83967, νd = 43.30. Glass: Unmatched (native nd=1.83967, vd=43.30; no tight six-vendor coordinate match). f = -35.058174 mm.

A separated negative meniscus follows the long rear airspace. Together with L9 it explains why the total rear group can remain negative despite the positive D3 pair and final singlet.

### L13 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.51680, νd = 64.20. Glass: H-K9L (CDGM coordinate equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = +58.846711 mm.

The final biconvex crown is positive, with equal-and-opposite source curvatures. It closes the rear group ahead of the variable image gap; field flattening or image-space telecentricity is not inferred solely from its position.

The executed cemented component EFLs are D1 (s7–s9): -21.297075 mm, D2 (s10–s12): +40.896907 mm, D3 (s18–s20): +62.793696 mm. These net values use the true intermediate media.

Six-digit model labels denote coordinate-equivalent classes, not named suppliers; the two CDGM names follow the labelling of the other Laowa models and are coordinate equivalents only. The unusual 1.88403/43.00 front medium is preserved; it is not replaced by the similar-looking 1.88300/43.00 L6 medium. Weak nearby matches are not promoted to physical identities. The runtime resolves the eight tight-match elements (L2, L3, L4, L5, L7, L8, L10, L13) to catalog dispersion curves at their coordinates and uses the Abbe-number fallback for the five unmatched elements (L1, L6, L9, L11, L12). Those curves are spectral proxies; vendor spectral values are not copied into the patent elements as if they were measured source data. OHARA S-/L-/legacy prefixes remain distinct in the evidence.

Positive ED elements L2/L3/L7 and negative higher-dispersion elements provide a plausible primary-color balancing strategy. That is an inference from power and Abbe number. The marketed “APO” name is retained for identification; secondary-spectrum performance is not established by these coordinates alone.

## Focus Mechanism

The source declares G1 fixed, G2 moving objectward, and G3 assisting that motion (¶0038). All three printed spacing columns are retained:

| Source gap, mm | Infinity | Source 1.0x | Source 2.0x |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| D(2), G1 to G2 | 43.8237 | 18.6230 | 1.2958 |
| D(15), stop to G3 | 1.2000 | 20.4940 | 42.5180 |
| D(24), last lens to image | 19.6820 | 25.6255 | 21.0222 |

G2's first vertex moves **−25.2007 mm** and **−42.5279 mm** relative to its infinity position. Relative to fixed G1, G3's first vertex moves **−5.9067 mm** then **−1.2099 mm**: it reverses direction between the two finite stations. D(24) likewise rises and then falls. The source gap sums yield tracks of 128.8533, 128.8901 and 128.9836 mm. The increases from infinity are 0.0368 mm and 0.1303 mm. That small nonconservation is retained; the image plane is not silently shifted to impose constant track. Figure 1 marks G1 with a ground symbol and draws one arrow each for G2 and G3, both ending toward the object; the G3 arrow is a schematic curve and does not show the reversal that the spacing table gives, so the table governs.

## Aperture, Geometry and Validation Limits

No numerical surface aperture or physical iris dimension is supplied. The model's stop radius **8.456025914 mm** is inferred from an exact axial d-line ray launched at EFL/(2 × 2.9). Agreement with F/2.9 is a calibration, not independent evidence of a manufactured iris. Physical rims are inferred from Figure 1's proportions and ray/geometry limits. An initial 8.6 mm estimate for L9 failed the actual shared-band clearance test; 8.1 mm passes without changing any source radius, thickness or gap. Figure 1 is drawn to scale at infinity (its vertex spacings reproduce the prescription to within one raster pixel, about 0.16 mm), so a later pass measured each rim against it: L2 was lowered from 15 to 14.0 mm, L3 from 14.5 to 13.0 mm and the L6/L7 doublet from 11.5 to 10.5 mm, each still above the f/2.9 axial marginal ray (13.79, 12.86 and 10.09 mm). A second reading of the same figure set the L4/L5 doublet to a single 11.5 mm (it was 12 mm at the front and the junction and 11.5 mm at the rear; the figure draws the L4 block and the L5 tip level at 11.35 mm) and L8 to 9.5 mm (from 10 mm; figure 9.39 mm, axial marginal ray 9.38 mm), so the rims of G2 step down by about 1 mm from element to element as drawn. The remaining rims agree with the figure within about 4 % and are unchanged. The flat lands the figure draws on the concave faces of L4, L6, L10 and L12 are not modeled; those faces run to the element's outer height.

The executed model has positive element thicknesses, allowed actual rim slopes and shared-band gaps at 41 focus-grid positions plus the source keyframes. Independent spherical endpoint checks also bound the shared radial bands across the piecewise-linear gap intervals. The smallest inferred edge thickness is approximately 0.137 mm at L11; L7, which was 0.011494 mm at its first-authored 11.5 mm rim, is 0.538 mm at the figure's 10.5 mm. These are mathematical geometry results, not manufacturing-feasibility certification. Actual project construction and validation pass, and actual element-render diagnostics report zero hidden trim. The authored silhouettes were inspected against the source topology. Five source/half-segment states and finite pupil/field grids are tested; this is not proof over all continuous controls.

The author’s meridional source-equivalent field test records 118 transmitted rays and 22 physical clips with no unresolved sample. An independent analytic-circle/Snell check uses a different 140-ray grid and records 120 transmitted rays and 20 clips, including full on-axis aperture and all sampled source-field chief rays. Formal sphere continuation is used only to aim rays outside physical apertures; every physical trace stops at its first clip. The actual project's larger skew test records 744 successful traces, 111 clipped traces, 19 raw failed intersections and one unresolved aim. Each of the 19 raw failures occurs in air before the finite front cap; independent analytic sphere intersections establish an outside-cap miss. They are not relabeled as native successes. The one unresolved pupil aim is at the nominal full-frame corner, 13.955° at infinity, beyond the source's 13.5° half-field. Complete corner throughput remains unavailable. These ray tallies were recorded with the first-authored L2, L3 and L6/L7 rims and were not re-run after the figure pass; with the lowered rims every axial ray at f/2.9 and every full-frame chief ray still transmits at all three source states, and the clipped share of off-axis bundles is slightly larger. The later L4/L5 and L8 change alters no ray: in a three-dimensional trace of about 257,000 rays per source state, out to 110 % of the full-frame corner, none changes between transmitted and clipped. Vignetting, exact best focus, chromatic/APO performance, MTF and continuous-focus image quality are not certified by these checks.
