**Patent:** CN 103823292 B

**Application Number:** 201310608351.6

**Filed:** 2013-11-19

**Granted:** 2016-06-29

**Inventor:** Xiaohua Zhang (张小华)

**Assignee:** Anhui Changgeng Optics Technology Co., Ltd. (安徽长庚光学科技有限公司)

**Title:** 一种超微距镜头 (A super macro lens)

**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 2, original millimetres, published infinity / nominal 1× / nominal 2× spacings

Example 2 is the closest of the patent's three embodiments to the LAOWA 60mm f/2.8 2X Ultra-Macro's focal length and documented focusing architecture. It remains a qualified patent model, not a manufacturer-confirmed factory prescription. All printed radii, thicknesses, d-line indices, Abbe numbers and variable spacings are retained without scaling, optimization or fitting to the production minimum distance.

1. The manufacturer explicitly associates the product with application 201310608351.6, without identifying a numerical example. The common nine-element/seven-component count supports that family association but cannot select an embodiment.
2. Example 1's 85 mm infinity focal length excludes it as the closest native 60 mm design, despite its compatible two-assembly mechanism and approximately matching rear travel.
3. Example 2 matches the nominal focal length and the observed motion of the rear pair. Savazzi describes separate cams moving both assemblies objectward, with substantially greater front travel. Cooper reports visible forward movement of the rearmost element. Example 3's explicit fixed rear element contradicts those observations.
4. Example 3 has a closer calculated minimum distance and field angle. The manufacturer's small schematic and the earlier supplied sectional raster are insufficient to identify weak surface curvature signs or establish a scaled factory drawing. Those weak shape clues do not outweigh the rear-motion contradiction.

Example 2 still disagrees with production. Its calculated 2× object–image distance exceeds 185 mm by 42.3156 mm, its rear travel exceeds the reported 7–8 mm, and its first-vertex/image track reaches 159.5307 mm. The source is therefore not an exact dimensional reconstruction of the production barrel. The shorter 200.0202 mm distance of Example 3 was not, by itself, evidence of a better mechanical match.

The production scale's 19 cm at 1× and 18.5 cm at 1.5×/2× shows that large magnification changes can occupy a small object–image distance interval. It does not validate either embodiment's numerical conjugates. The manual's approximately 5 cm subject-to-front-end working clearance has a mechanical reference and must not be substituted for an object-to-first-surface distance.

## Optical Architecture

Nine spherical or planar elements form seven air-separated components. G1 contains L1–L7, including cemented pairs L3–L4 and L5–L6, followed by the diaphragm at source surface 13. G2 is the rigid, air-spaced L8–L9 rear pair. The groups have isolated focal lengths of +50.238450 mm and −260.681686 mm, respectively. These sequential group powers include their internal separations; they are not sums of standalone element powers.

Both groups move toward the object as magnification increases. The main group travels farther, opening D13, while the rear pair's objectward movement increases D17. D15, the internal air gap in G2, remains exactly 1.4858 mm. No optical element is stationary relative to the image plane (Figure 3; ¶0046–0058).

The unchanged infinity prescription calculates EFL 60.040449 mm and Gaussian back focal distance 42.998605 mm. The source image plane remains at the printed D17 = 42.9988 mm. The first-to-last-vertex length is 31.7312 mm and the first-vertex/image track is 74.7300 mm. The source is not classified as retrofocus merely because it is an SLR lens: its infinity back focal distance is shorter than its focal length. All surfaces are spherical or planar; no aspheric coefficients or special-glass designation are published.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.77159, νd = 49.71. Glass: Unmatched high-index crown. f = +78.32 mm.

The front singlet contributes positive power to G1. Several catalog glasses lie near its optimized coordinates, so a unique supplier or material is not assigned. Source: rows 1–2, ¶0057.

### L2 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.61. Glass: FCD1 (HOYA qualified spectral proxy). f = +64.96 mm.

This low-index, high-Abbe singlet is convex toward the object and precedes the first cemented pair. The catalog coordinates agree with the source pair, supporting a dispersion proxy. The patent does not identify the glass as HOYA FCD1 or establish a production supplier. Source: rows 3–4, ¶0057.

### L3 — Biconcave Negative, cemented to L4

nd = 1.83147, νd = 29.38. Glass: Unmatched dense flint. f = −12.52 mm.

The negative member shares surface 6 with L4. Its higher index and lower Abbe number than L2 give a different chromatic contribution, but the source does not isolate its aberration correction or identify a commercial material. Source: rows 5–6, ¶0057.

### L4 — Positive Meniscus, cemented to L3

nd = 1.92286, νd = 20.88. Glass: N-SF66 (SCHOTT qualified spectral proxy). f = +27.99 mm.

The positive member uses very high index and low Abbe number. Its standalone focal length treats the element in air; the shared glass-to-glass interface has a different power in the assembled doublet. The inferred rear clear radius is smaller than the cemented-front radius to retain physical edge thickness and the following air gap. Source: rows 6–7, ¶0057.

### L5 — Biconcave Negative, cemented to L6

nd = 1.69895, νd = 31.75. Glass: Unmatched flint. f = −21.51 mm.

This is the negative member of the second cemented pair. Nearby catalog flints with the same nominal index have materially different Abbe numbers; they are not assigned as the source glass. Source: rows 8–9, ¶0057–0058.

### L6 — Biconvex Positive, cemented to L5

nd = 1.77084, νd = 49.79. Glass: Unmatched high-index crown. f = +20.79 mm.

L6 supplies positive power in the second pair. Its higher Abbe number than L5 is compatible with chromatic balancing. Multiple nearby catalog families leave its identity unresolved. Source: rows 9–10, ¶0058.

### L7 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.88340, νd = 40.41. Glass: Unmatched very-high-index crown. f = +34.47 mm.

The last G1 element lies immediately before the diaphragm. Its high index permits substantial positive power; its individual correction role is not published. Source: rows 11–12, ¶0058.

### L8 — Plano-Concave Negative

nd = 1.52003, νd = 63.80. Glass: Unmatched crown. f = −75.16 mm.

The plane-front singlet is the negative member of the moving rear pair. It moves rigidly with L9; the growing D13 is a separation between assemblies, not an internal rear-group spacing change. A nearby radiation-resistant catalog formulation does not establish the source's material identity. Source: rows 14–15, ¶0058.

### L9 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.88300, νd = 40.80. Glass: TAFD30 (HOYA qualified spectral proxy). f = +108.72 mm.

The weakly convex front surface has R16 = +470.3733 mm, followed by R17 = −120.4058 mm. Unlike Example 3's positive meniscus, this element moves with L8. The rear pair remains weakly negative overall. Source: rows 16–17, ¶0058.

## Focus Mechanism

The prescription uses two coupled moving assemblies, with the stop traveling with G1. The production lens is manually driven; separate cam motion is reported by firsthand examination. The patent gives three spacing states, not a continuous production cam profile.

| Source state | D13 (mm) | D15 (mm) | D17 (mm) | G1 objectward travel (mm) | G2 objectward travel (mm) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Infinity | 1.0000 | 1.4858 | 42.9988 | 0 | 0 |
| Nominal 1× | 37.2260 | 1.4858 | 50.1709 | 43.3981 | 7.1721 |
| Nominal 2× | 75.5119 | 1.4858 | 53.2876 | 84.8007 | 10.2888 |

At the authored image plane, paraxial magnifications are −1.001068 and −2.002452. The corresponding object-to-first-surface distances are 98.168348 and 67.784898 mm. Adding the current first-surface/image tracks gives object–image distances of 216.296448 and 227.315598 mm. The state EFLs are 51.489130 and 44.752739 mm. The small departures from nominal 1×/2× are retained rather than fitted away.

The focus control assigns ordered source coordinates 0 / 0.5 / 1 to infinity / nominal 1× / nominal 2×. This avoids using non-monotonic object–image distance as the mechanical coordinate. Both variable gaps interpolate continuously, and all three source rows are reached exactly. Between source rows the spacing path is a piecewise-linear visualization, not a measured production cam law. Distances remain numeric and use the current paraxial conjugate. Halfway through the first interval the readout is 25.6 cm; halfway through the second it is 21.7 cm. The source states read 21.6 cm and 22.7 cm, with whole-centimeter endpoint and station-button distances.

The effective-aperture estimate follows the current axial image cone relative to the infinity cone, retaining the marked f-number calibration. It accounts for the changed conjugate and pupil geometry without treating slider position as inverse distance. For the unchanged source model at marked f/2.9, the estimates are approximately f/6.11 at nominal 1× and f/9.29 at nominal 2×. They are first-order model estimates, not production exposure measurements. Finite MTF remains limited to the two actual source spacing states; interpolated states acquire no source certification.

## Verification Summary

An independent reduced-angle ABCD calculation and the shared runtime paraxial tracer agree on all three source states. The calculated infinity EFL differs from the printed summary by −0.000351 mm; Gaussian back focal distance differs from the authored D17 by −0.000195 mm. Neither residual is fitted away. The Petzval sum is 0.002853182 mm⁻¹. Extra computed digits support reproducibility, not measurement accuracy. The image plane and every printed optical input remain unchanged.

The physical stop radius, inferred by exact axial Snell tracing at source f/2.90, is 8.157121 mm. All element clear radii are inferred. Figure 3 was inspected at 600 dpi; the automatic envelope's horizontal-axis contamination invalidates its absolute scale, so clean optical rims and physical surface/gap checks determine the retained outline. The L4 rear step and the smaller clear radius of L8 are model bounds, not published production bevels. Surface, image-circle and axial-aperture diagnostics pass; the stated axial beam is retained. Full-field checks reach the modeled APS-C corner. Across 251 slider samples, both group travels remain monotonic and no declared rim requires render trimming. The display scale retains the entire 2× section with a fixed image-plane anchor. These checks establish internal model consistency and do not resolve the production-distance or dimensional discrepancies above.
