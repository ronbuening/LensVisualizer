**Title:** 移轴微距镜头 (Tilt-shift macro lens)

The prescription transcribes the selected native example associated with the LAOWA 55mm f/2.8 Tilt-Shift 1× Macro. The association is construction-based; Laowa has not confirmed that these exact numerical surfaces are the manufactured prescription. The evidence converges as follows:

1. The patent prescription contains 14 glass elements in 11 air-separated components and three cemented doublets; the official manual publishes the same element/component count.
2. The native infinity focal length of 55.0179 mm is consistent with the nominal 55 mm designation; the model retains the native scale.
3. Native f/2.87, the source 0.5x/1.0x stations, and the 67 mm image-circle statement align with the product’s macro/shift purpose.
4. The separated front components and main/rear floating assemblies agree topologically with the official production overlay. That illustration is a barrel overlay, not a dimensioned prescription. [4]

Example 1 is kept distinct from the other examples. The patent title, numerical tables and Figure 1 govern the model; production specifications govern the commercial identity. The current manual lists Sony E, Canon RF, Nikon Z, L-Mount, Fujifilm G and Hasselblad XCD variants. The data uses canonical mount identifiers and a full-frame reference format; 67 mm is separate source coverage metadata, not a 67 mm sensor diagonal. [1,2]

## Optical Architecture

The model contains 14 physical glass elements in 11 air-separated components. All refracting surfaces are spherical or planar. No aspheric coefficient, rear sensor plate, front filter, dummy optical plane or artificial cement layer is introduced. The source stop is retained as STO at its native axial position. [1]

The fixed front group, main moving group and auxiliary rear group have computed paraxial focal lengths of -235.520, +47.716 and -143.673 mm. These are separated group-in-air powers, not an attribution of each group’s in-situ aberration contribution.

The first two negative singlets form G1a, with focal length -40.779 mm. The following positive singlet weakens the complete fixed front group. This negative-positive-negative power distribution is the defining source architecture. [1, paragraph 0053]

At infinity, the implemented EFL is 55.018494 mm and the collimated back focal distance is 46.809061 mm. The source image gap remains 46.8077 mm. First-vertex-to-image track is 190.1122 mm, giving track/EFL=3.455424 and BFD/EFL=0.850788. The track and back-focus reference planes are stated explicitly; the selected infinity model is not assigned a telephoto or retrofocus label by appearance alone.

The front principal plane lies +69.480821 mm from the first vertex and the rear principal plane -8.209433 mm from the last vertex. The Gaussian entrance pupil position is +56.988477 mm from the first vertex; the exit pupil is -24.371487 mm from the last vertex. Positive coordinates point imageward.

The surface-by-surface Petzval sum is +0.001626655 mm⁻¹ at the d-line. This is a first-order surface-power sum, not the actual best-focus field curvature of a finite-aperture ray bundle.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

All focal lengths in the following first lines describe the individual element isolated in air, using its native central thickness and surface curvatures. For a cemented member, this deliberately differs from its actual interface environment. Nd and νd are the source d-line coordinates; no supplier or melt identity is implied.

### L1 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.61. Glass: H-FK61 (CDGM coordinate equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = -81.269398 mm (isolated in air).

The first member of the fixed negative front section has a weakly curved object face and a much stronger rear curvature. Its isolated negative power is part of G1a; its high Abbe number does not independently identify the supplier or establish anomalous partial dispersion.

### L2 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.49700, νd = 85.00. Glass: Unmatched (native nd=1.49700, vd=85.00; supplier unconfirmed). f = -100.856000 mm (isolated in air).

The second front member is biconcave and completes the two consecutive negative lenses identified as G1a in paragraph 0053. The unusual native index/Abbe pair is retained exactly. Replacing its Abbe number with the 81.61 value of other elements would change the source prescription.

### L3 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.57371, νd = 70.00. Glass: Unmatched (native nd=1.57371, vd=70.00; supplier unconfirmed). f = +68.115562 mm (isolated in air).

This thick positive singlet closes the fixed front group. Its positive power partly offsets the two preceding negative members, so the complete G1 is considerably weaker than G1a alone. The large following gap is the principal source-tabulated space consumed by main-group focusing.

### L4 — Plano-Convex

nd = 1.88300, νd = 40.80. Glass: 883408 — coordinate class; supplier unconfirmed. f = +107.246206 mm (isolated in air).

This plano-convex singlet begins the moving main focusing assembly. Its rear plane is explicitly printed inf. It is separated from the next positive member; no cement layer is inserted between them.

### L5 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.88300, νd = 40.80. Glass: 883408 — coordinate class; supplier unconfirmed. f = +120.195805 mm (isolated in air).

This positive meniscus is the second singlet of G2. It precedes the first cemented negative/positive pair. Its power and location are established by the prescription; a specific isolated coma or astigmatism contribution is not established by those facts.

### L6 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.85561, νd = 23.21. Glass: Unmatched (native nd=1.85561, vd=23.21; supplier unconfirmed). f = -20.536958 mm (isolated in air).

This biconcave negative element is the front member of D1. Its rear surface is the physical cemented boundary to L7. The isolated-in-air focal length replaces that neighboring glass with air for the calculation and must not be interpreted as the power of the actual cemented interface.

### L7 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.92286, νd = 20.88. Glass: 923209 — coordinate class; supplier unconfirmed. f = +21.130031 mm (isolated in air).

This biconvex positive member completes D1. The dense glass on both sides of the cemented interface makes the actual index step different from either isolated-air construction. The compound focal length is listed separately in the architecture discussion.

### L8 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.78. Glass: H-ZF52 (CDGM coordinate equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = -30.519113 mm (isolated in air).

This negative meniscus begins D2. The following low-index, high-Abbe positive member creates a strong material contrast at the shared surface. That contrast is directly supported; a quantitative chromatic correction claim would need wavelength-dependent optical evidence.

### L9 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.61. Glass: H-FK61 (CDGM coordinate equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = +38.591075 mm (isolated in air).

This biconvex positive member completes D2. Its native 81.61 Abbe number is lower dispersion than the preceding flint coordinate. No catalog spectrum or production ED assignment is copied onto it solely from that comparison.

### L10 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.78451, νd = 48.06. Glass: Unmatched (native nd=1.78451, vd=48.06; supplier unconfirmed). f = +82.750883 mm (isolated in air).

This positive meniscus closes G2 immediately ahead of the aperture. It moves with the main focusing assembly and the source stop. Its weak rear curvature does not make that surface a mathematical plane.

### L11 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.78346, νd = 32.08. Glass: Unmatched (native nd=1.78346, vd=32.08; supplier unconfirmed). f = -73.186303 mm (isolated in air).

This negative meniscus begins G3 after the variable stop-to-rear-group gap. G3 is the independently moving auxiliary correction assembly described by the patent; the numerical model does not derive a separate manufacturing cam or actuator law.

### L12 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.61. Glass: H-FK61 (CDGM coordinate equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = -30.121838 mm (isolated in air).

This negative element begins rear doublet D3. Its front, cemented and rear surfaces share one inferred 11.5 mm semi-diameter, because Figure 1 and Laowa’s construction diagram both draw the pair as a square-cut block. These clear-aperture estimates are not dimensions published by the patent.

### L13 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.53878, νd = 52.52. Glass: Unmatched (native nd=1.53878, vd=52.52; supplier unconfirmed). f = +43.665809 mm (isolated in air).

This positive meniscus completes D3. The actual shared interface retains the downstream glass index and element identity. Its isolated positive power is distinct from the net power of the compound pair. The model does not use a synthetic cement film.

### L14 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.88300, νd = 40.80. Glass: 883408 — coordinate class; supplier unconfirmed. f = +73.984568 mm (isolated in air).

This final biconvex positive singlet is separated from D3. It is part of the net-negative moving rear assembly. Positive element power at this location alone does not prove that the element independently flattens the field or controls a named aberration.

## Focus Mechanism

The source supplies three coaxial states: infinity, 0.5x and 1.0x. G1 remains fixed; G2 moves objectward as the main focusing group and G3 independently moves objectward as the auxiliary correction group. The stop travels with G2. The production lens uses manual focus. No continuous source cam law is supplied. [1,2]

| ------------ | --------------: | --------------------: | ----------------------: | -------------------------------------: | -------------------------------: |
| infinity     |      47.3370 mm |             1.4700 mm |              46.8077 mm |             Infinity rounding residual |          Infinity (source state) |
| 0.5x         |      27.0548 mm |             6.7287 mm |              61.8312 mm |                           -0.499168871 |                    297.955281 mm |

The patent's variable-spacing table names the three gaps D(6), D(19) and BF; Figure 1 marks the infinity value of D(6) as D12, the G1–G2 separation of condition 5. From infinity to the printed 0.5x state G2 moves -20.2822 mm and G3 moves -15.0235 mm; from infinity to the printed 1.0x endpoint, G2 moves -46.2870 mm and G3 moves -37.9166 mm in the fixed first-vertex frame. The negative signs mean objectward motion, the direction of both arrows in Figure 1. The increasing BF exactly compensates the moving assemblies so the first-vertex/image track remains fixed at the source precision.

The source labels are preserved despite computed magnifications of -0.499168871 and -0.962488251. No radius, glass coordinate, focus gap or image plane was retuned to force the labels to become exact Gaussian reproduction ratios. The source 1.0x state is therefore not described as an independently reproduced 1:1 result.

The modeled endpoint object-to-image distance is 0.265469480 m. The manufacturer’s minimum object-to-image specification is 270 mm and remains a separate production quantity. The intermediate control coordinate is 0.890970884340, calculated as endpoint/intermediate Gaussian object-to-image distance. It aligns the three named-state distance labels; piecewise gap interpolation and other slider distance labels are not a published or continuously verified focus law. No finiteConjugates certification is added from the manufacturer MFD.

The source table publishes half-field ω=31.543° and a 67 mm image-circle diameter. These define source design context; finite-field numerical performance is limited by the modeled apertures and the retained ray diagnostics. [1]

## Aperture and Field Limits

No numerical clear apertures or iris diameter are published. Every stored semi-diameter is inferred. Initial Figure 1 silhouette estimates were constrained by positive element edge thickness, actual spherical rim slope and shared-band air-gap clearance, and checked at the authored and representative interpolated states. Such inferred rims cannot establish the exact manufactured silhouette.

The physical stop radius is 10.927100395 mm, obtained by exact axial tracing of an entrance ray at EFL/(2×2.87). This reproduces the current application’s nominal-aperture calibration. The corresponding Gaussian EFL/paraxial-pupil-diameter ratio is 2.792258; the difference is retained rather than calling both definitions f/2.87. Matching the target after calibration is not independent evidence for a source-published diaphragm dimension.

Later passes measured the Figure 1 raster directly at about 0.285 mm per native pixel. Its drawn outer rims agree with the retained blank heights within about 5%. L1 now preserves its 29.5 mm rear optical cap and draws an opaque land to the 38.5 mm outer rim, restoring the flanged block. L2 retains its 32.5 mm outer radius, level with L3, but its front sphere ends at radius 29.615 mm on a 4.60 mm inferred cut, followed by a flat land. This replaces the former tapered L1 and the overextended L2 front curve. L11 (surfaces 20 and 21) remains 11.0 mm, below the 11.5 mm rear doublet as drawn.

The rear doublet’s surfaces 22, 23 and 24 share an inferred 11.5 mm semi-diameter, matching the square-cut block of Figure 1 (measured 11.4–11.5 mm) and of Laowa’s construction diagram. An earlier 10.0 mm entrance limit at surface 22 was withdrawn because neither drawing shows a reduced front aperture. The exact axial marginal ray through the full iris reaches 9.11 mm at surface 22 at infinity, 8.36 mm at the 0.5x station and 8.27 mm at the 1.0x station, so the calibrated on-axis f-number is unchanged. Wide-field rays that enter surface 22 above roughly 10.2 mm end at the cemented rim of surface 23 inside the glass; that is ordinary rim vignetting. Modeled geometric transmission at 15.1° and infinity is 0.91, against 0.87 with the withdrawn limit.

Surface 2 keeps its 29.5 mm clear cap. The front/rear curved profiles would touch near radius 29.86 mm if both were continued, so the new opaque lands preserve their finite cap ends instead. The existing 98% cross-gap allowance is unchanged and both lands pass it. Figure 1 suggests a surface-3 cap near q=0.90 of the 32.5 mm outer rim, with a one-native-pixel band of about ±0.016. A literal smaller cap fails the inferred-cut beam rule: the complete wide-open beam reaches 29.4978 mm at infinity / field 27.34°. The adopted 29.6152 mm cap clears it by 0.1174 mm, exceeding the required margin. The inferred-cut sweep passes all sampled fields and focus states; neither the cap estimate nor the narrow gap is a manufactured tolerance measurement.

Actual project validation and the sampled production-shape diagnostics find no hidden geometry trim. Default diagram, on-axis, nominal full-frame and actual analysis-mid samples are explicitly assessed. Some field rays are clipped at element rims or the iris. Native status ok denotes traversal of the modeled optical stack; it does not by itself certify an exact sensor-coordinate hit or complete image-circle coverage.

The additional source-circle and active analysis-edge samples are less complete. Peripheral aiming failures remain unresolved and unavailable; they are not counted as clips, transmitted rays or successful coverage checks. The model does not certify full 67 mm edge throughput, complete pupil coverage or a performance guarantee for shifted operation.

## Tilt-Shift Scope

The official manual specifies ±12 mm shift and ±10° tilt. Those are production mechanical limits, separate from the three centered patent prescriptions. The patent supplies neither a dimensioned hinge location nor a complete noncoaxial optical configuration. The data therefore keeps the coaxial source states as the prescription and declares the official limits as a rigid movement of that whole prescription relative to the fixed sensor. The tilt pivot is a rear-vertex fallback placed 46.8077 mm in front of the image plane, the infinity-state back focus; it is a deterministic tracing reference, not the manufactured hinge. The ±12 mm and ±10° limits apply to the full-frame mounts. [1,2]
