The prescription transcribes the selected native example associated with the LAOWA 100mm f/2.8 Tilt-Shift 1X Macro. The association is construction-based; Laowa has not confirmed that these exact numerical surfaces are the manufactured prescription. The evidence converges as follows:

1. The patent prescription contains 13 glass elements in 10 air-separated components and three cemented doublets; the official manual publishes the same element/component count.
2. The native infinity focal length of 101.9933 mm is consistent with the nominal 100 mm designation; the model retains the native scale.
3. Native f/2.87, the source 0.5x/1.0x stations, and the 67 mm image-circle statement align with the product’s macro/shift purpose.
4. The separated front components and main/rear floating assemblies agree topologically with the official production overlay. That illustration is a barrel overlay, not a dimensioned prescription. [4]

Example 1 is kept distinct from the other examples. The patent title, numerical tables and Figure 1 govern the model; production specifications govern the commercial identity. The current manual lists Sony E, Canon RF, Nikon Z, L-Mount, Fujifilm G and Hasselblad XCD variants. The data uses canonical mount identifiers and a full-frame reference format; 67 mm is separate source coverage metadata, not a 67 mm sensor diagonal. [1,2]

## Optical Architecture

The model contains 13 physical glass elements in 10 air-separated components. All refracting surfaces are spherical or planar. No aspheric coefficient, rear sensor plate, front filter, dummy optical plane or artificial cement layer is introduced. The source stop is retained as STO at its native axial position. [1]

The fixed front group, main moving group and auxiliary rear group have computed paraxial focal lengths of +3232.197, +62.803 and -227.037 mm. These are separated group-in-air powers, not an attribution of each group’s in-situ aberration contribution.

The front negative/positive singlets form a weak positive group. The overall distribution is positive-positive-negative, with most converging action concentrated in G2. The optical classification is a floating-focus macro architecture; nominal telephoto usage does not establish a compact telephoto optical ratio. [1, paragraph 0047]

At infinity, the implemented EFL is 101.992427 mm and the collimated back focal distance is 40.315996 mm. The source image gap remains 40.3171 mm. First-vertex-to-image track is 178.0940 mm, giving track/EFL=1.746149 and BFD/EFL=0.395284. The track and back-focus reference planes are stated explicitly; the selected infinity model is not assigned a telephoto or retrofocus label by appearance alone.

The front principal plane lies +72.970520 mm from the first vertex and the rear principal plane -61.676431 mm from the last vertex. The Gaussian entrance pupil position is +88.973654 mm from the first vertex; the exit pupil is -47.843720 mm from the last vertex. Positive coordinates point imageward.

The surface-by-surface Petzval sum is +0.001441909 mm⁻¹ at the d-line. This is a first-order surface-power sum, not the actual best-focus field curvature of a finite-aperture ray bundle.

The cemented-component powers are calculated separately from the standalone elements:

## Element-by-Element Analysis

All focal lengths in the following first lines describe the individual element isolated in air, using its native central thickness and surface curvatures. For a cemented member, this deliberately differs from its actual interface environment. Nd and νd are the source d-line coordinates; no supplier or melt identity is implied.

### L1 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.53031, νd = 67.42. Glass: Unmatched (native nd=1.53031, vd=67.42; supplier unconfirmed). f = -395.020514 mm (isolated in air).

This weak biconcave element is the first fixed front singlet. It remains separated from L2, unlike the cemented first pair in the patent’s second example. This topology is one of the reasons the selected Example 1 is not mixed with neighboring prescriptions.

### L2 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.87510, νd = 45.25. Glass: Unmatched (native nd=1.87510, vd=45.25; supplier unconfirmed). f = +356.815853 mm (isolated in air).

This positive meniscus nearly balances the preceding negative singlet, leaving a weakly positive G1. The large following air gap is consumed as the main focus assembly moves toward the object. No exact production glass supplier follows from the high index.

### L3 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.89141, νd = 33.77. Glass: Unmatched (native nd=1.89141, vd=33.77; supplier unconfirmed). f = +59.641402 mm (isolated in air).

This positive meniscus begins G2. It is followed by an air gap rather than a cemented interface. In the extended-edge diagnostic some rays miss its finite entrance cap from air; those rays are not counted as transmitted.

### L4 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.78737, νd = 23.92. Glass: Unmatched (native nd=1.78737, vd=23.92; supplier unconfirmed). f = -27.765335 mm (isolated in air).

This biconcave negative member begins D1. Its rear face is cemented to L5. The tabulated isolated-air focal length is a comparison quantity and is not the power of the glass-to-glass junction.

### L5 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.92286, νd = 20.88. Glass: E-FDS1 (HOYA, coordinate equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = +24.547727 mm (isolated in air).

This biconvex positive element completes D1. Its high-index, low-Abbe coordinate differs from that of its negative partner. The prescription establishes the coordinate contrast, but does not publish enough spectral data to isolate the pair’s secondary-spectrum correction.

### L6 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.91392, νd = 23.35. Glass: Unmatched (native nd=1.91392, vd=23.35; supplier unconfirmed). f = -24.172157 mm (isolated in air).

This biconcave negative member begins D2. It is cemented to a lower-index positive member, creating the large native index contrast at surface11. The model preserves both materials without substituting a catalog melt.

### L7 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.61. Glass: FCD1 (HOYA, coordinate equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = +45.818331 mm (isolated in air).

This biconvex positive member completes D2 and retains the 81.61 Abbe value. The manufacturer’s marketing of two ED elements is useful correlation evidence; it is not proof of the exact production assignment or supplier of this patent coordinate.

### L8 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.88100, νd = 40.15. Glass: TAFD33 (HOYA, coordinate equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = +84.364777 mm (isolated in air).

This biconvex positive singlet closes G2 ahead of the source aperture. It moves with the main focus assembly. Its isolated power does not by itself identify a unique aberration-correction contribution.

### L9 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.88564, νd = 21.55. Glass: Unmatched (native nd=1.88564, vd=21.55; supplier unconfirmed). f = -69.217179 mm (isolated in air).

This negative meniscus begins the independent rear correction assembly G3. The source variable stop-to-group gap separates its motion from that of the principal focusing assembly.

### L10 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.68893, νd = 31.16. Glass: E-FD8 (HOYA, coordinate equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = -36.148975 mm (isolated in air).

This biconcave negative member begins rear doublet D3. Its common interface with L11 uses the actual downstream index and element identifier. No intervening artificial cement surface or thickness is added.

### L11 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.90326, νd = 27.29. Glass: Unmatched (native nd=1.90326, vd=27.29; supplier unconfirmed). f = +31.521008 mm (isolated in air).

This biconvex positive member completes D3. Its isolated positive power is distinct from the compound power of the negative/positive pair. The substantial following air gap separates D3 from the next negative member.

### L12 — Plano-Concave

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.61. Glass: FCD1 (HOYA, coordinate equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = -65.848893 mm (isolated in air).

This plano-concave negative singlet retains source R22 = 0.0000 in the evidence record. The implemented rear surface uses the explicitly justified plane convention. Figure 1 (PDF page 10) shows that face planar; Example 3 surface 15 STOP on PDF page 9 is also printed 0.0000, directly demonstrating the same publication’s zero-plane notation. Only that sibling notation is used, with no mixing of prescriptions. A physical zero-radius sphere is not used.

### L13 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.87156, νd = 41.46. Glass: Unmatched (native nd=1.87156, vd=41.46; supplier unconfirmed). f = +68.254567 mm (isolated in air).

This final biconvex positive singlet closes the net-negative G3 assembly. Its final air gap is the source BF image distance, preserved at each of the three source states. No sensor cover plate is supplied or added.

## Focus Mechanism

The source supplies three coaxial states: infinity, 0.5x and 1.0x. G1 remains fixed; G2 moves objectward as the main focusing group and G3 independently moves objectward as the auxiliary correction group. The stop travels with G2. The production lens uses manual focus. No continuous source cam law is supplied. [1,2]

| Source label | D12 | Stop-to-G3 gap | Last-glass-to-image BF | Computed signed Gaussian magnification | Derived object-to-image distance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| infinity | 49.3020 mm | 1.9000 mm | 40.3171 mm | Infinity rounding residual | Infinity (source state) |
| 0.5x | 22.8415 mm | 15.0845 mm | 53.5931 mm | -0.498246374 | 415.041319 mm |
| 1.0x | 1.0000 mm | 27.2633 mm | 63.2558 mm | -0.956664253 | 336.966989 mm |

From infinity to the printed 1.0x endpoint, G2 moves -48.3020 mm and G3 moves -22.9387 mm in the fixed first-vertex frame. The negative signs mean objectward motion. The increasing BF exactly compensates the moving assemblies so the first-vertex/image track remains fixed at the source precision.

The source labels are preserved despite computed magnifications of -0.498246374 and -0.956664253. No radius, glass coordinate, focus gap or image plane was retuned to force the labels to become exact Gaussian reproduction ratios. The source 1.0x state is therefore not described as an independently reproduced 1:1 result.

The modeled endpoint object-to-image distance is 0.336966989 m. The manufacturer’s minimum object-to-image specification is 320 mm and remains a separate production quantity. The intermediate control coordinate is 0.811887814248, calculated as endpoint/intermediate Gaussian object-to-image distance. It aligns the three named-state distance labels; piecewise gap interpolation and other slider distance labels are not a published or continuously verified focus law. No finiteConjugates certification is added from the manufacturer MFD.

The source table publishes half-field ω=18.058° and a 67 mm image-circle diameter. These define source design context; finite-field numerical performance is limited by the modeled apertures and the retained ray diagnostics. [1]

## Aperture and Field Limits

No numerical clear apertures or iris diameter are published. Every stored semi-diameter is inferred. Initial Figure 1 silhouette estimates were constrained by positive element edge thickness, actual spherical rim slope and shared-band air-gap clearance, and checked at the authored and representative interpolated states. A later pass measured Figure 1 directly: the element heights agree with the drawing within about 3%. Figure 1 draws L1 and L10 as square-cut blocks, so both faces of each carry the drawn outer height (35 and 16 mm); L10's concave front therefore runs past the point where the figure's flat mounting annulus begins (about 12.2 mm), and its modeled rim is thicker than drawn. L12's concave front stops at 18.9 mm, where its drawn annulus begins, so its rim keeps close to the drawn thickness. L13 is 24 mm, one millimetre taller than L12 as drawn. The front face of L2 stays at 30.197 mm because the facing surfaces of L1 and L2 meet at 32.2 mm. Such inferred rims cannot establish the exact manufactured silhouette.

The physical stop radius is 12.468768132 mm, obtained by exact axial tracing of an entrance ray at EFL/(2×2.87). This reproduces the current application’s nominal-aperture calibration. The corresponding Gaussian EFL/paraxial-pupil-diameter ratio is 2.802261; the difference is retained rather than calling both definitions f/2.87. Matching the target after calibration is not independent evidence for a source-published diaphragm dimension.

Actual project validation and the sampled production-shape diagnostics find no hidden geometry trim. Default diagram, on-axis, nominal full-frame and actual analysis-mid samples are explicitly assessed. Some field rays are clipped at physical external boundaries or the iris. Native status ok denotes traversal of the modeled optical stack; it does not by itself certify an exact sensor-coordinate hit or complete image-circle coverage.

The additional source-circle and active analysis-edge samples are less complete. Peripheral aiming failures remain unresolved and unavailable; they are not counted as clips, transmitted rays or successful coverage checks. For the 100 mm model, the retained native S5 noBracket diagnostics are independently explained by near-branch sphere intersections outside its finite entrance cap from air. The raw engine failures remain visible. The model does not certify full 67 mm edge throughput, complete pupil coverage or a performance guarantee for shifted operation.

## Tilt-Shift Scope

The official manual specifies ±12 mm shift and ±10° tilt. Those are production mechanical limits, separate from the three centered patent prescriptions. The patent supplies neither a dimensioned hinge location nor a complete noncoaxial optical configuration. The three source states are therefore coaxial. The viewer's shift and tilt controls use the manual's ±12 mm and ±10° ranges and pivot tilt about the infinity-state rear vertex (40.3171 mm ahead of the image plane) as a deterministic tracing reference; that pivot is not the manufactured hinge, and no tilted surface prescription or movement law comes from the patent.
