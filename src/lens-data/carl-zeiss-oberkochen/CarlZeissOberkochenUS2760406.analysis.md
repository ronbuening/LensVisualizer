# CARL ZEISS 100mm f/2 (US 2,760,406 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [US 2,760,406 A, _Three Component Photographic Objective_](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2760406A/en)

**Inventors:** Johannes Berger

**Assignee:** Carl-Zeiss-Stiftung; the US publication names Carl Zeiss, Heidenheim (Brenz), Germany

**Filed:** July 8, 1953

**Published:** August 28, 1956

**Priority:** July 24, 1952, Germany

**Example details:** The sole numerical example, headed “Example,” on PDF page 2.

The patent describes a four-element, three-component f/2 objective with a stated image angle of ±14°. It is intended primarily for small focal lengths, consistent with a cine application, but no production lens is documented. The Swiss counterpart is [CH 314696 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/CH314696A/en).

The text states “about f = 100” without specifying a length unit. The model interprets its dimensions in millimetres at the printed numerical scale. The resulting EFL is 100.89 mm, 0.9% above 100 mm. This agrees with the approximate wording; isolated digit changes that bring EFL to 100 mm worsen the marginal spherical correction of the published prescription.

## Optical Architecture

The three components have positive, negative and positive net power: a front positive meniscus, a biconcave negative singlet, and a rear cemented pair. Elements 3 and 4 meet at R6, with no intervening air space. All seven refracting surfaces are spherical.

The diaphragm lies between the second and third components, as the text states and the drawing shows. The drawing gives an estimated station 9.0 mm behind R4 and 2.3 mm ahead of R5, preserving the printed 11.3 mm air separation. At the model's f/2 setting, the computed iris radius is 17.82 mm, close to the approximately 17.7 mm opening read from the drawing. Neither the exact station nor the bore is tabulated.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent calls its reference the D line, conventionally taken as sodium at 589.3 nm. The printed coordinates instead resemble d-line glass values for LaK9, SF12 and BaF5, although the BaF5 Abbe number differs. The model uses the helium d line, 587.6 nm, while retaining every printed index and Abbe number. The reference-line wording remains unresolved.

No available catalogue curve is assigned to the LaK9-like and SF12-like coordinates of elements 1–3. Hoya BAF5 has an index of about 1.60729 and an Abbe number of 49.34: differences of +0.00000225 and +0.137 from element 4. Its Abbe number falls outside the printed precision of 49.2, and it does not establish the proposed Schott identity, so element 4 is also unmatched. No historical supplier, melt, partial dispersion or apochromatic performance is established.

## Focus Mechanism

The model represents infinity focus only. The patent gives no close-focus distances, moving-group law or finite-conjugate prescription.

No back focus is printed. The image plane is the computed paraxial plane, 69.02 mm behind R7. The first-to-last surface track is 70.3 mm, and the first-surface-to-image distance is 139.32 mm.

## Field and Aperture

The full field is 28.0°, matching the patent's ±14.0°. A real chief ray at 14.0° reaches an image height of 24.80 mm at the paraxial image plane, giving the 49.6 mm image circle. This diameter follows the patent's field at the adopted millimetre scale; no film format or lens mount is identified.

The retained clear semi-diameters at R1–R7 are 28, 28, 24, 20.2, 22, 20.5 and 22 mm. They are estimates rather than printed dimensions. They pass the axial f/2 beam and the 14° chief ray. Portions of the off-axis bundle are vignetted, so chief-ray coverage does not imply complete pupil transmission.

The drawing suggests smaller rims, approximately 25.3, 23.7, 20.6, 17.2 and 20.7 mm for the front, middle and rear faces. The rear pair is drawn with a common outer cylinder, whereas the modeled R6 radius of 20.5 mm is smaller than the adjacent 22 mm rims. These differences remain unresolved.

## Patent Conditions

Using the computed EFL, R2 = 434.01 mm is below ten focal lengths; |R3| = 100 mm exceeds both three times R4 = 82.15 mm and 80% of EFL = 80.72 mm. The R4-to-R6 vertex distance is 35.3 mm, exceeding both 25% of EFL = 25.22 mm and 115% of |R6| = 27.27 mm. The prescription therefore satisfies the stated geometric conditions.
