## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 3,134,841 A  
**Title:** Tele-Photo Lens  
**Filed:** 11 September 1961  
**Granted:** 26 May 1964  
**Inventor:** Helmut Knutti  
**Assignee:** Carl-Zeiss-Stiftung (printed as Carl Zeiss, Heidenheim (Brenz))  
**Embodiment analyzed:** Sole numerical table, description column 2; normalized to f = 1 and scaled ×500 to the stated 500 mm focal length.

The probable production lens is the historical Hasselblad C Tele-Tessar f/8 500 mm, Cat. No. 10 46 03, shown on the Zeiss technical data sheet. The identification does not extend to the later Tele-Apotessar lenses or to every barrel and coating revision. The sheet and the scaled prescription agree as follows:

1. Five elements in three components, with a positive front and negative rear assembly.
2. EFL 500.093705 mm against the sheet's 500.1 mm, and the 203.2 mm vertex span exactly.
3. Principal-plane positions of −413.508756 / −125.530320 mm from the first vertex against the printed −413.5 / −125.5 mm.
4. Exit-pupil position −8.417141 mm from the last vertex against −8.4 mm. The entrance-pupil position differs by 0.503236 mm and the exit-pupil diameter by about 1 %; these residuals are kept, not fitted away.

The patent's printed back focus of 171.4 mm differs from the prescription's Gaussian 171.363385 mm. The model places the infinity image plane at the Gaussian value, a −0.036615 mm shift; no surface or glass parameter is changed.

## Optical Architecture

Five spherical glass elements form three air-separated components: one front singlet, a front cemented doublet and a rear cemented doublet. The positive front assembly and negative rear assembly produce the short telephoto layout. The model has eight refracting surfaces plus the diaphragm.

The patent splits its long air gap into 159.5 mm before the diaphragm and 5 mm after it, which puts the stop 191.7 mm behind the first vertex. The stop diameter is derived from f/8. The patent's 24 mm figure is the clear passage of the between-the-lens shutter it is designed for, not an iris measurement.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The element names LI to LV are the patent's own designations from its table and drawing.

- LI is the positive biconvex front singlet, nd 1.51009 / νd 63.37, 12 mm thick.
- LII is the positive member of the front doublet, nd 1.51821 / νd 65.18, 12 mm thick.
- LIII is its negative biconcave member, nd 1.75520 / νd 27.53, 8 mm thick. The two are cemented on one shared surface.
- LIV is the positive member of the rear doublet, nd 1.62004 / νd 36.34, 3.5 mm thick. As the patent requires, this positive member faces the diaphragm and is the more dispersive of the pair.
- LV is the negative biconcave final member, nd 1.62041 / νd 60.29, 3 mm thick. The rear doublet's net power is negative.

## Glass and Dispersion

The patent gives only the d-line index and Abbe number, and the model keeps those values unchanged. The glass names are the closest catalog matches, used for dispersion, not identified melts: BK1 (a legacy Schott type, with the SUMITA catalog's dispersion curve), PC2 (Hoya), E-FD4 (Hoya), F2 (Schott) and S-BSM16 (Ohara). Catalog dispersion and partial-dispersion behavior are not established by the two source constants. No apochromatic performance claim follows.

## Focus Mechanism

The whole optical assembly moves as a unit relative to the image plane, from the Gaussian infinity focus to the maker's 8.5 m object-to-image distance. The back gap grows from 171.36 mm to 206.21 mm. All internal spacings, including the stop position, stay fixed.

The patent prints no finite-distance state, and the factory focusing mechanism is not modelled, so the close-focus states are calculated, not published.

## Aspherical Surfaces

All eight refracting surfaces are spherical. No aspheric coefficients are supplied or inferred.

## Apertures, Field and Model Limits

Clear semi-diameters are not printed. The model's radii are measured from the patent's drawing, which is to scale (0.250 mm per pixel at 300 dpi, checked on the first-vertex-to-image-plane and front-to-rear-group spans): 39.4 mm for the front singlet, 37.3 mm for the front cemented doublet, which the drawing shows slightly narrower than the singlet, and 12.0 mm for the rear cemented doublet. All three are drawn square-cut, so each element carries one value on every face. The f/8 axial beam clears every rim. The drawing's diaphragm, at the printed stop position, has an opening of about 19 mm. That is schematic: the model's f/8 stop is 23.9 mm across, which matches the 24 mm shutter passage named in the text. The 86 mm filter size and 90 mm barrel diameter are outer bounds, not measured clear apertures. The rims set the off-axis vignetting: the front-group rims cut the outer part of the oblique beams.

The 6×6 format is 56 × 56 mm; the historical sheet gives 56.5 × 56.5 mm, a semi-diagonal of about 39.95 mm. The chief ray reaches that corner, but the beam there is partly vignetted.

The maker's white-light MTF curves have not been reproduced. The production identification is probable, not confirmed from factory data.
