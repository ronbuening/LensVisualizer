## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [CN 214795390 U](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN214795390U/zh)
**Filed:** June 2, 2021
**Granted:** November 19, 2021
**Inventors:** Jiannan Zhuang (庄建南), Yuanhua Ye (叶远华)
**Assignee:** Shenzhen Yongnuo Photographic Equipment Co., Ltd. (深圳市永诺摄影器材股份有限公司)
**Title:** An ultra-wide-angle imaging lens and imaging equipment

The design is identified with the YN16mm F1.8S DA DSM, Yongnuo's APS-C wide-angle prime for Sony E. The patent is Yongnuo's own and matches the lens in its 10 elements in 8 groups, a single moving double-aspheric focus element, and 16.34 mm, f/1.78 and 2ω = 83° (¶0062) against the marketed 16 mm, f/1.8 and 82.62°. The closest tabulated focus state lies 0.17 m from the image at 0.15× magnification, the lens's published minimum focus distance and maximum magnification, and the patent's aberration plots carry the design-file name "16F1.8E APS-C". Yongnuo has not published a factory prescription; the model is the patent's sole embodiment (Tables 1–4, pages 7–8; Figure 1, page 9), in the patent's millimetres, unscaled.

## Optical Architecture

Ten elements form eight air-separated groups. Three front menisci (G1–G3) feed two cemented doublets, GJ1 (G4–G5) and GJ2 (G6–G7), with the stop between them 34.9 mm behind the first vertex (claim 4). A positive double-aspheric meniscus (G8) is the only moving element, followed by a fixed negative/positive rear pair (G9–G10). The element and group labels are the patent's own (¶0044, Figure 1). A 2 mm plane-parallel plate (GL) between the last lens and the image is traced as a rear plate and not drawn.

Paraxially GR1 (G1–G7) is positive (+25.9 mm), GR2 (G8) positive (+29.2 mm) and GR3 (G9–G10) weakly negative (−141 mm), as claim 1 and ¶0042 state; the abstract's positive–negative–positive order is wrong. ¶0042 also places the stop between GR2 and GR3 and moves GR2 toward the image, while Tables 1 and 3 and Figure 1 put the stop between the doublets and move G8 toward the object. The model follows the tables and Figure 1.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Focal lengths are for each element alone in air.

### G1: Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.730766, νd = 40.501. Glass: M-LAF81 (HOYA). f = +150.9 mm.

Weak positive front meniscus. Table 4 gives F11 = 150 mm, inside the 80–400 mm range of condition (1), which the patent links to compactness and to lateral-colour control in the front group (¶0046). Claim 3 defines F11 as this element's focal length; ¶0046 calls it the focal length of GR1, which Table 4 does not support.

### G2: Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.72916, νd = 54.49924. Glass: TAC8P (HOYA). f = −32.4 mm.

First of two strong negative menisci, concave to the image, that give the retrofocus front its divergence. With G1 and G3 it forms the F13 sub-group: Table 4 gives F13 = −17.0292 mm for condition (2) (¶0047–0049); the prescription gives −17.09 mm.

### G3: Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.496999, νd = 81.61466. Glass: H-FK61 (CDGM) — low-dispersion fluor crown. f = −30.7 mm.

Second negative meniscus. Its low dispersion limits the lateral colour of the strongly diverging front group. It is one of the two special-dispersion elements Yongnuo lists; the position is inferred from the glass.

### G4: Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.900696, νd = 37.05356. Glass: Unmatched (patent nd 1.900696 / νd 37.05). f = −69.0 mm.

High-index negative meniscus at the front of GJ1. The patent describes GJ1 as an achromatic pair added to correct lateral colour, which is hard to control at this field angle (¶0050–0052). GJ1 as a whole is positive (f ≈ +25.8 mm).

### G5: Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.437, νd = 90.70237. Glass: Unmatched (patent nd 1.437 / νd 90.70) — fluorophosphate extra-low-dispersion class. f = +18.5 mm.

Positive half of GJ1. Condition (3) requires νd > 85 for this element; below that, the patent says, the front group's lateral colour becomes too large (¶0051–0052). Its nd/νd place it in the fluorophosphate class, but no catalog row matches both values.

### G6: Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.910822, νd = 35.25001. Glass: TAFD35 (HOYA). f = +16.1 mm.

High-index front half of GJ2, just behind the stop.

### G7: Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.71852, νd = 33.45655. Glass: ADF455 (HOYA) catalog equivalent. f = −17.0 mm.

Rear half of GJ2. The pair is weakly positive (f ≈ +103 mm) and closes GR1.

### G8: Positive Meniscus (2× Asph)

nd = 1.726, νd = 54. Glass: Unmatched (patent nd 1.726 / νd 54). f = +29.2 mm.

The whole focus group GR2: one positive meniscus, concave to the object, aspheric on both faces and 7.95 mm thick. Condition (4) holds its centre thickness between 6 and 9 mm: thinner leaves the spherical aberration of the fixed groups under-corrected, thicker makes the element too heavy for fast focusing (¶0053–0055). The patent text calls it an aspheric negative lens; its radii make it positive.

### G9: Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.761819, νd = 26.55365. Glass: S-TIH14 (OHARA). f = −18.5 mm.

Dense-flint negative element of the fixed rear group GR3. Condition (5) requires the rear element's νd to exceed this one's (56.42 > 26.55). The patent says GR3 corrects its own colour and that its negative spherical aberration offsets the positive spherical aberration of GR1 and GR2 (¶0056–0058).

### G10: Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.696732, νd = 56.42017. Glass: Unmatched (patent nd 1.696732 / νd 56.42). f = +24.6 mm.

Last element. Its rear vertex sits 17.56 mm from the image including the 2 mm plate, inside the 13–19 mm range of condition (6) set for mirrorless mounts (¶0059–0061).

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent prints d-line nd/νd only, with no glass names. Six glasses match catalog rows within 1 × 10⁻⁴ in nd and 0.15 in νd: M-LAF81 (G1), TAFD35 (G6) and HOYA's discontinued ADF455 (G7) reproduce the printed values to the last digit, and TAC8P (G2), H-FK61 (G3) and S-TIH14 (G9) lie within 0.04 in νd. G4, G5, G8 and G10 have no exact row and keep the patent values. The plate keeps nd 1.516330 and νd 64.15015. The printed values of G3, G9 and the plate (Table 4 prints G9's νd as 26.553651) equal OHARA's discontinued FPL51, PBH14 and BSL7 to every printed digit, so the design file probably used an older OHARA catalog beside HOYA's.

Yongnuo's catalog legend marks low-dispersion, extra-low-dispersion, high-index and aspherical elements. G3 (νd 81.6) and G5 (νd 90.7) are the only low-dispersion glasses in the prescription, both in the front group that the patent makes responsible for lateral colour (¶0052); G4 and G6 (nd 1.90 and 1.91) are the high-index elements.

## Focus Mechanism

Only G8 (surfaces 14 and 15) moves; GR1 and GR3 stay fixed (¶0032, ¶0042). Table 3 prints three states: INF, X20 (object at 640.38 mm) and MOD (91.094 mm, the closest; ¶0069). The gaps d13/d15 are 5.51/2.26, 5.17/2.60 and 3.62/4.15 mm, always summing to 7.77 mm, so G8 travels 0.34 mm and 1.89 mm toward the object. ¶0042 says the group moves toward the image; the table and the arrow in Figure 1 show the opposite.

The patent does not say where the object distances are measured from. Measured from the first surface, both states focus within 0.02 mm of the printed image plane, and MOD lies 171.9 mm from the image at a paraxial magnification of −0.151, matching Yongnuo's 0.17 m and 0.15×. Table 3's focal lengths (16.207 and 15.677 mm) differ slightly from the prescription's 16.220 and 15.641 mm. Figure 3 is captioned as the infinity state (¶0075) but labelled configuration 3 of 3, and its distortion (about −7% at 42.6°) matches the MOD state rather than infinity (−4.2%). The model shows the infinity state only.

## Aspherical Surfaces

Surfaces 14 and 15 use conic sag plus an even polynomial in h⁴, h⁶, h⁸ and h¹⁰ (millimeters), with K = 0. The patent prints no sag formula; its C4–C10 map directly to A4–A10.

- Surface 14: C4 = −1.18330e−4, C6 = −7.26147e−7, C8 = −2.56514e−10, C10 = −6.45424e−11.
- Surface 15: C4 = −1.81750e−5, C6 = −2.43417e−7, C8 = +7.78733e−10, C10 = −8.46798e−12.

The patent keeps every element of GR1 and GR3 spherical, so the moving lens carries all of the asphericity (¶0055).

## Model Scope and Limitations

The model is the Table 1 infinity state at the patent's scale: EFL 16.351 mm against the printed 16.34 mm, f/1.78, and 80.78 mm from the first surface to the image. Paraxial focus falls 1.063 mm behind the plate, against the printed 1.06 mm. Table 4's Bf of 17.56 mm is the physical distance including the plate; its air-equivalent length is 16.88 mm.

The patent prints no clear apertures, so all semi-diameters are inferred from Figure 1, which is drawn close to scale. S7 (9.11 mm) and S9 (9.19 mm) are just large enough to pass the full axial bundle at the model's f/1.78 stop, which is set from the paraxial entrance pupil; Figure 1 draws that doublet at about 9.3 mm. Figure 1 also draws G2, G3 and G8 as square blanks in which the shorter face ends in a flat land at the edge, and the model draws them the same way; G8's aspheric front face ends at 7.5 mm, inside its 9.5 mm edge. Off axis the lens vignettes strongly: about 89% of the axial bundle reaches 20.75° and about 30% reaches the 41.5° field edge. The 41.5° chief ray lands at 13.87 mm (−4.1% distortion), about 98% of the 14.18 mm APS-C half-diagonal the site uses.

G4, G5, G8 and G10 carry only the patent's nd/νd. G5's partial dispersion, the one that matters most for colour correction, is unknown. With Abbe-number dispersion for those four, the model's paraxial C and F foci fall 13 and 28 µm behind the d focus, against about 12 and 26 µm in Figure 2, and its d-line spherical aberration matches Figure 2 within about 2 µm at 50% and 70% of the pupil.
