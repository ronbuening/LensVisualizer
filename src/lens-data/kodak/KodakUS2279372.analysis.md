# KODAK 100mm f/3.5 (US 2,279,372 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,279,372 A, “Photographic Objective”

**Inventors:** Maximilian J. Herzberger

**Assignee:** Eastman Kodak Company

**Filed:** October 3, 1940

**Published:** April 14, 1942

**Example details:** Example VI, numerical table at the upper right of PDF page 3.

The [patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2279372A/en) describes six triplet examples and calls Examples I–IV preferable for quality of correction. Example VI supplies the f/3.5 prescription shown here. No manufactured product is documented for this example.

The printed table gives focal length 100 mm and back focus 78.5 mm. Its radii, spacings and sodium-D indices compute to EFL 100.75 mm and paraxial back focus 79.41 mm. Examples I–V show the same loose focal-length normalization; ordinary rounding does not establish an explanation for the residual. The model retains the printed prescription and the computed paraxial image plane. R2 = +251.0 mm would reduce the discrepancy, but it is only an unconfirmed possibility: the page clearly prints +241.0 mm, which remains in the model.

## Optical Architecture

Three air-spaced spherical singlets form a positive–negative–positive triplet. A positive front meniscus and a positive rear biconvex element surround a biconcave negative element. There are no cemented interfaces or aspherical surfaces. The first-to-last powered-vertex distance is 32.9 mm.

The patent describes choosing suitable glass ranges from the required power, spacing and aberration balance, rather than fixing particular glasses at the outset. All three elements have sodium-D indices greater than 1.6. The drawing on PDF page 1 accompanies Example I and establishes the general arrangement, rather than dimensions for Example VI.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### Element 1 — Positive Meniscus

nD = 1.6160, νD = 55.1. Glass: Unmatched. Focal length in air: +59.39 mm.

The front collector has radii +32.1/+241.0 mm and thickness 6.7 mm. Its front surface supplies most of its positive power; the much weaker rear surface bends in the same direction.

### Element 2 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.6490, νD = 33.8. Glass: Unmatched. Focal length in air: −33.47 mm.

The negative middle element has radii −71.4/+31.6 mm and thickness 2.2 mm. Its lower Abbe number makes it more dispersive than the two positive elements, providing the opposing power and dispersion used in the triplet's correction balance.

### Element 3 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.6160, νD = 55.1. Glass: Unmatched. Focal length in air: +52.29 mm.

The rear collector has radii +82.8/−51.0 mm and thickness 7.1 mm. It shares the front element's published glass coordinates and completes the positive–negative–positive power distribution.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The claims explicitly identify the D line, so the model retains sodium-D indices at approximately 589.3 nm. Neither published index/Abbe pair has an exact catalog match within Δn = 0.0001 and Δν = 0.15. All three glasses remain unmatched, with the printed coordinates stated in their labels.

The patent supplies no measured spectral-index series or partial dispersion. Curves calculated from index and Abbe number do not establish the historical melt or measured chromatic performance.

## Stop, Clear Apertures and Focus

The patent prints neither a diaphragm station nor its diameter. The model places the stop halfway through the 7.5 mm rear air space, 3.75 mm from either adjacent vertex. Its inferred radius is 11.075 mm for the nominal f/3.5 aperture. Finite marginal-ray geometry gives an entrance-aperture equivalent of approximately f/3.55.

Clear semi-diameters are estimated: 18/18 mm for Element 1, 14/14 mm for Element 2, and 18/18 mm for Element 3. The middle element's equal rim heights follow the proportions of the general drawing on PDF page 1, which depicts Example I rather than dimensions for Example VI. All six rims pass the axial f/3.5 beam and the 26.0° chief ray. Wide-open geometric transmission is about 69.8% at 26.0°; the front element partly clips the off-axis bundle. No rim lands or cuts into the glass thickness are inferred.

The model is focused at infinity. The patent gives no close-focus distances or focusing movement. No cover plate, filter or mechanical baffle is added.

## Field and Model Scope

Example VI states “covering power 26°” without explicitly defining full or half angle. The model reads this as a half-angle because, across all six examples, the stated covering power follows the onset of large tangential field curvature. For Example VI the tangential focus departs by about −1.64 mm at 26.0°, compared with less than 1 mm through 24.0° and a nearly flat field at 13.0°. This is an interpretation of the source, not an explicit angle definition in the patent.

The model uses a 52.0° full field and a 26.0° tracing limit. At the retained image plane, the real 26.0° chief-ray height is 48.80 mm, giving an image-circle diameter of 97.61 mm, rounded upward. This describes the interpreted patent field at the model's dimensions; it does not identify a film format or guarantee edge sharpness. The chief ray clears the rims, while the front element partly vignettes the edge bundle.
