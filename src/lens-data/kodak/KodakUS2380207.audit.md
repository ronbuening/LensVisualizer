# KODAK 100mm f/5.6 (US 2,380,207 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,380,207 A, “Telephoto Objective”](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2380207A/en), by George H. Aklin, assigned to Eastman Kodak Company. Filed 14 December 1943, application serial number 514,230; published 10 July 1945. The sole numerical example appears on the drawing sheet, PDF page 1, and again on PDF page 2, the first specification page. PDF page 3 gives claim 5's approximate focal-length ratios and defines the refractive index at the D line, taken as sodium, 589.3 nm.

Schott's [optical-glass catalog](https://www.schott.com/en-us/products/optical-glass-p1000267/downloads) and Sumita's [optical-glass catalog](https://www.sumita-opt.co.jp/en/download/) supply the comparison curves for N-BK10 and K11. No production-lens source is documented.

## Transcription Check

All eight signed radii, five glass thicknesses, two air spaces, five D-line indices and five Abbe numbers agree with the page images of both numerical tables. The drawing confirms five spherical elements in three air-spaced groups, with cemented interfaces at R2 and R5. No transcription mismatch was found. Dimensions retain the printed millimetre scale; there are no aspherical surfaces or variable spacings.

Both tables print S2 = 0.05 mm. The prose instead gives 0.005f, or 0.5 mm at f = 100 mm, while claim 5 specifies only S2 < 0.01F. The repeated table value is retained because the drawing shows the rear elements almost touching, and 0.05 mm gives closer agreement with the stated focal length and distortion pattern. R6 and R7 remain separate air boundaries despite the equal glass coordinates on either side. The positive rear air gap widens away from the axis.

## First-Order Check

The printed values are 100 mm, f/5.6 and back focus 53.2 mm. The prescription gives EFL 100.23 mm, paraxial f/5.60 and BFD 53.66 mm. The image plane lies at the calculated paraxial focus, 0.46 mm behind the printed back-focus position and 84.61 mm behind the first vertex. No single misprint explains both differences.

The first-to-last-vertex length is 30.95 mm, consistent with the printed 0.31f. The Petzval sum is +0.00008355 mm⁻¹ against the printed 0.000. The inferred iris radius is 6.515 mm. The real axial marginal ray for an exact f/5.60 entrance aperture would require about 6.70 mm there; the retained iris gives approximately f/5.75 by the real-ray entrance-aperture measure.

The surfaces stay clear of each other, and the iris sets the axial aperture. All chief rays through 15.0° reach the image without rim blockage. At 15.0° the chief-ray height is 26.85 mm, giving an inferred image-circle diameter of 53.71 mm. Off-axis bundles are partly vignetted: about 62% of one meridional bundle side is clipped at the rear surface at 15.0°. This is a bundle-width comparison, not a light-transmission percentage.

The patent gives distortion of 0.024%, 0.038% and 0.013% of focal length at 9.0°, 12.0° and 15.0°. The model gives about +0.016%, +0.022% and −0.001%, retaining similarly small distortion and the turn near 15.0° without exactly reproducing the printed values.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The text suggests a diaphragm about 9 units behind R3, but supplies no exact bore. The stop is 9 mm after R3 and 12.1 mm before R4, 15.1 mm behind the first vertex; its radius follows paraxial f/5.6.

Clear semi-diameters are estimates: 9.8/9.8/10 mm on the front doublet, 7.4 mm on the rear doublet and 7.8 mm on the rear singlet. The drawing suggests about 8.6 mm at the front, 5.9 mm on the rear doublet and 6.4 mm on the rear singlet. The real f/5.6 axial beam reaches about 8.95 mm at R1, so the drawn front outline alone would be too small. The larger modeled rear rims preserve illumination through the stated field; the physical clear apertures remain unprinted.

The 30.0° full field follows the patent's distortion points through 15.0°. The 53.71 mm image circle follows the real chief ray at that angle; it is not a printed film format or a fully unvignetted field. The patent gives no close-focus state: `focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. The selectable f-stops extend from f/5.6 to f/32; the source does not establish f/32 as a minimum iris opening.

The patent supplies no glass supplier or partial-dispersion data. N-BK10 provides a spectral proxy for Element 1, and K11 matches Element 3 within the printed precision at the sodium D line. Both supply catalog dispersion curves without establishing historical suppliers or melts. All printed index and Abbe pairs are retained. Elements 2, 4 and 5 remain unmatched and use Abbe-based dispersion estimates; their secondary spectrum remains unknown. No anomalous-dispersion designation is supported.

## Product Identification

No manufactured lens is documented for this prescription. The title identifies the Eastman Kodak patent design at its printed 100 mm scale. No marketed focal length, marketed aperture, production minimum focus distance, mount, image format, diaphragm blade count or blade shape is assigned.

## Remaining Questions

- The cause of the printed 53.2 mm back focus and the conflicting 0.005f rear-gap statement remains unknown.
- The physical clear apertures are not printed. The modeled rear rims remain larger than the drawing to preserve illumination; exact production apertures cannot be established from this source.
- Elements 2, 4 and 5 have no catalog dispersion curves within tolerance. Historical glass suppliers, melt identities, partial dispersions and secondary-spectrum behavior remain unknown for all elements.
- No source establishes production use, mount, image format, diaphragm blade count or shape, minimum iris opening, marketed specifications or a close-focus mechanism. Those production values remain omitted.
