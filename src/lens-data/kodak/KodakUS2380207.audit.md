# KODAK 100mm f/5.6 (US 2,380,207 Design) — Audit

## Source

US 2,380,207 A, “Telephoto Objective,” by George H. Aklin, assigned to Eastman Kodak Company. Filed 14 December 1943; published 10 July 1945. The sole numerical example appears on the drawing sheet and again in the specification. Claim 5 gives rounded focal-length ratios and identifies the D line, taken as sodium, 589.3 nm.

## Transcription

Two independent readings of the page images agree on all eight signed radii, five glass thicknesses, two air spaces, five D-line indices, and five Abbe numbers. The drawing confirms five elements in three groups with cemented interfaces at R2 and R5. The text places the diaphragm about 9 units behind R3; the 21.1 mm gap is modeled as 9/12.1 mm around it.

Both tables print S2 = 0.05 mm, whereas the prose gives 0.005f, or 0.5 mm at f = 100 mm. Claim 5 specifies S2 < 0.01F. The repeated table value is retained because the figure, focal length, and distortion pattern support it. R6 and R7 remain separate air boundaries despite equal glass coordinates on either side.

## First-order

The printed nominal values are 100 mm, f/5.6, and back focus 53.2 mm. The prescription computes to EFL 100.226982799 mm, paraxial f/5.6, and back focus 53.655204174 mm. The stored image plane is the paraxial plane, 0.455204174 mm beyond the printed position. No single misprint explains both focal-length and back-focus differences.

The first-to-last-vertex length is 30.95 mm; the image plane is 84.605204174 mm behind the first vertex. The Petzval sum is +0.00008355 mm⁻¹ against the printed 0.000. The inferred iris radius is 6.514638065 mm. The real-ray entrance-aperture measure is approximately f/5.75.

## Not printed

The patent supplies no clear semi-diameters, precise diaphragm bore, film format, mount, close-focus state, or partial-dispersion data. The retained rims are estimates. The image-circle diameter is derived from the real chief ray at the patent's 15° distortion point rather than from a named film format.

## Product

No manufactured lens is documented for this prescription. The name identifies the Kodak patent design.

## Corrections

The printed prescription and calculated infinity image plane are retained. The previous ±5° field understated the patent's distortion discussion through 15°. The source notes distinguish printed values from inferred apertures and calculated quantities.

## Remaining questions

Open: rear rims versus the figure. The drawing suggests about 5.9 mm semi-diameter for Elements III/IV and 6.5 mm for Element V, compared with the retained 8 mm. Front rims are also larger than drawn, but the real f/5.6 axial beam needs about 8.95 mm at R1. The cause of the printed back-focus discrepancy, the conflicting rear-gap prose, historical glass identities, and any production use remain unresolved.
