# KODAK 100mm f/1.5 (US 2,259,004 Design) — Audit

## Source

US 2,259,004 A, _Photographic Objective_, names Willy Schade as inventor and
Eastman Kodak Company as assignee. It was filed 8 June 1939 and published
14 October 1941. The Figure 2 table accompanies the Figure 1 section; Figure 3
is another seven-element prescription and Figure 5 an eight-element example.
The 9 December 1941 Certificate of Correction changes claim 5 wording only.

## Transcription

The eleven signed radii, ten thicknesses and air separations, seven sodium-D
indices and seven Abbe numbers agree with the page images. The inferred stop
split 12.50 + 2.11 mm preserves the printed 14.61 mm air space.

An early assignment of the elements to components was rejected: it gave a focal
length of 38.398804 mm and back focus of −13.269023 mm. The patent's description
and section instead establish I | II+III | IV | V+VI+VII, with cemented
interfaces R4/R9/R10. The accepted assignment follows that source evidence.

## First-order

The printed focal length is 100 mm at f/1.5. The transcribed prescription gives
EFL 100.025778598 mm and paraxial BFD 46.997125373 mm, with the image plane at
that computed back focus. The optical track is 90.44 mm and the total distance
to the image is 137.437125373 mm. No back focus is printed.

The nominal aperture is f/1.500 with stop radius 19.517957701 mm. Earlier
real-ray calculations gave entrance-pupil-equivalent f/1.549087980 and
image-space working f/1.551479003. The separate 20.198021 mm stop-radius
estimate corresponds to admitting the exact nominal axial marginal ray; that
larger opening was not substituted for the model's derived iris.

Earlier geometry checks found a minimum glass-edge thickness of 0.558786357 mm,
a minimum air clearance of 0.778193341 mm, a maximum spherical rim angle of
61.088904309°, and maximum shared-radius air-gap sag fraction of 0.811027731,
below the 0.9 limit. The inferred blanks had positive separations without
clearance exceptions. The surface-7 rim is around z = 52.0 mm, the stop at
55.37 mm, and the surface-8 rim around 60.2 mm; neither rim crosses the stop.

Earlier calculations within ±10° found every chief ray clear, with off-axis
loss at the front rim. Small changes in the inferred stop station and opening
changed that loss. Geometric sodium-D MTF at the paraxial image plane was
numerically stable within that field, with a largest successive refinement
change of 0.0004769153. This is not measured MTF or evidence of broadband
performance, and it does not establish performance at ±20°.

## Not printed

The exact stop station and bore, clear semi-diameters, image-plane gap, trade
glass identities, close-focus movement, film format and historical f-stop range
are not given. The stop station and rims are inferred, the image plane and iris
radius are calculated, and the glasses remain unmatched. The f-stop sequence
through f/22 is chosen for display. No finite-focus motion is modeled.

## Product

No product is documented for this patent. The later Schade US 2,397,565 A,
associated on the site with the Kodak Ektar 52mm f/1.5, has the same 7/4
arrangement but different radii and glass coordinates, including element II
1.617 instead of 1.568; the two prescriptions are distinct. The Ektra 50 mm
f/1.9 Ektar has a different Biotar-type arrangement.

## Corrections

The earlier Figure 1 example label identified the section rather than the
numerical example; it is now Figure 2. The earlier claim that the field was not
numerically stated is closed: the specification explicitly gives covering
power greater than 20° from the axis. The model now uses a 20° half-field.

## Remaining questions

Open: Figure 1 shows flat edges around 2–3 mm on III and VII, whereas the model
has near-knife edges. A later semi-diameter comparison should examine these
edges and the differences around IV without reducing admitted light. No rim
change was made here. Exact historical glass identities and a product
attribution remain unresolved.
