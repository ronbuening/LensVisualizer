# KODAK 100mm f/1.5 (US 2,259,004 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,259,004 A, _Photographic Objective_](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2259004A/en), by Willy Schade, assigned to Eastman Kodak Company, was filed with priority on 8 June 1939 and published on 14 October 1941. The application serial number is 278,046. Figure 2 supplies this numerical example on PDF page 1, repeated on PDF page 4 (printed page 2, first column); Figure 1 supplies its section. Figure 3 gives another seven-element prescription with the same arrangement, while Figure 5 accompanies the eight-element section in Figure 4. The Certificate of Correction of 9 December 1941 amends claim 5 wording only, without changing the prescription.

Schade's later [US 2,397,565 A, _Large Aperture Objective_](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2397565A/en) provides design context. [Oldlens's Kodak Ektar 52 mm f/1.5 account](https://www.oldlens.com/ektar%2052mm.html) associates a specimen with that later patent, without identifying a product for US 2,259,004.

## Transcription Check

All eleven signed radii, seven glass thicknesses, three air spaces, seven sodium-D indices and seven Abbe numbers agree with both page-image tables. No transcription mismatch was found. Dimensions retain the printed 100 mm focal-length scale in millimetres. The inferred 12.50 + 2.11 mm stop split preserves the printed 14.61 mm air space.

The section and text establish four components in the order 1 | 2+3 | 4 | 5+6+7, with cemented surfaces R4, R9 and R10. The shapes agree with every printed radius sign. All eleven refracting surfaces are spherical; there are no variable spacings. A different assignment of elements to components gives EFL 38.40 mm and BFD −13.27 mm, inconsistent with the printed focal length and section.

## First-Order Check

The printed focal length and aperture are 100 mm and f/1.5. The calculated paraxial EFL is 100.026 mm, 0.026 mm above the printed value, and the nominal aperture is f/1.50 with a stop radius of 19.518 mm. The paraxial BFD is 46.997 mm; no back focus is printed. The image plane is at this back focus behind R11, 137.437 mm from R1. The first-to-last-surface distance is 90.44 mm, with negligible paraxial defocus at the stored image plane.

The real-ray entrance-pupil equivalent and image-space working aperture are both about f/1.55. A stop radius of 20.198 mm would admit the exact axial marginal ray for an entrance radius of 33.342 mm; this is an alternative aperture calculation, not a printed bore or the retained stop setting.

The surfaces stay clear of each other. Minimum glass-edge thickness is about 0.559 mm and minimum air clearance about 0.778 mm. The maximum spherical rim angle is 61.1°. The R7 rim is near z = 52.0 mm, the stop at 55.37 mm and the R8 rim near 60.2 mm; neither rim crosses the stop plane. The iris sets the full aperture without axial rim clipping.

The 20.0° chief ray reaches an image height of 36.073 mm without rim blockage, covering the stated field. The rear rims vignette part of the off-axis bundle; complete marginal-bundle transmission at 20.0° is not established. Within 10.0°, chief rays are clear but the front rim causes marginal-ray loss, whose extent depends on the inferred stop station and opening. Image quality at 20.0° is not established.

## Product Identification

No production product is documented for this exact prescription. The later US 2,397,565 design has the same seven-element/four-component arrangement but different radii and glass coordinates, including an element-2 index of 1.617 instead of 1.568. Its association with the Kodak Ektar 52 mm f/1.5 does not identify this earlier lens. The Ektra 50 mm f/1.9 Ektar has a different, Biotar-type arrangement.

No marketed focal length, marketed f-number, marketed minimum focus distance, mount, production image format or diaphragm blade information is assigned. The title retains the patent's 100 mm design scale.
