# KODAK 100mm f/1.5 (US 2,259,004 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,259,004 A, _Photographic Objective_

**Inventors:** Willy Schade

**Assignee:** Eastman Kodak Company

**Filed:** 8 June 1939

**Published:** 14 October 1941

**Example details:** Figure 2 numerical table; optical section in Figure 1.

The patent gives three numerical examples: Figures 2 and 3 use the seven-element
section in Figure 1, while Figure 5 accompanies the eight-element section in
Figure 4. This model follows Figure 2, whose table appears on PDF page 1 and is
repeated in the specification on PDF page 4. It states a focal length of 100 mm
and an aperture of f/1.5. All radii, thicknesses, air spaces, refractive indices and
Abbe numbers follow that table. The Certificate of Correction dated 9 December
1941 changes claim 5 wording only; it does not amend the optical prescription.

No product is documented for this patent. It shares the seven-element/four-group
layout of Schade's later [US 2,397,565 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2397565A/en),
already shown as the [Kodak Ektar 52mm f/1.5](/lens/kodak-ektar-52f15), but uses a
different prescription.

## Optical Architecture

The seven elements form four air-spaced components: a positive front singlet,
a cemented negative–positive doublet, an air-spaced negative singlet, and a
cemented positive–negative–positive rear triplet. Their order is
1 | 2+3 | 4 | 5+6+7. The shared surfaces are R4 in the doublet and R9/R10
in the triplet. All eleven refracting surfaces are spherical.

The front singlet collects light into the following components. The second
component is net positive, with a computed focal length of +124.50 mm; its
negative meniscus works with the stronger positive meniscus behind it. The
biconcave fourth element supplies negative power immediately before the
diaphragm. The rear triplet combines positive outer elements with a negative
middle element and is net positive, with a focal length of +64.64 mm. These
component powers differ from the individual element powers in air.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are calculated for each thick element in air. The
patent's indices refer to the sodium D line, approximately 589.3 nm, rather than
the helium d line. No trade-glass identity is established.

### Element 1 — Positive Meniscus

nD = 1.618, ν = 53.8. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.618 / ν 53.8). f = +141.32 mm.

The front collector has radii +73.3 and +425.8 mm and a center thickness of
12.77 mm. Its front face carries most of its positive power. It forms the first
air-spaced component by itself.

### Element 2 — Negative Meniscus

nD = 1.568, ν = 42.7. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.568 / ν 42.7). f = −151.26 mm.

The second element has radii +58.8 and +33.7 mm and is 5.62 mm thick. Its stronger
rear curvature makes it negative in air. It is the front member of the cemented
doublet and shares R4 with element 3.

### Element 3 — Positive Meniscus

nD = 1.637, ν = 55.7. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.637 / ν 55.7). f = +64.77 mm.

The rear member of the doublet has radii +33.7 and +151.3 mm and is 15.38 mm
thick. Its higher index and stronger positive power make the cemented component
net positive. The inferred rims leave a thin edge that needs further comparison
with Figure 1.

### Element 4 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.672, ν = 32.2. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.672 / ν 32.2). f = −43.88 mm.

The air-spaced negative element has radii −303.7 and +32.8 mm and is 3.37 mm
thick. It has the lowest Abbe number in the prescription and sits immediately
before the diaphragm gap. Its negative power balances the positive components
on either side.

### Element 5 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.637, ν = 55.7. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.637 / ν 55.7). f = +40.07 mm.

The front member of the rear triplet has radii +118.0 and −30.6 mm and is
18.36 mm thick. It uses the same printed glass coordinates as element 3 and
supplies strong positive power behind the diaphragm. Its rear face is cemented
to element 6 at R9.

### Element 6 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.522, ν = 50.7. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.522 / ν 50.7). f = −39.85 mm.

The middle member of the rear triplet has radii −30.6 and +67.4 mm and is
3.37 mm thick. It supplies negative power between the two positive members and
has a lower index than either neighbor. It is cemented at both R9 and R10.

### Element 7 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.671, ν = 47.0. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.671 / ν 47). f = +66.99 mm.

The final element has radii +67.4 and −125.9 mm and is 11.23 mm thick. It forms
the positive rear member of the triplet. Like element 3, its inferred rims leave
a thin edge; the patent drawing shows a more substantial flat edge.

## Glass Identification and Selection

All seven sodium-D indices and Abbe numbers are printed. None of the available
catalog rows matches both coordinates at the printed precision within the
matching limits of 0.0001 in index and 0.15 in Abbe number. The glass labels
therefore retain the source coordinates as unmatched, including the repeated
1.637 / 55.7 pair in elements 3 and 5.

The patent provides no additional spectral indices or partial-dispersion data.
Dispersion estimated from an Abbe number does not establish the historical melt
or measured broadband performance. The model's strongest spectral basis is the
printed sodium-D prescription; apochromatic correction is not established.

## Focus Mechanism and Aperture

The patent supplies one fixed prescription for this example and no close-focus
spacing or movement law. The model is therefore infinity-only.

The computed paraxial focal length is 100.025779 mm, 0.0258% above the stated
100 mm. The catalog design value is rounded to 100.03 mm. The patent prints no
back focus; the image plane is the computed paraxial BFD of 46.997125 mm. The
first-to-last-surface distance is 90.44 mm and the first-surface-to-image distance
is 137.437125 mm.

Figure 1 places the diaphragm between elements 4 and 5 in the printed
14.61 mm air space. Its precise station and bore are not dimensioned. The model
splits the space into 12.50 mm before the stop and 2.11 mm after it, putting the
stop 55.37 mm behind the first surface. The derived stop radius is 19.517958 mm
for nominal paraxial f/1.5. The real-ray entrance-pupil equivalent is about
f/1.5491 and the image-space working aperture about f/1.5515; these use different
definitions from the nominal paraxial aperture.

A larger inferred radius of 20.198021 mm would admit the exact axial marginal
ray for an entrance radius of 33.341926 mm. That is a separate aperture
calculation, not a printed diaphragm dimension or the model's stop setting.
The f-stop sequence through f/22 is a display choice rather than a documented
historical iris range.

## Field and Model Limitations

The specification on PDF page 3 states covering power greater than 20° from
the optical axis. The model therefore uses a 20° half-field, or 40° full field.
At the paraxial image plane, the real chief ray at 20° reaches a height of
36.072592 mm, giving the modeled image-circle diameter of 72.1452 mm. This
number is calculated from the stated field, not a printed film format. The
chief ray clears every current rim; this does not imply uniform illumination
or a measured image-quality limit.

All clear semi-diameters are inferred. Figure 1 broadly supports the overall
outline, but elements 3 and 7 have near-knife edges in the model where the
drawing shows roughly 2–3 mm flat edges. Those differences remain unresolved.
The current surfaces have positive glass and air clearances, and no rim cuts
through the stop plane. The smallest glass-edge thickness
is about 0.559 mm and the smallest air clearance about 0.778 mm.

The rear rims also vignette part of the 20° bundle.
