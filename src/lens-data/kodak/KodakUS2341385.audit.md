# KODAK 100mm f/3.5 (US 2,341,385 Design) — Audit

## Source

US 2,341,385 A, Rudolf Kingslake and Paul W. Stevens, assigned to Eastman Kodak Company; filed November 6, 1941 and published February 8, 1944. The only numerical example appears twice, on PDF pp. 1 and 3. The figure is on PDF p. 1. The supplied source readings and a further inspection of the page images agree on the printed prescription.

## Transcription

Both independent readings checked all nine signed radii, five glass thicknesses, three airspaces, five sodium D-line indices and five Abbe values. Both copies of the table agree. The figure and claims support five elements in four groups, with IV and V cemented at R8 and a stop between II and III. No numerical transcription error was found. The model divides the printed 14.3 mm airspace around the stop; its sum remains unchanged.

## First-order Results

The complete objective computes to EFL 100.077588422290 mm and paraxial back focus 101.527022794263 mm, against the printed 100 mm and 101.7 mm. The 0.172977 mm back-focus difference is consistent with the precision of the table. The retained image plane is the calculated paraxial one. Lens track is 82 mm; first vertex to image plane is 183.527023 mm.

The rear member means elements II–V, not just the cemented pair. Its calculated focal length is 67.948442 mm against the text's 67.6 mm; this discrepancy is unresolved. The cemented IV–V pair alone computes to 68.61 mm.

Nominal paraxial aperture is f/3.5 with stop radius 13.695005 mm. Prior independent exact tracing found entrance-equivalent f/3.586752 and image-space sine f/3.578376. A collimated ray at the nominal paraxial entrance height would require a 14.056933 mm stop radius; the source does not dimension the iris. The accepted axial beam clears the glass rims. Earlier geometry checks found positive glass and air clearances, with minimum glass thickness 3.323258 mm and minimum air clearance 4.780422 mm; no trimming or extra baffles were applied.

## Values Not Printed

The exact stop split of 11.7 / 2.6 mm, iris diameter, and all semi-diameters are inferred. The drawing supports the stop near R5, 40.8 mm behind the first vertex. The image plane is calculated from the table instead of using its rounded back-focus label. The patent gives no close-focus data, named glasses, additional spectral indices, film format, or production mount.

The earlier 25° half-field and 92.670134 mm image circle were modeling choices. The patent describes more than 30° from the axis, so the revised field is 30° with an image circle derived from the real chief ray. Clear chief-ray coverage does not imply full marginal-ray transmission at the field edge.

## Product Identification

No production product is documented. The “WA Ektanar” caption on the modern Commons drawing comes from a user's redraw of the patent. Kodak's April 1958 Signet 30 manual documents the Ektanar name on a later product, but supplies no connection to this prescription. The analysis cites both sources and retains the patent-design name. Earlier comparisons found no duplicate prescription in the collections examined; that does not establish complete coverage of every historical or inaccessible source.

## Corrections

The original optical table, inferred stop position, clear radii, and calculated image-plane spacing are retained. Bibliographic metadata, field coverage, element descriptions, glass wording, and public prose are corrected below.

## Remaining Questions

The text's rear-member focal length and the historical glass identities remain unresolved. R2 and R6 differ from figure readings. No commercial attribution, mount, format, or finite-focus behavior has been established.
