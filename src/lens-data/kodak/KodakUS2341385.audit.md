# KODAK 100mm f/3.5 (US 2,341,385 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,341,385 A, _Wide-angle lens_](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2341385A/en), by Rudolf Kingslake and Paul W. Stevens, assigned to Eastman Kodak Company. Filing and priority date: November 6, 1941; publication: February 8, 1944. The sole numerical example appears on the drawing sheet and printed specification page 2 (PDF pp. 1 and 3). The figure is on PDF p. 1.

The [modern Wikimedia Commons drawing](<https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kingslake_%26_Stevens_US2341385A_(WA_Ektanar,_1941).svg>) supplies the “WA Ektanar” caption. Kodak's [_Signet 30 Camera_ manual](https://www.cameramanuals.org/kodak_pdf/kodak_signet_30.pdf), April 1958 printing, final specifications page, documents a later 44 mm f/2.8 Ektanar. Neither establishes a production connection to this prescription.

The [CDGM H-LaK12 data sheet](https://www.cdgmgd.com/webapp/pdf/H-LaK12.pdf) supplies the modern comparison glass's nd 1.69680, νd 56.18 and dispersion constants.

## Transcription Check

All nine signed radii, five glass thicknesses, three airspaces, five sodium D-line indices and five Abbe values were re-read against both printed tables in the page images. The copies agree; no numerical transcription mismatch was found. Dimensions retain the printed millimetre units and 100 mm scale. Both tables head the indices with capital D and dispersion with V; no spectral-line formula for V is given.

The drawing and claims agree on five elements in four airspaced components, with IV and V cemented at R8. The stop lies between II and III, nearer III. Its 11.7 / 2.6 mm split sums to the printed 14.3 mm airspace. All surfaces are spherical and all inter-element spacings are fixed.

## First-Order Check

Calculated EFL is 100.08 mm, against the printed 100 mm. Paraxial back focus is 101.53 mm, against 101.7 mm. The 0.17 mm difference is consistent with table rounding. The image plane remains at calculated paraxial focus, 183.53 mm behind the first vertex, after an 82 mm lens track; it has not been moved to minimize blur.

The positive rear member comprises elements II–V. Its calculated focal length is 67.95 mm, against the text's 67.6 mm. This discrepancy remains unresolved. The cemented IV–V pair alone computes to +68.61 mm. The third component's curvature magnitudes reproduce the printed 0.0024 and 0.0217 mm⁻¹ and the printed 41.5 mm reciprocal of their sum.

The nominal paraxial aperture is f/3.50 with stop radius 13.695 mm. Exact axial tracing gives entrance-equivalent f/3.59 and image-space sine f/3.58. A collimated ray at the nominal paraxial entrance height would require stop radius 14.057 mm; the patent does not dimension the iris. The admitted axial beam clears the glass rims. Minimum glass thickness is about 3.32 mm and minimum air clearance about 5.81 mm; the surfaces stay clear of each other, with no extra baffles or rim cuts.

At 30.0°, the chief ray reaches image height 57.444 mm and clears every rim. This supports the retained field-derived image circle without establishing full marginal-ray transmission.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The exact stop split, iris diameter and every semi-diameter are inferred. The drawing supports the stop near R5, 40.8 mm behind the first vertex. R1–R9 have clear radii of 29, 23.6, 24, 24, 22, 19.7, 25, 25 and 25 mm. Drawing readings are approximately R1 29.1, R2 23.6, R3/R4 23.8, R5 21.5, R6 19.7 and R7–R9 25.6 mm. The curved faces end in bevels at R2 and R6; no flat lands are inferred.

The patent describes more than 30° from the axis generally, without a separate numerical field for this example. The selected 30.0° half-field follows that description. The image-circle diameter is twice the real chief-ray height, rounded upward to 114.89 mm; it is not a documented film format.

`focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. No close-focus motion or finite-conjugate spacing is given. The selectable stops from f/3.5 to f/22 are retained; the source gives only the full aperture, not the minimum iris opening. No blade count or blade shape is printed.

The glass names, suppliers, additional spectral indices and partial dispersions are absent. H-LaK12 supplies a modern dispersion comparison for element 2; BACD16 and F4 supply spectral proxies for elements 1 and 3–4, respectively. These curves do not identify the historical materials. Element 5 remains unmatched because no catalog row lies within index 0.002 and Abbe number 0.25 at sodium D. No anomalous-dispersion designation is inferred, and the proxies and element 5's Abbe-number estimate do not establish secondary-spectrum correction or measured broadband performance.

## Product Identification

No production lens is documented as using this prescription. The Commons caption is a modern patent redraw; Kodak's later Signet manual supplies no link to this 1941 design. The name therefore identifies the maker and patent design. No other published copy of the prescription is known, though the historical record is incomplete.
