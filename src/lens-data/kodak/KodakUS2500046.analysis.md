# KODAK 100mm f/2 (US 2,500,046 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [US 2,500,046 A, Petzval-type photographic objective](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2500046A/en)\
**Inventors:** Willy Schade\
**Assignee:** Eastman Kodak Company\
**Filed:** January 3, 1948\
**Published:** March 7, 1950\
**Example details:** Sole numerical example, Figure 2; section in Figure 1 and aberration curves in Figures 3–4.

The patent describes a four-element Petzval objective at a nominal focal length of 100 mm and f/2. Claim 3 gives normalized prescription ranges rather than another numerical example. No production lens is documented for this prescription.

The printed prescription computes to EFL 99.98 mm and paraxial back focus 56.45 mm, compared with the stated 100 mm and 56.5 mm. The model retains the computed paraxial image plane. The first-to-last glass vertex distance is 81 mm, placing that plane 137.45 mm behind the first vertex.

## Optical Architecture

The four elements form three groups: a cemented positive front doublet followed by an air-spaced negative–positive rear pair. R2 is the sole cemented interface, and R3 is the planar rear face of the front doublet. The front doublet and rear pair have focal lengths of approximately 171.4 mm and 143.5 mm respectively.

The central air space is 49.3 mm and the rear pair is separated by 0.9 mm. The patent seeks flatter field curvature without the additional negative element near the focal plane used in some earlier Petzval designs. All curved surfaces are spherical. The diagram uses the patent’s Roman numerals I–IV for the four elements.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### Element 1 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.523, νD = 58.6. Glass: C12 (Hoya), a catalog match in index and dispersion; historical supplier unknown. Standalone focal length in air: +62.85 mm.

The front positive element collects the incident beam. Its negative rear radius forms the cemented interface with Element 2.

### Element 2 — Plano-Concave Negative

nD = 1.617, νD = 38.5. Glass: unmatched. Standalone focal length in air: −92.04 mm.

The more dispersive negative member balances the positive power of Element 1. Its planar rear face ends the cemented front doublet.

### Element 3 — Negative Meniscus

nD = 1.649, νD = 33.8. Glass: PBM22 (Ohara), spectral proxy. Standalone focal length in air: −97.38 mm.

The negative meniscus begins the rear pair after the long central air space. Its stronger rear curvature supplies negative power ahead of the final positive element.

### Element 4 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.697, νD = 56.1. Glass: H-LaK12 (CDGM), spectral proxy. Standalone focal length in air: +58.21 mm.

The final positive element makes the rear pair convergent. Its index and dispersion are consistent with a lanthanum-type crown, although the patent identifies neither a composition nor a supplier.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent defines its indices at the D line, taken as sodium at 589.3 nm; the model uses the sodium-doublet center at approximately 589.294 nm. All four printed index and Abbe pairs are retained.

Hoya C12 matches Element 1 at sodium D, evaluating to nD 1.5230 and νD 58.63 (ΔnD −0.0000063, ΔνD +0.03). Element 3 uses Ohara PBM22 as a spectral proxy (ΔnD −0.00148, ΔνD −0.01). Element 4 uses CDGM H-LaK12 as a spectral proxy, at nD 1.69669 and νD 56.17 (ΔnD −0.00031, ΔνD +0.07). All differences are catalog minus printed at sodium D. The site’s spectral-proxy tolerance is 0.002 in index and 0.25 in Abbe number. The catalog curves supply the wavelength dependence, with the printed pairs retained. These row names do not identify Kodak's historical suppliers or melts.

Element 2 remains unmatched because no catalog row is within those tolerances at sodium D; its dispersion is estimated from the printed index and Abbe number. The patent supplies no additional spectral indices or anomalous-dispersion data, so broadband and secondary-spectrum predictions remain uncertain.

## Focus Mechanism and Stop

The model is focused at infinity; the patent gives no close-focus data or focusing movement law.

The stop follows the patent-stated principal-ray crossing 18.9 mm behind the front doublet, used for Figure 4's field-curvature calculations. R3 lies 19.5 mm behind the first vertex, so the stop is at 38.4 mm, dividing the 49.3 mm air space into 18.9 mm and 30.4 mm. No physical diaphragm is drawn, and its diameter is inferred.

The derived stop radius is 20.02 mm at paraxial f/2. Real marginal rays give entrance-aperture and image-sine equivalents both near f/2.06. A real f/2 marginal ray would require a radius near 20.70 mm. These aperture conventions explain the small difference from the nominal setting. Figure 3 labels f/2 and f/2.8; the model opens at the former.

## Field and Aberration Correction

Figure 4 extends to a 7.0° half-field, which sets the model's 14.0° full field. The real 7.0° chief ray reaches an image height of 12.28 mm at the retained image plane and clears all rims. Twice that height gives the catalog image circle of 24.56 mm. This is an optical field derived from the patent's plot, with no documented camera format or mount.

At 5.0°, 6.0° and 7.0°, calculated sagittal focus shifts of approximately −0.23, −0.33 and −0.44 mm and tangential shifts of −0.07, −0.08 and −0.08 mm reproduce Figure 4 at the stated stop location. Calculated spherical-aberration shifts at full, 0.85, 0.7 and 0.5 pupil are approximately +0.10, −0.15, −0.21 and −0.15 mm, consistent with Figure 3. The text's broader 8.0–10.0° design aims do not extend the modeled field beyond the plotted 7.0°.

The front-to-rear focal-length ratio is 1.19, consistent with the patent's rounded 1.2, and the curvature sum is approximately 1.19/F. R1/f1 is about 0.41 against the stated 0.4, and −R4/R7 is 0.43. The stated f1 = 1.8F and −R2 = 0.32f1 compare with approximately 1.71F and 0.33f1 from the table; these are rounded design descriptions, and the tabulated prescription is retained.

## Clear Apertures and Limits

The inferred semi-diameters at R1–R7 are 25.8, 25.8, 25.8, 18, 18, 18 and 18 mm. The front doublet has the square rim drawn in Figure 1. Its common radius includes clearance for the nominal f/2 axial beam, which reaches 25.0 mm at R1, and the off-axis bundles. The drawing gives about 24.1 mm at the front and 17.1 mm at the rear; the rear outer rims agree within 6%. The small bevel drawn at R5 is omitted because its shorter optical arc would remove substantial edge light. The axial beam and the 7.0° chief ray pass all surfaces. Wide-open geometric transmission is 100% on axis, 97.3% at 4.7° and 88.9% at 7.0°, with ordinary off-axis vignetting.

At the 18 mm rear rim, the 0.9 mm air space retains about 0.317 mm clearance. The smallest glass edge thickness is about 3.331 mm, and the largest surface-rim angle is about 27.0°. The clear apertures and stop diameter are estimates, so calculated vignetting and MTF are not measured production performance.
