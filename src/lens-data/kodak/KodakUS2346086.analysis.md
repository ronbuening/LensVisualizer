# KODAK 100mm f/4.5 (US 2,346,086 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [US 2,346,086 A, “Photographic Objective”](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2346086A/en)\
**Inventors:** Willy Schade and Karl Tolle\
**Assignee:** Eastman Kodak Company\
**Filed:** June 1, 1942\
**Published:** April 4, 1944\
**Example details:** Example 2, repeated on the drawing sheet and the first specification page; dimensions in millimetres.

The patent gives two numerical examples of a four-element, three-group
photographic objective at nominal f = 100 mm and f/4.5. Example 2 has a plane
rear surface on the front element and a high-index positive element at the
rear. Both copies of its table agree on every radius, thickness, air space,
glass coordinate and the printed back focus.

No product is documented as using this prescription. The Tessar-type layout
alone does not identify a particular Kodak Ektar.

## Optical Architecture

The three components are a positive front singlet, a negative middle singlet
and a positive rear doublet. The doublet combines a biconcave negative element
with a biconvex positive element. R6 is the sole cemented interface; R2 is a
refracting plane between glass and air. All powered surfaces are spherical.

The patent describes an improvement to the class represented by Rudolph's
US 721,240 and Schade and Frederick's US 2,158,178. It emphasizes a rear
positive element with index above 1.7 and a cemented radius greater in
magnitude than the last radius. Example 2 gives R6 = +48.3 mm and
R7 = −44.5 mm. The patent describes Example 2 as almost eliminating coma,
with slightly greater field curvature than Example 1 while retaining a very
flat field (specification p. 1).

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### Element 1 — Plano-Convex Positive

nD = 1.697, νD = 56.1. Glass: H-LaK12 (CDGM). Focal length in air: +37.88 mm.

The curved front face supplies positive power; the rear face is plane.
Its relatively high Abbe number complements the more dispersive negative
elements. The patent prefers a front index between 1.65 and 1.8 and an Abbe
number between 45 and 65, both satisfied here.

### Element 2 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.605, νD = 43.6. Glass: Unmatched. Focal length in air: −31.57 mm.

The middle element balances the two positive components. Its rear face,
R4 = +24.7 mm, is more strongly curved than its front face,
R3 = −85.6 mm, as the patent describes.

### Element 3 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.621, νD = 36.2. Glass: Unmatched. Focal length in air: −50.57 mm.

This is the negative front member of the rear doublet, C1. It has the lowest
Abbe number in the prescription and shares R6 directly with Element 4.
Its front radius, −90.5 mm, lies within the patent's preferred magnitude
of one half to one nominal focal length.

### Element 4 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.744, νD = 45.8. Glass: Unmatched. Focal length in air: +31.71 mm.

The high-index rear element supplies the doublet's converging power.
The cemented pair has a combined focal length of +79.05 mm; the separate
element focal lengths describe each member in air rather than its power
inside the assembly.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent explicitly defines its refractive indices at the sodium D line,
589.3 nm, and gives an Abbe number for each element (specification p. 2).
Source coordinates remain unchanged. Modern CDGM H-LaK12 approximates
Element 1's dispersion; its sodium-D index is 1.69669, against
the printed 1.697. Elements 2–4 remain Unmatched. The historical supplier
and melt remain unidentified. The patent prints no additional line
indices or partial dispersions, so measured broadband and anomalous-dispersion
performance remain unknown.

## Focus Mechanism

The model is focused at infinity. The patent supplies no close-focus
distances, focus states or movement law.

## Image Plane, Aperture and Field

The printed 89.7 mm back focus remains the image-plane distance from R7.
The computed EFL is 100.20 mm and the paraxial back focus is 89.99 mm,
placing paraxial focus 0.29 mm beyond the image plane. The
prescription is retained as printed; the reason for this small residual is
unconfirmed. Example 1's shared nominal 100 mm heading is looser still,
with its table computing to 92.07 mm.

The first-to-last surface length is the printed 19.5 mm, giving a total
distance to the stored image plane of 109.2 mm. The patent draws no stop.
The inferred stop lies 4.2 mm after R4 and 3.3 mm before R5, at
z = 11.2 mm from the first vertex. Its derived radius is 9.775 mm.
The nominal aperture is f/4.5; real-ray measures give an entrance-equivalent
f/4.56 and an image-space sine f/4.58. These distinctions depend on
the inferred diaphragm.

The patent states a flat field to 27° from the axis (specification p. 1).
The model therefore uses a 54° full field and a 27° maximum analysis
half-field. At the retained image plane, the real chief ray at 27° lands
50.74 mm from the axis. Twice that height, rounded upward, gives the
101.49 mm image circle; no film format is inferred from it. The chief ray
clears every current rim, while marginal rays show some mechanical vignetting.
Clearance alone does not establish the patent's claimed aberration
correction across this field.

The semi-diameters remain inferred: 13.3 mm for Element 1, 12.85 mm for
Element 2 and 13.15 mm for the rear doublet. They give thinner edges than
the drawing, approximately 0.31 mm on Element 1 and 0.19 mm on Element 4,
against about 1.3 and 1.6 mm drawn. The drawing therefore does not establish
the retained clear apertures.
