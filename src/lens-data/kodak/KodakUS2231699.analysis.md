# KODAK 100mm f/3.8 (US 2,231,699 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [US 2,231,699 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2231699A/en)  
**Filed:** October 28, 1939  
**Granted:** February 11, 1941  
**Inventor:** Harold F. Bennett  
**Assignee:** Eastman Kodak Company  
**Title:** Telephoto objective  
**Example details:** Figure 1, tabulated on drawing sheet 1 and in the specification; f = 100 mm, f/3.8.

The KODAK 100mm f/3.8 (US 2,231,699 Design) reproduces the first of the patent's two numerical examples (Figure 1). No Kodak product is documented as using this design, and no mount or film format is assigned. The design is of interest as an unusually simple four-element telephoto whose stated aims are very low distortion and a larger aperture than earlier lenses of its type, which the patent puts at about f/6 or f/7.

## Optical Architecture

Two cemented doublets are separated by a 39.5 mm air gap. The front component is positive (+72.16 mm); the rear is negative (−145.58 mm). The rear cemented interface is convex toward the front component and separates glasses with a large index difference. All six radii, four center thicknesses, the group separation and the nD/V values are copied unchanged from the patent. Figure 2 is a different example and supplies none of this model's values.

The patent does not show or locate an iris. The stop at the midpoint of the air gap is a modeling choice, not a recovered factory location. The clear semi-diameters, 13.5 mm on the front doublet and 10 mm on the rear, are estimates, not printed dimensions. The iris radius is set from the f/3.8 rating by the paraxial rule. No value was adjusted to improve performance.

The patent prints sodium-D indices and Abbe numbers only; it gives no C, F or g indices and names no glass. For L1 to L3 a modern catalog glass reproduces the printed nD and V at the printed precision (within 0.05 in V), and its catalog dispersion curve stands in for the unrecorded 1939 melt in the colour and MTF analyses. No catalog glass matches L4 at the D line; its dispersion is estimated from nD and V on the normal partial-dispersion line. Secondary colour is therefore modeled, not taken from the production glasses. The index rises by 0.166 across the front cemented face; the patent asks for a "high break" in index there as well as at the rear cemented face.

## Focus and Aperture

Infinity focus only; the patent gives no close-focus data. The image plane is the computed paraxial focus at the sodium D line, 33.45 mm behind the last vertex, not a best-focus plane chosen for the open aperture.

The midpoint iris has a radius of 8.24 mm from the f/3.8 rating, giving an entrance pupil radius of 13.17 mm. The front doublet's spherical aberration carries the real marginal ray slightly higher at the stop (8.44 mm) than the paraxial ray, so the outermost rays of the full f/3.8 axial beam are clipped at the iris.

## Aspherical Surfaces

None. All refracting surfaces are spherical or flat.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The two printed tables, on drawing sheet 1 and in the specification, agree with each other and with this model in every radius, thickness, spacing, index and Abbe number. The computed focal length is 100.06 mm against the printed 100 mm (+0.06 %), a difference kept rather than rescaled away. The length from the front vertex to the image is 90.75 mm, a telephoto ratio of about 0.91.

The patent's stated proportions hold for this example. The rear component's outer radii are 0.16 F and 0.34 F (close to the "about 1/6 F" and "about 1/3 F" of the text), its cemented face is 0.525 F, and the components are 0.395 F apart. Across that cemented face the index rises by 0.123 and the Abbe number falls by 8.6, inside the claimed limits of at least 0.07 and at most 15.

Some limits come from the model's estimates, not from the patent. The 10 mm rear clear radius is smaller than the rear doublet drawn in Figure 1, so toward the edge of the field the rear doublet cuts away much of the oblique beam. The patent states no field angle; the default half-field of about 12.6° is the viewer's own setting. The colour behaviour rests on the glass substitutes described above. No claim is made about how well the model reproduces the distortion correction the patent states.
