# KODAK 100mm f/1.9 (US 2,252,681 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [US 2,252,681 A, High Aperture Objective](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2252681A/en)

**Inventors:** George H. Aklin

**Assignee:** Eastman Kodak Company

**Filed:** 17 May 1940

**Published:** 19 August 1941

**Example details:** Sole numerical example; matching tables on the drawing sheet and specification page 2 (PDF pages 1 and 3).

The patent prints F = 100 mm and f/1.9 for a seven-element objective in four air-separated groups. Its signed radii, thicknesses, air spaces and seven D-line index/Abbe pairs are retained. The prescription gives EFL 100.05 mm, about 0.05 mm above the stated focal length. The paraxial back focus is 63.27 mm; the patent does not print a back focus.

The design is possibly related to the Kodak Ektra's 50 mm f/1.9 Ektar, introduced with the camera in 1941. Kodak's contemporary catalogue identifies the f/1.9 Ektar as a seven-element lens, while later accounts report four groups. The aperture, layout and date make a connection plausible, but no cited source identifies this patent as the production prescription. Aklin also refers to several applications filed concurrently, so timing alone cannot distinguish them. The model retains the patent-design name and 100 mm dimensions; the Ektra lens's mount, format and focusing distances are not assigned to it.

## Optical Architecture

The objective follows a modified double-Gauss arrangement with a positive front singlet, two inner meniscus doublets concave toward the diaphragm, and a positive rear doublet. The diagram labels the elements I–VII as in the patent. The four components are I, II–III, IV–V and VI–VII. The three cemented interfaces are R4, R7 and R10, identified as C1, C2 and C3.

The two inner components have negative net power; their standalone focal lengths are about −169.18 and −349.61 mm. The rear doublet has positive net power, about +85.28 mm. These group powers differ from the powers of their individual members in air.

The patent emphasizes the thin, high-index third component and its negatively curved cemented interface. Its thickness is 3.7 + 7.9 = 11.6 mm, or 0.116F. Both rear doublets have an index increase of 0.055 from their negative to positive members. Their cemented radii are −73.1 and +60.8 mm, agreeing with the text's approximate magnitudes of 0.73F and 0.61F.

R9 is a planar refracting entrance face of element 6. The table clearly prints R10 = +60.8 mm on both pages; the searchable text's reading of 80.8 is incorrect.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### Element 1 — Positive Meniscus

nD = 1.573, ν = 57.4. Glass: BAK1 (Sumita), spectral proxy. Standalone focal length in air: +123.30 mm.

The front singlet supplies positive power before the inner components. Its index lies within the patent's preferred 1.55–1.59 range. The patent associates this lower-index front member, combined with the rear-component construction, with flatter field correction without excessive spherical aberration.

### C1 — Front Inner Meniscus Doublet

Element 2: nD = 1.611, ν = 57.2. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.611 / ν 57.2). Positive meniscus; standalone focal length +82.98 mm.

Element 3: nD = 1.605, ν = 38.2. Glass: F5 (Schott), spectral proxy. Negative meniscus; standalone focal length −46.92 mm.

The pair is cemented at R4 = +214 mm and presents its strongly concave rear face toward the diaphragm. The more dispersive negative member balances the positive front member while the assembly has weak negative power.

### C2 — Rear Inner Meniscus Doublet

Element 4: nD = 1.689, ν = 30.9. Glass: L-TIM28 (Ohara), spectral proxy. Negative meniscus; standalone focal length −65.78 mm.

Element 5: nD = 1.744, ν = 45.8. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.744 / ν 45.8). Positive meniscus; standalone focal length +96.04 mm.

The pair is cemented at R7 = −73.1 mm. Its negative front member has an index above 1.65, and the positive rear member is higher by 0.055. The patent identifies this combination of high indices, thin construction and cemented curvature as improving spherical aberration and marginal-ray correction.

### C3 — Positive Rear Doublet

Element 6: nD = 1.689, ν = 30.9. Glass: L-TIM28 (Ohara), spectral proxy. Plano-concave negative; standalone focal length −88.24 mm.

Element 7: nD = 1.744, ν = 45.8. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.744 / ν 45.8). Biconvex positive; standalone focal length +45.14 mm.

The pair is cemented at R10 = +60.8 mm and restores positive power after the inner menisci. Its index increase repeats that of C2, with the cemented curvature facing the opposite way. The patent associates the rear doublet with improved coma and upper marginal-ray correction. The planar R9 and rear radius −68.3 mm also satisfy the stated condition 1/R9 − 1/R11 > 1/F.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The tables print capital N_D and ν. The model retains the D line, taken as sodium at 589.3 nm. The patent gives no supplier, melt identity, partial dispersion or additional spectral indices.

Four elements use catalog curves as spectral proxies. Differences are catalog minus printed at sodium D:

- Element 1: BAK1 (Sumita), ΔnD = −0.00059 and Δν = +0.08.
- Element 3: F5 (Schott), ΔnD = −0.00172 and Δν = −0.18.
- Elements 4 and 6: L-TIM28 (Ohara), ΔnD = +0.00029 and Δν = +0.11.

These rows meet the site's spectral-proxy tolerance of 0.002 in index and 0.25 in Abbe number at sodium D. The model keeps the printed index and Abbe number and borrows the catalog curve's wavelength dependence. Kodak's historical supplier and melt remain unknown.

Elements 2, 5 and 7 remain Unmatched because no catalog row lies within those tolerances. Their dispersion is estimated from the printed Abbe numbers. Neither the proxies nor these estimates establish the historical glasses' full spectral behavior; no anomalous-dispersion designation or measured broadband performance is claimed.

## Focus Mechanism

The model is focused at infinity; the patent gives no close-focus data or focusing movement law. The prescription remains fixed, with its image plane at the computed paraxial back focus, 63.27 mm after R11. The first-to-last vertex distance is 78.7 mm and the first-vertex-to-image distance is 141.97 mm.

## Aperture, Field and Model Limits

The text places the diaphragm between the two inner menisci. The model splits the printed 17.8 mm R5–R6 gap into 9.0 mm before the stop and 8.8 mm after it. The stop is 39.8 mm behind the first vertex and clears the neighboring glass rims by about 1.30 and 1.14 mm.

The paraxial f/1.9 aperture gives a stop radius of 16.80 mm. A real axial ray entering at the nominal entrance-pupil radius instead reaches the stop at about 16.44 mm. At the larger paraxial iris, the real-ray entrance and image-sine aperture equivalents are both about f/1.86. These describe different aperture conventions; the patent does not supply an iris measurement to choose between them.

All clear semi-diameters are estimated from the drawing on PDF page 1 and the ray clearances. Both faces of the front singlet end at 30.08 mm to give the square edge shown in the drawing. R1 lies just above the 30.079 mm height required by the 5.0° marginal bundle at the modeled iris. The front inner doublet has a square 26 mm outer rim at R3/R4, the rear inner doublet a square 23 mm rim at R7/R8, and the rear doublet a square 25 mm rim at R9–R11. The diaphragm-facing curves end at 18 mm at R5 and 19 mm at R6, matching the higher bevel transition on the image side of the diaphragm in the drawing. Straight sloping edges join these curves to the taller outer rims. The axial beam, 5.0° chief ray and full meridional edge bundle clear every rim. Calculated geometric transmission is 100% at each evaluated field angle through 5.0°. The exact manufactured apertures remain undocumented.

The ±5.0° display field is illustrative. The patent claims high covering power without giving a numerical field, image format or image circle. No image-circle diameter is assigned. The computed longitudinal spherical aberration is about 0.29 mm at the full pupil; the patent supplies no numerical aberration curve for direct comparison. Image-quality calculations depend on the inferred diaphragm, rims, approximate dispersion and paraxial image plane and are not measurements of an Ektra production lens.

Every curved surface is spherical; R9 is planar. The f-stop sequence through f/22 is a display choice rather than a published iris scale.
