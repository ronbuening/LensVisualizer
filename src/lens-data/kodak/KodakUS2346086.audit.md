# KODAK 100mm f/4.5 (US 2,346,086 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,346,086 A, “Photographic Objective”](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2346086A/en), by Willy Schade and Karl Tolle, assigned to Eastman Kodak Company; filed June 1, 1942 and published April 4, 1944. No earlier priority date is printed. Example 2 appears on the drawing sheet (PDF p. 1) and the first specification page (PDF p. 2). PDF p. 3 defines the sodium-D indices and identifies R6 as the only cemented surface. Dimensions are in millimetres.

The modern [CDGM H-LaK12 data sheet](https://www.cdgmgd.com/webapp/pdf/H-LaK12.pdf), glass code 697562, supplies optical properties for a dispersion approximation to Element 1. It does not identify the historical glass or a production lens.

## Transcription Check

Both copies of Example 2 were re-read against the page images. All seven radii and signs, four glass thicknesses, two air spaces, four nD/ν pairs, the 89.7 mm back focus and T = 19.5 mm agree. No transcription discrepancy was found. R2 is plane; R6 is the sole cemented interface between Elements 3 and 4. The 7.5 mm rear air space is divided into 4.2 + 3.3 mm around the inferred stop without changing the total separation.

The surfaces stay clear of each other. At the retained rims, minimum glass edge thickness is 0.188 mm and minimum air clearance is 1.03 mm. No rim reduction is needed for surface clearance, and the iris sets the full axial aperture.

## First-Order Check

The printed nominal focal length is 100 mm; calculated EFL is 100.20 mm, 0.2% longer. The nominal f-number is 4.50 and the derived stop radius is 9.775 mm. Real-ray measures give an entrance-equivalent f/4.56 and an image-space sine f/4.58; these depend on the inferred diaphragm. A paraxial aperture calculation gives a stop radius of about 9.915 mm.

The printed 89.7 mm back focus is retained as the distance from R7 to the image plane. Paraxial back focus is 89.99 mm, placing paraxial focus 0.29 mm beyond that plane. No single-digit, sign or transposition change is better supported than the printed table. The cause of the difference remains unknown. Example 1 shares the 100 mm and 89.7 mm headings but calculates to EFL 92.07 mm and back focus 82.39 mm.

The first-to-last surface distance is 19.5 mm; the first-vertex-to-image distance is 109.2 mm. At 27.0°, the real chief ray reaches an image height of 50.74 mm and clears every retained rim. The corresponding image-circle diameter is 101.49 mm after rounding upward. Off-axis marginal rays remain vignetted: the front surfaces clip about 12.0% of one side of the edge bundle by width. This is not a measurement of total transmission or proof of the patent's claimed field flatness and coma correction.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The patent draws no stop and gives no iris diameter. The stop is inferred at z = 11.2 mm from the first vertex, 4.2 mm after R4 and 3.3 mm before R5; its radius follows the nominal f/4.5 aperture. The selectable range extends from f/4.5 to f/22, but no source specifies the minimum iris opening, blade count or blade shape.

Every clear semi-diameter is inferred: 13.3 mm for Element 1, 12.85 mm for Element 2 and 13.15 mm for the rear doublet. The drawing suggests about 11.3, 10.5 and 10.45–10.6 mm respectively, making the retained radii about 18–25% larger. The drawing scale is about 33.4 pixels/mm at 600 dpi. Its doublet edge thickness is about 4.40 mm, against 4.36 mm calculated at the drawn rim. At the retained rims, Elements 1 and 4 have edges about 0.31 and 0.19 mm thick, against about 1.3 and 1.6 mm drawn. Smaller drawing-based rims admit the axial beam and chief ray but increase off-axis vignetting, with one-sided clipping reaching about 35.0% at some surfaces. The middle front surface retains about 0.08 mm of axial-ray clearance at a 10.5 mm radius.

The patent states a flat field to 27.0° from the axis. The 54.0° full field follows that statement; the calculated image-circle diameter is not a printed format specification. No film format is inferred from it.

`focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. No close-focus distance, movement law or additional focus state is published.

The four source index/Abbe pairs retain their sodium-D reference. H-LaK12 approximates Element 1's dispersion; Elements 2–4 use index-and-Abbe estimates. No named historical melts, additional line indices or measured partial dispersions are supplied. At sodium D, nearby catalog curves differ from Element 2 by about +0.000498 / +0.098 (Ohara S-BAM4), from Element 3 by −0.000196 / −0.328 (Ohara TIM11), and from Element 4 by −0.000145 / −0.91 (Hoya LAF2), expressed as index / Abbe differences. None met both earlier comparison limits of |ΔnD| ≤ 0.0001 and |ΔνD| ≤ 0.15, so their Unmatched labels remain.

## Product Identification

No product is documented as using this prescription. Its four-element, three-group Tessar-type construction alone does not identify a named Kodak Ektar. The patent cites Rudolph's US 721,240 and Schade and Frederick's US 2,158,178 as antecedents; neither supplies this prescription.

No production mount, image format, diaphragm blade count or shape, marketed focal length, marketed aperture or minimum focus distance is assigned. The title retains the patent's 100 mm design scale.

## Corrections, 2026-11-21

- The key and title were changed to identify Kodak, the 100 mm f/4.5 design and the patent. `maker` changed from absent to Kodak and `visible` from false to true. The patent number gained its A suffix, the authors' surnames were expanded to Willy Schade and Karl Tolle, and the example label changed from Example II to the printed Example 2. The year, example id and assignee were retained.
- The subtitle now reads “US 2,346,086 A, Example 2 — Eastman Kodak / Schade & Tolle,” replacing the Tessar-type description with the assignee and inventor surnames. The specifications now read “4 ELEMENTS / 3 GROUPS”, “ALL SPHERICAL”, “DESIGN f = 100 mm” and “DESIGN F/4.5”. Longer source and calculation notes moved into prose.
- The catalog design focal length changed from 100 to 100.20 mm, rounded from calculated EFL. The new 100 mm specification chip separately states the patent's printed focal length. The nominal aperture and element/group counts were retained.
- Full field and maximum half-field changed from 20.0° / 10.0° to 54.0° / 27.0° to follow the patent. The earlier 35.23 mm image circle was replaced by twice the real chief-ray height at 27.0°; the resulting diameter is now rounded upward to 101.49 mm. Separate meridional tracing agrees with that height, replacing an earlier approximate 101.9 mm estimate. No image format was added.
- The explicit `rays.offAxisFieldFrac` value of 1 was removed because it described the earlier selected field. The default fraction of 0.6 gives a displayed angle of 16.2°; the maximum analysis half-field remains 27.0°.
- Element captions changed from L1–L4 to Element 1–Element 4. Generic shape descriptions became Plano-Convex Positive, Biconcave Negative, Biconcave Negative and Biconvex Positive. Optical roles and in-air focal lengths of +37.88, −31.57, −50.57 and +31.71 mm were added. The rear pair's combined focal length is +79.05 mm.
- C1 was added to Elements 3 and 4, with its annotation spanning surfaces 5–7. Group brackets identify the front positive singlet, middle negative singlet and rear doublet, following the patent's three-component description.
- Long glass descriptions were shortened to Unmatched labels carrying the printed index/Abbe pairs. Element 1 now uses H-LaK12 (CDGM) as a modern dispersion approximation: catalog d-line Δnd = −0.0002 and Δνd = +0.08. At the patent's sodium D line the differences are ΔnD = −0.000311 and ΔνD = +0.066. Neither comparison establishes a historical supplier or melt. Every source index and Abbe number is unchanged (ΔnD = 0, ΔνD = 0), and all four sodium-D references remain. Elements 2–4 retain their Unmatched labels; no additional spectral values were added.
- The infinity distance was written as `1e15` without changing its value. The focus description states that no close-focus data are given; no product minimum focus distance was added.
- The source header and analysis now distinguish the printed image plane and field from the inferred diaphragm and clear apertures. The analysis follows the patent identification, architecture, individual elements, glass, focus, image plane/aperture/field and sources. Public descriptions use rounded optical values and omit unrelated calculation history.

After these changes, EFL remains 100.20 mm, nominal f-number 4.50, paraxial back focus 89.99 mm, retained back focus 89.70 mm, stop radius 9.775 mm and total track 109.20 mm. The final text and metadata changes leave these values, all radii, thicknesses, source indices, Abbe numbers, clear apertures, stop position and the 27.0° half-field unchanged.

## Remaining Questions

- The retained clear apertures differ from the drawing and produce thinner edges. Their relationship to physical clear apertures and mechanical vignetting needs further comparison; no rim change is made here.
- The stop station and physical iris diameter are inferred. No source specifies a stop range, blade count or blade shape.
- Historical suppliers and glass melts remain unidentified. H-LaK12 is a modern dispersion approximation, and Elements 2–4 lack matching catalog labels within the stated comparison limits. Measured broadband and anomalous-dispersion behavior remain unknown.
- The cause of the 0.29 mm back-focus difference is unconfirmed. The printed prescription and image plane are retained.
- No source establishes a production product, mount, format, marketed specifications or close-focus mechanism.
- Chief-ray clearance does not establish the patent's claimed flatness, color correction or near-elimination of coma across the 27.0° half-field.
