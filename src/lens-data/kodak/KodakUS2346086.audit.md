# KODAK 100mm f/4.5 (US 2,346,086 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,346,086 A, “Photographic Objective”](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2346086A/en), by Willy Schade and Karl Tolle, assigned to Eastman Kodak Company; filed June 1, 1942 and published April 4, 1944. No earlier priority date is printed. Example 2 appears on the drawing sheet (PDF p. 1) and the first specification page (PDF p. 2). PDF p. 3 defines the sodium-D indices and identifies R6 as the only cemented surface. Dimensions are in millimetres.

The modern [CDGM H-LaK12 data sheet](https://www.cdgmgd.com/webapp/pdf/H-LaK12.pdf), glass code 697562, supplies optical properties for a dispersion approximation to Element 1. It does not identify the historical glass or a production lens.

## Transcription Check

Both copies of Example 2 were re-read against the page images. All seven radii and signs, four glass thicknesses, two air spaces, four nD/ν pairs, the 89.7 mm back focus and T = 19.5 mm agree. No transcription discrepancy was found. R2 is plane; R6 is the sole cemented interface between Elements 3 and 4. The 7.5 mm rear air space is divided into 4.2 + 3.3 mm around the inferred stop without changing the total separation.

The surfaces stay clear of each other. At the retained rims, minimum glass edge thickness is 0.31 mm and minimum air clearance is 1.10 mm. No rim reduction is needed for surface clearance, and the iris sets the full axial aperture.

## First-Order Check

The printed nominal focal length is 100 mm; calculated EFL is 100.20 mm, 0.2% longer. The nominal f-number is 4.50 and the derived stop radius is 9.775 mm. Real-ray measures give an entrance-equivalent f/4.56 and an image-space sine f/4.58; these depend on the inferred diaphragm. A real marginal ray at the nominal f/4.5 entrance height would need a stop radius of about 9.915 mm.

The printed 89.7 mm back focus is retained as the distance from R7 to the image plane. Paraxial back focus is 89.99 mm, placing paraxial focus 0.29 mm beyond that plane. No single-digit, sign or transposition change is better supported than the printed table. The cause of the difference remains unknown. Example 1 shares the 100 mm and 89.7 mm headings but calculates to EFL 92.07 mm and back focus 82.39 mm.

The first-to-last surface distance is 19.5 mm; the first-vertex-to-image distance is 109.2 mm. At 27.0°, the real chief ray reaches an image height of 50.74 mm and clears every retained rim. The corresponding image-circle diameter is 101.49 mm after rounding upward. Off-axis marginal rays remain vignetted: the front surfaces clip about 12.0% of one side of the edge bundle by width. This is not a measurement of total transmission or proof of the patent's claimed field flatness and coma correction.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The patent draws no stop and gives no iris diameter. The stop is inferred at z = 11.2 mm from the first vertex, 4.2 mm after R4 and 3.3 mm before R5; its radius follows the nominal f/4.5 aperture. The selectable range extends from f/4.5 to f/22, but no source specifies the minimum iris opening, blade count or blade shape.

Every clear semi-diameter is inferred: 13.3 mm for Element 1 and 12.4 mm for Element 2 and the rear doublet. The drawing suggests about 11.3, 10.5 and 10.45–10.6 mm respectively, making the retained radii about 18% larger. The drawing agrees with Example 2's scale. Its doublet edge thickness is about 4.40 mm, against 4.36 mm calculated at the drawn rim. At the retained rims, Elements 1 and 4 have edges about 0.31 and 0.62 mm thick, against about 1.3 and 1.6 mm drawn. Smaller drawing-based rims admit the axial beam and chief ray but give only 59.5% geometric transmission at 27°, versus 92.0% at the retained rims. The middle front surface retains about 0.08 mm of axial-ray clearance at a 10.5 mm radius.

The patent states a flat field to 27.0° from the axis. The 54.0° full field follows that statement; the calculated image-circle diameter is not a printed format specification. No film format is inferred from it.

`focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. No close-focus distance, movement law or additional focus state is published.

The four source index/Abbe pairs retain their sodium-D reference. H-LaK12 approximates Element 1's dispersion; Elements 2–4 use index-and-Abbe estimates. No named historical melts, additional line indices or measured partial dispersions are supplied. At sodium D, nearby catalog curves differ from Element 2 by about +0.000498 / +0.098 (Ohara S-BAM4), from Element 3 by −0.000196 / −0.328 (Ohara TIM11), and from Element 4 by −0.000145 / −0.91 (Hoya LAF2), expressed as index / Abbe differences. None meets both catalog exact-match limits of |ΔnD| ≤ 0.0001 and |ΔνD| ≤ 0.15, and none is assigned.

## Product Identification

No product is documented as using this prescription. Its four-element, three-group Tessar-type construction alone does not identify a named Kodak Ektar. The patent cites Rudolph's US 721,240 and Schade and Frederick's US 2,158,178 as antecedents; neither supplies this prescription.

No production mount, image format, diaphragm blade count or shape, marketed focal length, marketed aperture or minimum focus distance is assigned. The title retains the patent's 100 mm design scale.

## Corrections, 2026-11-21

- The key and title were changed to identify Kodak, the 100 mm f/4.5 design and the patent. `maker` changed from absent to Kodak and `visible` from false to true. The patent number gained its A suffix, the authors' surnames were expanded to Willy Schade and Karl Tolle, and the example label changed from Example II to the printed Example 2. The year, example id and assignee were retained.
- The subtitle now reads “US 2,346,086 A, Example 2 — Eastman Kodak / Schade & Tolle,” replacing the Tessar-type description with the assignee and inventor surnames. The specifications now read “4 ELEMENTS / 3 GROUPS”, “ALL SPHERICAL”, “DESIGN f = 100 mm” and “DESIGN F/4.5”. The focus description states that no close-focus data are given, and the descriptions distinguish the printed image plane and field from the inferred diaphragm and clear apertures.
- The catalog design focal length changed from 100 to 100.20 mm, rounded from calculated EFL. The 100 mm specification chip separately states the patent's printed focal length. The nominal aperture and element/group counts were retained.
- Full field and maximum half-field changed from 20.0° / 10.0° to 54.0° / 27.0° to follow the patent. The earlier 35.23 mm image circle was replaced by 101.49 mm, twice the real 50.74 mm chief-ray height at 27.0° rounded upward; this replaces an earlier approximate 101.9 mm estimate. No image format was added.
- The explicit `rays.offAxisFieldFrac` value of 1 was removed because it described the earlier selected field. The default fraction of 0.6 gives a displayed angle of 16.2°; the maximum analysis half-field remains 27.0°.
- Element captions changed from L1–L4 to Element 1–Element 4. Generic shape descriptions became Plano-Convex Positive, Biconcave Negative, Biconcave Negative and Biconvex Positive. Optical roles and in-air focal lengths of +37.88, −31.57, −50.57 and +31.71 mm were added. The rear pair's combined focal length is +79.05 mm.
- C1 was added to Elements 3 and 4, with its annotation spanning surfaces 5–7. Group brackets identify the front positive singlet, middle negative singlet and rear doublet, following the patent's three-component description.
- Elements 2–4 are labelled with their printed coordinates: `Unmatched (nD 1.605 / ν 43.6)`, `Unmatched (nD 1.621 / ν 36.2)` and `Unmatched (nD 1.744 / ν 45.8)`. Element 1 is labelled H-LaK12 (CDGM) as a modern dispersion approximation: catalog d-line Δnd = −0.0002 and Δνd = +0.08, inside the printed precision; at the patent's sodium D line the differences are ΔnD = −0.00031 and ΔνD = +0.066. Neither comparison establishes a historical supplier or melt. Every source index and Abbe number is unchanged (ΔnD = 0, ΔνD = 0), all four sodium-D references remain, and no additional spectral values were added.

2026-11-21 rim review:

- The unnumbered section on the drawing sheet (PDF p. 1), shared by the two examples, agrees with Example 2's plane R2 and dimensions. At 600 dpi, the 19.5 mm first-to-last vertex span measures about 644 pixels, giving 33 pixels/mm. R2–R5 spans about 353 pixels for 10.6 mm, agreeing within 1%; the front element's approximately 132-pixel axial thickness agrees within 3%. Across the axis, the front arc's roughly 2.6 mm edge sag agrees with the 2.5 mm calculated at the drawn height, and the rear arc's approximately 1.3 mm sag agrees with R7. These checks support approximate size readings rather than printed clear apertures.
- The drawn square-cut radii are about 11.3 mm at R1/R2 and 10.5 mm at R3–R7. R3/R4 changed from 12.85 to 12.4 mm; their equal heights follow the middle singlet's square rim and the rear doublet's drawn height. Restoring R3/R4 alone adds no light anywhere from 0° to 27° with the other final rims retained.
- R5/R6/R7 changed from 13.15 to 12.4 mm. The rear doublet keeps a common height through its cemented junction, and its last element's edge thickens from 0.19 to 0.62 mm. Restoring this doublet alone increases 27° transmission from 92.0% to 94.9%; its maximum cost is 2.9 percentage points, also at 27°. Reducing the middle and rear components together to 12.3 mm gives 91.6% at 27°, a 3.3-point loss, so 12.4 mm is the smallest common tenth-millimetre value within the three-point limit.
- R1/R2 remain at 13.3 mm. With the other final rims, reducing them to 13.2 mm gives 91.1% at 27°, a 3.8-point loss. The retained front-to-middle height ratio is 1.073, close to the drawing's approximately 1.076. All three components remain about 18% taller than drawn because smaller apertures lose too much light. No land or rim cut was added.
- Wide-open geometric transmission before and after is 100% through 18°, 99.5% at 21°, 97.7% to 96.9% at 24° and 94.9% to 92.0% at 27°. The maximum loss is 2.9 percentage points at 27°. This is pupil transmission, excluding natural illumination falloff and glass losses.
- The f/4.5 axial beam and 27° chief ray clear all rims. The smallest axial margin is 1.98 mm at R3; the smallest chief-ray margin is 7.58 mm at R1. Surface clearances pass, the iris sets the aperture, and the 101.49 mm image circle has no undersized surfaces. The chief ray still reaches 50.74 mm at 27°. The half-field remains 27.0° and the nominal f-number remains 4.50. The matched-scale section comparison confirms the square rims, equal middle/rear heights and thicker rear edge; the retained oversize is visible.

After these corrections the model's EFL is 100.20 mm, nominal f-number 4.50, paraxial back focus 89.99 mm, retained back focus 89.70 mm, stop radius 9.775 mm, total track 109.20 mm and half-field 27.0°. All radii, thicknesses, source indices, Abbe numbers and the stop position are unchanged.

## Remaining Questions

- Physical clear apertures are not printed. The retained rims preserve off-axis light within the stated limit and remain about 18% taller than drawn; true mechanical vignetting remains unknown.
- The stop station and physical iris diameter are inferred. No source specifies a stop range, blade count or blade shape.
- Historical suppliers and glass melts remain unidentified. H-LaK12 is a modern dispersion approximation, and Elements 2–4 lack matching catalog labels within the stated comparison limits. Measured broadband and anomalous-dispersion behavior remain unknown.
- The cause of the 0.29 mm back-focus difference is unconfirmed. The printed prescription and image plane are retained.
- No source establishes a production product, mount, format, marketed specifications or close-focus mechanism.
- Chief-ray clearance does not establish the patent's claimed flatness, color correction or near-elimination of coma across the 27.0° half-field.
