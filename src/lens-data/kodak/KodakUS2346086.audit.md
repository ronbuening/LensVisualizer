# KODAK 100mm f/4.5 (US 2,346,086 Design) — Audit

## Source

US 2,346,086 A, “Photographic Objective,” by Willy Schade and Karl Tolle,
assigned to Eastman Kodak Company; filed June 1, 1942 and published April 4, 1944. The drawing sheet and specification both give Examples 1 and 2. The
available three-page scan supplies the section, both tables and the
definition of the D-line indices.

## Transcription

Two independent readings of the page images found no transcription errors
in Example 2: all seven radii and signs, six internal thicknesses and air
spaces, four nD/ν pairs, the 89.7 mm back focus and T = 19.5 mm agree.
The 7.5 mm second air space is represented by 4.2 + 3.3 mm around the
inferred stop. R2 is plane; R6 is the sole cemented interface.

## First-order

The printed nominal EFL is 100 mm; the calculated EFL is 100.2038043 mm.
The printed and stored back focus is 89.7 mm, while paraxial back focus is
89.9904099 mm, 0.2904099 mm farther. No single misread value was better
supported than the table as printed. Example 1's shared 100 mm heading is
loose: its table computes to EFL 92.07 mm and back focus 82.39 mm.

The nominal f-number is 4.5 and the derived stop radius is 9.7746798 mm.
Earlier real-ray calculations gave entrance-equivalent f/4.5627067 and
image-space sine f/4.5773172, distinct from the nominal aperture. The
first-to-last surface distance is 19.5 mm and total track to the stored
image plane is 109.2 mm.

Earlier checks found positive glass and air clearance with no rim trimming:
minimum glass edge 0.1881251 mm and minimum air gap 1.0300035 mm.

## Not printed

The stop position, its physical radius and every clear semi-diameter are
inferred. The stop at z = 11.2 mm lies in the rear air space. Clear
semi-diameters are 13.3 / 12.85 / 13.15 mm for the front singlet, middle
singlet and rear doublet. No close-focus law, named glass melts or
partial-dispersion measurements are supplied. The earlier 10° field and
35.2266125 mm image circle were modeling choices, now replaced by the
patent's stated field and the corresponding real chief-ray height.

## Product

No product is documented as using this patent. Its Tessar-type construction
does not establish a link to a named Kodak Ektar. The patent itself cites
Rudolph US 721,240 and Schade and Frederick US 2,158,178 as antecedents;
these are design context rather than this prescription's source.

## Corrections

The prescription remains unchanged. The public text now distinguishes the
printed image plane and field from the inferred stop and clear apertures,
and identifies Example 2 with the patent's Arabic numeral.

## Remaining questions

The figure suggests smaller clear semi-diameters, approximately 11.3 mm on
Element 1, 10.5 mm on Element 2 and 10.45–10.6 mm on the doublet. The
current rims are approximately 18–25% larger. Their Element 1 and Element 4
edge thicknesses are about 0.31 / 0.19 mm, versus about 1.3 / 1.6 mm in the
drawing. The doublet's drawn total edge, about 4.40 mm, agrees with the
4.36 mm calculated at the figure's rim. The smaller-rim comparison admitted
the axial beam and chief ray but increased off-axis vignetting. No rim
change is made in this pass.
