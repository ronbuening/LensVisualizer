# KODAK 100mm f/1.5 (US 2,336,207 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [US 2,336,207 A, _Lens_](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2336207A/en)\
**Inventors:** George H. Aklin\
**Assignee:** Eastman Kodak Company\
**Filed:** 8 October 1941\
**Published:** 7 December 1943\
**Example details:** Example 1, Figure 1 and the upper table on specification page 2; Figures 2 and 3 show its rim-ray aberrations.

The patent gives two numerical examples at nominal f = 100 mm and f/1.5. This model follows Example 1, whose prescription appears both beneath Figure 1 and in the specification. No production lens is documented for this prescription.

The printed table computes to EFL 100.675959 mm and paraxial back focal distance 49.100584 mm, against the stated 100 mm and 48.7 mm. No individual numerical correction is better supported than the printed values. The image plane follows the printed 48.7 mm back focus: comparison with the 18° and 21° rim-ray curves gives an RMS discrepancy of about 0.020 mm there, versus 0.039 mm at the paraxial plane. The modeled first-vertex-to-image distance is 140.30 mm.

The certificate of correction dated 15 February 1944 changes claim wording only, leaving the numerical prescription unchanged.

## Optical Architecture

Eight spherical elements form four air-spaced components: **1 | 2+3 | 4+5+6 | 7+8**. This is a Gaussian arrangement with positive outer components and two compound inner menisci strongly concave toward the central airspace. The inner doublet and triplet have weak net negative power; their calculated focal lengths are −289.05 mm and −803.43 mm. The final doublet is positive, with focal length +99.23 mm.

The cemented interfaces are R4, R7, R8 and R11. The front airspace is 0.4 mm; the central R5–R6 airspace is 13.9 mm. The patent weakens the central concave surfaces to improve oblique spherical aberration and reduce troublesome interreflections, distributing axial spherical correction among the first three components (specification pages 1–2).

The diaphragm is not drawn or dimensioned. Its inferred station is 9.35 mm after R5 and 4.55 mm before R6: R5 is at z = 35.90 mm, the stop at 45.25 mm, and R6 at 49.80 mm. The chief-ray zeros of Figures 2 and 3 support a position 9.35–9.75 mm behind R5. At the retained station the stop plane clears the adjacent glass rims by about 1.17 mm and 0.49 mm.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below describe each element in air. Cemented-component focal lengths include the shared interfaces and differ from the individual element values.

### Element 1 — Positive Meniscus

nD = 1.755, ν = 47.2. Glass: Unmatched. f = +140.04 mm.

This high-index singlet is the front positive component. The patent favors an index above 1.70 here to reduce the axial spherical undercorrection that accompanies weaker central concave surfaces.

### Elements 2–3 — Front Inner Doublet, C1

Element 2: nD = 1.617, ν = 55.0. Glass: Unmatched. Biconvex positive, f = +60.85 mm.\
Element 3: nD = 1.649, ν = 33.8. Glass: Unmatched. Biconcave negative, f = −40.42 mm.

The pair forms the front inner meniscus. Its R4 cemented boundary has negative refracting power. The patent uses that boundary to reduce axial spherical undercorrection while limiting its strength to avoid worsening the lower rim rays. The more dispersive negative element also balances the positive member's chromatic power.

### Elements 4–6 — Rear Inner Triplet, C2

Element 4: nD = 1.558, ν = 45.5. Glass: Unmatched. Biconcave negative, f = −40.92 mm.\
Element 5: nD = 1.511, ν = 63.5. Glass: Unmatched. Biconvex positive, f = +79.29 mm.\
Element 6: nD = 1.755, ν = 47.2. Glass: Unmatched. Positive meniscus, f = +111.79 mm.

The low-index positive middle element is cemented between a higher-index negative front element and a still higher-index positive rear element. The patent credits this arrangement with improved oblique spherical correction and control of Petzval curvature and coma, particularly through the second cemented surface. Its total thickness is 19.9 mm, below the stated preference for one quarter of the nominal focal length.

### Elements 7–8 — Rear Positive Doublet, C3

Element 7: nD = 1.617, ν = 38.5. Glass: Unmatched. Negative meniscus, f = −110.10 mm.\
Element 8: nD = 1.755, ν = 47.2. Glass: Unmatched. Biconvex positive, f = +52.98 mm.

The lower-index negative element precedes a high-index positive element. The index difference is 0.138, and the cemented R11 radius is 55.8 mm. This combination meets the patent's rear-component conditions and helps reduce upper rim-ray aberration. The enlarged rear opening admits more of the oblique bundles.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent specifies the D line, taken as sodium at 589.3 nm, and an Abbe number for every element. All eight printed pairs are retained. None of the six distinct pairs has a sufficiently close catalog match, so the glass labels state the printed values as Unmatched. No supplier, glass code or melt identity is established.

The contrast between the triplet's low-index middle glass and its high-index rear glass is central to the patent's correction strategy. The published pairs permit an approximate chromatic model, but the patent gives no individual C/F/g indices or partial dispersions; detailed broadband behavior and measured spectral performance remain unknown.

## Focus Mechanism

Infinity focus only; the patent gives no close-focus data or focusing motion. The image plane is held at the printed 48.7 mm back focus, 0.400584 mm ahead of the paraxial focal plane.

The f/1.5 setting gives a paraxially derived physical iris radius of 20.114387 mm. A real axial marginal ray launched at the nominal entrance-pupil radius would require 20.288966 mm at the stop. At the retained iris opening, the real entrance-aperture equivalent is approximately f/1.5128 and the image-sine equivalent f/1.5065. These definitions differ slightly from the paraxial f-number.

## Field, Rims and Image Plane

The patent describes sharp images to about 22° from the axis, with rim-ray plots at 18° and 21°. The model therefore uses a 44° full field and a 22° maximum traced half-field. At the printed back focus, the real 22° chief ray reaches image height 40.218953 mm; twice that height gives the 80.438 mm image circle. This diameter describes the stated angular field, rather than a documented camera format.

Figure 1 supports rear semi-diameters of about 27.2 mm on R10–R12. At 22°, the chief ray meets R12 at radius 22.324803 mm, beyond the former 22 mm rim but within the enlarged opening. The other rims remain inferred bounds. The axial f/1.5 beam and the 22° chief ray clear every optical rim; edge bundles can still be vignetted. Reaching the chief ray does not establish uniform illumination or measured image quality across the circle.

All twelve refracting boundaries are spherical. No cover glass, filter, baffle dimensions or production barrel geometry is supplied. The remaining rims have not been established from the figure, and the difference between the printed focal quantities and the paraxial calculation remains unexplained.
