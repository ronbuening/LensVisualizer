# KODAK 100mm f/8 (US 2,388,869 Design)

## Patent and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,388,869 A, “Photographic Objective”\
**Inventors:** Max Reiss\
**Assignee:** Eastman Kodak Company\
**Filed:** 12 September 1944\
**Published:** 13 November 1945\
**Example details:** Second numerical table on PDF page 2; the comparison chart calls it Example II.

The page 1 drawing and table depict Example I. No product is documented for this prescription.

## Optical Architecture

Three air-spaced singlets form a positive–negative–positive triplet. All curved surfaces are spherical; R5 is plane. The printed diaphragm position is about 2 mm behind the rear vertex; the model uses exactly 2 mm and a derived f/8 iris radius of 5.44 mm (PDF page 2).

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### Element 1 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.611, ν = 58.8. Glass: Unmatched. In-air focal length: +52.83 mm.

The front collector has a much weaker rear curvature.

### Element 2 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.605, ν = 38.0. Glass: Unmatched. In-air focal length: −32.53 mm.

Its greater dispersion supports chromatic balance; the patent associates its nearly equiconcave form with reduced astigmatism and field curvature.

### Element 3 — Plano-Convex Positive

nD = 1.611, ν = 58.8. Glass: Unmatched. In-air focal length: +52.70 mm.

This element precedes the rear diaphragm and shares the front element's glass coordinates.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent specifies sodium D, distinct from helium d, and V without its spectral-line definition. Neither glass pair matches an exact catalog row. Suppliers, melts, and full dispersion curves remain unknown.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent describes front-element focusing without numerical travel or object distances. The model is focused at infinity.

## First-Order Values and Field Coverage

Printed EF 100 mm and BF 89.5 mm compare with computed EFL 99.59 mm and glass-rear BF 89.11 mm; both BF/EF ratios are approximately 0.895. Example I computes 0.6% above 100 mm, consistent with approximate EF labels. The 0.39 mm BF difference exceeds the printed precision. The image plane is at paraxial focus, 87.11 mm behind the stop. The element-power chart is approximate.

Printed coverage exceeds 27.0° from the axis. The 27.0° half-field and 54.0° full field give a real chief-ray height of 51.05 mm and a 102.10 mm circle, rounded upward. No film format is specified. Every rim clears the chief ray; the front surface partly clips the edge bundle.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The clear semi-diameters are inferred at 15/12.5/10 mm, with both faces of each singlet at one height. The middle and rear elements are about 83% and 67% of the front element's height, within about 5% of Example I's drawn proportions. That drawing does not establish Example II's clear dimensions or rim profiles. All rims clear the f/8 axial beam and the 27.0° chief ray. Wide-open geometric transmission is 97.6% at 27.0°; this is not measured illumination or broadband transmission. The additional f/11–22 settings are modeled. Geometric MTF calculations do not establish measured or broadband performance.
