# KODAK 100mm f/8 (US 2,388,869 Design)

## Patent and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,388,869 A, “Photographic Objective”
**Inventors:** Max Reiss
**Assignee:** Eastman Kodak Company
**Filed:** 12 September 1944
**Published:** 13 November 1945
**Example details:** Second numerical table on PDF page 2; the comparison chart calls it Example II.

The page 1 drawing and table depict Example I. No product is documented for this prescription.

## Optical Architecture

Three air-spaced singlets form a positive–negative–positive triplet. All curved surfaces are spherical; R5 is plane. The printed diaphragm position is about 2 mm behind the rear vertex; the model uses exactly 2 mm and a derived f/8 iris radius of 5.444415 mm (PDF page 2).

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### Element 1 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.611, ν = 58.8. Glass: Unmatched. In-air focal length: +52.83 mm.

The front collector has a much weaker rear curvature.

### Element 2 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.605, ν = 38.0. Glass: Unmatched. In-air focal length: −32.53 mm.

Its greater dispersion supports chromatic balance; the patent associates its nearly equiconcave form with reduced astigmatism and field curvature.

### Element 3 — Plano-Convex Positive

nD = 1.611, ν = 58.8. Glass: Unmatched. In-air focal length: +52.70 mm.

This element precedes the rear diaphragm and shares the front element's glass coordinates.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent specifies sodium D, distinct from helium d, and V without its spectral-line definition. Neither glass pair matches an exact catalog row. Suppliers, melts, and full dispersion curves remain unknown.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent describes front-element focusing without numerical travel or object distances. The model represents infinity focus only.

## First-Order Values and Field Coverage

Printed EF 100 mm and BF 89.5 mm compare with computed EFL 99.59 mm and glass-rear BF 89.11 mm; both BF/EF ratios are approximately 0.895. The EF label is rounded: Example I computes 0.57% above 100 mm. The 0.389360 mm BF difference exceeds the printed precision. The model retains paraxial focus, 87.110640 mm behind the stop. The source's element-power chart is also approximate.

The printed coverage exceeds 27° from the axis. The model uses a 27° half-field and 54° full field. Its real chief-ray height of 51.048874 mm gives a 102.097748 mm circle, without specifying a film format. Every rim clears the chief ray; the front surface partly clips the edge bundle.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The 15/12.5/10 mm semi-diameters are inferred; Example I's smaller drawn rims do not establish Example II's dimensions. The additional f/11–22 settings are modeled. Geometric MTF calculations do not establish measured or broadband performance.
