# KODAK 100mm f/5.6 (US 2,380,207 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [US 2,380,207 A, “Telephoto Objective”](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2380207A/en)

**Inventors:** George H. Aklin

**Assignee:** Eastman Kodak Company

**Filed:** 14 December 1943

**Published:** 10 July 1945

**Example details:** Sole numerical example; the drawing-sheet table and specification table repeat the same prescription. Claim 5 gives approximate focal-length ratios.

The patent describes a compact telephoto objective intended to combine low distortion with correction of field curvature and astigmatism. It specifies a nominal focal length of 100 mm and an aperture of f/5.6. No manufactured lens is documented for this prescription.

The printed table computes to an effective focal length of 100.23 mm and a back focus of 53.66 mm, against the stated 100 mm and 53.2 mm. The image plane is at the calculated paraxial focus, 0.46 mm behind the printed back-focus position. No single misprint has been identified that explains both differences.

## Optical Architecture

The five spherical elements form three air-spaced groups: a front cemented doublet, a rear cemented doublet, and a positive rear singlet. The front doublet is positive, with a calculated focal length of +59.99 mm. The second doublet is negative at −43.95 mm; together with the closely spaced positive singlet, it forms the rear negative member of the telephoto system.

The cemented interfaces are R2 and R5. A 21.1 mm air space separates the two doublets and contains the diaphragm. The first-to-last-vertex length is 30.95 mm, consistent with the patent's stated 0.31f. The paraxial image plane lies 84.61 mm behind the first vertex.

Both numerical tables give a 0.05 mm air space between R6 and R7. The prose instead gives 0.005f, which would be 0.5 mm at the stated focal length; claim 5 gives only the bound S2 < 0.01F. The model follows the two tables: the figure shows the rear elements nearly touching, and their 0.05 mm spacing agrees better with the stated focal length and distortion pattern. Elements IV and V have the same printed glass coordinates but remain separated by air.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below describe each element in air; the cemented groups have different combined powers.

### Element 1 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.498, ν = 67.0. Glass: N-BK10 (Schott), spectral proxy. f = +25.09 mm.

The strongly curved front element collects light for the positive front member. Its lower dispersion complements the negative element cemented behind it.

### Element 2 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.656, ν = 40.5. Glass: Unmatched. f = −38.13 mm.

The higher-index, more dispersive negative element completes the front doublet. Its index exceeds the first element's by 0.158, consistent with the patent's discussion of the front pair.

### Element 3 — Negative Meniscus

nD = 1.500, ν = 61.6. Glass: K11 (Sumita), exact match at the D line. f = −26.02 mm.

Its strongly concave front surface supplies the principal diverging power of the rear doublet. The meniscus faces its concave side toward the object.

### Element 4 — Positive Meniscus

nD = 1.755, ν = 47.2. Glass: Unmatched. f = +69.24 mm.

This high-index positive element is cemented behind Element 3. The patent associates the rear doublet's large index difference, 0.255 here, with correction of field curvature and astigmatism.

### Element 5 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.755, ν = 47.2. Glass: Unmatched. f = +91.43 mm.

The rear singlet has a weakly curved front surface and a more strongly curved rear surface. It completes the rear negative member across the thin air space, whose curved boundaries give the air lens positive power.

## Glass Identification and Selection

Claim 5 identifies the D line, taken as sodium, 589.3 nm. All five printed refractive indices and Abbe numbers are retained on that reference line. The patent provides no partial-dispersion data or glass supplier.

Element 1 uses N-BK10 (Schott) as a spectral proxy: catalog minus printed differences at the sodium D line are Δn = −0.000246 and Δν = −0.0545. These fall within the proxy limits of 0.002 in index and 0.25 in Abbe number. The catalog curve supplies the dispersion shape while the printed index and Abbe number are kept.

Element 3 matches K11 (Sumita) within the printed precision, with Δn = +0.00006 and Δν = −0.02 at the sodium D line. It also uses a catalog dispersion curve while retaining the printed coordinates. Neither row identifies Kodak's historical supplier or melt. Elements 2, 4 and 5 remain unmatched because no catalog row falls within the proxy limits; their chromatic behavior uses Abbe-number estimates, with secondary spectrum unresolved.

## Focus Mechanism and Aperture

The model is focused at infinity. The patent gives no close-focus distances, moving-group law, or additional focus states.

The text places a favorable diaphragm position about 9 units behind the third surface. The model uses 9 mm after R3 and 12.1 mm before R4, putting the stop 15.1 mm behind the first vertex. The stop radius is inferred from f/5.6, giving 6.51 mm in the paraxial calculation. A real axial marginal ray for the nominal entrance aperture would require about 6.70 mm at the stop; the retained iris corresponds to approximately f/5.75 by the real-ray entrance-aperture measure.

## Field and Model Limits

The patent reports distortion at 9.0°, 12.0°, and 15.0° from the axis. The modeled full field is therefore 30.0°, with tracing through a 15.0° half-field. At the paraxial image plane, the real 15.0° chief ray reaches an image height of 26.85 mm, giving an image-circle diameter of 53.71 mm. This circle comes from the stated field at the model's dimensions; the patent specifies no film format or mount.

The patent's distortion values are 0.024%, 0.038%, and 0.013% of focal length at those three angles. The model gives about +0.016%, +0.022%, and −0.001%, respectively: similarly small values with a turn near 15.0°, rather than exact reproduction. The calculated Petzval sum is +0.00008355 mm⁻¹, close to the printed 0.000.

Clear semi-diameters are estimated: 9.8/9.8/10 mm across the front doublet, 7.4 mm across the rear doublet and 7.8 mm across the rear singlet. The drawing on page 1 suggests about 8.6, 5.9 and 6.4 mm, respectively. The front outline is smaller than the 8.95 mm height of the stated f/5.6 axial beam. The larger modeled rear rims preserve illumination while keeping the singlet taller than the adjacent doublet. The axial beam and all chief rays through 15.0° clear the rims; off-axis bundles remain partly vignetted, with about 52.4% geometric transmission at 15.0°. These inferred apertures are not printed dimensions. The positive 0.05 mm rear air gap widens away from the axis.
