# KODAK ANASTIGMAT 100mm f/2 (US 1,620,337 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 1,620,337 A, *Objective*  
**Application Number:** Serial No. 613,270  
**Filed:** January 17, 1923  
**Granted:** March 8, 1927  
**Inventors:** Charles W. Frederick; Fred E. Altman  
**Assignee:** Eastman Kodak Company  
**Example details:** the single numerical example, printed twice: as a table on the drawing sheet (with signed radii) and in the specification (radii as magnitudes only); "aperture = F/2; focal length = 100"

The patent describes an objective "more particularly" for projection: wide aperture, well corrected over a narrow field. Its one worked example is a four-element, four-group f/2 lens with a focal length of 100, computed for a field of 7° from the axis. The specification says that within that field the lens is corrected for spherical and chromatic aberration, astigmatism, coma and flatness of field.

The KODAK ANASTIGMAT 100mm f/2 (US 1,620,337 Design) takes its name from the Kodak Anastigmat f/1.9 25 mm cine lens, which Kodak engraved with this patent number. No production prescription has been published, so the model is the patent's example rather than a measured production lens. The evidence:

1. Both inventors, of Rochester, New York, assigned the patent to Eastman Kodak Company.
2. A Kodak Anastigmat f/1.9 25 mm cine-camera lens in the Ingenium collection (artifact 2010.0223.001) is engraved "U.S.A. PAT. NOS. 1,620,337 - 1,690,607". The second number, US 1,690,607 (Otto Wittel, Kodak, 1928), covers a focusing objective mount, so the optical patent on that lens is this one.
3. A Kodascope projection lens in the Ingenium collection (artifact 2009.0389.002), part of a home-movie projector outfit of the Library Kodascope period (about 1929–1932), is engraved "U.S.A. PAT. NO. 1,620,337".
4. The example's stated purpose and field fit the projector lens better than the camera lens. Kodascope projectors for 16 mm film used a 2-inch projection lens (for example the 1928 Kodascope Model B in the Science Museum Group collection). The 16 mm frame's half-diagonal of about 6.4 mm subtends about 7.2° at 50.8 mm, which matches the patent's 7° semi-field. A 25 mm camera lens on the same frame needs about 14°. This match is an inference from the field angle alone.
5. Limits: no source found gives the Kodascope lens's aperture or either production prescription, and no construction diagram of either product was found. The engravings show that both products fall under the patent's claims. They do not show that either used this exact example.

## Optical Architecture

The lens has four air-spaced singlets in two components. The outer lenses are positive and the inner lenses negative, the classic dialyte arrangement. Each component pairs an outer biconvex barium crown with an inner dense-flint negative lens:

- Front component: L1 biconvex positive, then a 1.5 air space (S1), then L2 biconcave negative. Computed focal length +166.9.
- Central air space S2 = 18.0, the largest spacing in the lens.
- Rear component: L3 negative meniscus, then a 3.0 air space (S3), then L4 biconvex positive. Computed focal length +165.4.

L4 is identical to L1 in glass and curvature and is mounted reversed, so the outer lenses are symmetrical about the centre. The two negative lenses use the same flint but differ in shape: L2 is biconcave and L3 a meniscus, concave towards L4. The patent names this asymmetry, together with the very narrow front spacing S1, as the main features that distinguish the design from earlier objectives of the same type.

Both inner air spaces have the form of a positive meniscus. They are chosen so that each negative lens touches its positive neighbour at the edge, which, the patent notes, sets the spacing without spacer rings. With the printed radii, S1 closes at about 23.9 mm from the axis and S3 at about 22.3 mm.

The claims add some proportions, all of which the example meets:

- every radius exceeds half the focal length (the smallest is R6 = 52.4);
- the whole lens is shorter than 0.55 of the focal length (54.70 / 100.08 = 0.547);
- each inner air space is no more than a quarter of the adjacent positive lens's thickness (S1 / T1 = 0.125; S3 / T4 = 0.25).

The Petzval sum of the printed prescription corresponds to a Petzval radius of about 147 (about 1.5 focal lengths), curved towards the lens. That is acceptable over the narrow 7° field the patent targets.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Biconvex Positive (front crown)

`nd = 1.61088, νd = 57.2` (D line, as printed), barium crown; G′ index 1.62448 printed. Standalone in-air `f = +71.18`.

L1 is a nearly equi-convex collector with R1 = +64.0 and R2 = −126.0. It carries most of the front component's positive power. Its strongly curved front surface sets the entrance-side height of the f/2 beam.

### L2 — Biconcave Negative (front flint)

`nd = 1.61680, νd = 36.4` (D line), dense flint; G′ index 1.63920 printed. Standalone in-air `f = −112.25`.

L2 has a strong front surface (R3 = −77.2) and an almost flat rear (R4 = +684.5). It sits 1.5 behind L1, less than a quarter of T1, with the curvature of R3 steeper than R2 so the two lenses meet at the rim. L2 achromatises the front component. As in other dialytes, its strongly curved front surface is probably the main source of the overcorrected spherical aberration that balances the positive lenses. That is an interpretation; the patent does not say so.

### L3 — Negative Meniscus (rear flint)

`nd = 1.61680, νd = 36.4` (D line), the same dense flint as L2. Standalone in-air `f = −112.21`.

L3 has R5 = +222.2 and R6 = +52.4: convex towards the object and strongly concave towards L4. Its power is almost the same as L2's, but it is bent very differently. The patent's claims single out a meniscus inner negative lens, concave side facing its positive partner, as the departure from symmetrical predecessors. Read as a design choice, the extra bending gives a free parameter for astigmatism and coma without altering the matched powers; the patent itself claims only the shape.

### L4 — Biconvex Positive (rear crown)

`nd = 1.61088, νd = 57.2` (D line), the same barium crown as L1. Standalone in-air `f = +71.18`.

L4 repeats L1 reversed (R7 = +126.0, R8 = −64.0), 3.0 behind L3, and touches L3 at the rim.

## Glass Identification

The patent prints, for each glass, the index for the D line and the G′ line together with the dispersive ratio ν. It names the glass types only in words: barium crown for L1 and L4, dense flint for L2 and L3.

| Glass | D index | G′ index | ν | Used in | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Barium crown | 1.61088 | 1.62448 | 57.2 | L1, L4 | positive collectors |
| Dense flint | 1.61680 | 1.63920 | 36.4 | L2, L3 | negative achromatising lenses |

The model stores the D index and ν, marked as D-line values (sodium, 589.3 nm), which is how the patent labels them. Neither pair matches a current catalogue glass at the D or d line, and the 1920s melts are not identified, so both glasses are labelled as unmatched with their printed values. The G′ indices (434.0 nm) are not stored, because the file format has no G′ field. Colour in the model therefore follows the printed ν, not a fitted dispersion curve.

The pairing is a conventional achromatising choice. The crown and flint have almost the same index (a difference of 0.006) but very different ν. With nearly equal indices, the Petzval sum depends almost only on how power is split between the positive and negative lenses, so field flattening comes from the spacing and bending of the lenses rather than from an index difference.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent gives only the infinity-conjugate prescription and says nothing about focusing. A projector lens is focused by moving the whole objective. The model is therefore infinity-only, with no internal spacing change, and the image plane is the computed paraxial back focus of 74.34 behind R8. The patent prints no back focus.

## Model Scope and Limitations

- Radii, thicknesses, air spaces, D indices and ν are the patent's printed values. Radius signs come from the drawing-sheet table and agree with the figure.
- Computed D-line focal length: 100.08 against the printed 100 (+0.08 %). Total length from R1 to R8: 54.70. Paraxial back focus: 74.34.
- The diaphragm is not printed: the figure shows none and a projection lens of this kind usually has no iris. The model places a stop at the middle of S2, 9.0 behind R4. At f/2 its paraxial semi-diameter is 20.93, for an entrance-pupil radius of 25.02.
- Clear semi-diameters are not printed and are estimated: 26 for R1 and R8, 22.9 and 22.8 for R2 and R3, 23 for R4 and R5, and 21 for R6 and R7. R2 and R3 are set just high enough to pass the f/2 axial beam, short of the 23.9 mm height where L1 and L2 meet. Because the patent states that these lenses touch at their edges, the drawing lets the two surfaces come slightly closer across S1 than it normally allows (0.91 of the gap instead of 0.9).
- At the patent's 7° semi-field (image height 12.3), no surface blocks the chief ray. The estimated rims cut part of the oblique beam, about 12–13 % on one side at R2 and R3.
