# KODAK 100mm f/3.5 (US 2,341,385 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,341,385 A, _Wide-angle lens_

**Inventors:** Rudolf Kingslake; Paul W. Stevens

**Assignee:** Eastman Kodak Company

**Filed:** November 6, 1941

**Published:** February 8, 1944

**Example details:** Sole numerical example, repeated on the drawing sheet and specification page 2 (PDF pp. 1 and 3).

The patent describes a five-element, four-group photographic objective at 100 mm and f/3.5. Its negative front member increases back focus while remaining close to the positive rear member. This is an early retrofocus layout, with back focus slightly longer than the complete objective's focal length.

No production lens is documented as using this prescription. The modern Wikimedia Commons drawing calls it a “WA Ektanar,” based on a user's redraw of this patent. Kodak's April 1958 Signet 30 manual documents a later 44 mm f/2.8 Ektanar, without establishing a connection to this 1941 design. The name identifies the patent design. [2, 3]

## Optical Architecture

The four groups have negative–positive–negative–positive power. Element I is a negative meniscus. Elements II and III form the positive and negative singlets within the rear member, followed by the positive cemented doublet of elements IV and V. All nine refracting surfaces are spherical. The cemented interface is R8.

The patent treats the front and rear members as a jointly corrected system. It emphasizes field curvature and spherical aberration rather than independently correcting each member. The weaker front surface and stronger rear surface of element III are part of its field-correction strategy. The cemented rear component provides additional freedom for chromatic, oblique spherical, and distortion correction. [1, specification pp. 1–2]

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below describe each thick element in air, rather than its separate contribution inside the assembled objective.

### Element I — Negative Meniscus

`nD = 1.620, ν = 60.4. Glass: BACD16 (Hoya), spectral proxy. f = −87.73 mm.`

The stronger concave rear surface gives the front meniscus negative power. It diverges the incoming beam and extends the back focus of the combined objective. Its 10.6 mm separation from element II keeps the negative and positive members compact.

### Element II — Biconvex Positive

`nD = 1.697, ν = 56.1. Glass: H-LaK12 (CDGM), spectral proxy. f = +55.23 mm.`

This is the front positive component of the rear member. Its front surface is more strongly curved than its rear surface, as the patent specifies. It supplies converging power after the front negative meniscus.

### Element III — Biconcave Negative

`nD = 1.617, ν = 36.6. Glass: F4 (Sumita), spectral proxy. f = −66.90 mm.`

The central negative component of the rear member uses a more dispersive glass than element II or the last element. Its surface-curvature magnitudes round to the patent's 0.0024 and 0.0217 mm⁻¹, whose summed reciprocal is about 41.5 mm. The patent links this bending to field flatness. [1, specification p. 2]

### Element IV — Negative Meniscus

`nD = 1.617, ν = 36.6. Glass: F4 (Sumita), spectral proxy. f = −92.14 mm.`

The negative front element of the rear doublet uses the same printed glass coordinates as element III. It is cemented to element V at R8 and supplies the more dispersive partner of the pair.

### Element V — Biconvex Positive

`nD = 1.611, ν = 57.2. Glass: Unmatched. f = +42.01 mm.`

The thick positive last element makes the cemented pair net positive, with a combined focal length of +68.61 mm. Its strongly convex rear surface follows the patent's fourth-component construction. The rear member also includes elements II and III.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The tables print sodium D-line indices and Abbe values headed V, without glass names, suppliers, partial dispersion, or further spectral indices. Both printings give 1.617 / 36.6 for elements III and IV.

Element I uses BACD16 (Hoya) as a spectral proxy, with catalog-minus-printed differences at sodium D of ΔnD +0.00032 and Δν −0.07. Element II uses H-LaK12 (CDGM) as a spectral proxy, with ΔnD −0.00031 and Δν +0.07. Elements III and IV use F4 (Sumita) as a spectral proxy, with ΔnD −0.00056 and Δν +0.01. [4–6]

The model keeps the printed indices and Abbe numbers and uses the catalog rows only for dispersion-curve shape in the colour analysis. The historical suppliers and melts are unknown. Element V uses an Abbe-number estimate because no catalog row falls within 0.002 in index and 0.25 in Abbe number at sodium D. The patent supplies insufficient spectral data to establish secondary-spectrum correction, anomalous dispersion, or measured broadband performance.

## Focus Mechanism

The model is focused at infinity; the patent gives no close-focus data. No element motion or finite-conjugate spacing is inferred.

## Image Plane, Aperture, and Field

The printed prescription computes to EFL 100.08 mm and paraxial back focus 101.53 mm, compared with the stated 100 mm and 101.7 mm. The 0.17 mm back-focus difference is consistent with table rounding. The image plane remains at paraxial focus, 183.53 mm behind the first vertex, after an 82 mm lens track; it has not been shifted to minimize blur.

The text gives 67.6 mm for the positive rear member, comprising elements II–V, against 67.95 mm calculated from the table. The difference remains unexplained.

The drawing places the diaphragm between elements II and III, nearer III. The inferred position divides the 14.3 mm airspace into 11.7 mm before the stop and 2.6 mm after it, placing the stop 40.8 mm behind the first vertex. Its calculated radius is 13.695 mm at nominal paraxial f/3.50. Exact axial tracing gives entrance-equivalent f/3.59 and image-space sine f/3.58. The physical iris diameter is not printed.

The patent describes coverage beyond 30° from the axis. The model uses a 30.0° half-field, or 60.0° full field. The real chief ray at 30.0° reaches image height 57.444 mm, giving image-circle diameter 114.89 mm rounded upward to 0.01 mm. This follows the patent's general angular field; no field is specified separately for this example or tied to a film format. The chief ray clears the rims; marginal rays are partly vignetted, particularly at R8 and R9. No performance claim beyond 30.0° is made.

All lens semi-diameters are inferred from the drawing on PDF page 1: R1–R9 are 29, 23.6, 24, 24, 22, 19.7, 25, 25, and 25 mm. R2 and R6 follow the bevelled curve ends; straight joins approximate the bevels without flat lands. The square-cut second element and rear doublet retain equal rim heights. Every rim clears the axial beam and the 30.0° chief ray. Wide-open geometric pupil transmission is 95.1% at 24.0°, 83.5% at 27.0° and 64.3% at 30.0°; these figures exclude illumination losses from obliquity, absorption and reflections.
