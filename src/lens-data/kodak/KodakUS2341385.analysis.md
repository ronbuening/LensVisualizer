# KODAK 100mm f/3.5 (US 2,341,385 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,341,385 A, _Wide-angle lens_

**Inventors:** Rudolf Kingslake; Paul W. Stevens

**Assignee:** Eastman Kodak Company

**Filed:** November 6, 1941

**Published:** February 8, 1944

**Example details:** Sole numerical example, repeated on the drawing sheet and specification page 2 (PDF pp. 1 and 3).

The patent describes a five-element, four-group photographic objective at 100 mm and f/3.5. Its negative front member increases back focus while remaining close to the positive rear member. This is an early retrofocus layout, with back focus slightly longer than the complete objective's focal length.

No production lens is documented as using this prescription. The modern Wikimedia Commons drawing calls it a “WA Ektanar,” but that identification rests on a user's redraw of this patent. Kodak's Ektanar name is documented on the later Signet line, including the 44 mm f/2.8 lens in the April 1958 Signet 30 manual; that does not establish a connection to this 1941 design. The catalog name therefore identifies the patent design rather than a commercial model. [2, 3]

## Optical Architecture

The four groups have negative–positive–negative–positive power. Element 1 is a negative meniscus. Elements 2 and 3 form the positive and negative singlets within the rear member, followed by the positive cemented doublet of elements 4 and 5. All nine refracting surfaces are spherical; the cemented interface is R8.

The patent treats the front and rear members as a jointly corrected system. It emphasizes field curvature and spherical aberration rather than independently correcting each member. The weaker front surface and stronger rear surface of element 3 are part of its field-correction strategy. The cemented rear component provides additional freedom for chromatic, oblique spherical, and distortion correction. [1, specification pp. 1–2]

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below describe each thick element in air, rather than its separate contribution inside the assembled objective.

### Element 1 — Negative Meniscus

`nD = 1.620, ν = 60.4. Glass: Unmatched. f = −87.73 mm.`

The stronger concave rear surface gives the front meniscus negative power. It diverges the incoming beam and extends the back focus of the combined objective. Its 10.6 mm separation from element 2 keeps the negative and positive members compact.

### Element 2 — Biconvex Positive

`nD = 1.697, ν = 56.1. Glass: Unmatched. f = +55.23 mm.`

This is the front positive component of the rear member. Its front surface is more strongly curved than its rear surface, as the patent specifies. It supplies converging power after the front negative meniscus.

### Element 3 — Biconcave Negative

`nD = 1.617, ν = 36.6. Glass: Unmatched. f = −66.90 mm.`

The central negative component of the rear member uses a more dispersive glass than element 2 or the last element. Its surface-curvature magnitudes round to the patent's 0.0024 and 0.0217 mm⁻¹, whose summed reciprocal is about 41.5 mm. The patent links this bending to field flatness. [1, specification p. 2]

### Element 4 — Negative Meniscus

`nD = 1.617, ν = 36.6. Glass: Unmatched. f = −92.14 mm.`

The negative front element of the rear doublet uses the same printed glass coordinates as element 3. It is cemented to element 5 at R8 and supplies the more dispersive partner of the pair.

### Element 5 — Biconvex Positive

`nD = 1.611, ν = 57.2. Glass: Unmatched. f = +42.01 mm.`

The thick positive last element makes the cemented pair net positive, with a combined focal length of +68.61 mm. Its strongly convex rear surface follows the patent's fourth-component construction. The pair is distinct from the entire rear member, which also includes elements 2 and 3.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The tables print sodium D-line indices, retained as `indexReference: "D"`, and Abbe values headed `V`. They do not identify glass names, suppliers, partial dispersion, or additional spectral indices. All five glasses retain their published numerical values and remain unmatched to a catalog row within the required index and Abbe tolerances. The repeated 1.617 / 36.6 pair is preserved on elements 3 and 4.

The Abbe values support an approximate dispersion model, but do not establish secondary-spectrum correction or measured broadband performance. No anomalous-dispersion designation is inferred.

## Focus Mechanism

Infinity focus only; the patent gives no close-focus data. No element motion or finite-conjugate spacing is inferred.

## Image Plane, Aperture, and Field

The printed prescription computes to EFL 100.077588 mm and paraxial back focus 101.527023 mm, compared with the stated 100 mm and 101.7 mm. The 0.17 mm back-focus difference is consistent with rounding of the table. The image plane remains at the calculated paraxial position, 183.527023 mm behind the first vertex, after an 82 mm lens track. It has not been shifted to minimize blur.

The text separately gives 67.6 mm for the positive rear member, comprising elements 2–5. The table computes to 67.948442 mm for that member. This difference remains unexplained; the printed radii, thicknesses, and indices are retained.

The drawing places the diaphragm between elements 2 and 3, nearer element 3. The inferred position divides the 14.3 mm airspace into 11.7 mm before the stop and 2.6 mm after it, placing the stop 40.8 mm behind the first vertex. Its calculated radius is 13.695005 mm at nominal paraxial f/3.5. Exact axial tracing gives an entrance-equivalent f-number of 3.586752 and an image-space sine f-number of 3.578376. These are properties of the inferred iris, whose physical diameter is not printed.

The patent describes coverage extending beyond 30° from the axis. The model uses a 30° half-field, or 60° full field. The real chief ray at 30° reaches an image height of 57.444 mm at the retained image plane, giving a rounded image-circle diameter of 114.9 mm. This diameter follows the patent's angular field, rather than a documented film format. The chief ray clears the rims; marginal field rays are partly vignetted, particularly at R8 and R9. No performance claim beyond the modeled 30° half-field is made.

All lens semi-diameters are inferred: R1–R9 are 29, 27, 24, 24, 22, 22, 25, 25, and 25 mm. They pass the axial beam without a rim limit. R2 and R6 are larger than the drawing shows (about 23.6 and 19.4 mm).
