# KODAK 100mm f/8 (US 2,388,869 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,388,869 A, _Photographic Objective_](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2388869A/en), by Max Reiss, assigned to Eastman Kodak Company. Filing and recorded priority date: 12 September 1944; publication and grant: 13 November 1945. Example II is the second numerical table on PDF page 2, the first specification page. It prints EF 100 mm, f/8 and BF 89.5 mm. The drawing and table on PDF page 1 depict Example I; PDF page 3 contains the claims. Both examples have three air-spaced elements and a rear diaphragm, without cemented pairs. No production source establishes use of Example II in a marketed lens.

## Transcription Check

All six signed radii, three glass thicknesses, two internal air spaces and three D-line index/Abbe-number pairs agree with the page images. R5 is plane. No prescription digit or sign correction is indicated. The patent explicitly specifies the D line, distinct from helium d, but does not define V by its spectral lines. The text places the Example II diaphragm about 2 mm behind the rear vertex and describes focusing by moving the front element alone.

The source's summary chart is approximate. Calculated element focal lengths are +52.83/−32.53/+52.70 mm, against printed magnitudes 0.51F/0.31F/0.52F; the decimal in the first entry is faint. The magnitude of R2/R1 is 48.75 against 48, while R4/R3 is 0.766 against 0.77. Both index differences equal the printed 0.006, and the prescription satisfies the claim 5 ranges. No single source error has been established.

## First-Order Check

The calculated EFL is 99.59 mm, 0.4% below the printed 100 mm; the paraxial f-number is 8.00. Glass-rear back focus is 89.11 mm against the printed 89.5 mm. Their BF/EF ratios nearly agree, at 0.8947 and 0.895. Example I calculates to 100.57 mm, consistent with approximate EF labels, but this does not establish the cause of the Example II discrepancy. The 0.389 mm back-focus difference exceeds the printed 0.1 mm precision.

The image plane is at calculated paraxial focus, 89.11 mm behind R6 and 87.11 mm behind the diaphragm; the first-vertex-to-image distance is 108.91 mm. Because the diaphragm is the last modeled surface, back focus measured from it is the shorter 87.11 mm. Its derived f/8 radius is 5.44 mm. Exact axial tracing gives an entrance-equivalent f/8.00 and image-space sine f/8.03; these aperture conventions do not establish historical iris dimensions.

The surfaces stay clear of each other, with minimum glass thickness and air clearance of 1.108 mm and 2.806 mm. No rim trimming or exceptional gap allowance is needed. The iris sets f/8 without a rim, ray-path or iris-opening limit. All lens surfaces clear the stated image circle, and the 27.0° corner chief ray reaches the image. The front surface clips about 9.0% of one side of the edge bundle's width; this is not a flux-throughput measurement.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The patent gives no clear semi-diameters, iris diameter, numerical close-focus data, glass trade names or complete dispersion curves. The retained element semi-diameters are inferred at 15/12.5/10 mm. Exactly 2 mm realizes the approximate diaphragm position, and the iris opening follows from f/8. The f/11, f/16 and f/22 settings are additional model choices, not a documented historical stop range.

The field is based on the patent's coverage statement exceeding 27.0° from the axis. The 27.0° half-field and 54.0° full field give a real chief-ray height of 51.05 mm; twice that height, rounded upward, gives the 102.10 mm image circle. The diameter is derived, not printed, and establishes neither a film format nor an unvignetted edge bundle. `focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. No numerical front-element travel or finite-focus state is supplied.

## Product Identification

No product is documented for this prescription. The four-element US 2,518,719 Wide-Field Ektar is a different design. No mount, film format, blade count or shape, minimum focus distance, marketed focal length or f-number, or production glass supplier can be assigned from the available evidence.
