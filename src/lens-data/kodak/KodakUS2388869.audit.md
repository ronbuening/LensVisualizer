# KODAK 100mm f/8 (US 2,388,869 Design) — Audit

## Source

US 2,388,869 A, “Photographic Objective,” Max Reiss, assigned to Eastman Kodak Company; filed 12 September 1944 and published 13 November 1945. The second table on PDF page 2 supplies Example II. The drawing and its table on page 1 belong to Example I. The two examples share a three-element, three-group arrangement with a rear diaphragm; neither contains a cemented pair.

## Transcription

Two independent readings of the page images agree on all six signed radii, five internal distances, three D-line index/Abbe-number pairs, and the printed EF 100 mm, f/8, and BF 89.5 mm. R5 is plane. No prescription digit or sign correction is indicated. The text explicitly puts the Example II diaphragm about 2 mm behind the rear vertex and mentions focusing by moving the front element alone.

## First-Order

The initial model computed EFL 99.594153 mm and glass-rear paraxial BF 89.110640 mm, compared with the printed 100 mm and 89.5 mm. These nearly preserve BF/EF: 0.894738 versus 0.895. Example I computes EFL 100.567 mm, supporting a rounded EF label. The 0.389360 mm BF difference exceeds the printed 0.1 mm precision; the retained image plane is computed paraxial focus, not a transcription of printed BF.

The stop lies 2 mm behind R6, so its image distance is 87.110640 mm. Total first-vertex-to-image distance is 108.910640 mm. A back-focus display measured from the final modeled stop has that shorter reference distance. The nominal f/8 setting derives a 5.444415 mm iris radius. Earlier exact axial tracing gave an entrance-equivalent f/8.002707 and image-space sine f/8.028717; these are distinct aperture conventions, not measured iris dimensions.

The element focal lengths are +52.831227/−32.532923/+52.700491 mm. The source's approximate chart instead gives magnitudes 0.51F/0.31F/0.52F; R2/R1 computes to 48.753799 against 48 in that chart. R4/R3 computes to 0.765864 against 0.77, and both index differences equal the printed 0.006. The prescription satisfies the claim 5 ranges. No individual source error is established; an unresolved source note records the summary discrepancies.

Earlier geometry checks found minimum common-band glass and air clearances of 1.107842 mm and 2.805959 mm, no trimming, and no need for a gap-clearance override. Sampled rays through 25° reached the image; separate entrance-pupil checks found clipping, without establishing flux throughput. Earlier sodium-D geometric MTF calculations converged at the sampled fields; they did not establish measured performance or broadband correction. The four-element US 2,518,719 Wide-Field Ektar is a different design.

## Not Printed

The source does not give clear semi-diameters, iris diameter, numerical close-focus data, glass trade names, or complete dispersion curves. The 15/12.5/10 mm element rims are inferred. Exactly 2 mm realizes the source's approximate diaphragm distance, and its opening follows from f/8. The f/11, f/16, and f/22 settings are additional model choices. The former 25° half-field was chosen below the source's stated coverage; the former 93.441132 mm circle followed that chief ray. Both have been replaced with values derived from the stated 27° half-field.

## Product

No product is documented for this prescription. There is no established basis for assigning a product mount, film format, minimum focus distance, or production glass supplier.

## Corrections

The prescription, diaphragm position, and paraxial image plane remain unchanged. The metadata and public descriptions now distinguish printed data from derived dimensions, identify the first-example drawing correctly, and describe only the infinity state. Detailed field, identity, wording, and glass-label changes appear in the dated entry below.
