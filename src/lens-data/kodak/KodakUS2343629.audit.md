# KODAK 100mm f/3.5 (US 2,343,629 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,343,629 A, _Lens_](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2343629A/en), by Fred E. Altman, assigned to Eastman Kodak Company, Rochester, New York. The application was filed May 11, 1942, serial 442,421, and the patent was published March 7, 1944; no earlier priority is stated on the patent. Example 2 appears as the section in Figure 3 and the prescription in Figure 4 on drawing sheet 1 (PDF page 1), with a rounded duplicate table on specification page 2 (PDF page 4). The field statement is on specification page 1 (PDF page 3), and specification page 3 explicitly names the sodium D line. The patent contains three numerical examples; Figure 7 is a variation of Example 1.

The [Kodak Reflex account](https://medfmt.pages.dev/mf/kodakreflex.html) describes the four air-spaced elements of the Anastigmat Special / Anastar family. The [Science Museum Group's Kodak Reflex II record](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co8206352/kodak-reflex-ii-twin-lens-reflex-camera), object 1990-5036/2865, documents an 80 mm f/3.5 Anastar on the 1948–1954 camera. Neither source identifies this patent as that lens's prescription.

## Transcription Check

The page images agree with all eight surface radii, including their signs and the planar second surface, all seven internal axial intervals, and all four sodium-D index/Abbe pairs. The printed focal length is 100 mm, the aperture is f/3.5, back focus is 80.4 mm and the first-to-last vertex distance is 37.05 mm. No numerical transcription mismatch was found for Example 2. Four air-spaced singlets form four groups; there are no cemented or aspherical surfaces.

Figure 4 gives the rear radii as −65.74 / +42.04 / +176.60 / −43.84 mm. The specification gives −65.7 / +42.0 / +176.6 / −43.8 mm, consistent at its lower precision. The drawing's more precise values are retained at the printed millimetre scale. Example 3 has conflicting refractive indices between its drawing and specification tables; Example 2 is unaffected.

## First-Order Check

The calculated EFL is 100.26 mm, 0.3% above the printed 100 mm. The paraxial aperture is f/3.50, matching the printed f/3.5, with a stop radius of 11.197 mm. At this opening, the entrance-pupil and image-sine f-numbers are both approximately 3.58.

Calculated BFD is 80.61 mm, 0.21 mm longer than the printed 80.4 mm. The image plane is at paraxial infinity focus, 117.66 mm behind the first vertex. Example 1 gives EFL 100.63 mm and BFD 81.90 mm against 100 / 81.2 mm printed; Example 3's drawing prescription gives 99.92 / 93.11 mm against 100 / 93.2 mm printed. These departures do not establish an erratum in Example 2.

The surfaces stay clear of each other, and the iris sets the axial aperture. Chief rays through the 27.0° half-field clear the rims; the corner chief ray reaches an image height of 50.57 mm. Some off-axis marginal rays are clipped, so this field is not uniformly illuminated. The minimum glass-edge thickness is 1.65 mm, the minimum air clearance at the rim endpoints is 0.32 mm and the maximum rim angle is 22.6°. The facing surface sags take up at most 88.5% of an axial air gap. No rim crosses the stop plane.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The patent supplies no diaphragm station, bore diameter, clear semi-diameters, image diameter, close-focus motion or diaphragm setting range. The stop divides the printed 10.15 mm rear air gap into 6.0 mm after surface 6 and 4.15 mm before surface 7, placing it 27.3 mm behind the first vertex. Its radius follows paraxial f/3.5. The final image gap is the calculated paraxial BFD, rather than the printed back focus.

All clear semi-diameters are inferred: surfaces 1–8 use 19.3 / 19.3 / 16.1 / 12.25 / 12.25 / 12.3 / 16 / 16 mm. Figure 3 suggests about 15.6 mm for both outer elements, 13.7 mm for the middle elements' full height and 12.3 mm at the rear bevel's inner end. The front rims exceed the drawing to preserve wide-open illumination; the facing middle rims preserve their air clearance. Surfaces 7 / 8 at 16 / 16 mm agree approximately with the drawing. No numerical clear diameters are printed.

The stated design study extends upwards of 27° from the axis; no separate field is tabulated for Example 2. The model uses 54.0° full field and 27.0° half-field. Its 101.14 mm image circle is twice the real chief-ray height at that angle on the paraxial image plane, rounded upward to two decimals. It is an inferred angular-coverage diameter, not a documented production format or a measure of edge image quality.

`focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. The relative element positions remain fixed. The selectable f-stops through f/22 and `aperture.maxFNumber` of 22 are model choices; neither is a published limit for this exact design.

Elements II and III use E-F5 (Hoya) and PBM22 (Ohara) within the site's spectral-proxy tolerance. Element IV uses J-SK16 (Hikari), which matches the printed sodium-D coordinates at their stated precision. Element I remains unmatched and uses an Abbe-based estimate. All four printed indices and Abbe numbers are retained. The patent gives no C-, F- or g-line indices, partial dispersion or transmission.

## Product Identification

A connection to the Kodak Reflex / Reflex II 80 mm f/3.5 Anastigmat Special / Anastar family is possible because of the four air-spaced elements and the similar aperture. The cited sources do not connect that production lens to this patent or distinguish Example 1 from Example 2. No production product is confirmed for this prescription, so the title retains the patent's 100 mm design scale.

No production mount, image format, diaphragm blade count or shape, marketed focal length or aperture, marketed minimum focus distance, converter compatibility or alternative optical configuration is assigned. The museum's f/3.5–22 range describes its Anastar, whose use of this prescription remains unconfirmed.

## Remaining Questions

- The physical stop station and bore, numerical clear diameters and close-focus behavior remain unknown. The inferred outline preserves illumination and the middle air clearance. Figure 3 shows beveled middle edges without dimensions.
- The historical glass suppliers and melts remain unknown. Elements II and III use spectral proxies, IV has a catalog match at the printed precision and I uses an Abbe-number estimate.
- The cause of the 0.26 mm EFL and 0.21 mm back-focus residuals is unresolved. A rear radius of −43.74 mm would reproduce the stated focal length and back focus, but both tables support −43.84 or −43.8 mm; −43.74 mm remains unsupported and is not used.
- No source confirms a production lens using Example 2, its mount, image format, blade count or shape, marketed specifications, minimum focus distance, diaphragm range, converter compatibility or alternative optical configuration.
- Chief-ray coverage at 27.0° does not establish edge MTF or uniform illumination. No measured wide-field image-quality data are available for this prescription.
