# KODAK 100mm f/3.5 (US 2,343,629 Design) — Audit

## Source

US 2,343,629 A, Fred E. Altman, assigned to Eastman Kodak Company; filed May 11, 1942 and published March 7, 1944. Example 2 is shown in Figures 3–4. The patent has three numerical examples; Figure 7 is a variation of Example 1.

## Transcription

Two independent readings of the page images agree on all eight signed radii, seven internal axial intervals, four sodium-D indices and four Abbe numbers, the 80.4 mm printed back focus, and the 37.05 mm first-to-last vertex distance. No mismatch was found. The model uses the two-decimal rear radii from Figure 4; the specification rounds them to −65.7 / +42.0 / +176.6 / −43.8 mm. Surface 2 is planar. Four separate singlets form four groups, with no cemented or aspherical surfaces.

## First-Order

The printed EFL / BF values are 100 / 80.4 mm; the prescription computes to 100.259568 / 80.607861 mm. The retained paraxial image plane is 0.207861 mm behind the printed back-focus position. Examples 1 and 3 also show small discrepancies: their computed EFL / BF values are 100.63 / 81.90 and 99.92 / 93.11 mm, with printed back foci of 81.2 and 93.2 mm.

The nominal paraxial aperture is f/3.500. At the saved opening, the entrance-pupil and image-sine definitions give approximately f/3.57694 and f/3.58103. The inferred stop radius is 11.196523 mm; an earlier 11.454385 mm estimate was examined separately and was never adopted. Earlier checks found positive glass-edge and air-gap clearance, no stop-plane crossing, and partial marginal-ray vignetting within ±10°. Geometric sodium-D MTF calculations over that smaller field converged, with a maximum final refinement change of approximately 0.000235; those results do not establish performance at 27°.

## Not Printed

Stop station, stop bore, clear semi-diameters, close-focus behavior and the available diaphragm settings are not tabulated. The stop divides the printed 10.15 mm rear air gap as 6.0 + 4.15 mm. Its radius follows the nominal f/3.5 aperture. The stored image gap is the computed paraxial BFD. The infinity-only focus setting and f-stops through f/22 are model choices. The patent does state a field extending upwards of 27° from the axis; the earlier statement that it supplied no numerical field was incorrect.

## Product

The [Kodak Reflex account](https://medfmt.pages.dev/mf/kodakreflex.html) describes a four-element, air-spaced Anastigmat Special / Anastar family. The [Science Museum Group record](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co8206352/kodak-reflex-ii-twin-lens-reflex-camera) documents the Reflex II's 80 mm f/3.5 Anastar. The family relationship is plausible but unconfirmed; available evidence does not distinguish Example 1 from Example 2. Other Altman patents reviewed previously have different prescriptions; no duplicate was identified in that comparison.

## Corrections

The printed prescription has not been changed. A rear radius of −43.74 mm would reproduce the stated EFL and BF, but the page images support −43.84 mm in Figure 4 and −43.8 mm in the specification. The proposed radius remains unconfirmed and was not applied. The example identifier was previously normalized to the string "2"; its display label is now also Arabic, matching the patent.

## Remaining Questions

Open: Figure 3 suggests semi-diameters of about 16 / 16 mm on surfaces 1 / 2, 13.7 mm on surface 3, and 13 mm on surface 6, compared with the retained 20 / 19 / 18 / 14 mm. Surfaces 7 / 8 already use 16 / 16 mm. The large front rims reduce the first element's edge thickness and exaggerate the second element's taper. These observations await a separate semi-diameter review; this pass does not alter them.

Open: The physical stop station and bore, historical glass identities, explanation of the small first-order residual, production association and wide-field image quality remain unresolved.
