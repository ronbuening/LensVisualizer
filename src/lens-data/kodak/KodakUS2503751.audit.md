# KODAK 100mm f/2.5 (US 2,503,751 Design) — Audit

## Source

US 2,503,751 A, Example 2, is repeated in Figure 3 on PDF p. 1 and column 4 on PDF p. 3. The patent was filed September 28, 1948 and published April 11, 1950. Walter Litten, George H. Aklin and Fred E. Altman are the inventors; Eastman Kodak Company is the assignee.

An earlier review used the patent prose and a separate reading of the tables. The page images have now been inspected directly, closing the earlier uncertainty about access to the numerical tables.

## Transcription

Two independent readings of the page images agree on all six signed radii, five internal thicknesses/spaces, three D-line index/Abbe pairs, nominal 100 mm focal length, f/2.5 aperture and ±14° field. The Figure 3 and Example 2 tables agree with each other and with the prescription. No source number was corrected.

## First-order Results

The printed EFL is 100 mm; the computed EFL is 100.036129153 mm (+0.0361%). The paraxial aperture is f/2.500 and the inferred stop radius is 13.620699889 mm. The computed BFD, used for the image plane, is 67.679265130 mm; no BFD is printed. The lens-only track is 52.61 mm and the first-vertex-to-image distance is 120.289265130 mm.

Earlier exact axial tracing found an entrance-pupil-equivalent f/2.560745547 and an image-sine equivalent f/2.555668444. A separate finite-ray estimate of stop radius 13.975482277 mm was considered but not adopted. The saved nominal f/2.5 opening and stop position remain unchanged. Earlier geometric calculations established convergence through 10° at the sodium D line; they do not establish measured, broadband or full-14° MTF performance.

## Values Not Printed

The stop divides the 17.66 mm rear air space into 11.00 and 6.66 mm at z = 40.65 mm. Its position and opening are inferred. Figure 1 depicts Example 1, not Example 2, and supplies no numerical stop position or diameter for this prescription. All six clear radii are inferred, as is the paraxial image-plane distance.

The modeled rims retain positive glass thickness and air clearance. Earlier geometric checks found minimum edge thickness 0.926252279 mm, minimum air clearance 4.515867823 mm, and maximum rim angle 35.5392°. The default air-gap allowance remains sufficient. The chief ray clears at 14°; about 78% meridional transmission there reflects ordinary off-axis vignetting, rather than a measured transmission rating.

## Product

No product is documented for Example 2. The cine-camera and projection uses stated for other examples do not establish a product identity for this prescription.

## Corrections and Source Rounding

The summary table prints R4/−R3 = 0.43 and −P2·F = 2.79; the printed radii give approximately 0.425 and 2.784. These small rounding differences do not justify changing the radii. The other checked relationships are an index difference of 0.045, t1/t3 = 2.58, s1/s2 = 0.38 and overall length/F = 0.526.

## Remaining Questions

Open: The rear element's inferred 20 mm rims leave only about 0.93 mm edge thickness (approximately 0.92 mm in the earlier rounded observation). Review the rims in the later semi-diameter pass; the patent provides no Example 2 section drawing from which to measure them directly.

Open: Exact historical glass identities and partial dispersion are unresolved. The patent identifies Kodak borate crowns for the positive elements and a catalogue dense flint for the negative element, without exact melt data.
