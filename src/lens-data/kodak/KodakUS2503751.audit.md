# KODAK 100mm f/2.5 (US 2,503,751 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,503,751 A, _Photographic Objective of the Cooke Triplet Type_](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2503751A/en), by Walter Litten, George H. Aklin and Fred E. Altman, assigned to Eastman Kodak Company. Priority and filing date: September 28, 1948; publication and grant: April 11, 1950. Example 2 appears in Figure 3 on PDF p. 1 and again in column 4 on PDF p. 3 of the [patent facsimile](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/2503751). The D-line definition is in column 3; the summary ratios and glass discussion are on PDF p. 4, columns 5–6.

The patent contains seven numerical examples. Example 2 prints F = 100 mm, f/2.5 and a ±14.0° field. No product source identifies a production lens using this exact prescription.

## Transcription Check

All six signed radii, three glass thicknesses, two air spaces and three sodium D-line index/Abbe pairs were re-read against the page images. The Figure 3 and Example 2 tables agree with each other and with the optical data. No transcription mismatch was found. Dimensions retain the printed 100 mm scale, without rescaling. The design has three air-spaced spherical elements in three groups and no cemented interfaces.

The summary table prints R4/−R3 = 0.43 and −P2·F = 2.79; the printed radii give approximately 0.425 and 2.784. These small source-rounding differences do not justify altering the radii. The other relationships agree: the positive-to-negative index difference is 0.045, t1/t3 = 2.58, s1/s2 = 0.38 and overall length/F = 0.526.

## First-Order Check

The calculated paraxial EFL is 100.04 mm, about 0.04 mm above the printed 100 mm. The paraxial aperture is f/2.50, matching the printed f/2.5, with a stop radius of 13.62 mm. The calculated BFD is 67.68 mm; no back focus is printed. The image plane is at paraxial infinity focus, 67.68 mm behind surface 6. The first-to-last vertex distance is 52.61 mm and the first-vertex-to-image distance is 120.29 mm.

Exact axial tracing gives entrance-pupil and image-sine aperture estimates of about f/2.56. An opening defined by an exact axial f/2.5 entrance pupil would have a radius of about 13.98 mm; the model retains the 13.62 mm paraxial opening. The iris sets the full aperture and no rim clips the axial beam.

The surfaces stay clear of each other. Minimum edge thickness is approximately 0.93 mm at the rear element, minimum air clearance is 4.52 mm and maximum rim angle is 35.5°. No rim crosses the stop plane. The 14.0° chief ray clears all rims and reaches an image height of 24.89 mm, covering the declared design-field radius. Distortion there is approximately −0.2%. Meridional geometric transmission is about 96.0% at 10.0° and 78.0% at 14.0°; these estimates describe off-axis vignetting, without establishing measured light transmission or an unvignetted edge bundle.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The stop position and diameter are inferred. The stop divides the printed 17.66 mm rear air space into 11.00 mm after surface 4 and 6.66 mm before surface 5, at an axial coordinate of 40.65 mm from the first vertex. Figure 1 illustrates Example 1 and shows the general rear-air-space stop arrangement; it supplies no numerical stop coordinate or diameter for Example 2.

All six clear semi-diameters are inferred: 25/25 mm on the front element, 22/20 mm on the negative element and 20/20 mm on the rear element. There is no Example 2 section drawing from which to measure its rims directly. The rear element's approximately 0.93 mm edge remains thin; its physical rim dimensions are unknown.

The image-plane distance is calculated. The ±14.0° field is printed, while the 49.78 mm image-circle diameter is inferred from twice the real chief-ray image height at that angle. It describes the design field at the 100 mm scale, without establishing a production camera format. The selectable aperture extends from f/2.5 to f/22; the patent gives neither the intermediate stops nor a minimum iris opening.

`focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. The model has no focusing movement or near-focus state; the patent gives no minimum focus distance for Example 2.

The positive elements are identified as Kodak borate crowns and the negative element as a glass listed in manufacturers' catalogues. Exact melt identities, further spectral indices and partial dispersion are not supplied. The two crowns carry the TAC4 (Hoya) catalog curve as a present-day equivalent; the flint uses L-TIM28 (Ohara) as a spectral proxy, retaining the printed D-line index and Abbe number while using the catalog dispersion-curve shape. No anomalous-dispersion glass is identified for Example 2.

## Product Identification

No production lens is documented for Example 2. The patent's cine-camera uses concern Example 1, and its projection use concerns Example 5; neither establishes a product for this prescription. The title therefore retains the patent's 100 mm design scale and identifies the patent.

No mount, production image format, diaphragm blade count or shape, marketed focal length, marketed aperture or marketed minimum focus distance is assigned. No cited source establishes teleconverter compatibility or another optical configuration for this design.
