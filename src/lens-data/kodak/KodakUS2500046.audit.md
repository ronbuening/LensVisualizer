# KODAK 100mm f/2 (US 2,500,046 Design) — Audit

## Source

US 2,500,046 A, Willy Schade, assigned to Eastman Kodak Company; filed January 3, 1948 and published March 7, 1950. Figure 2 gives the sole numerical example, also tabulated on PDF page 3. Claim 3's normalized ranges do not constitute a second example. Figure 1 shows the Petzval section; Figures 3–4 give spherical-aberration and field-curvature calculations.

## Transcription

Two independent readings of the page images agree with all seven radii, four glass thicknesses, two air spaces, the printed back focus, and all four D-line indices and Abbe numbers. R3 is planar, and R2 is the sole cemented interface. No source prescription value requires correction.

The 49.3 mm central air space is represented as 18.9 mm before the stop and 30.4 mm after it. The text on PDF page 3 specifies principal rays crossing the axis 18.9 mm behind the front doublet. This is the patent-stated stop station used for Figure 4; the physical diaphragm is not drawn.

## First-Order Results

The prescription gives EFL 99.983761 mm, paraxial f/2.000, and back focus 56.452649 mm against the printed 100 mm and 56.5 mm. The retained image plane is paraxial. Glass-to-glass track is 81 mm, and total first-vertex-to-image length is 137.452649 mm. Stop radius is 20.019665 mm.

The front doublet has focal length 171.40 mm; the rear pair is approximately 143.5 mm. Their ratio, 1.19, and the curvature sum, approximately 1.19/F, reproduce the patent's design checks. The statements f1 = 1.8F and −R2 = 0.32f1 are rounded descriptions of values near 1.71F and 0.33f1. Figure 3's spherical curve and Figure 4's field curves agree with calculations using the printed prescription and stated principal-ray crossing.

## Values Not Printed

The clear semi-diameters, stop diameter, and stop-down settings are inferred. R1–R7 use 28, 26, 25, 18, 18, 18 and 18 mm. The 0.9 mm rear air space retains 0.316913 mm clearance at the 18 mm rim. Minimum glass clearance over shared apertures is 3.330871 mm; the smallest glass edge thickness is 2.941696 mm. The maximum surface-rim angle is 27.246982°.

The model's real entrance-aperture and image-sine equivalents are approximately f/2.063 and f/2.060. A real f/2 marginal ray would need a stop radius near 20.698298 mm. These results describe the inferred aperture rather than a measured iris bore. No close-focus state, production mount, or camera format is documented.

## Product Evidence

No production lens is documented for this prescription. Kodak's other Petzval projection designs do not establish a product connection to this four-element example. The related Schade US 2,430,587 is design context rather than evidence of a named commercial lens.

## Corrections

The former wording treated the 18.9 mm stop station as wholly assumed and the 5° half-field as the model's limit. The patent states the principal-ray crossing, and Figure 4 plots to 7°. The stored radii, spacings, glass coordinates, rims, stop position, and image plane remain unchanged.

## Remaining Questions

Open: Figure 1 suggests a square-edged front doublet near 24 mm semi-diameter, compared with the modeled 28/26/25 mm front rims. A real f/2 axial beam requires about 25 mm, so a later rim assessment must reconcile the drawing with the ray envelope. The rear drawing is near 17 mm versus the modeled 18 mm. The physical iris diameter, original glass suppliers, and production identity remain unknown.
