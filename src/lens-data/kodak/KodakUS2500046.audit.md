# KODAK 100mm f/2 (US 2,500,046 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,500,046 A, _Petzval-type photographic objective_](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2500046A/en), by Willy Schade, assigned to Eastman Kodak Company, Rochester, New York, a New Jersey corporation. The filing and priority date is January 3, 1948; publication was March 7, 1950. Figure 2 on the drawing sheet, PDF page 1, gives the sole numerical example at a 100 mm focal length. Its table is repeated in column 3 on PDF page 3. Figure 1 shows the lens section; Figures 3–4 give spherical-aberration and field-curvature curves. Claim 3 on PDF page 4 gives approximate normalized values for the same design, not another numerical example. No production source identifies a commercial lens with this prescription.

## Transcription Check

All seven radii, four glass thicknesses, two air spaces, the printed back focus and all four D-line index/Abbe pairs agree with the page images of Figure 2 and the repeated table. Radius signs are explicitly printed. R3 is planar, and R2 is the sole cemented interface. No transcription discrepancy was found; the printed millimetre scale is retained.

The 49.3 mm central air space is divided into 18.9 mm before the stop and 30.4 mm after it. Column 4 on PDF page 3 states that the principal rays used for Figure 4 cross the axis 18.9 mm behind the front doublet. With R3 at 19.5 mm behind the first vertex, this places the stop at 38.4 mm. No physical diaphragm is drawn.

## First-Order Check

The prescription gives EFL 99.98 mm and paraxial f/2.00, compared with the printed 100 mm and f/2. Calculated back focus is 56.45 mm, 0.05 mm shorter than the printed 56.5 mm. The image plane is at the calculated paraxial focus, 56.45 mm behind R7. The glass-to-glass track is 81 mm and the first-vertex-to-image distance is 137.45 mm. The stop radius is 20.02 mm.

The front doublet has focal length 171.40 mm and the rear pair approximately 143.5 mm. Their ratio is 1.19, against the patent's rounded 1.2, and the curvature sum is approximately 1.19/F. R1/f1 is approximately 0.41, against the stated 0.4, and −R4/R7 is 0.43. The statements f1 = 1.8F and −R2 = 0.32f1 describe table values near 1.71F and 0.33f1; these differences are consistent with rounded design descriptions. The tabulated prescription is retained.

Spherical-aberration shifts at full, 0.85, 0.7 and 0.5 pupil are approximately +0.10, −0.15, −0.21 and −0.15 mm, consistent with Figure 3. At 5.0°, 6.0° and 7.0°, sagittal focus shifts are approximately −0.23, −0.33 and −0.44 mm and tangential shifts are −0.07, −0.08 and −0.08 mm, consistent with Figure 4 at the stated stop station.

The surfaces stay clear of each other and the iris sets the full aperture. The axial beam and the 7.0° chief ray clear all rims. The latter reaches an image height of 12.28 mm, approximately the radius of the 24.56 mm image circle. Some off-axis marginal rays remain vignetted.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The clear semi-diameters, physical iris diameter and stop-down range are not printed. R1–R7 use inferred clear radii of 25.8, 25.8, 25.8, 18, 18, 18 and 18 mm. Figure 1 gives a square-edged front doublet near 24.1 mm semi-diameter and rear rims near 17.1 mm. The front drawing is smaller than the 25.0 mm radius needed by the nominal f/2 axial beam. The common 25.8 mm front rim preserves the square outline and limits the loss of edge illumination; the 18 mm rear rims agree with the drawing within 6%.

At the 18 mm rear rim, the 0.9 mm air space retains 0.317 mm clearance. Minimum glass clearance over shared apertures and the smallest glass edge thickness are both 3.331 mm. The maximum surface-rim angle is 27.0°.

The stop position follows the stated principal-ray crossing, while its 20.02 mm radius follows paraxial f/2.00. Real entrance-aperture and image-sine equivalents are both approximately f/2.06; a real f/2 marginal ray would require a stop radius near 20.70 mm. These are calculated aperture properties, with no measured iris bore. Figure 3 labels f/2 and f/2.8; the selectable stops extend to f/22 without a published minimum iris opening.

Figure 4 prints the 7.0° half-field. The 24.56 mm image circle is derived from twice its chief-ray image height at the calculated image plane, not from a camera format. The text's broader 8.0–10.0° design aims do not define a second example or extend the plotted field. The image plane uses the calculated back focus rather than the rounded 56.5 mm table value.

`focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. No near-focus state or focusing movement law is given. The printed sodium-D indices and Abbe numbers are retained. C12, PBM22 and H-LaK12 supply catalog dispersion curves for Elements 1, 3 and 4; Element 2 uses dispersion estimated from its printed pair. No additional spectral indices or anomalous-dispersion data are supplied, so secondary-spectrum, broadband, vignetting and MTF results are not measured production performance.

## Product Identification

No production lens is documented for this four-element prescription. Other Kodak Petzval projection designs do not establish its commercial identity. Schade's US 2,430,587, listed among this patent's references, provides related design context without identifying a product using this example.

The title retains the patent's 100 mm f/2 design scale. No marketed focal length or f-number, marketed minimum focus distance, mount, camera format, diaphragm blade count or blade shape is assigned.
