# Kodak US 2,279,372 A — Example VI audit

## Source

US 2,279,372 A, “Photographic Objective,” by Maximilian J. Herzberger, assigned to Eastman Kodak Company; filed October 3, 1940 and published April 14, 1942. Example VI is on PDF page 3. The page-1 drawing accompanies Example I. The source contains six examples and describes Examples I–IV as preferable for correction quality.

## Transcription

Two independent readings of the page images agree on all six signed radii, the three element thicknesses, both air spaces and all three index/Abbe pairs. The claims specify the D line. No prescription values have been changed. Inserting a stop divides the printed 7.5 mm rear air space into two equal 3.75 mm gaps without changing the element positions.

## First-order

The image plane is the computed paraxial infinity plane behind surface 6. The powered-vertex track is 32.9 mm and total first-vertex-to-image distance is 112.314318 mm. Nominal f/3.5 corresponds to a stop radius of 11.075146727 mm; finite entrance geometry gives approximately f/3.54823. The inferred stop clears the preceding optical rim by about 0.48 mm.

## Not printed

The stop location and size, clear semi-diameters and close-focus behavior are absent from the patent. The model uses a midpoint rear-space stop, estimated rims of 18/18/19/14/18/18 mm, and infinity focus only. The image circle is calculated from the interpreted covering angle rather than a stated film format.

## Product

No manufactured product is documented for Example VI. Similar focal length and aperture alone do not connect this three-element design to Kodak's contemporary Tessar-type f/3.5 lenses.

## Corrections

The printed table remains unchanged. Ordinary rounding has not explained the focal-length discrepancy. A hypothetical R2 of +251.0 mm brings the results closer to the summary, but no source supports that replacement and the page clearly prints +241.0 mm. No source correction is applied.

## Remaining questions

The historical stop, clear apertures and glass identities remain unknown. The patent does not explicitly define its covering angle; its interpretation as a half-angle rests on the behavior of the six examples' tangential fields.
