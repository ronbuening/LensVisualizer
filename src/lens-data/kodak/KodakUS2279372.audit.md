# KODAK 100mm f/3.5 (US 2,279,372 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,279,372 A, “Photographic Objective”](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2279372A/en), by Maximilian J. Herzberger, assigned to Eastman Kodak Company; filed October 3, 1940 and published April 14, 1942. Example VI appears at the upper right of PDF page 3, printed page 2. It specifies focal length 100 mm, f/3.5, back focus 78.5 mm and covering power 26°. The drawing on PDF page 1 accompanies Example I. The patent contains six examples and describes Examples I–IV as preferable for correction quality. The [patent text](https://www.freepatentsonline.com/2279372.html) also reproduces the design discussion and claims.

## Transcription Check

The page images were read again against all six signed radii, three element thicknesses, both air spaces and all three index/Abbe pairs. No disagreement was found. The dimensions retain the printed millimetre scale. The claims explicitly identify the D line, supporting the sodium-D index reference on every element. The three spherical singlets have no cemented interfaces.

The printed 7.5 mm rear air space is divided into two 3.75 mm gaps around the inferred stop without moving either element. The page clearly prints R2 = +241.0 mm. A hypothetical +251.0 mm would bring the focal length and back focus closer to the summary, but no source supports that substitution; +241.0 mm is retained.

## First-Order Check

The calculated EFL is 100.75 mm, 0.8% above the printed 100 mm. Calculated back focus is 79.41 mm, 0.91 mm longer than the printed 78.5 mm. The image plane lies at the paraxial infinity focus, 79.41 mm behind surface 6, with no paraxial defocus. The powered-vertex track is 32.90 mm and the first-vertex-to-image distance is 112.31 mm.

The nominal paraxial f-number is 3.50, agreeing with the printed f/3.5. It requires an inferred stop radius of 11.075 mm; finite entrance-aperture geometry gives approximately f/3.55. The preceding optical rim stays about 0.48 mm ahead of the stop plane. The surfaces stay clear of each other, and the iris sets the axial aperture without a rim or tracing limit.

The 26.0° chief ray reaches an image height of 48.80 mm and clears every rim. This establishes chief-ray coverage; the front element partly vignettes the edge bundle. Examples I–V also show discrepancies in the patent's focal-length normalization. Ordinary rounding has not explained the residual for Example VI.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The patent gives no diaphragm station, diaphragm diameter or clear semi-diameters. The stop is placed halfway through the rear air space and sized from nominal f/3.5. The estimated clear semi-diameters, from front to rear, are 18/18/19/14/18/18 mm. The page-1 drawing gives no numerical rim or stop dimensions for Example VI.

The source leaves full versus half covering angle undefined. The model reads 26° as a half-angle because the stated covering powers across the six examples follow the onset of large tangential field curvature. For Example VI the tangential focus departs by about −1.64 mm at 26.0°, compared with less than 1 mm through 24.0° and a nearly flat field at 13.0°. The retained field is 52.0° full and 26.0° half. Twice the real chief-ray image height gives an inferred diameter of 97.60 mm, rounded upward to 97.61 mm in `catalog.imageCircleMm`. This is derived from the interpreted patent angle, without assigning a film format or promising an unvignetted edge bundle.

`focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. No close-focus distance or focusing movement is printed. The image plane is calculated rather than set to the printed back focus. The selectable f-stops extend from f/3.5 to f/22, but the patent supplies no minimum iris opening.

The patent supplies index/Abbe pairs without trade names, measured spectral-index series or partial dispersions. All three glass labels remain Unmatched. Dispersion calculated from those pairs does not establish historical suppliers, melts or measured chromatic performance.

## Product Identification

No manufactured product is documented for Example VI. Similar focal length and aperture alone do not connect this three-element design to Kodak's contemporary Tessar-type f/3.5 lenses. No marketed specifications, mount, production image format, diaphragm blade count or shape, or marketed minimum focus distance is assigned.

## Remaining Questions

- The cause of the printed-versus-calculated focal-length and back-focus discrepancy remains unknown. No source establishes R2 = +251.0 mm as a correction.
- The patent does not explicitly define its covering angle. Its half-angle interpretation remains inferred.
- Element 2's 19/14 mm rims produce a sloping edge where the general drawing shows similar heights. Their dimensions need further comparison with the drawing, which depicts Example I; neither rim was changed.
- The historical stop location and diameter, clear apertures, glass suppliers and melt identities remain unknown. Neither printed glass pair has an exact catalog match, and no measured spectral-index series or partial dispersion is available.
- No source identifies a production lens or supplies a mount, production image format, blade count or shape, marketed focal length or aperture, minimum iris opening, marketed minimum focus distance or close-focus mechanism. Those metadata remain omitted; f/22 is not a published limit.
