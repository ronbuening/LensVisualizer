# KODAK 100mm f/1.9 (US 2,252,681 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,252,681 A, High Aperture Objective](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2252681A/en), by George H. Aklin, assigned to Eastman Kodak Company. Filed and claiming priority from 17 May 1940; published 19 August 1941. The sole numerical example prints F = 100 mm and f/1.9 on the drawing sheet and specification page 2 (PDF pages 1 and 3). Application serial number 335,782; seven claims.

Kodak's [Kodaks and Brownies 1941](https://www.libraryweb.org/~digitized/tradecats/kodak/Kodaks_and_Brownies_1941.pdf), printed page 4 and the Ektra listing, documents the contemporary seven-element f/1.9 Ektar. Mike Eckman's [Kodak Ektra (1941)](https://mikeeckman.com/2019/07/kodak-ektra-1941/), 23 July 2019, reports seven elements in four groups. Neither source identifies this patent as the production prescription.

## Transcription Check

The radii, thicknesses, air spaces and seven N_D/ν pairs were re-read against both page-image tables. All 11 surface curvatures, including planar R9, the ten thicknesses and spaces, and the cemented interfaces at R4, R7 and R10 agree. The dimensions retain the printed 100 mm scale in millimetres. The signed radii agree with the drawn element shapes and left-to-right light path; no printed prescription value needs correction.

Both tables print R10 = +60.8 mm. The searchable text's 80.8 reading is incorrect; the prose's approximate 0.61F supports the page images. The third component's thickness is 3.7 + 7.9 = 11.6 mm, or 0.116F. Each rear doublet has an index increase of 0.055; the magnitudes of R7 and R10 agree with about 0.73F and 0.61F. The stated condition 1/R9 − 1/R11 > 1/F also holds. Every curved surface is spherical.

## First-Order Check

The calculated EFL is 100.05 mm against the printed 100 mm, a difference of about +0.05 mm. The paraxial f-number is 1.90, matching the printed f/1.9. Calculated back focus is 63.27 mm; none is printed. The image plane lies at this paraxial focus behind R11. The 78.7 mm first-to-last vertex distance places the image 141.97 mm behind the first vertex.

The inferred stop radius is 16.80 mm. A real axial ray at the nominal entrance-pupil radius reaches the stop at about 16.44 mm; real-ray entrance and image-sine aperture equivalents at the modeled iris are both about f/1.86. Longitudinal spherical aberration is about 0.29 mm at the full pupil. The patent supplies no numerical aberration curve for comparison.

The surfaces stay clear of each other. The iris sets the axial aperture without a limiting glass rim, and the full meridional bundle at 5.0° clears every refracting rim.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The text places the diaphragm between the inner menisci, without specifying its exact station or opening; no diaphragm is drawn. The model divides the printed 17.8 mm R5–R6 space into 9.0 mm before the stop and 8.8 mm after it. This places the stop 39.8 mm behind the first vertex, with about 1.30 mm clearance from the front glass rim and 2.06 mm from the rear glass rim. Its opening follows paraxial f/1.9.

All clear semi-diameters are inferred. R1 is 30.08 mm, just above the 30.079 mm height required by the 5.0° marginal bundle. The drawing suggests R1/R2 near 32 mm, the square-cut II/III rim near 26 mm, R7/R8 near 23 mm and the rear doublet near 25 mm. R5's curved rim is near 18 mm; R6's curved clear zone also ends near 18 mm, with a wider outer land reaching about 23 mm. These approximate drawing dimensions do not establish exact clear apertures.

The ±5.0° field is illustrative. The patent gives no numerical field, image format or image-circle diameter, so no image-circle diameter is assigned. The f-stop sequence through f/22 is a display choice; no minimum iris opening is documented. The model is focused at infinity: `focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. The source gives no close-focus distance or focusing movement law.

The tables print capital N_D and ν. Sodium-D indexing at 589.3 nm is retained. No supplier, melt, partial dispersion or additional spectral indices are specified. Dispersion estimated from Abbe numbers does not establish the historical glasses' full spectral behavior; no anomalous-dispersion or measured broadband-performance claim is made.

## Product Identification

The design is possibly related to the Kodak Ektra's 50 mm f/1.9 Ektar, introduced in 1941. Its aperture, seven elements and four groups support that possibility, but no cited source identifies this patent as the production prescription. Aklin refers to several applications filed concurrently, so the date alone cannot distinguish them.

The name retains the patent's 100 mm dimensions. No Ektra mount, image format, blade count, blade shape, marketed focal length, marketed aperture or marketed minimum focus distance is assigned to this design.
