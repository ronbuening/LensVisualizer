# KODAK 100mm f/1.9 (US 2,432,387 Design) — Audit

## Source

US 2,432,387 A, _Lenses for Cinematography_, was filed on 9 February 1946 and published on 9 December 1947. The printed inventor is Philip E. Creighton and the assignee is Eastman Kodak Company. Example III appears in Figure 4 on PDF page 1 and again in column 2 on PDF page 2. Figure 1 shows the common four-element form; Figures 2–4 give three numerical examples. Claim 4 is a rounded expression of Example III in focal-length units.

## Transcription

Two independent readings of the page images agree on every radius and sign, all seven internal distances, the four sodium D-line indices and four Abbe numbers, and the printed BF. The two Example III tables agree. There are four air-spaced singlets in four groups. The D-line definition is explicit below the text table.

Radii in order are +61.0, +576.0, +48.2, +63.5, −193.0, +35.7, +84.5 and −63.0 mm. Element thicknesses are 12.3, 10.6, 3.7 and 23.2 mm, with air spaces 0.7, 9.3 and 13.2 mm. The index / Abbe pairs are 1.588/53.4, 1.611/57.2, 1.720/29.3 and 1.697/56.1. All remain as printed.

## First-Order Properties

The printed f = 100 mm and f/1.9 compare with calculated EFL 100.029572487 mm and paraxial f/1.900. The printed BF is 63.9 mm; calculated paraxial BFD and the retained last gap are 64.003778675 mm. Lens vertex track is 73.0 mm and the image plane is 137.003778675 mm from the first vertex. The printed BF appears to describe best focus, but the patent does not explicitly settle that interpretation.

The inferred stop radius is 17.666752560 mm. An exact nominal f/1.9 marginal ray would require 18.004105755 mm there; the stored stop admits about f/1.935 by entrance-pupil diameter and f/1.936 by image-space sine. The rear-element absolute-radius sum is 2.771 against the printed 2.77.

## Values Not Printed

The stop station is inferred 10.0 mm after R6 and 3.2 mm before R7, or 46.6 mm from the first vertex. All surface semi-diameters are estimated: 31/31/29/26/23/23/27/27 mm. R6 sags about 8.40 mm at its rim, so it does not cross the stop plane. The patent gives neither a physical iris size nor close-focus, mount or film-format data.

Earlier geometry checks found minimum glass thickness 4.670508 mm, minimum air clearance 2.357784 mm and maximum spherical rim angle 40.110043°. Straight glass tapers enclosed admitted ray segments. Earlier iris-radius changes of ±2% and station changes of ±0.5 mm explored sensitivity without changing the printed prescription; an alternative 16.42052 mm iris radius was not adopted. The 0–10° sodium D-line geometric MTF converged numerically, with a largest final refinement change of 0.000274899 and no unaccounted flux. These findings do not establish mechanical dimensions, production performance or the 12° edge MTF.

## Product Association

The design is possibly related to the four-element 25 mm f/1.9 Cine Ektar discussed by G. H. Aklin in [Journal of the SMPTE 69(4), 1960](https://journal.smpte.org/periodicals/Journal%20of%20the%20SMPTE/69/4/20/07268486.pdf). Kodak attribution, layout, aperture and use agree, but no source ties a particular example to production. Aklin's six-element 25 mm f/1.9 is a different design. The patent Design name is retained.

## Corrections

The earlier numerical comparison correctly identified a +0.103779 mm difference between the stored final gap and printed BF. This is retained as an unresolved image-plane interpretation, not treated as a transcription mistake. No radius, glass coordinate, internal distance, stop position, clear semi-diameter or image-plane distance changed in this cleanup.

Earlier prose restricted the model to ±10° and omitted an image circle. The stated patent range permits a ±12° field; the real chief ray clears the current rims there. The previous absence of local page-image evidence is now closed by direct inspection and the two independent readings. Earlier computation and display checks apply to the previous field and glass labels only.

## Remaining Questions

The production connection, historical glass identities, physical iris station and clear apertures remain undocumented. Figure 1 supports the relative rim heights, but it does not establish these estimated semi-diameters numerically. The printed BF's focus convention remains uncertain.
