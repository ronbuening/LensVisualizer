# KODAK 100mm f/1.9 (US 2,432,387 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,432,387 A, _Lenses for Cinematography_](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2432387A/en), names Philip E. Creighton as inventor and Eastman Kodak Company as assignee. The filing and priority date is 9 February 1946; publication and grant followed on 9 December 1947. Example III appears in Figure 4 on PDF page 1 and again in column 2 on PDF page 2. Figure 1 shows the common four-component form; Figures 2–4 give three numerical examples. Claim 4 gives Example III in rounded focal-length units.

G. H. Aklin's [“Two New f/1.9 Lenses for 16mm and Vidicon Cameras,” _Journal of the SMPTE_ 69(4), April 1960, pp. 288–290](https://journal.smpte.org/periodicals/Journal%20of%20the%20SMPTE/69/4/20/07268486.pdf), describes the four-element 25 mm f/1.9 Cine Ektar. It supplies context for a possible production connection, without identifying a particular Creighton example.

## Transcription Check

All eight radii and signs, four glass thicknesses, three air spaces, four sodium D-line indices and four Abbe numbers agree with both Example III tables in the page images. Both tables print BF = 63.9 mm. Figure 4 also prints EF = 100 mm and f/1.9; the text above the numerical tables gives 100 mm for each example, and column 3 states apertures up to f/1.9. The D-line definition is explicit below the text table. No transcription mismatch was found.

Radii in order are +61.0, +576.0, +48.2, +63.5, −193.0, +35.7, +84.5 and −63.0 mm. Glass thicknesses are 12.3, 10.6, 3.7 and 23.2 mm; air spaces are 0.7, 9.3 and 13.2 mm. The index / Abbe pairs are 1.588/53.4, 1.611/57.2, 1.720/29.3 and 1.697/56.1. These values retain the printed 100 mm scale, without rescaling.

There are four air-spaced singlets in four groups, with eight spherical refracting surfaces. The first two elements are positive menisci, the third biconcave and the fourth biconvex. The patent permits a cemented component as a variation, but Example III contains none. Figure 1 supports the relative rim heights: the first element is tallest, the second and fourth similar, and the third shortest. It does not establish numerical clear apertures.

The EFL is about 0.03 mm above the printed 100 mm. The adopted image plane is at paraxial focus, 64.004 mm after R8, or 0.104 mm beyond the printed BF. The printed BF may describe best focus: longitudinal spherical-aberration offsets at relative pupil heights 1, 0.85, 0.7 and 0.5 are about +0.04, −0.46, −0.51 and −0.33 mm. The patent does not explicitly settle its focus convention. The rear-element absolute-radius sum is 2.771, agreeing with the printed 2.77 and the stated 2.7–2.8 range.

The inferred stop radius is 17.667 mm, giving a 35.334 mm opening. An exact nominal f/1.9 axial marginal ray would require an 18.004 mm radius at that station. The adopted opening admits an entrance radius of 25.844 mm, corresponding to about f/1.94 by either entrance-pupil diameter or image-space sine. The paraxial aperture is f/1.90.

The surfaces stay clear of each other, with minimum glass thickness about 4.671 mm, minimum air clearance about 2.358 mm and maximum spherical rim angle 40.1°. The straight glass edges enclose admitted rays. The iris sets the axial aperture without a rim limit. The 12.0° chief ray clears every surface and reaches an image height of about 21.097 mm, consistent with the 42.19 mm image circle; no surface is too small for that chief ray.

The front surface vignettes about 13.0%, 20.0% and 32.0% of one side of the meridional bundle at 10.0°, 12.0° and 15.0°, respectively; the chief rays still clear. These are bundle-width estimates, not area-weighted illumination measurements. At 0–10.0°, the sodium D-line geometric MTF has numerical sensitivity below 0.00028 under refinement, with no unexplained flux loss. This does not establish measured production performance or the 12.0° edge MTF.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

Figure 1 shows no diaphragm. Its inferred station is 10.0 mm after R6 and 3.2 mm before R7, or 46.6 mm from the first vertex. The radius follows paraxial f/1.9; neither that physical opening nor the station is documented. R6's rim sag is about 8.40 mm, leaving the stop beyond the glass. All clear semi-diameters are estimates: 31/31/29/26/23/23/27/27 mm.

The patent states fields up to ±12.0° or ±15.0° without assigning either limit to a particular example. The model adopts the lower limit, ±12.0°, or 24.0° full field. The 42.19 mm image-circle diameter comes from twice the real chief-ray image height at that field and is not a documented film format.

`focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. No close-focus distance, movement law or finite-conjugate state is printed. The selectable f-stops span f/1.9–f/22, but the source gives no minimum iris opening or production stop range.

The first three glass labels remain Unmatched. H-LaK12 (CDGM) is a catalog equivalent for the fourth element, not an identification of Kodak's glass. At sodium D, its calculated index is about 1.69669 and Abbe number about 56.17, giving catalog-minus-printed differences ΔnD ≈ −0.00031 and ΔνD ≈ +0.07. The index rounds to 1.697. The same catalog row's helium d-line values are about 1.69680/56.18, with differences Δnd ≈ −0.00020 and Δνd ≈ +0.08. The authored indices remain sodium D values. No source supplies historical maker or melt identities, measured partial dispersion or an anomalous-dispersion designation. Chromatic and broadband results depend on estimated dispersion for the unmatched glasses.

## Product Identification

The design is possibly related to the four-element 25 mm f/1.9 Cine Ektar. Aklin describes that lens as a lower-priced alternative to the seven-element 25 mm f/1.4 Cine Ektar. Kodak attribution, four air-spaced elements, relative aperture and cinematography use support the association, but no source identifies which example, if any, entered production. Aklin's separate six-element 25 mm f/1.9 design belongs to US 2,682,198 A and is a different prescription.

The title retains the patent's 100 mm design scale. No confirmed product supports a marketed focal length or f-number, mount, film format, diaphragm blade count or roundedness, or marketed minimum focus distance; those fields are omitted.

## Corrections, 2026-11-21

- The inventor-only identity was replaced by the Kodak maker-and-patent identity, with the title “KODAK 100mm f/1.9 (US 2,432,387 Design)” and a corresponding catalog key. The maker changed from unspecified to Kodak, and the lens became publicly listed. The title retains the patent scale because the 25 mm Cine Ektar connection is unconfirmed.
- The patent number gained the publication suffix A. The abbreviated author and assignee became Philip E. Creighton and Eastman Kodak Company. The 1947 year and Example III identification were retained.
- An unresolved source note records the printed 63.9 mm BF against the calculated 64.004 mm paraxial BFD. The image plane remains at paraxial focus; no radius, internal spacing or printed glass coordinate was altered to remove the difference.
- The subtitle is “US 2,432,387 A, Example III — Eastman Kodak / Creighton.” The specifications are “4 ELEMENTS / 4 GROUPS”, “ALL SPHERICAL”, “DESIGN f = 100 mm” and “DESIGN F/1.9”. They identify the source and printed design values without implying a confirmed production lens.
- Element labels became Element 1 through Element 4. Generic singlet descriptions became Positive Meniscus, Positive Meniscus, Biconcave Negative and Biconvex Positive, matching the radii. Optical roles and calculated in-air focal lengths +115.01, +259.28, −41.56 and +55.36 mm were added.
- Unidentified-glass descriptions became explicit Unmatched labels for L1–L3. L4 gained H-LaK12 (CDGM), with sodium D-line differences ΔnD ≈ −0.00031 and ΔνD ≈ +0.07. The catalog name describes an optical equivalent; the glass Kodak used remains unknown. All four printed index / Abbe pairs and their sodium D references were retained.
- The half-field increased from 10.0° to the patent's lower stated limit of 12.0°, giving a 24.0° full field. The 42.19 mm image circle was added from the clear 12.0° chief ray. Stop position, clear semi-diameters and image-plane distance remained unchanged.
- The catalog design focal length changed from the patent's nominal 100 mm to the calculated 100.03 mm. The printed design aperture remains f/1.9; no production specifications were inferred from the possible Cine Ektar connection.

The resulting first-order values are EFL 100.03 mm, paraxial f/1.90, BFD 64.004 mm and stop radius 17.667 mm, with a 12.0° half-field. The image plane is 137.00 mm behind the first vertex.
