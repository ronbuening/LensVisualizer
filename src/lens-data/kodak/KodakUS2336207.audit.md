# KODAK 100mm f/1.5 (US 2,336,207 Design) — Audit

## Source

US 2,336,207 A, _Lens_, George H. Aklin, assigned to Eastman Kodak Company; filed 8 October 1941 and published 7 December 1943. Example 1 appears in Figure 1 and the upper table on specification page 2. Example 2 is a different numerical prescription. The certificate of correction dated 15 February 1944 changes claim wording only.

## Transcription

Two independent readings of the page images agree on all twelve signed radii, eleven internal thicknesses and air separations, eight D-line indices, eight Abbe numbers, the three cemented groups and the printed 48.7 mm back focus. The drawing table and specification table agree. The stop divides the printed 13.9 mm central gap into 9.35 mm and 4.55 mm; that division is inferred.

The original optical model used the calculated paraxial image plane, estimated rear rims of 23/22/22 mm and a ±5° field. Earlier axial and limited-field checks found sound surface geometry and no axial aperture restriction. Their field restriction did not represent the patent's stated coverage.

## First-order Quantities

The patent prints f = 100 mm, f/1.5 and BF = 48.7 mm. The unchanged radii, thicknesses and indices compute to EFL 100.675959 mm, paraxial BFD 49.100584 mm and paraxial f/1.500. The stop radius is 20.114387 mm. The printed image plane is 0.400584 mm ahead of the paraxial plane; total track to that plane is 140.30 mm.

The printed 48.7 mm plane gives about 0.020 mm RMS disagreement with the Figures 2–3 rim-ray curves, against 0.039 mm at the paraxial plane. Alternative individual numerical changes are not better supported than the printed prescription. The source discrepancy remains unresolved.

## Not Printed

The patent does not give the stop position, iris diameter, element diameters, clear semi-diameters, close-focus data, glass suppliers, individual spectral line indices or a production camera format. The stop station is supported by the Figures 2–3 chief-ray zeros, which fit 9.35–9.75 mm after R5. The iris opening is derived from f/1.5. Rear rims follow the Figure 1 section; the remaining rims are estimates.

## Product

No production lens is documented for this prescription. The patent describes sample objectives; neither a product name nor a production prescription is established by its attribution to Kodak.

## Corrections

The prescription's printed radii, internal distances, indices and Abbe numbers are retained. The source's focal-length discrepancy is recorded as unresolved. The image plane uses the printed BF; this restores the source value rather than changing the prescription to fit a focal-length target. The rear clear apertures and angular field follow the patent's figure and text.

## Remaining Questions

Open: the cause of the 100 mm versus 100.675959 mm EFL difference and the 48.7 mm versus 49.100584 mm paraxial back-focus difference is unknown. The non-rear rims still require comparison with Figure 1. Glass identities, partial dispersions, focusing mechanics, barrel dimensions and a production association remain unknown.
