# KODAK 100mm f/1.5 (US 2,336,207 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,336,207 A, _Lens_](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2336207A/en), George H. Aklin, assigned to Eastman Kodak Company. The filing and priority date is 8 October 1941; publication was 7 December 1943. Example 1 appears beneath Figure 1 on the drawing sheet and in the upper table on specification page 2 (PDF page 3). Figures 2 and 3 give its rim-ray aberrations at 18.0° and 21.0°. Example 2 is a different numerical prescription.

The certificate of correction dated 15 February 1944 changes claim wording only, leaving the numerical prescription unchanged. No production source is documented for this design.

## Transcription Check

All twelve signed radii, eleven internal thicknesses and air separations, eight D-line indices, eight Abbe numbers and the printed 48.7 mm back focus agree with the page images. The drawing table and specification table agree. Dimensions retain the printed millimetre scale at nominal f = 100 mm. No transcription error was found.

The eight elements form four air-spaced components: 1 | 2+3 | 4+5+6 | 7+8. The cemented interfaces are R4, R7, R8 and R11. All refracting surfaces are spherical. The stop divides the printed 13.9 mm central gap into 9.35 mm after R5 and 4.55 mm before R6; that division is inferred.

## First-Order Check

The patent prints f = 100 mm, f/1.5 and BF = 48.7 mm. The retained prescription gives EFL 100.68 mm, 0.7% above nominal, paraxial BFD 49.10 mm, 0.40 mm above the printed value, and paraxial f/1.50. The physical stop radius is 20.114 mm.

The image plane is 48.7 mm behind R12, 0.40 mm ahead of the paraxial focal plane. The first-vertex-to-image distance is 140.30 mm. At that plane, the RMS disagreement with the Figures 2–3 rim-ray curves is about 0.020 mm, compared with 0.039 mm at paraxial focus. No single numerical correction is better supported than the printed prescription; the focal-length and back-focus differences remain unexplained.

At the retained stop opening, the real entrance-aperture and image-sine equivalents both round to f/1.51. An axial marginal ray at the nominal entrance-pupil radius would require a 20.289 mm stop radius.

The surfaces stay clear of each other and the iris sets the axial aperture. The stop plane clears the adjacent glass rims by about 1.17 mm and 0.49 mm. The 22.0° chief ray clears every optical rim and reaches image height 40.219 mm, corresponding to an inferred 80.44 mm image-circle diameter. This establishes chief-ray coverage; it does not establish full edge illumination or measured image quality.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The patent gives no stop position, iris diameter, element diameters or clear semi-diameters. The chief-ray zeros in Figures 2 and 3 support a stop 9.35–9.75 mm after R5; the retained position is 9.35 mm after R5. Its axial station is 45.25 mm from the first vertex, between R5 at 35.90 mm and R6 at 49.80 mm. The opening follows paraxial f/1.5.

Rear clear radii of 27.2 mm on R10–R12 follow Figure 1. The remaining clear radii are estimates, so no single origin describes all the rims. The 22.0° half-field follows the patent's stated angular coverage. The image-circle diameter is twice the calculated chief-ray height at that field, rounded upward to 80.44 mm; it is not a printed diameter or a production camera format.

`focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. No close-focus distance or focusing motion is supplied. The selectable f-stops extend from f/1.5 to f/22, but the patent does not establish the minimum iris opening or a production stop range.

All printed glass coordinates and their D-line references are retained, with D taken as sodium at 589.3 nm. The six distinct pairs have no matching catalog row within the printed precision. All eight elements therefore retain Unmatched labels and an Abbe-based dispersion estimate. No glass supplier, code, melt identity, individual C/F/g index, partial dispersion or anomalous-dispersion property is established. No cover glass, filter, baffle dimensions or barrel geometry is supplied.

## Product Identification

No production lens is documented for this prescription. The patent describes sample objectives without naming a product. Its attribution to Eastman Kodak does not establish a production prescription. No marketed focal length or f-number, mount, image format, marketed minimum focus distance, diaphragm blade count or blade shape is assigned.

## Corrections, 2026-11-21

- The key and title were replaced by the maker-and-patent form, with the title KODAK 100mm f/1.5 (US 2,336,207 Design). The maker changed from unspecified to Kodak and the lens became publicly visible. No production product is implied.
- The patent designation gained the kind code A. The example label changed from Example I to Example 1, retaining id 1. George H. Aklin, Eastman Kodak Company and the 1943 publication year were retained. The focal-length and back-focus differences were recorded as unresolved. The blanket estimate designation for the rims was removed because the rear rims follow Figure 1.
- R10/R11/R12 clear radii increased from 23/22/22 mm to 27.2/27.2/27.2 mm, following Figure 1. The 22.0° chief ray meets R12 at radius 22.32 mm, beyond the former 22 mm opening. At 18.0°, the admitted entrance-height span is approximately −39.4 to +1.9 mm, compared with −39.4 to −9.4 mm at the smaller rims and −39.2 to +1.6 mm in Figure 2. The enlarged rear opening agrees with the patent's explanation of reduced oblique vignetting.
- The last air gap changed from the calculated paraxial back focus of 49.10 mm to the printed 48.7 mm, which agrees more closely with the rim-ray curves. The first-vertex-to-image distance changed from 140.70 mm to 140.30 mm. The printed radii, internal separations and glass coordinates were retained.
- The full field increased from 10.0° to 44.0° and the maximum half-field from 5.0° to 22.0°, following the patent's stated coverage. The inferred 80.44 mm image circle was added from the chief-ray height at 22.0°. The design focal-length metadata changed from nominal 100 mm to the calculated EFL of 100.68 mm; the printed f/1.5 remains the design aperture.
- The subtitle is “US 2,336,207 A, Example 1 — Eastman Kodak / Aklin.” The specifications are “8 ELEMENTS / 4 GROUPS”, “ALL SPHERICAL”, “DESIGN f = 100 mm” and “DESIGN F/1.5”.
- Element labels changed from L1–L8 to Element 1–Element 8. Their shapes follow the signed radii: positive meniscus, biconvex positive, biconcave negative, biconcave negative, biconvex positive, positive meniscus, negative meniscus and biconvex positive. Individual in-air focal lengths were added: +140.04, +60.85, −40.42, −40.92, +79.29, +111.79, −110.10 and +52.98 mm.
- Cemented labels G2/G3/G4 changed to C1/C2/C3, with spans covering the front inner doublet, rear inner triplet and rear positive doublet. The four component brackets describe the front positive singlet, front inner doublet, rear inner triplet and rear positive doublet.
- Glass descriptions changed to Unmatched labels with the printed pairs: Elements 1, 6 and 8, 1.755/47.2; Element 2, 1.617/55.0; Element 3, 1.649/33.8; Element 4, 1.558/45.5; Element 5, 1.511/63.5; Element 7, 1.617/38.5. Every label change retained Δn = 0 and Δν = 0 relative to the printed prescription. No catalog glass or anomalous-dispersion tag was assigned.

The resulting EFL is 100.68 mm, paraxial f-number 1.50, paraxial BFD 49.10 mm and stop radius 20.114 mm. These first-order values are unchanged from the original prescription. The stop remains 9.35 mm after R5 and the half-field is 22.0°.
