# Kodak Ektar 100mm f/3.5 (Kodak Medalist)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,279,384
**Filed:** 1941-01-09
**Granted:** 1942-04-14
**Inventor:** Fred E. Altman
**Assignee:** Eastman Kodak Company

The patent contains eleven numerical examples. This model uses Example 10, nominally 100 mm f/3.5. [Kodak Enlarging Ektar 100mm f/4.5](/lens/kodak-enlarging-ektar-100mm-f45) uses Example 6, a distinct prescription for finite-conjugate enlarging from the same patent.

The proposed Medalist association rests on the following evidence and limitations:

1. Kodak's Medalist II brochure identifies a 100 mm f/3.5 lens with five elements and 54° coverage, consistent with the broad specification of Example 10.
2. The patent shows two cemented doublets and a central negative singlet. A product cross-section has been reported with the same arrangement, but the primary retail construction diagram has not been independently compared here.
3. A secondary historical source associates the Medalist with Altman's design. This is not a manufacturer patent inscription and does not uniquely identify Example 10 among the patent's f/3.5 examples or establish exact factory radii.

Example 10 is associated with the Medalist, but its exact correspondence to the factory prescription remains unproven. The patent distinguishes Example 6's intentionally undercorrected longitudinal color for enlarging from its later Examples 7–11. Example 10's 26° half-field, or 52° full field, remains distinct from Kodak's 54° retail statement.

## Optical Architecture

Five spherical glass elements form three air-spaced groups: a positive front cemented doublet, a central negative singlet and a positive rear cemented doublet. Each outer doublet pairs a biconvex positive element with a biconcave negative element.

The radii are [+30.7, −44.6, +727.1, −71.3, +29.9, −751.7, +40.5, −56.7] mm, with successive intervals [8.49, 1.55, 2.86, 1.80, 6.54, 1.59, 6.00] mm. Source dimensions are already millimetres and are used without scaling. Both printed tables give R5 as +29.9 mm; the earlier reading of 20.9 mm was incorrect. The model retains the printed prescription without fitting its dimensions.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 + L2 — Front cemented doublet

L1 — Biconvex Positive: nD = 1.611, V = 57.2. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.611 / V 57.2).

L2 — Biconcave Negative: nD = 1.573, V = 57.5. Glass: BAK1 (Sumita spectral proxy; Kodak melt unconfirmed).

The two elements share the −44.6 mm interface and form cemented pair C1, followed by the air gap to the central singlet. Their similar Abbe numbers do not establish a named glass pair or measured chromatic performance.

### L3 — Biconcave Negative, central singlet

nD = 1.605, V = 38.2. Glass: F5 (Schott spectral proxy; Kodak melt unconfirmed).

L3 lies between the two cemented groups. Its −71.3 mm and +29.9 mm surfaces define the central negative element. The larger air gap behind it contains the inferred iris. Its separate numerical aberration contribution is not established.

### L4 + L5 — Rear cemented doublet

L4 — Biconcave Negative: nD = 1.605, V = 38.2. Glass: F5 (Schott spectral proxy; Kodak melt unconfirmed).

L5 — Biconvex Positive: nD = 1.744, V = 45.7. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.744 / V 45.7).

The pair shares the +40.5 mm interface and forms cemented pair C2 before the image space. L4 has the same published index and Abbe coordinates as L3; this does not establish an identical historical melt.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent's sodium D-line indices and Nu values are approximated as helium d-line indices and conventional Abbe numbers, following Example 6. This leaves the focal-length residual unresolved. L2 uses BAK1 (Sumita); L3/L4 use F5 (Schott) as spectral proxies within the site's proxy tolerance. Catalog curves supply dispersion at the printed indices, without identifying suppliers or exact production glasses, which remain unconfirmed.

L1 and L5 remain Unmatched: no catalog or vendor row meets the site's proxy tolerance. P-SK60 (V = 57.90) and LAF2 (44.90) miss L1's 57.2 and L5's 45.7. L5's coordinates suggest Kodak lanthanum glass; the melt remains unconfirmed. L2 retains the specification's V = 57.5 rather than the drawing's 57.4.

## Focus Mechanism

The prescription represents infinity focus only. The calculated infinity back focal length is 84.173575804 mm; this image interval is not printed in the patent. No near-focus motion or mount/register is modeled, and Example 6's finite-conjugate enlarger state is separate.

The [Medalist II manual](https://www.cameramanuals.org/kodak_pdf/kodak_medalist-ii.pdf), final specifications page, describes a unit-focusing lens with a 3.5 ft (1.0668 m) minimum distance. The whole lens moves for focusing. That minimum distance is recorded as a product specification; the optical model remains at infinity.

## Inferred Stop and Clear Apertures

The source supplies neither a numerical iris coordinate nor clear-aperture dimensions. The model assumes an iris at the midpoint of the 6.54 mm air gap, leaving 3.27 mm on each side. This position is inferred rather than measured from the retail lens section.

The three groups have inferred semi-diameters of 16, 13.5 and 15.5 mm, with common rims on each cemented pair. Figure 4 explicitly represents Example 10, but its local axial and curvature scales are inconsistent, so it supports relative proportions rather than measured apertures. Its group rim-height ratios are approximately 1 : 0.82 : 1, compared with the model's 1 : 0.844 : 0.969; all three groups have square-cut rims without resolved lands. These values use half-millimetre increments and a 0.9 rim/gap limit. The clearance between the central singlet's rear surface and the iris plane is only 0.04883 mm at its full rim. No iris station is marked in Figure 4. This positive geometric clearance does not establish manufacturing tolerance, shutter fit or blade thickness. The rims remain unmeasured against retail hardware.

At f/3.5 the iris radius derived from the f-number is 11.766110125 mm, not a factory measurement. The aperture range extends through f/32, as listed in the Medalist II manual and brochure.

## Model Scope and Field Limits

The prescription computes to an EFL of 101.245322971 mm, versus the patent's nominal 100 mm: a +1.2453% difference (about +1.25%), recorded as the design focal length. Both printed prescription tables agree on the radii and intervals; no single source-supported misprint correction has been found. Example 6 computes to 100.355 mm (+0.36%) on the same normalization. The Example 10 difference remains a source inconsistency, with the printed radii, thicknesses, gaps and glass coordinates retained.

The inferred rims transmit the full axial aperture but clip part of the off-axis bundle. The engine's full-pupil lattice transmits approximately 77.9% at the declared 26° half-field, relative to 100% on axis. This geometric vignetting does not establish retail illumination or measured transmission.

At the declared 26° half-field, the image height is 49.378512726 mm, or 97.779233% of the canonical 6x9 semi-diagonal of 50.5 mm. The declared projection field limits that result; the corner chief ray clears the rims at 26.515684°. Neither the 52° source field nor the 54° retail claim is established as fully illuminated coverage, and the declared source field does not reach the full canonical 6x9 corner. The model does not establish exact factory-example identification or manufacturing performance.
