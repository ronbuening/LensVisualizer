# KODAK 100mm f/6 (US 1,880,394 Design) — Audit

## Source

US 1,880,394 A, _Photographic Objective_, Fred E. Altman, assigned to Eastman Kodak Company. Filed December 11, 1930; published October 4, 1932. The sole numerical example is on PDF p. 2, its section on PDF p. 1 and additional focal lengths and claims on PDF p. 3. The patent's 100-unit dimensions are treated as millimetres in the model.

## Transcription

Two independent readings of the page images agree on all eight radius magnitudes, five glass thicknesses, two air spaces, five D indices, five Abbe numbers and five G′ indices. The radius signs follow the drawing: +, −, −, −, +, −, +, −. R4 and R7 are cemented. There are no aspheres or variable gaps.

The printed G′ indices are 1.62448, 1.62448, 1.56559, 1.53633 and 1.62448 for elements 1–5. They are recorded in the analysis, without treating G′ as lowercase g. The printed individual focal lengths are +40.53, +44.67, −16.66, −35.71 and +21.86; calculated values are +40.53, +44.65, −16.65, −35.72 and +21.87 mm. The maximum difference is 0.018 mm.

## First-Order Properties

The printed focal length 100 gives a calculated D-line EFL of 100.011790567 mm, a difference of +0.012%. The paraxial aperture is f/6.000. The calculated back focus is 88.635299002 mm, matching the stored image plane; no back focus is printed. The optical track is 22.21 mm and the total distance to the image plane is 110.845299002 mm.

The middle and rear doublets calculate to −25.83 and +52.85 mm, against printed −25.82 and +52.85. The printed “front component” focal length −148.12 agrees approximately with the assembly through R5, calculated −148.08 mm; it does not describe the positive front singlet.

## Values Not Printed

The stop's exact station, its bore, all clear semi-diameters and the final image gap are inferred. The patent locates the stop in the 7.54 mm air space between the doublets. Its drawn position supports a 3.11/4.43 mm split; this gives an astigmatic node near 14.0°, whereas the former 4.6/2.94 mm split gives 15.9° against the stated node below 15°.

The field is read as a 15° half-angle because the surrounding discussion explicitly measures astigmatic nodes from the optical axis. The 53.6 mm image circle is twice the real 15° chief-ray image height, not a stated film format. The f/8–f/22 settings are conventional additions. No close-focus data, mount, glass supplier or manufactured aperture dimensions are supplied.

## Product Association

No production lens is documented for this prescription. The three-element f/6.8 design of US 1,880,393 is a different prescription.

## Corrections

The initial text incorrectly described the G′ indices and printed element focal lengths as unavailable. Both are present on PDF p. 2 and are now recorded in the analysis. The inferred stop station and angular coverage are corrected below; the printed prescription and paraxial image plane are retained.

## Remaining Questions

The catalogue does not establish the historical glass identities. The small differences between printed and calculated element/component focal lengths remain; rounding has not been demonstrated as their sole cause. The clear semi-diameters are estimates rather than measurements of the drawn rims.
