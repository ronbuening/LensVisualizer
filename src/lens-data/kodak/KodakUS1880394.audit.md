# KODAK 100mm f/6 (US 1,880,394 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 1,880,394 A, _Photographic Objective_](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/1880394), Fred E. Altman, assigned to Eastman Kodak Company. Filed December 11, 1930; published October 4, 1932. The sole numerical example is on PDF p. 2, its section on PDF p. 1 and additional focal lengths and claims on PDF p. 3. No earlier priority is stated. The patent gives a focal length of 100 units without naming a physical unit; each unit is treated as one millimetre in the model.

## Transcription Check

All eight radius magnitudes, five glass thicknesses, two air spaces, five D-line indices, five Abbe numbers and five G′ indices agree with the patent page images. No numerical transcription mismatch was found. Radius signs follow the drawing: +, −, −, −, +, −, +, −. R4 and R7 are cemented interfaces. There are no aspheres or variable gaps.

The printed G′ indices are 1.62448, 1.62448, 1.56559, 1.53633 and 1.62448 for elements 1–5. G′ is distinct from lowercase g; these values are documented in the analysis without being used as g-line indices. The printed individual focal lengths are +40.53, +44.67, −16.66, −35.71 and +21.86 units; calculated values at the chosen scale are +40.53, +44.65, −16.65, −35.72 and +21.87 mm. The largest difference is 0.018 mm for element 2.

## First-Order Check

The calculated D-line EFL is 100.012 mm against the printed 100 units, a difference of +0.012 mm at the chosen scale. The paraxial aperture is f/6.00, matching the printed f/6. The calculated back focus is 88.64 mm, and the image plane is that distance behind R8; no back focus is printed. The first-to-last surface distance is 22.21 mm and the first-vertex-to-image distance is 110.85 mm.

The middle and rear doublets have calculated focal lengths of −25.83 and +52.85 mm, against printed −25.82 and +52.85. The printed “front component” focal length of −148.12 agrees approximately with the assembly through R5, calculated −148.08 mm; it does not describe the positive front singlet.

The paraxial stop radius is 6.977 mm. An axial real ray at the nominal entrance-pupil radius reaches a stop height of about 7.014 mm. The finite-aperture f-number is within 3% of f/6, and the iris sets the axial aperture without a rim limit. The surfaces stay clear of each other. The 15.0° chief ray reaches an image height of 26.80 mm without blockage, covering the recorded image radius. Front and rear rims remove about 14% of the meridional bundle width on one side at the field edge; this is not a measured transmission percentage.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The exact stop station, its bore, all clear semi-diameters and the final image gap are inferred. The patent puts the diaphragm in the 7.54 mm space between the middle and rear doublets. Its drawn position supports a split of 3.11 mm after R5 and 4.43 mm before R6. The stop is 13.59 mm behind R1, and its opening follows paraxial f/6. This position gives a D-line astigmatic node near 14.0°, consistent with the stated node below 15° from the axis. The earlier 4.60/2.94 mm split gives about 15.9°.

The field is read as a 15.0° half-angle because the surrounding text explicitly measures astigmatic nodes from the optical axis, including the approximately 23.0° node of ordinary aerial objectives. The model therefore covers 30.0° in total. The 53.6 mm image circle is twice the real chief-ray height at 15.0°, derived from this angular field at the chosen scale. It is not a stated film format. The calculated astigmatic fields lie within about 0.11 mm of the image plane over this field.

The estimated clear radii are 10.6 mm at R1/R2, 9.2 mm at R3/R4, 10 mm at R5, 10.4 mm at R6 and 8.6 mm at R7/R8. The axial f/6 beam and 15.0° chief ray clear them. The narrowest shared glass edge is about 0.204 mm in element 2; elements 1 and 5 have edges of about 0.380 and 0.422 mm. Minimum shared air clearance is about 4.176 mm and the steepest rim angle is about 35.9°.

The model is focused at infinity. `focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. The patent gives no close-focus distance or focusing mechanism. The selectable f/8–f/22 settings and maximum f-number of 22 are conventional additions; only f/6 is printed.

The D line is taken as sodium at 589.3 nm. All five elements retain their printed index/Abbe pairs, with estimated dispersion from those pairs. The printed G′ indices do not establish a separate dispersion curve or anomalous dispersion. The patent's claim of zonal spherical-aberration variation below 0.004% of focal length at f/6 is a source performance statement; first-order agreement and geometric MTF do not establish measured or complete spectral performance.

## Product Identification

No production lens is documented for this prescription. US 1,880,393 describes a different three-element f/6.8 design. No cited product source establishes a mount, film format, diaphragm blade count or shape, marketed focal length or aperture, minimum focus distance or production stop range for this exact design.

## Corrections, 2026-11-21

- The lens received the Kodak maker attribution and a maker-and-patent title and key, replacing the inventor-based identity. It is visible in the catalogue. The patent designation gained the A kind code and the inventor's name changed from Fred Altman to Fred E. Altman, following the patent's heading.
- The subtitle is “US 1,880,394 A, sole example — Eastman Kodak / Altman.” The specifications are “5 ELEMENTS / 3 GROUPS”, “ALL SPHERICAL”, “DESIGN f = 100 mm” and “DESIGN F/6”; the millimetre scale follows the stated one-millimetre-per-unit convention.
- Elements 1–5 received plain shape descriptions, optical roles and calculated individual focal lengths. C1 identifies the middle doublet and C2 the rear doublet, with annotations spanning R3–R5 and R6–R8. The three group captions identify their positive, negative and positive powers, matching the patent's components.
- Glass descriptions became explicit Unmatched labels. Elements 1/2/5 retain 1.61088/57.2, element 3 retains 1.54995/45.8 and element 4 retains 1.52301/50.5; ΔnD = 0 and Δν = 0 for every element. The sodium D reference is retained. No catalogue glass is assigned. Nearby Sumita KF5 for element 4 differs by approximately ΔnD +0.000002 and Δν +0.421, outside the printed Abbe precision.
- The stop split changed from 4.60/2.94 to 3.11/4.43 mm, retaining the printed 7.54 mm air space. Its station moved from 15.08 to 13.59 mm behind R1 and its paraxial radius from 7.061 to 6.977 mm. The drawing and the stated astigmatic node below 15° support the new station; adjacent glass clears the stop plane.
- Total field changed from 10.0° to 30.0° and half-field from 5.0° to 15.0°, following the source's angular discussion. The derived 53.6 mm image circle was added from twice the 15.0° chief-ray height. Design focal-length metadata changed from 100 to 100.01 mm to reflect the calculated EFL. Element and group counts are five and three.
- The printed G′ indices remain separate from the g line because the spectral references differ. The printed radii, glass thicknesses, air-space totals, indices, Abbe numbers, estimated rims, scale and paraxial image plane are retained despite the small element/component focal-length differences.

After these corrections, EFL is 100.012 mm, paraxial aperture f/6.00 and BFD 88.64 mm, unchanged from the original prescription. The stop radius is 6.977 mm and the half-field is 15.0°.

## Remaining Questions

- Historical glass suppliers and melts are unidentified; the three distinct index/Abbe pairs have no catalogue match within the printed precision.
- The small differences between printed and calculated element/component focal lengths remain unexplained; rounding has not been established as their sole cause. The meaning of the printed negative “front component” focal length remains uncertain despite its approximate agreement with the assembly through R5.
- The clear radii, diaphragm bore and exact axial station are not dimensioned. Agreement between the estimated rims and the drawing's optical edges is unestablished.
- No exact production product, mount, film format, blade count or shape, marketed specifications, near-focus behaviour or production aperture range is documented.
- The source prints D/G′ indices and Abbe numbers without a full dispersion curve. Measured spectral performance and the stated zonal spherical-aberration limit remain unestablished.
