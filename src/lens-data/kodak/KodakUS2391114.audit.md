# KODAK 100mm f/2.25 (US 2,391,114 Design) — Audit

## Source

US 2,391,114 A, _Rapid Objective_, George H. Aklin, assigned to Eastman Kodak Company. Filed November 30, 1943; published December 18, 1945. Example 2 is the f/2.25 prescription printed in Figure 2 and repeated on PDF page 2. Page 3 defines the refractive index at the D line (taken as sodium, 589.3 nm).

Source: [patent text and images](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2391114A/en), [official facsimile](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/2391114).

## Transcription

Two independent readings of the page images agree with all six signed radii, three center thicknesses, two air spaces, three indices, three Abbe numbers and the printed back focus. The two copies of the Example 2 table agree. Example 1 is a different f/2.5 design and supplies none of this prescription.

The three components are separate spherical singlets: biconvex positive, biconcave negative and biconvex positive. The 10.2 mm rear air space is preserved as 7.2 + 3.0 mm around the inferred stop.

## First-order

The printed focal length is 100 mm and printed back focus is 73.5 mm. Calculation gives EFL 99.920681699852 mm and paraxial back focus 73.493161419651 mm. The first-to-last vertex track is 61.7 mm, and the image plane is 135.193161419651 mm behind the first vertex. The small differences from the printed values are consistent with the table's precision.

The aperture is f/2.25 paraxially. The estimated stop radius of 16.035314033274 mm accepts an axial entrance radius of about 21.543782 mm, corresponding to a real-ray working aperture of f/2.319014. A nominal f/2.25 real marginal ray would instead need a stop radius of about 16.565678 mm. The stored aperture has not been adjusted to remove that difference.

Standalone element focal lengths are +66.01, −35.92 and +54.73 mm. The outer-element focal lengths sum to 1.207 times the stated focal length, within the claimed 1.15–1.30 range; all radii satisfy claim 2.

## Not printed

The stop position, physical iris radius, clear semi-diameters and image-circle diameter are inferred. The model uses the computed paraxial image distance in place of the printed, rounded back focus. No close-focus data, mount, film format, diaphragm-blade geometry or measured multi-line dispersion is supplied.

The retained stop split is 7.2/3.0 mm, at axial position 40.8 mm. Figure 4 supports the rear air space: sagittal shift −0.45 mm versus about −0.48 mm at image height 27 mm. A front-space stop gives tangential +0.2 mm versus about −1.4 mm at 36 mm. The exact rear-space position remains uncertain.

The clear semi-diameters remain 28/28/27/23/25/25 mm. Minimum glass edge thickness is 7.579893 mm; the neighboring air-space minima are 4.644001 and 5.424420 mm. The full rear rim of the middle element clears the stop plane by 0.531804 mm.

The half-field is 15°, within the 15°/18°/24° labels in Figure 4. The real 15° chief-ray image height is 26.742045 mm. The 53.430606349813 mm image-circle diameter is 0.999 times the corresponding chief-ray diameter.

## Product

No manufactured product is documented for this prescription. The architecture is a high-index Kodak triplet; the front positive element's nD = 1.755 is consistent with a lanthanum-type crown, without identifying its composition or melt. Earlier comparison found no matching prescription among the reviewed Aklin designs.

## Corrections

The prescription, stop location, rims and paraxial image plane are retained. Public descriptions now distinguish the printed f/2.25 from the estimated real-ray f/2.319 aperture, and the 15° modeled field from the larger angles marked in Figure 4.

Figure 3 gives an additional comparison: at entrance heights 8/12/16/20/22 mm, the modeled longitudinal spherical-aberration shifts are −0.22/−0.43/−0.55/−0.27/+0.23 mm, versus approximate plotted values −0.29/−0.48/−0.53/−0.31/+0.20 mm.

## Remaining questions

Open: Figure 1 suggests clear semi-diameters about 22.9/22.9/21.2/18.1/22.9/22.9 mm and a chamfer at the middle element's rear. These smaller rims reduce calculated transmission at 15° from about 91% to 70%. The retained larger rims require a separate review; they are not figure measurements.

Open: The exact stop position and iris diameter, historical glass compositions and melts, manufactured-product identity, and performance beyond the modeled 15° half-field remain undocumented.
