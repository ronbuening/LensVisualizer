# KODAK 100mm f/2.25 (US 2,391,114 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,391,114 A, _Rapid Objective_](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2391114A/en), George H. Aklin, assigned to Eastman Kodak Company. Filing and priority date: November 30, 1943; publication: December 18, 1945. Example 2 is the 100 mm f/2.25 prescription printed in Figure 2 on PDF page 1 and repeated on PDF page 2. PDF page 3 defines the index at the D line, taken as sodium at 589.3 nm. The [official facsimile](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/2391114) supplies the page images.

[HOYA optical-glass data](https://www.hoya-opticalworld.com/english/datadownload/index.html), ZEMAX catalog including discontinued glasses, July 7, 2026, supplies the TAC4 curve and its listed d-line coordinates, 1.73400/51.05. No product source identifies a manufactured lens using this prescription.

## Transcription Check

Both Example 2 tables were re-read against the page images. All six signed radii, three center thicknesses, two air spaces, three refractive indices, three Abbe numbers and the printed back focus agree; no transcription disagreement was found. The dimensions retain the printed 100 mm scale. Example 1 is a separate f/2.5 prescription.

The section contains three air-spaced spherical singlets: biconvex positive, biconcave negative and biconvex positive. The printed 10.2 mm rear air space is preserved as 7.2 + 3.0 mm around the inferred stop. There are no cemented interfaces, aspherical surfaces or variable spacings.

## First-Order Check

The printed focal length is 100 mm; calculation gives 99.92 mm, a difference of −0.08 mm. Printed back focus is 73.5 mm; the paraxial result is 73.493 mm, a difference of −0.007 mm. The first-to-last vertex distance is 61.7 mm. The image plane lies 73.493 mm behind the last vertex, or 135.19 mm behind the first vertex. These small differences are consistent with the table's precision.

The paraxial aperture is f/2.25, matching the printed aperture. The estimated stop radius of 16.04 mm accepts an exact axial entrance radius of about 21.54 mm, corresponding to a working aperture of f/2.32. A real marginal ray at nominal f/2.25 would need a stop radius of about 16.57 mm. The iris remains at the paraxial size.

Standalone element focal lengths are +66.01, −35.92 and +54.73 mm. The positive-element focal lengths sum to 120.74 mm, or 1.207 times the stated focal length, within the claimed 1.15–1.30 range. All six radius magnitudes satisfy claim 2.

Figure 3 compares spherical aberration and the sine condition with a prior-art triplet. At entrance heights 8/12/16/20/22 mm, calculated longitudinal spherical-aberration shifts are −0.22/−0.43/−0.55/−0.27/+0.23 mm, versus approximate plotted values −0.29/−0.48/−0.53/−0.31/+0.20 mm. The general curve shape agrees with the drawing.

The surfaces stay clear of each other, and the iris sets the full aperture without axial rim clipping. Minimum glass edge thickness is 7.58 mm; the two air-space minima are 4.64 and 5.42 mm. The rear rim of the middle element clears the stop plane by 0.532 mm. No surface is too small for the inferred image circle, and the corner chief ray reaches its edge at the modeled 15.0° half-field.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The stop position, physical iris radius, clear semi-diameters and image-circle diameter are inferred. The image plane uses the calculated paraxial back focus in place of the printed rounded value.

The stop lies 7.2 mm into the 10.2 mm rear air space, 40.8 mm behind the first vertex. Figure 4 supports this air space: at image height 27 mm, calculated sagittal focus shift is −0.45 mm versus about −0.48 mm in the drawing. A front-space stop gives tangential shift +0.2 mm at 36 mm image height, versus about −1.4 mm in the patent. The exact rear-space position is not established.

Clear semi-diameters remain 28/28/27/23/25/25 mm. Figure 1 suggests approximately 22.9/22.9/21.2/18.1/22.9/22.9 mm, including a chamfer behind the middle element. Its five axial spacings agree with Example 2 within about 3%. The larger retained rims are estimates, rather than measurements of the drawing. Using the smaller figure readings reduces calculated transmission at 15.0° from about 91% to 70%.

Figure 4 marks 15.0°, 18.0° and 24.0°. The retained half-field is 15.0°; the larger markers do not establish usable coverage. The exact chief-ray image height at 15.0° is 26.74 mm. The inferred 53.44 mm image circle comes from that field, retaining about a 0.1% margin inside the chief-ray diameter. It is not a published film format or product dimension.

`focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. No close-focus distance, movement or focusing law is printed. All groups retain fixed relative spacings. The selectable f-stops run from f/2.25 to f/22; the source gives no minimum iris opening.

The patent supplies no glass names, individual C-, F- or g-line indices, measured partial dispersion or historical melt data. L1 and L2 use Abbe-based dispersion estimates. L3 uses the TAC4 catalog curve while retaining the printed D-line index and Abbe number. The catalog name does not establish Kodak's supplier or glass composition.

## Product Identification

No manufactured product is documented for Example 2. The high-index Kodak triplet's front positive element, nD = 1.755, is consistent with a lanthanum-type crown, without establishing composition or melt. Aklin's other known prescriptions differ from this one. No mount, production image format, diaphragm blade count or shape, marketed focal length, marketed aperture or marketed minimum focus distance is assigned.

## Corrections, 2026-11-21

- The catalog key and title were replaced by the maker-and-patent form. The title is “KODAK 100mm f/2.25 (US 2,391,114 Design)”; maker changed from unassigned to Kodak, and the lens became visible in the catalog. These identify the patent attribution and printed design scale without claiming a manufactured product.
- The patent number gained its A suffix, and the example label changed from Example II to Example 2 to follow the printed Arabic numeral. Publication year, inventor and assignee were confirmed and retained. The D-line description now says it is taken as sodium at 589.3 nm; the patent itself says only D line. The index reference and all printed glass values remain unchanged.
- The subtitle became “US 2,391,114 A, Example 2 — Eastman Kodak / Aklin” to name the patent, example, assignee and inventor. The specifications became “3 ELEMENTS / 3 GROUPS”, “ALL SPHERICAL”, “DESIGN f = 100 mm” and “DESIGN F/2.25”, distinguishing the printed scale from the computed EFL. The focus description states infinity focus only, and the descriptions distinguish paraxial f/2.25 from the estimated exact-ray f/2.32 aperture and the modeled field from the larger angles marked in Figure 4. Element and group counts remain three each.
- `catalog.focalLengthMm.design` changed from 100 to 99.92 mm to state the computed EFL. The design aperture remains the printed f/2.25.
- Repeated element labels became Element 1, Element 2 and Element 3. Generic shape descriptions became Biconvex Positive, Biconcave Negative and Biconvex Positive to match the radius signs. Optical roles and standalone focal lengths +66.01/−35.92/+54.73 mm were added. Group captions identify the front positive, central negative and rear positive groups.
- L1 and L2 are labelled with their printed coordinates, `Unmatched (nD 1.755 / ν 47.2)` and `Unmatched (nD 1.720 / ν 29.3)`, with Δn = 0 and Δν = 0. L3 is labelled TAC4 (Hoya): its listed d-line pair is 1.73400/51.05, and its curve evaluated at D gives nD = 1.73387 and νD = 51.05. Catalog minus printed differences are ΔnD = −0.00013 and ΔνD = −0.05, inside the printed precision. The name supplies the dispersion curve without changing the printed pair. No anomalous-dispersion designation was added.
- The stop position, iris size, clear radii, 15.0° half-field and paraxial image plane are unchanged.

After these corrections the model's EFL is 99.92 mm, paraxial f/2.25, BFD 73.493 mm, stop radius 16.04 mm and half-field 15.0°. Radii, thicknesses, air spaces, printed indices and Abbe numbers are unchanged.

## Remaining Questions

- The retained clear radii exceed the Figure 1 readings. The smaller rims and middle-element chamfer would reduce calculated 15.0° transmission from about 91% to 70%; the patent does not establish Example 2's true clear apertures.
- The exact stop position and iris diameter are not documented. Exact-ray working aperture remains about f/2.32 against printed f/2.25.
- L1 and L2 have no assigned catalog curves. The historical compositions, suppliers and melts of all three glasses remain unidentified; TAC4 establishes only an optical equivalent for L3.
- No source establishes a manufactured product, mount, production format, diaphragm blade count or shape, minimum iris opening, marketed specifications, or close-focus mechanism; the f/22 end of the model's stop range is not a published limit.
- Usable performance beyond the modeled 15.0° half-field is not established by the larger angles in Figure 4.
