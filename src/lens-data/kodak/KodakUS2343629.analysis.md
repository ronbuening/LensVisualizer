# KODAK 100mm f/3.5 (US 2,343,629 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,343,629 A

**Inventors:** Fred E. Altman

**Assignee:** Eastman Kodak Company

**Filed:** May 11, 1942

**Published:** March 7, 1944

**Example details:** Example 2; Figure 3 section and Figure 4 prescription table, with a second table in the specification.

The patent gives three numerical examples of a four-element photographic objective. Example 2 is published at a nominal focal length of 100 mm and f/3.5. Its Figure 4 table supplies the more precise rear radii; the specification rounds these to −65.7, +42.0, +176.6 and −43.8 mm. The drawing and tables agree at their respective precision.

The prescription may belong to the family of 80 mm f/3.5 Kodak Anastigmat Special and Anastar lenses fitted to the Kodak Reflex and Reflex II. An account of the Reflex describes four air-spaced elements, and the Science Museum Group records a Reflex II with an 80 mm f/3.5 Anastar. These facts support a possible family relationship, but neither source ties a production lens to this patent or distinguishes Example 1 from Example 2. The design name therefore retains the patent identification. [Reflex account](https://medfmt.pages.dev/mf/kodakreflex.html); [Science Museum Group, Kodak Reflex II](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co8206352/kodak-reflex-ii-twin-lens-reflex-camera).

## Optical Architecture

Four air-spaced singlets form four groups, with positive–positive–negative–positive power in front-to-rear order. The layout resembles a triplet with an additional weak positive meniscus behind the front collector. Element 1 has a planar rear optical surface; that surface is separate from the aperture stop. All curved surfaces are spherical, and there are no cemented interfaces.

The first-to-last vertex distance is 37.05 mm. The stop is inferred in the 10.15 mm air space between Elements 3 and 4, 6.0 mm behind surface 6 and 4.15 mm ahead of surface 7. Its axial position is 27.3 mm from the first vertex. The patent supplies neither a diaphragm station nor a bore diameter.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### Element 1 — Plano-Convex Positive

nD = 1.611, νD = 57.2. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.611 / ν 57.2). Standalone focal length: +82.32 mm.

The front collector has a +50.3 mm front radius, a planar rear surface and a 5.5 mm center thickness. It supplies substantial positive power before the two more dispersive middle elements.

### Element 2 — Positive Meniscus

nD = 1.605, νD = 38.0. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.605 / ν 38.0). Standalone focal length: +438.48 mm.

The +50.0 / +57.2 mm surfaces enclose a 9.5 mm thick meniscus. Its net power is weakly positive, although its lower Abbe number gives it greater dispersion than either outer element. It lies 0.5 mm behind Element 1 and 2.8 mm ahead of Element 3.

### Element 3 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.649, νD = 33.8. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.649 / ν 33.8). Standalone focal length: −39.08 mm.

The −65.74 / +42.04 mm surfaces and 3.0 mm center thickness form the sole negative element. This is the most dispersive glass in the prescription. Its negative power opposes the positive outer elements and provides the principal negative contribution to chromatic balance.

### Element 4 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.620, νD = 60.2. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.620 / ν 60.2). Standalone focal length: +57.20 mm.

The rear element has radii +176.6 / −43.84 mm and a 5.6 mm center thickness. It supplies the strongest positive standalone power and brings the beam emerging from the stop toward the image plane.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent explicitly specifies the sodium D line, represented at 589.2938 nm. All four printed indices and Abbe numbers are retained. The outer glasses have higher Abbe numbers than the middle glasses; this supports the design's chromatic balancing strategy, but does not establish a glass supplier or melt.

No catalog curve meets the adopted index and Abbe tolerances for these pairs, so every glass remains unmatched. No C-, F- or g-line indices or partial-dispersion values are published. Dispersion estimated from an Abbe number does not establish the historical spectral curve, broadband MTF or anomalous-dispersion performance.

## Focus Mechanism and Aperture

The model is focused at infinity. The patent gives no close-focus data; no moving groups, changing gaps or production minimum focus distance are specified.

The inferred stop radius is 11.197 mm, derived from the nominal paraxial f/3.5 aperture. Real-ray definitions differ slightly: the opening corresponds to entrance-pupil and image-sine f-numbers of about 3.58. The f-stop sequence through f/22 is a model choice rather than a printed diaphragm specification.

## First-Order Values and Image Plane

The printed focal length and back focus are 100 and 80.4 mm. The transcribed prescription gives EFL 100.26 mm and paraxial BFD 80.61 mm. The model retains that paraxial image plane, at 117.66 mm from the first vertex, 0.21 mm behind the printed back-focus position.

The other examples show similarly loose agreement: Example 1 computes to EFL 100.63 mm and BF 81.90 mm against a printed BF of 81.2 mm; Example 3 computes to 99.92 / 93.11 mm against 100 / 93.2 mm. A trial rear radius of −43.74 mm would reconcile Example 2's stated focal length and back focus, but the two tables print −43.84 and −43.8 mm. That possibility remains unconfirmed, and the Figure 4 value is retained.

## Field and Clear Apertures

The specification describes a design study extending upwards of 27° from the axis. The model therefore uses a 54.0° full field and a 27.0° half-field. At that angle the real chief ray reaches an image height of 50.57 mm on the retained paraxial plane, giving an image-circle diameter of 101.14 mm rounded upward. This diameter describes the stated angular field in this model; it does not establish a production format or measured edge quality.

Chief rays through the stated field clear the inferred rims, and the axial beam is unobstructed. Some off-axis marginal rays are clipped, so chief-ray coverage does not imply uniform illumination. The estimated clear semi-diameters follow Figure 3 where illumination and air clearance allow. Element 1 is square-cut at 19.3 mm on both faces, and surface 3 reaches 16.1 mm; these front rims exceed the drawing to preserve wide-open light across the field. Surfaces 4 and 5 remain at 12.25 mm to preserve their narrow air gap. Surface 6 reaches 12.3 mm at the inner end of the drawn bevel, while the rear element is square-cut at 16 mm, close to the drawing's 15.6 mm. The full height of the middle elements' drawn edges is not reproduced. Wide-open geometric transmission is 100% on axis and 63.8% at 27°. No rim crosses the stop plane.

The smallest glass-edge thickness is 1.65 mm and the smallest air clearance at the rim endpoints is 0.32 mm. The largest rim angle is 22.6°, and the facing surface sags take up at most 88.5% of an axial air gap. These clearances establish consistency of the inferred outlines rather than dimensions of a surviving lens.
