## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP 2017-116646 A<br>
**Application Number:** 特願2015-249745<br>
**Filed:** 2015-12-22<br>
**Published:** 2017-06-29<br>
**Inventor:** Dayong Li<br>
**Applicant:** Tamron Co., Ltd.<br>
**Title:** 広角ズームレンズ及び撮像装置 (Wide-angle zoom lens and imaging apparatus)<br>
**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 1

The modeled prescription is Example 1 of JP 2017-116646 A. The patent describes a five-group high-ratio zoom whose group-power sequence is positive / negative / positive / negative / positive, with the aperture stop immediately ahead of the third group, the second group used for focusing, and the fourth group movable transversely for image stabilization (¶0056–¶0059). Tables 1–3 provide the prescription, aspherical coefficients, and three infinity-focus zoom states used by the data file.

The production correlation used here is the TAMRON 18-400mm f/3.5-6.3 Di II VC HLD, Model B028. It is a fixed correlation for this model, but it is not a manufacturer statement that Example 1 is B028. The identification instead rests on convergent evidence:

1. Example 1 contains 16 physical lenses arranged as 11 air-separated optical units, matching Tamron's published 16-element / 11-group construction for B028.
2. The patent's three design control points are 18.5105, 85.0242, and 389.00 mm; Tamron markets B028 as an 18–400 mm zoom. The modeled paraxial endpoint EFLs are 18.5104686 and 388.6624094 mm, so the marketing range remains separate from the exact design values.
3. The patent places the focusing function in G2 and transverse stabilization in G4 (¶0059), while the production lens is an HLD autofocus lens with VC.
4. The patent application predates the production lens and was published on 2017-06-29; Tamron gives a B028 release date of 2017-07-20 for both Canon EF and Nikon F versions.
5. Tamron identifies the production lens as APS-C, Canon EF / Nikon F, with a 0.45 m minimum object distance and 1:2.9 maximum magnification. Those product facts are used only for identity and the explicitly disclosed close-focus reconstruction, not to overwrite the patent prescription.

The patent values are d-line data at 587.6 nm. No uniform scale factor is applied to Example 1.

## Optical Architecture

The design contains **16 physical lenses in 11 air-separated optical units**, organized into **five moving zoom groups**. The TypeScript model has 17 element entries because the 0.1600 mm medium at the first G2 lens is represented separately from its substrate; this does not change the physical element count.

The five zoom groups have independently recomputed d-line paraxial focal lengths:

| Zoom group | Power | EFL (mm) | Principal function in the modeled architecture |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| G1 | Positive | +128.273913 | Large-diameter front collector and positive front group |
| G2 | Negative | -14.544146 | Strong negative variator and sole focusing group |
| G3 | Positive | +22.991451 | Positive relay group immediately behind the moving stop |
| G4 | Negative | -32.927868 | Cemented transverse VC group |
| G5 | Positive | +65.743340 | Rear positive group and final imaging relay |

These are group powers, not standalone element powers. Cemented-unit powers and individual-element powers differ and are treated separately below.

The aperture stop is located immediately before G3 and moves axially with G3, as stated by the patent (¶0059). The patent publishes the stop position but not its clear radius. The data file therefore uses an inferred physical stop semi-diameter of 9.389093 mm. With the final prescription, that common stop produces modeled f-numbers of 3.405871 at the wide control point, 5.605121 at the middle point, and 6.482832 at the telephoto point. These values reproduce the patent's rounded f/3.4, f/5.6, and f/6.5 statements (¶0065); they are intentionally distinct from the marketed f/3.5–6.3 range.

From wide to telephoto, all five group fronts move toward object space when the image plane is held fixed. G3 and the stop have identical axial motion. The G1–G2 spacing expands strongly, the G2–G3 spacing contracts overall, and the rear inter-group spacings change to maintain focus while the system lengthens. The D(22) gap is non-monotonic across the three control points, but none of the group fronts reverses direction.

The system changes first-order character across the zoom range. At the wide state, the computed back focal distance exceeds the EFL (`BFD/EFL ≈ 2.148`), so that state meets the project's retrofocus criterion. At the telephoto state, the front-to-image track is shorter than the EFL (`TL/EFL ≈ 0.663`), so that state meets the project's telephoto criterion. The middle state meets neither criterion. These labels are therefore state-specific rather than names for the entire zoom.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### G1 — Front positive group

#### D1: L1 + L2 cemented pair

**L1:** `nd = 1.91082`, `νd = 35.25`. Glass: `911353 coordinate class`. Standalone `f = -176.381 mm`.<br>
**L2:** `nd = 1.49700`, `νd = 81.61`. Glass: `497816 coordinate class`. Standalone `f = +137.915 mm`.<br>
**Cemented pair:** net `f = +627.949 mm`.

L1 is a negative meniscus followed directly by the positive L2. The large index and dispersion contrast allows substantial refractive action at the shared interface without requiring the pair itself to carry much net positive power. The pair's computed net focal length is far longer than either component's standalone focal length, so its role must not be confused with the stronger positive power of G1 as a whole.

Within G1, this weakly positive cemented pair precedes L3 and provides the front section with a negative/positive internal power split. That arrangement gives the front group more degrees of freedom than a single strong positive lens while retaining a net positive group power.

#### L3: positive meniscus

**L3:** `nd = 1.55032`, `νd = 75.49`. Glass: `550755 coordinate class`. Standalone `f = +160.343 mm`.

L3 is the rear positive element of G1. Its relatively high Abbe number contrasts with the high-index negative component at the front of the group. Because D1 is only weakly positive as a cemented unit, L3 contributes materially to the positive sign of G1 while also providing an air-spaced rear boundary before the strongly negative G2.

### G2 — Negative variator and focusing group

#### L4: composite negative element, modeled as L4a + L4b

**L4a thin layer:** `nd = 1.51460`, `νd = 49.96`. Glass: `Unmatched (thin aspheric layer; patent nd=1.51460, vd=49.96)`. Standalone `f = -947.959 mm`.<br>
**L4b substrate:** `nd = 1.91082`, `νd = 35.25`. Glass: `911353 coordinate class`. Standalone `f = -23.123 mm`.<br>
**Composite physical lens:** net `f = -22.569 mm`.

The patent inserts a 0.1600 mm medium at source surface 6 ahead of the high-index substrate. The data model retains both media because they are optically distinct, but they represent one physical G2 lens for the 16-element count. The patent does not identify the thin medium as resin or provide a manufacturing material name, so the analysis does not assign one.

The layer itself has very weak paraxial power; the high-index substrate supplies nearly all of the composite lens's strong negative power. The object-side surface of the thin layer is aspherical (`6A`), giving the first element of the variator a non-spherical correction surface at a location where ray heights change substantially with zoom.

#### D2: L5 + L6 cemented pair

**L5:** `nd = 1.61800`, `νd = 63.39`. Glass: `618634 coordinate class`. Standalone `f = -22.167 mm`.<br>
**L6:** `nd = 1.84666`, `νd = 23.78`. Glass: `847238 coordinate class`. Standalone `f = +18.851 mm`.<br>
**Cemented pair:** net `f = +104.936 mm`.

L5 is biconcave and strongly negative as a standalone lens; L6 is strongly positive. Their cemented combination is only moderately positive. Within the negative G2 group, this positive cemented subunit is flanked by the strong negative L4 composite and the rear negative L7, allowing G2 to remain strongly negative while distributing power across several interfaces.

The large `νd` contrast between L5 and L6 is consistent with a chromatic-balancing role, but the prescription contains no element-specific `nC`, `nF`, `ng`, or `dPgF` values. The model therefore does not assert anomalous partial dispersion or apochromatic correction for this pair.

#### L7: rear negative meniscus

**L7:** `nd = 1.88100`, `νd = 40.14`. Glass: `881401 coordinate class`. Standalone `f = -38.695 mm`.

L7 closes G2 with additional negative power. Together with the L4 composite it ensures that the positive D2 subunit does not change the sign of the group. Because G2 is the sole focusing group, L7's rear surface also forms one boundary of the focus-sensitive air space leading to the stop and G3.

### G3 — Positive relay group behind the stop

#### L8: front biconvex positive

**L8:** `nd = 1.51680`, `νd = 64.20`. Glass: `517642 coordinate class`. Standalone `f = +61.243 mm`.

L8 is the first powered surface immediately behind the moving stop. Its moderate positive power begins the strong positive G3 relay. The stop-to-L8 spacing is fixed at 1.2000 mm within the G3/stop assembly, so the stop and this lens maintain their relative axial geometry throughout zooming.

#### L9: second biconvex positive

**L9:** `nd = 1.49700`, `νd = 81.61`. Glass: `497816 coordinate class`. Standalone `f = +55.873 mm`.

L9 is another positive, very high-Abbe element within G3. It follows L8 across a narrow air space and contributes to the group's positive power while keeping a low-dispersion material in a high-convergence region of the optical system.

#### D3: L10 + L11 cemented pair

**L10:** `nd = 1.48749`, `νd = 70.44`. Glass: `487704 coordinate class`. Standalone `f = +35.431 mm`.<br>
**L11:** `nd = 1.84666`, `νd = 23.78`. Glass: `847238 coordinate class`. Standalone `f = -51.621 mm`.<br>
**Cemented pair:** net `f = +102.401 mm`.

The rear G3 cemented pair combines a strong positive L10 with a negative, high-index L11. Its net power remains positive, but much weaker than L10 alone. This gives the rear portion of G3 a positive contribution while providing a strong refractive-index and dispersion transition at the cemented interface.

G3 as a complete group has `f = +22.991451 mm`, substantially stronger than any one of its cemented subunits. The group value is an in-situ combination of L8, L9, D3, and the intervening separations, not a substitute for the standalone values listed above.

### G4 — Negative VC group

#### D4: L12 + L13 cemented pair

**L12:** `nd = 1.85135`, `νd = 40.10`. Glass: `851401 coordinate class`. Standalone `f = -17.314 mm`.<br>
**L13:** `nd = 1.80809`, `νd = 22.76`. Glass: `808228 coordinate class`. Standalone `f = +35.436 mm`.<br>
**Cemented pair / G4:** net `f = -32.928 mm`.

G4 consists entirely of this cemented pair. L12 is a biconcave negative element with an aspherical object-side surface (`23A`); L13 is a positive meniscus. The net group remains negative. The patent identifies G4 as the lens group that may move perpendicular to the optical axis to compensate image displacement from camera shake (¶0059).

Because the stabilizing group is compact and cemented, its internal spacing is invariant in the centered model. The data file models the on-axis nominal state only; the patent does not provide a quantitative transverse decenter range for a displaced VC state.

### G5 — Rear positive group

#### L14: biconvex positive with two aspherical surfaces

**L14:** `nd = 1.59201`, `νd = 67.02`. Glass: `592670 coordinate class`. Standalone `f = +37.480 mm`.

L14 supplies strong positive power at the front of G5. Both optical surfaces are aspherical (`26A` and `27A`), making this the only physical lens in the prescription with two authored aspherical faces. The pair of non-spherical surfaces provides correction freedom on both entry and exit from the element without introducing another air-spaced optical unit.

#### D5: L15 + L16 rear cemented pair

**L15:** `nd = 1.91082`, `νd = 35.25`. Glass: `911353 coordinate class`. Standalone `f = -17.670 mm`.<br>
**L16:** `nd = 1.60342`, `νd = 38.01`. Glass: `603380 coordinate class`. Standalone `f = +23.036 mm`.<br>
**Cemented pair:** net `f = -92.582 mm`.

The final cemented pair is negative as a unit even though L16 is positive. It follows the much stronger positive L14, so G5 remains positive overall at `f = +65.743340 mm`. This is a useful example of why the sign of a cemented subunit cannot be inferred from the sign of the complete zoom group.

The rear surface of L16 is the last powered surface in the active model. The final variable spacing from that surface to the image plane is the normalized source D(31) spacing discussed below.

The palette spans very high-Abbe positive materials and dense, lower-Abbe high-index materials. The patent's aberration figures identify d, C, and g wavelengths for plotted curves, but those plot wavelengths are not per-element line-index measurements. Chromatic calculations based on this file therefore remain limited to the dispersion information actually stored in the data.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent states that G2 alone moves for focusing from infinity toward a finite object (¶0059). It does not publish close-focus spacings. Tamron publishes a production minimum object distance of 0.45 m, measured in the manufacturer specification as minimum object distance for B028. The data file therefore marks focus as **`CONSTRAINED_RECONSTRUCTION`** rather than `PUBLISHED`.

The reconstruction holds G1, the stop/G3 assembly, G4, G5, and the image plane fixed at each of the three published zoom states. G2 is translated toward object space while the adjacent gap sum `D(5)+D(14)` is conserved. The G2 translation is solved independently from the finite-conjugate condition at a 0.45 m subject-to-image-plane distance.

| Zoom control point | G2 objectward shift (mm) | D(5) close (mm) | D(14) close (mm) | Paraxial `|m|` | Reciprocal |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 18.5105 mm | 1.020873 | 0.835227 | 39.616973 | 0.056820 | 1:17.599 |
| 85.0242 mm | 2.459055 | 41.559745 | 15.007555 | 0.217239 | 1:4.603 |
| 389.00 mm | 12.449382 | 76.249818 | 13.749382 | 0.337071 | 1:2.967 |

The reconstructed telephoto magnification of approximately 1:2.967 is close to Tamron's marketed 1:2.9 maximum magnification, but the two values are not treated as identical. The manufacturer value is a rounded production specification; the modeled value is a paraxial result obtained from the patent prescription under the disclosed single-group focus constraint.

Tamron identifies HLD as the production autofocus drive. HLD is a mechanical drive specification rather than an optical-prescription parameter; the optical model records only the G2 translation required by the patent mechanism.

## Aspherical Surfaces

Example 1 has four aspherical surfaces: source NS6, NS23, NS26, and NS27, labeled `6A`, `23A`, `26A`, and `27A` in the data file. Surfaces `26A` and `27A` are the two faces of L14.

The patent writes the conic portion as

$$
Z = \frac{y^2}{R\left[1+\sqrt{1-HK\,y^2/R^2}\right]} + \sum C_p y^p.
$$

LensVisualizer uses the standard form containing `1 + K` inside the square root. The conversion is therefore

$$
K = HK - 1.
$$

Table 2 explicitly associates its polynomial columns with radial powers 4, 6, 8, 10, and 12. The data follows those explicit radial powers rather than reinterpreting the prose coefficient letters. No uniform scale is applied, so the aspherical polynomial coefficients are copied at source scale and no `A_p / s^(p-1)` transformation is performed. `K` is likewise unchanged except for the patent-to-standard conic convention above.

| Surface | `K` | `A4` | `A6` | `A8` | `A10` | `A12` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `6A` | -95.0000 | +1.23678e-5 | -1.16542e-8 | -1.13405e-10 | +7.30168e-13 | -1.27023e-15 |
| `23A` | +0.6893 | +8.42359e-6 | +4.45558e-9 | +2.18109e-10 | -4.42521e-12 | +2.61402e-14 |
| `26A` | +2.5416 | +2.18061e-6 | -1.04542e-7 | +1.57166e-10 | +1.35803e-11 | -1.33982e-13 |
| `27A` | -16.8612 | -2.12029e-5 | +5.01726e-8 | -7.40334e-10 | +1.65997e-11 | -1.34975e-13 |

The TypeScript records carry `A14 = 0` for all four surfaces; the patent table's non-zero series ends at the twelfth radial power.

Because the patent does not publish clear-aperture semi-diameters, departures are quoted only at the independently validated modeled semi-diameters rather than at an invented “patent aperture.”

| Surface | Modeled SD (mm) | Departure from base sphere at SD (µm) |
|---|---:|---:|
| `6A` | 14.0 | +212.390 |
| `23A` | 8.6 | +45.432 |
| `26A` | 10.2 | -47.195 |
| `27A` | 10.2 | +28.982 |

The signs show the net modeled departure relative to each spherical base at the stated radius. Surface `6A` places a positive departure on the thin object-side layer of G2; `23A` does the same on the negative VC component; and the two L14 surfaces use opposite-sign departures, providing two-sided non-spherical correction in G5. The patent does not specify whether these surfaces were polished, molded, or made by a particular composite manufacturing process.

## Modeling Normalizations and Source Corrections

Several changes are required to convert the printed Example 1 table into an ordinary active sequential model. They are source corrections or modeling inferences, not silent substitutions.

**Coincident NS10/NS11 plane.** The source prints NS10 and NS11 at the same `R = +18.8700 mm` with zero air thickness between them. The model collapses this inactive air-out/air-in bookkeeping split into a direct cemented transition. The retained interface is labeled `10` and uses the downstream L6 medium and element identity. This preserves axial position and first-order behavior.

**Rear-plane contradiction.** Table 1 prints a fixed `D30 = 37.4800 mm` after the last powered surface, then a flat air/air NS31 followed by variable `D(31)`. Applying both distances moves the image plane approximately 37.48 mm beyond the independently computed paraxial focus. By contrast, `D(31)` alone agrees with the computed back focal distance to source precision at all three zoom positions. The active model therefore omits the inactive NS31 reference plane and maps source `D(31)` directly to surface `30` as the rear image spacing. No sensor cover, filter, or compensating plate is inserted.

**Physical versus modeled L4 count.** The source's 0.1600 mm medium at NS6 is retained as a separate modeled element entry (`L4a`) ahead of the high-index substrate (`L4b`). It remains one physical lens for `elementCount = 16`; the TypeScript array contains 17 entries solely to represent the two optical media.

**Stop size and semi-diameters.** The axial stop position is source-published, but the stop radius and all surface clear apertures are not. The stop SD of 9.389093 mm and the surface SDs are modeling inferences derived from the published f-numbers, ray envelopes, Figure 1 proportions, and mechanical bounds, then tested against edge thickness, actual rim slope, conic limits, shared-band cross-gap intrusion, and off-axis containment.

**Paragraph ¶0058 group label.** The source prose contains a G4/G5 labeling slip in the description of the final inter-group spacing. The five-group power sequence, Figure 1, and the numerical table make the intended final interval the G4–G5 spacing; the model uses that unambiguous interpretation.

**Scaling.** No uniform scaling is used. Radii, thicknesses, image distances, and asphere coefficients remain at Example 1 scale. Consequently there is no asphere coefficient rescaling beyond the conic-convention conversion `K = HK - 1`.

## Image Stabilization

The patent states that G4 moves in a direction perpendicular to the optical axis to correct image-plane motion caused by shake (¶0059). In the centered prescription G4 is a compact negative cemented pair with net `f = -32.927868 mm`.

Tamron identifies the production B028 stabilization system as VC. That manufacturer term supports the production correlation, but the patent prescription is the source for which optical group performs the correction. Neither the patent numerical example nor the final data file defines a transverse displacement magnitude, so no synthetic VC decenter state is added.

The centered optical model therefore represents the nominal zero-shift configuration. Its purpose is to preserve the group power, axial zoom motion, and optical interfaces that the stabilizing group has before a transverse correction is applied.

## Verification Summary

The final data file has 29 listed optical surfaces including exactly one `STO`, 17 modeled element entries representing 16 physical lenses, and five annotated moving zoom groups. Independent reduced-angle and angle-matrix traces agree to floating-point precision.

The infinity-focus first-order results are:

| State | Source focal length (mm) | Recomputed EFL (mm) | BFL from final powered surface (mm) | Authored rear gap (mm) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Wide | 18.5105 | 18.510468623 | 39.761982459 | 39.7621 |
| Mid | 85.0242 | 85.024807057 | 83.427659678 | 83.4278 |
| Tele | 389.00 | 388.662409378 | 99.857149803 | 99.8643 |

The wide and middle states reproduce the source focal lengths essentially at the printed precision. The telephoto state differs by -0.337591 mm, or about -0.0868%, which is consistent with the limited printed precision of the surface table and the rounded 389.00 mm state. The rear-gap comparison independently supports the D(31)-only rear normalization described above.

The surface-by-surface Petzval sum, computed as `φ/(n·n′)` at every refracting surface, is `+0.0016675410 mm⁻¹`, corresponding to a reciprocal magnitude of approximately 599.685 mm. This is a computed prescription property, not a manufacturer specification.

The inferred aperture and semi-diameter model was checked in every authored infinity and reconstructed close-focus state. All modeled elements retain positive rim edge thickness; after the Figure 1 refinement, the minimum is approximately 0.151 mm at the thin L4 layer. The maximum actual rim-slope angle is approximately 53.80°, the positive-`K` conic limits remain clear, and the minimum shared-gap clearance margin under the current 0.90-gap policy is approximately 0.140 mm. Production render diagnostics report zero hidden semi-diameter trim. Representative off-axis bundles do not first clip at a cemented interface; the sampled extreme telephoto bundle that vignettes is intercepted at the first optical surface.

These semi-diameter results are validation of the authored geometric model, not evidence that the patent published those apertures. The same distinction applies to the 9.389093 mm stop SD and to the 0.45 m close-focus spacing reconstruction.
