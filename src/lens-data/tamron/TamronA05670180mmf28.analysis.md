# TAMRON 70-180mm f/2.8 Di III VXD — Optical Design Analysis

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP 2021-43375 A<br>
**Application Number:** JP 2019-166542<br>
**Filed:** September 12, 2019<br>
**Published:** March 18, 2021<br>
**Inventor:** Wataru Kusaka<br>
**Applicant:** Tamron Co., Ltd.<br>
**Title:** ズームレンズ及び撮像装置 (Zoom Lens and Imaging Apparatus)<br>
**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 1<br>

This analysis treats Example 1 of JP 2021-43375 A as the fixed production correlation for the TAMRON 70-180mm f/2.8 Di III VXD selected by the job card. The patent does not identify Model A056 by name, and Tamron's product literature does not identify this patent or example. The relationship is therefore a production correlation established from convergent design evidence rather than an explicit manufacturer attribution.

The principal correlation points are unusually specific. First, the patent example and the production lens both have 19 physical elements in 14 groups. Second, the patent publishes focal-length states of 72.102, 101.789, and 174.600 mm at f/2.910, closely bracketing the marketed 70–180 mm f/2.8 range without any scaling. Third, the patent example has four aspherical surfaces distributed over three physical lens elements, while Tamron states that the A056 uses three glass-molded/hybrid aspherical elements in total. Fourth, Example 1 uses two independently moving rear focus groups and publishes a near-focus endpoint at 850 mm across all three zoom positions; Tamron specifies 0.85 m AF minimum object distance throughout the zoom range and describes a floating focus system operated by two VXD linear-motor units. Fifth, the patent application predates the lens's May 14, 2020 launch.

The marketed and modeled quantities are intentionally kept separate. The production lens is specified by Tamron as 70–180 mm and f/2.8. The patent's published design states are 72.102–174.600 mm at f/2.910, while independent paraxial calculation from the final data arrays gives 72.106516, 101.789596, and 174.597431 mm. No uniform dimensional scale is applied (`s = 1.0`).

The final model omits the patent's sensor cover-glass plate at surfaces 37–38, as required by the LensVisualizer data model. Its optical-path effect is retained by replacing the original 29.793 mm rear air gap with the verified air-equivalent distance

$$
29.793 + \frac{2.500}{1.51633} + 1.000 = 32.441718\ \mathrm{mm}.
$$

This changes the model reference plane, not the powered prescription. The last active surface is therefore 36A and its authored rear spacing is 32.441717627 mm.

The aperture-stop plane itself is directly published as patent surface 21 and is represented by the single `STO` surface. Its semi-diameter is a modeling quantity derived from the exact design aperture f/2.910 rather than from the patent's effective-clear-diameter value $\phi=29.223$ mm. Treating that $\phi$ value as the physical wide-open iris would give a paraxial f-number near f/2.8 rather than the patent's f/2.910. The modeled wide-state stop semi-diameter is therefore 14.055016 mm; the corresponding paraxial requirements at the middle and tele positions are 14.227786 and 14.011281 mm.

The patent provides effective diameters for the optical surfaces, so the model does not infer lens semi-diameters. Those published diameters are carried as $sd=\phi/2$ on the active optical surfaces. The two very thin $n_d=1.51460$, $\nu_d=49.96$ media ahead of physical L8 and L17 are retained as separate optically active modeled entries. The patent does not call these layers resin; their treatment as bonded composite-asphere layers is a modeling inference supported by their geometry and by Tamron's statement that the production lens contains hybrid aspherical elements.

## Optical Architecture

Example 1 is a seven-group refractive zoom with the power sequence

$$
G1(+)\;–\;G2(-)\;–\;G3(+)\;–\;G4(+)\;–\;Gf(-)\;–\;G6(-)\;–\;G7(+).
$$

The patent combines G3 and G4 into an overall positive block $Gp$, and combines Gf, G6, and G7 into an overall negative block $Gn$ (¶0098). The aperture stop lies inside G4. Gf and G6 form the two-group floating focus mechanism, while G4 and G7 are fixed relative to the image plane through the published zoom states to table precision.

The independently computed focal lengths of the individual lens groups are:

| Group | Computed group EFL (mm) | Function in the model |
|---|---:|---|
| G1 | +155.649604 | Front positive collector group |
| G2 | −44.443720 | Strong negative variator group |
| G3 | +56.482118 | Positive aspheric group |
| G4 | +54.209792 | Positive stop-containing group |
| Gf | −40.747053 | First floating focus group |
| G6 | −78.539719 | Second floating/aspheric negative group |
| G7 | +67.202517 | Fixed rear positive group |

These group focal lengths are not the same thing as standalone element focal lengths or cemented-pair net powers. The `fl` field attached to each modeled element is its standalone power in air. A cemented stack must be traced as a whole to obtain its net power, and the larger aggregate blocks $Gp$ and $Gn$ must additionally include their actual in-situ air spacings.

That distinction is especially important in the weak front cemented pairs. D1 (L1+L2) has opposing standalone element focal lengths of −291.325 and +237.240 mm, but the cemented pair has a net focal length of about +1297.559 mm. D2 (L5+L6) combines −94.938 and +103.879 mm standalone powers yet has a weak net focal length of about −1100.495 mm. The large magnitudes of those cemented-net focal lengths indicate near cancellation, not strong net power.

The remaining verified cemented or bonded-stack net focal lengths are approximately −244.217 mm for D3 (L10+L11), −81.833 mm for D4 (L12+L13), −40.747 mm for D5 (L15+L16, identical to Gf because Gf consists only of that cemented doublet), +56.482 mm for H1 (the modeled L8 bonded layer plus substrate, identical to G3), and −78.540 mm for H2 (the modeled L17 bonded layer plus substrate, identical to G6).

Because G3 and G4 are separated by a zoom-dependent air gap, their combined in-situ power changes slightly with zoom even though each group's internal prescription is fixed. The computed $Gp$ EFL is +33.907588 mm at wide, +33.301672 mm at the middle state, and +33.079687 mm at tele. The negative $Gn$ aggregate similarly changes from −38.683283 to −37.943862 to −38.795796 mm as the Gf–G6–G7 spacings vary.

The three infinity-focus states produce normalized optical tracks of 163.594718, 179.096718, and 192.564718 mm. Their track/EFL ratios are 2.2688, 1.7595, and 1.1029. Under the project's strict classification rule, which reserves the optical-design term *telephoto* for $TL/EFL<1$, none of the three modeled states qualifies, even though both the patent and the commercial product use long-focus/telephoto-zoom language in their descriptive context. The design is also not retrofocus under the project criterion $BFD>EFL$. Using the computed paraxial back focal distance from surface 36A, the BFD/EFL ratios are approximately 0.4500, 0.3187, and 0.1858 at wide, middle, and tele, respectively, all below unity.

### Zoom kinematics

The numerical zoom table is retained at all three control points because Gf and G6 reverse direction between the middle and tele positions. Relative to the normalized image plane, the computed group-reference motions from wide to tele are:

| Group | Wide → middle (mm) | Middle → tele (mm) | Wide → tele (mm) | Reversal |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| G1 | −15.502 | −13.468 | −28.970 | No |
| G2 | +11.797 | +18.710 | +30.507 | No |
| G3 | +1.641 | +0.618 | +2.259 | No |
| G4 | −0.002 | +0.001 | −0.001 | No; fixed to table precision |
| Gf | +0.633 | −0.848 | −0.215 | Yes |
| G6 | +0.807 | −0.923 | −0.116 | Yes |
| G7 | ~0 | ~0 | ~0 | No; fixed |

Positive motion in this table is imageward and negative motion is objectward. Patent ¶0106 contains a direct conflict for G6: its prose says that G6 moves imageward from the wide to tele end, but Table 3 places G6 imageward at the middle state and then objectward, ending 0.116 mm objectward of its wide-position location. The model follows the numerical table rather than silently changing either source statement.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The following focal lengths are the standalone-in-air values stored in the final data file unless a cemented or bonded net is explicitly identified. The patent's element descriptions in ¶0099–0105 provide the physical shape sequence; the optical roles below remain at the level supported by the group architecture, signs, glass coordinates, and verified powers.

### G1 — Front positive group

#### L1 — Negative meniscus, cemented in D1

**$n_d=1.80610$, $\nu_d=33.27$. Glass: 806333 — high-index optical glass (vendor unresolved). Standalone $f=-291.325$ mm.**

L1 is the object-side negative meniscus of the front cemented pair. Its negative standalone power is largely cancelled by L2 at the cemented interface: the complete D1 pair is only weakly positive, with a verified net focal length near +1297.559 mm. This makes L1 primarily part of a balancing achromatizing/power-distribution pair rather than the principal source of G1's positive power.

#### L2 — Biconvex positive, cemented in D1

**$n_d=1.43700$, $\nu_d=95.10$. Glass: 437951 — ultra-low-dispersion crown (vendor unresolved). Standalone $f=+237.240$ mm.**

L2 supplies positive power against L1 and has the highest Abbe number in the design. The patent's general discussion of G1 states that a combination of positive and negative lenses is preferred for controlling spherical and chromatic aberration (¶0015). The data support that group-level statement, but do not identify the production vendor or proprietary XLD/LD designation of L2.

#### L3 — Biconvex positive

**$n_d=1.49700$, $\nu_d=81.61$. Glass: 497816 — fluorophosphate low-dispersion crown (vendor unresolved). Standalone $f=+175.790$ mm.**

L3 is the second positive component of G1 and supplies more direct converging power after the nearly self-cancelling D1 pair. Together the three physical elements give G1 a verified EFL of +155.649604 mm, matching the patent's Table 22 value of +155.650 mm at printed precision.

### G2 — Negative variator group

#### L4 — Biconcave negative

**$n_d=1.69680$, $\nu_d=55.46$. Glass: 697555 — lanthanum-crown class (vendor unresolved). Standalone $f=-100.490$ mm.**

L4 begins the strongly negative G2 group. The patent associates G2 with zooming and with control of field curvature and chromatic aberration through its mix of negative and positive elements (¶0016). Its independent negative power is comparable in magnitude to the other two negative components in G2.

#### L5 — Biconcave negative, cemented in D2

**$n_d=1.49700$, $\nu_d=81.61$. Glass: 497816 — fluorophosphate low-dispersion crown (vendor unresolved). Standalone $f=-94.938$ mm.**

L5 is a negative low-dispersion member cemented directly to the high-index positive L6. The individual element powers nearly cancel in the cemented pair, whose verified net focal length is about −1100.495 mm. The low-dispersion/high-dispersion pairing is therefore optically significant even though D2 contributes only weak net negative power by itself.

#### L6 — Positive meniscus, cemented in D2

**$n_d=1.92286$, $\nu_d=20.88$. Glass: 923209 — ultra-high-index dense-flint class (vendor unresolved). Standalone $f=+103.879$ mm.**

L6 is the high-index, high-dispersion positive partner of L5. Its sign opposes L5 while its dispersion coordinate is very different, providing the kind of paired power/dispersion leverage expected in a cemented correcting component. No anomalous partial-dispersion claim is made because the patent supplies only $n_d$ and $\nu_d$ for this material.

#### L7 — Biconcave negative

**$n_d=1.69680$, $\nu_d=55.46$. Glass: 697555 — lanthanum-crown class (vendor unresolved). Standalone $f=-92.721$ mm.**

L7 closes G2 with another substantial negative contribution. Once the air-spaced L4, weak D2 pair, and L7 are traced together, G2 has a verified EFL of −44.443720 mm, matching the patent's Table 22 value of −44.444 mm.

### G3 — Positive aspheric group / physical L8

#### L8a — Modeled bonded aspheric layer

**$n_d=1.51460$, $\nu_d=49.96$. Glass: Unmatched (bonded aspheric optical layer; physical material not identified by patent). Standalone modeled $f=+806.675$ mm.**

L8a is not counted as an additional physical production element. It represents the patent's 0.250 mm optical medium between surfaces 13A and 14. Surface 13A carries the first asphere. Its weak standalone power and very small thickness are consistent with a bonded correction layer, but the patent does not identify the material as resin and the model does not assign a catalog glass name.

#### L8 — Biconvex positive substrate

**$n_d=1.83481$, $\nu_d=42.72$. Glass: 835427 — high-index lanthanum-glass class (vendor unresolved). Standalone $f=+60.569$ mm.**

The L8 substrate supplies most of physical L8's converging power. Traced together with L8a, the complete H1 bonded element has an EFL of +56.482118 mm and constitutes all of G3. This distinction prevents the thin correction layer's standalone focal length from being mistaken for the power of the physical lens element.

### G4 — Positive stop-containing group

#### L9 — Biconvex positive

**$n_d=1.59282$, $\nu_d=68.62$. Glass: 593686 — low-dispersion crown class (vendor unresolved). Standalone $f=+88.529$ mm.**

L9 begins G4 with positive power ahead of the stop. G4 is the larger positive central group and contains two cemented correcting pairs plus the aperture stop. Patent ¶0026 specifically favors locating the stop within $Gp$ to control downstream beam diameter.

#### L10 — Biconvex positive, cemented in D3

**$n_d=1.49700$, $\nu_d=81.61$. Glass: 497816 — fluorophosphate low-dispersion crown (vendor unresolved). Standalone $f=+93.262$ mm.**

L10 is the positive low-dispersion side of D3. It is cemented to the substantially higher-index, lower-Abbe L11, creating an internal dispersion contrast without introducing an air interface.

#### L11 — Biconcave negative, cemented in D3

**$n_d=1.85451$, $\nu_d=25.16$. Glass: 855252 — high-index flint class (vendor unresolved). Standalone $f=-66.317$ mm.**

L11 opposes L10 in both sign and dispersion. The complete D3 pair remains weakly negative compared with either standalone element, with verified net focal length about −244.217 mm. It acts within an overall positive G4 rather than as an independent negative group.

#### L12 — Plano-concave negative, cemented in D4

**$n_d=1.90366$, $\nu_d=31.31$. Glass: 904313 — high-index lanthanum/flint class (vendor unresolved). Standalone $f=-32.357$ mm.**

L12 is the strongest negative standalone element in G4. Its object-side surface is plane at the modeled patent stop region, and it is cemented to the positive L13. The patent describes this physical component as a plano negative lens joined to a biconvex positive lens (¶0102).

#### L13 — Biconvex positive, cemented in D4

**$n_d=1.49700$, $\nu_d=81.61$. Glass: 497816 — fluorophosphate low-dispersion crown (vendor unresolved). Standalone $f=+54.228$ mm.**

L13 provides the positive low-dispersion partner to L12. The D4 cemented pair remains net negative, with a verified focal length of about −81.833 mm, but the surrounding L9, L14, and other positive components keep G4 as a whole positive.

#### L14 — Biconvex positive

**$n_d=1.60342$, $\nu_d=38.03$. Glass: 603380 — F5/TIM5-class flint (vendor unresolved). Standalone $f=+42.116$ mm.**

L14 is the rear positive element of G4 and the strongest standalone positive element within that group. The full G4 stack has a verified EFL of +54.209792 mm. Together with G3, it forms the patent's overall positive $Gp$ block.

### Gf — First floating focus group

#### L15 — Biconvex positive, cemented in D5

**$n_d=1.92286$, $\nu_d=20.88$. Glass: 923209 — ultra-high-index dense-flint class (vendor unresolved). Standalone $f=+107.510$ mm.**

L15 is the positive member of the two-element Gf cemented focus group. Its high index allows substantial refractive action within a compact physical element, while its sign is opposed by the much stronger negative L16.

#### L16 — Biconcave negative, cemented in D5

**$n_d=1.77250$, $\nu_d=49.62$. Glass: 773496 — lanthanum-flint class (vendor unresolved). Standalone $f=-29.310$ mm.**

L16 dominates the net sign of Gf. The complete D5 cemented doublet has a verified EFL of −40.747053 mm, exactly the group EFL because Gf contains no additional air-spaced elements. The patent specifically identifies Gf as the first negative group within $Gn$ and uses it as one of the focus groups in Example 1 (¶0103, ¶0107).

### G6 — Second floating/aspheric negative group / physical L17

#### L17a — Modeled bonded aspheric layer

**$n_d=1.51460$, $\nu_d=49.96$. Glass: Unmatched (bonded aspheric optical layer; physical material not identified by patent). Standalone modeled $f=-361.150$ mm.**

L17a represents the 0.150 mm optical layer beginning at surface 30A. Like L8a, it is retained because it is optically active and carries an aspherical surface, but it is not counted as an extra physical production element. Its negative standalone power is weak compared with the substrate.

#### L17 — Negative meniscus substrate

**$n_d=1.85451$, $\nu_d=25.16$. Glass: 855252 — high-index flint class (vendor unresolved). Standalone $f=-100.569$ mm.**

The substrate provides most of physical L17's negative power. H2, the layer-plus-substrate bonded stack, has a verified EFL of −78.539719 mm and constitutes G6. This group moves independently from Gf during focusing and reverses direction during the published zoom sequence.

### G7 — Fixed rear positive group

#### L18 — Biconvex positive

**$n_d=1.91082$, $\nu_d=35.25$. Glass: 911353 — high-index optical glass (vendor unresolved). Standalone $f=+27.406$ mm.**

L18 is a strong positive element at the front of the fixed rear group. It is followed by a strongly shaped negative meniscus, so the G7 pair should be interpreted as a coupled rear corrector rather than from the magnitude of either standalone focal length alone.

#### L19 — Negative meniscus with two aspherical surfaces

**$n_d=1.85108$, $\nu_d=40.12$. Glass: 851401 — high-index optical glass (vendor unresolved). Standalone $f=-36.346$ mm.**

L19 is the final physical lens element and carries aspheres on both surfaces, 35A and 36A. Despite its negative standalone power, the combined two-element G7 group remains positive at +67.202517 mm. Its location at the rear of the system is consistent with the patent's preference for aspherical correction in the image-side portion of $Gn$ (¶0024), but the specific aberration balance is not assigned beyond what the prescription verifies.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent gives only d-line refractive index and Abbe number. It does not name glass suppliers, melts, line indices, or anomalous partial-dispersion values. The final data file therefore uses six-digit coordinate classes or explicit `Unmatched (...)` labels rather than asserting vendor-specific glass identities.

Authoritative public catalog data from OHARA, HOYA, SCHOTT, HIKARI, CDGM, and SUMITA were compared with the patent coordinates. Several coordinates have exact or near-exact equivalents from multiple manufacturers, so coordinate agreement alone cannot identify the production supplier. The data-file labels deliberately preserve that uncertainty.

Tamron's production literature states that the A056 contains six XLD and LD elements combined. ## Focus Mechanism

The focus model is `PUBLISHED`; it is not a constrained reconstruction. Patent Table 4 gives the three variable gaps at a shooting distance of 850.000 mm for every zoom state, and ¶0107 states that Gf moves imageward while G6 moves objectward when focusing from infinity toward a near object. The other groups remain fixed during focusing.

The final data file preserves the published endpoint pairs exactly:

| Zoom state | D26 infinity → close (mm) | D29 infinity → close (mm) | D32 infinity → close (mm) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Wide | 2.115 → 3.817 | 15.413 → 12.267 | 2.419 → 3.863 |
| Middle | 2.750 → 5.978 | 15.587 → 10.495 | 1.612 → 3.475 |
| Tele | 1.901 → 10.907 | 15.512 → 5.179 | 2.535 → 3.862 |

These gap changes correspond to the following group travel from infinity to the published near state:

| State | Gf motion | G6 motion | Local D26+D29+D32 change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Wide | 1.702 mm imageward | 1.444 mm objectward | ~0.000 mm |
| Middle | 3.228 mm imageward | 1.863 mm objectward | −0.001 mm |
| Tele | 9.006 mm imageward | 1.327 mm objectward | 0.000 mm |

The 0.001 mm middle-state closure residual is the precision of the published spacing table. No compensating movement is inserted.

Tamron independently describes the production A056 as using a floating system driven by two electronically controlled VXD linear-motor units and specifies 0.85 m AF minimum object distance across the full zoom range. This is one of the strongest production-correlation points because it matches both the number of independently moving focus groups and the patent's published 850 mm finite-focus state.

The literal 850 mm patent spacings do not place the normalized d-line Gaussian model at exact paraxial focus. With the cover-glass-normalized rear reference, the independent calculation gives image-plane shifts to zero paraxial $B$ of approximately +0.173, +0.214, and +0.284 mm at wide, middle, and tele. The source endpoint is nevertheless retained unchanged. It is a published optimized finite-focus prescription, not a datum to be replaced by a reconstructed Gaussian conjugate.

The manufacturer's separate manual-focus 0.27 m wide-end mode is not modeled. The patent Example 1 supplies only the 850 mm finite-focus state used here, and the data file does not invent an additional 0.27 m internal configuration.

## Aspherical Surfaces

Example 1 has four aspherical surfaces: 13A on the modeled L8 bonded layer, 30A on the modeled L17 bonded layer, and both 35A and 36A on physical L19. These four surfaces occur on three physical lens elements, consistent in count with Tamron's statement that the production lens uses three GM/hybrid aspherical elements combined. The source does not identify which patent element is glass-molded and which is hybrid, so no one-to-one manufacturing assignment is made.

Patent ¶0114 defines the sag as

$$
X(Y)=\frac{CY^2}{1+\sqrt{1-(1+K)C^2Y^2}}
+A_4Y^4+A_6Y^6+A_8Y^8+A_{10}Y^{10}+A_{12}Y^{12}+A_{14}Y^{14}.
$$

This is the same standard conic convention used by LensVisualizer, so the patent's $K$ values are copied directly. All four Example 1 conic constants are $K=0$. No scaling is applied, so the polynomial coefficients are not transformed; with $s=1$, $A_p/s^{p-1}=A_p$.

## Chromatic Correction Strategy

The chromatic strategy visible in the prescription is based on repeated pairing of large dispersion contrasts rather than on a single isolated low-dispersion element. G1 combines the very high-Abbe L2 ($\nu_d=95.10$) with the high-index, low-Abbe L1 ($\nu_d=33.27$), followed by another low-dispersion positive element L3 ($\nu_d=81.61$). G2 combines the $\nu_d=81.61$ negative L5 with the $\nu_d=20.88$ positive L6. G4 repeats the pattern in D3 and D4, placing $\nu_d=81.61$ positive elements against higher-index lower-Abbe negative partners.

The pattern is consistent with the patent's stated objective of controlling chromatic aberration in G1, G2, and the rear groups, and with Tamron's statement that six XLD/LD elements are used in the production lens. The data do not support identification of exactly which patent elements carry the commercial XLD versus LD labels, nor do they establish secondary-spectrum or apochromatic correction. Those stronger claims would require validated line-index, Sellmeier, or anomalous-partial-dispersion data for the actual materials.

Condition (9) reinforces the importance of low dispersion in G2 by constraining the maximum $\nu_d$ among its negative lenses to the range $60<\nu_{2n\max}<110$. Example 1's published value is 81.607; the available surface table rounds the corresponding material coordinate to 81.61.
