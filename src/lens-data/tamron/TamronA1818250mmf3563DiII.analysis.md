# TAMRON AF 18-250mm f/3.5-6.3 Di II LD Aspherical [IF] MACRO (A18)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP 2008-003195 A  
**Application Number:** JP 2006-171066  
**Filed:** 2006-06-21  
**Published:** 2008-01-10  
**Inventors:** Yuichi Muramatsu; Akio Arakawa  
**Applicant:** Tamron Co., Ltd.  
**Title:** High variable magnification zoom lens  
**Example details:** Example 2, Figure 5; numerical paragraphs 0081-0095

Example 2 of this Japanese publication is the prescription modelled here. No grant or translated counterpart supplies other numbers. It is a patent design associated with the Tamron AF18-250mm F/3.5-6.3 Di II LD Aspherical [IF] MACRO (Model A18) by its construction, not a prescription Tamron has confirmed.

1. Example 2 gives 16 elements in 13 air-separated groups, Tamron's published count. Two further 0.20 mm rows are bonded resin layers that do not count as elements, so the model holds 18 media.
2. The four groups hold 3+4+5+4 elements. Tamron's construction diagrams agree with the cemented pairs L1-L2, L8-L9 and L15-L16.
3. Tamron's English brochure (page 2) marks hybrid aspherical elements at the first G2 element and the first rear-group element, which are L4 and L13, the two resin-layer positions. It marks LD on the large G1 biconvex element, L2, and on one rear element; L11 is the only rear element with νd above 80, so it fits that mark. It marks AD on the G1 meniscus, L3, whose printed coordinates are ordinary glass (see L3). These are diagram correlations, not patent spectral statements.
4. The patent prints 18.43-241.83 mm and F3.47-6.35. The 241.83 mm tele end is the patent's own value against the marketed 250 mm; neither the focal lengths nor the f-numbers are rescaled.
5. The patent and Tamron both describe inner focusing. The table gives no near-focus state, so the marketed 0.45 m minimum distance and 1:3.5 ratio are not modelled.

The patent prints two examples, and Example 1 (Figure 1) does not fit the product. It has 15 elements in 13 groups and three hybrid aspheres (r6, r18, r25), and it fails to close at tele as printed: its table computes to 246.15 mm against the stated 241.85 mm, with D32 1.40 mm short of the computed back focus. Example 2 alone matches Tamron's 16 elements, 13 groups and two hybrid aspheres.

Tamron's archive records release in 2007, and its Japanese launch brochure gives a planned introduction of 2007-02-22. The English brochure's timeline says 2006.

Production metadata uses the Canon EF, Nikon F, Sony A and Pentax K mount families, APS-C format, seven diaphragm blades and a 62 mm filter thread. The 29 mm image circle is inferred by doubling the 14.5 mm image height on the patent's plots; it is not a published measurement.

## Optical Architecture

The design is a four-group positive-negative-positive-positive zoom. The groups' computed focal lengths are:

| Group | Surfaces | Elements | Focal length (mm) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| G1 | r1-r5 | 3 | +95.468902 |
| G2 | r6-r14 | 4 | -12.712700 |
| G3 | r16-r24 | 5 | +43.768108 |
| G4 | r25-r32 | 4 | +46.214504 |

G2 is the inner-focus group (¶0083). The stop is r15, 1.00 mm ahead of r16, and it moves with G3. G3 contains a negative-positive cemented pair, a positive meniscus, another positive lens and a final negative lens. ¶0054-0058 and ¶0082 tie this three-positive-lens arrangement to aberration control at high zoom ratios and to low manufacturing sensitivity.

The model carries every printed medium, including both resin layers. No sensor cover plate, filter, stabilization group or barrel is added.

At the wide station the first-to-last-vertex span is 89.245 mm and the first-vertex-to-image track is 128.828 mm; at tele they are 117.476 mm and 206.860 mm. Back focus exceeds EFL at wide, a retrofocus condition, and total track is below EFL at tele, a telephoto condition.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

All focal lengths below are thick-element values in air, not in-system contributions. The resin layers are bonded to their elements and do not add to Tamron's element count. Glass names are catalog equivalents for the printed coordinates; the patent names no supplier. Role statements are interpretations unless a patent paragraph is cited.

### L1: Negative Meniscus
nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.78. Glass: S-TIH53 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = -209.5853 mm.
This negative meniscus is the high-index, low-Abbe front member of the bonded L1-L2 pair. Its opposite power to L2 supplies a first-order achromatizing opportunity; no supplier or measured residual chromatic correction is inferred.

### L2: Biconvex Positive
nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.61. Glass: FCD1 (HOYA catalog equivalent) — LD position, inferred. f = +120.9032 mm.
The positive, high-Abbe member completes the front cemented pair and meets the patent's preferred low-dispersion role in G1 (¶0060-0062). Tamron's diagram marks this position LD, so it carries an inferred anomalous-dispersion tag. The patent gives no partial-dispersion data.

### L3: Positive Meniscus
nd = 1.62299, νd = 58.15. Glass: S-BSM15 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +139.8826 mm.
The separate positive meniscus completes G1. Its nd meets condition (6), and its Abbe number lies within condition (5). Tamron's diagram marks this position AD, but the example's coordinates match ordinary S-BSM15, which sits on the normal partial-dispersion line (ΔPgF -0.0001). That is a sign the production glass may differ from the example, so this element carries no anomalous-dispersion tag.

### L4f: Hybrid Aspheric Resin Layer (1× Asph)
nd = 1.51460, νd = 49.96. Glass: Unmatched (hybrid optical resin; spectrum unpublished). f = -374.7135 mm.
This 0.20 mm layer sits on the object side of L4. Its outer surface r6 is aspherical; the r7 interface is spherical. The patent does not call it resin: the hybrid reading follows from the thin row and Tamron's hybrid aspherical mark at this position. The resin itself is not identified.

### L4: Negative Meniscus
nd = 1.80400, νd = 46.58. Glass: S-LAH65V (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = -21.0989 mm.
This strong negative meniscus carries the r6 resin layer, and Tamron marks the position hybrid aspherical. Its rear surface r8 is strongly curved and faces the 5.40 mm air gap, which constrains the modelled semi-diameter and outer-field passage. Figure 5 ends that curve at L5's rim and carries the element out to its edge on a flat land, and Tamron's section shows the same shoulder.

### L5: Biconcave Negative
nd = 1.83481, νd = 42.72. Glass: TAFD5G (HOYA catalog equivalent). f = -25.0969 mm.
This biconcave lens adds to the negative power of G2. An air gap separates it from the hybrid L4.

### L6: Biconvex Positive
nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.78. Glass: S-TIH53 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +16.7286 mm.
This positive lens balances the negative lenses within G2. Its high index and low Abbe number set it against the higher-Abbe negative members for colour balance.

### L7: Biconcave Negative
nd = 1.88300, νd = 40.78. Glass: S-LAH58 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = -22.3737 mm.
This biconcave negative lens completes G2 before the variable gap to the stop. The 0.42 mm air space between r12 and r13 is a tight geometric constraint.

### L8: Negative Meniscus
nd = 1.80400, νd = 46.58. Glass: S-LAH65V (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = -93.5116 mm.
The negative front of the G3 cemented pair is the patent's lens 231 (¶0082). It shares surface r17 with L9.

### L9: Biconvex Positive
nd = 1.58913, νd = 61.18. Glass: S-BAL35 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +38.2366 mm.
The positive partner is the patent's lens 232. L8-L9 form one cemented component of +65.74 mm.

### L10: Positive Meniscus
nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.21. Glass: S-FSL5 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +66.8668 mm.
This positive meniscus is the patent's lens 233, with the larger radius on the image side (¶0082). It is one of the three positive lenses central to the G3 arrangement.

### L11: Positive Meniscus
nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.61. Glass: FCD1 (HOYA catalog equivalent) — LD position, inferred. f = +49.7084 mm.
This positive high-Abbe lens is the patent's lens 234 and meets condition (4), νd >= 80. It is the only rear element that fits the rear position Tamron's diagram marks LD, so it carries an inferred anomalous-dispersion tag; no APO claim follows.

### L12: Biconcave Negative
nd = 1.83400, νd = 37.17. Glass: S-LAH60 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = -29.2111 mm.
The biconcave negative rear member is the patent's lens 235. The final variable gap separates G3 and G4.

### L13f: Hybrid Aspheric Resin Layer (1× Asph)
nd = 1.51460, νd = 49.96. Glass: Unmatched (hybrid optical resin; spectrum unpublished). f = +67340.6619 mm.
This second 0.20 mm layer begins at r25. The r25 asphere and spherical r26 share one paraxial radius, so the layer has almost no paraxial power while its higher-order sag departure is substantial.

### L13: Biconvex Positive
nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.21. Glass: S-FSL5 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +42.9163 mm.
This positive lens carries the r25 resin layer and begins G4, and Tamron marks the position hybrid aspherical. Layer and lens stay separate media with one shared boundary.

### L14: Biconvex Positive
nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.21. Glass: S-FSL5 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +54.2126 mm.
This positive lens has a very weakly curved object surface and a stronger image-side curvature. Its front radius is the printed 8369.5957 mm, not a plane.

### L15: Biconcave Negative
nd = 1.80400, νd = 46.58. Glass: S-LAH65V (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = -22.4617 mm.
The negative member of the final cemented pair has a high index and shares surface r31 with L16. The pair's combined focal length is -46.53 mm.

### L16: Positive Meniscus
nd = 1.54072, νd = 47.23. Glass: S-TIL2 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +42.3977 mm.
The positive final member exits at r32. The printed D32 runs from this last vertex to the image; no cover plate is printed or added.

## Focus Mechanism and Zoom Motion

Paragraph 0083 moves G2 toward the object to focus, but the patent prints no travel, object distance or near-focus spacing. Its table also does not name the object conjugate. The model takes it as infinity because computed back focus matches D32 within 0.005 mm at all three stations; that is an inference, not a printed label.

Both focus columns hold the same gaps, so the model stays at infinity focus and the focus slider changes nothing. The 0.45 m in the data is Tamron's stated minimum focus distance, not a modelled state, and finite-distance MTF is unavailable. As a consistency note, a paraxial solve moving G2 alone to focus at 0.45 m object to image gives 1:3.53 at tele with 8.96 mm of travel, and needs 0.80 mm at wide, inside the 1.766 mm D5, which agrees with Tamron's 1:3.5 but is no part of the model.

The printed zoom spacings are:

| Gap (mm) | Wide | Intermediate | Tele |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| D5 | 1.766 | 30.152 | 59.613 |
| D14 | 25.227 | 11.984 | 1.411 |
| D24 | 7.782 | 3.768 | 1.982 |
| D32 | 39.583 | 67.213 | 89.384 |

D5 widens while D14 and D24 narrow (¶0083). Against the fixed image plane, the first vertices of G1/G2/G3/G4 move objectward by 78.032/20.185/44.001/49.801 mm from wide to tele. These values follow from the printed gaps; the patent gives no cam law.

Only those three stations are printed. The 126 stations between them are inferred, and the path is not a factory cam. G3 travel is the path parameter: D24 and the G1 position follow smooth monotone curves through the printed values, and G2 is solved at each station to hold focus, with the small printed image-plane residual interpolated between stations. ¶0083 fixes only the direction of the gap changes; the model adds the choice that no group reverses, so every gap and every group moves one way from wide to tele. G2 moves slowly near both ends and fastest through the middle of the range. Computed defocus stays within 0.005 mm at the stations and under 0.01 mm between them. Stations are spaced geometrically in focal length on each side of the printed intermediate state.

## Aspherical Surfaces

The patent's equation is

x = (H²/r) / [1 + sqrt(1 - A(H/r)²)] + A4 H⁴ + A6 H⁶ + A8 H⁸ + A10 H¹⁰.

The patent's A = 1 is K = A - 1 = 0 in the standard form, so both surfaces have spherical bases. H and x are in mm, and each coefficient Ap has units mm^(1-p). There is no scaling and no odd-order term.

| Surface | Location | K | A4 | A6 | A8 | A10 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 6A | L4f front resin surface | 0 | 6.08226e-06 | 3.27658e-09 | -8.68205e-11 | 4.65488e-13 |
| 25A | L13f front resin surface | 0 | -4.66282e-05 | 6.30079e-09 | -1.16447e-11 | -4.36431e-14 |

At the inferred semi-diameters, r6A at 13.5 mm departs +0.219667 mm from its spherical base and r25A at 9.5 mm departs -0.376192 mm. These are evaluations at modelled rims, not published apertures. Positive departure at r6 increases sag relative to its base; negative departure at r25 reduces it. Neither sign alone proves which aberration is corrected.

Both aspheres are hybrid: the patent prints each as a 0.20 mm row, and Tamron calls them hybrid aspherical elements. Both layers keep positive thickness across their modelled apertures.

## Model Scope and Limitations

| Printed station | Computed EFL (mm) | Printed EFL (mm) | Computed BFD (mm) | Printed D32 (mm) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Wide | 18.432340 | 18.43 | 39.582603 | 39.583 |
| Intermediate | 57.803991 | 57.80 | 67.215076 | 67.213 |
| Tele | 241.839642 | 241.83 | 89.389024 | 89.384 |

Computed focal lengths and back focus match the printed values within 0.01 mm at all three stations.

The physical iris diameter is not printed. The stop is sized for a paraxial entrance-pupil diameter of F/N at each zoom position, which gives radii of 7.5635/7.5245/7.5489 mm at the printed stations. Between them the f-number follows a smooth iris curve through those radii, so it rises steadily with focal length. This is an inferred iris law, not a published mechanism.

All clear semi-diameters are estimated from Figure 5, then checked against real rays at the 14.5 mm plot height and against positive edge and gap geometry. The figure draws the wide end only, to one scale in both directions, so its rims can be read directly; its image plane is not to scale. L4's rear curve stops at 9.5 mm, just short of where r8 would meet L5's front surface, and a flat land carries it out to 13 mm. That near-contact sets the gap-sag allowance for this lens to 0.99.

No rim clips the axial beam at any zoom station. The rims keep full-field vignetting rather than being enlarged past the figure: wide open, geometric transmission at the 14.5 mm corner is 53.0/61.5/60.8% at the printed wide, intermediate and tele stations. In the meridional plane the corner bundle is bounded on one side by r8 at wide, r1 at the intermediate station and r4 at tele, and on the other side by r29 at all three. No production vignetting pattern is implied.

The printed half-fields 38.2/14.1/3.45° match atan(14.5/f). Exact chief rays at those angles land at 13.57/15.06/14.90 mm, while chief rays reach the 14.5 mm plot height at 40.00/13.63/3.36° without clipping, so the printed angles are not exact chief-ray angles at that height. The model's analysis field reaches the 14.5 mm corner at every zoom station. The patent's aberration curves are not claimed reproduced.

MTF is evaluated at infinity only, because no finite conjugate is published, and only the bandwidth the viewer reports as reliable for each field applies.
