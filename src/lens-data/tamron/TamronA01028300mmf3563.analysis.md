The prescription represented here is the final LensVisualizer transcription of Example 7 in JP 2013-254160 A. The patent supplies the radii, axial spacings, d-line refractive indices and Abbe numbers, five aspherical surfaces, and three infinity-focus zoom states used by the model (¶0059–¶0061). Figure 25 on patent p. 33 shows the same five functional groups and the stop immediately ahead of G3 that are represented in the data file.

The production correlation is to the TAMRON 28-300mm f/3.5-6.3 Di VC PZD (A010). This assignment is a source-based correlation, not a claim that Tamron explicitly identified Example 7 as the production prescription. Several independent features converge:

1. The patent applicant is Tamron, and the December 2013 publication precedes the A010's 2014 commercial release.
2. The native patent states span 28.7760–291.2621 mm, closely matching the marketed 28–300 mm range without scaling. The patent figures give a nearly constant approximately 21.63 mm semi-image height, consistent with 135/full-frame coverage.
3. Reconciliation of the thin S33–S34 hybrid layer yields 19 physical optical elements in 15 air-separated groups, matching the manufacturer specification. The LensVisualizer `elements` array contains 20 refractive media entries only because the resin layer and its glass substrate are modeled separately.
4. The prescription contains three physical glass aspherical elements plus one hybrid aspherical element, expressed as five aspherical surfaces. This matches Tamron's published construction of three molded-glass aspherical elements and one hybrid aspherical element.
5. The first zoom group contains a very high-index front negative element followed by two low-dispersion-coordinate positive elements, consistent with Tamron's published description of the front UXR element and the placement of LD elements in the first group.
6. The patent's five-group `+ − + − +` architecture allows G4 to serve as a lateral image-stabilization group. The production Canon EF and Nikon F versions use VC; the Sony A version represented by the same optical family omits lens VC mechanically because stabilization is provided in the camera body.

Marketing and design quantities are kept separate. The public lens name retains the marketed 28–300 mm f/3.5–6.3 designation, while the modeled infinity states use the patent values 28.7760, 91.5000, and 291.2621 mm with FNO 3.628, 3.628, and 6.459 respectively. No dimensional scaling is applied, so the patent radii, spacings, and aspherical coefficients remain at native scale.

## Optical Architecture

Example 7 is a five-functional-group zoom with power sequence `+ − + − +`. The 19 physical elements are arranged in 15 air-separated groups. The stop lies between G2 and G3 and is fixed 1.0000 mm in front of the first G3 surface within each published zoom state, so it travels with G3 as required by patent claim 10 and ¶0021.

The equivalent paraxial focal lengths of the five functional groups, recomputed from the final data arrays, are:

| Group | Data surfaces | Equivalent focal length in air |
|---|---|---:|
| G1 | 1–7 | +91.5267 mm |
| G2 | 8A–15 | −12.9668 mm |
| G3 | 17–23 | +21.0544 mm |
| G4 | 24A–26 | −35.9313 mm |
| G5 | 28–37 | +66.7974 mm |

These are in-situ group prescriptions reduced to air-to-air equivalent power; they are not the same quantities as the standalone focal lengths assigned to individual elements below. At the tele state, the combined G3+G4+G5 subsystem has an equivalent focal length of 24.1946 mm.

G1 is the large positive front group. Its four-element negative-positive-positive-positive sequence is the central compactness and chromatic-correction device of the patent. Paragraphs ¶0024–¶0030 specifically constrain the refractive indices and Abbe numbers of these four elements so that front diameter and tele-end aberrations can be controlled without reverting to a three-element first group.

G2 is the strong negative variator. Its computed focal length, −12.9668 mm, is much shorter in magnitude than G1, and the patent treats the G1/G2 power balance as critical to a compact high-ratio zoom (¶0031). The wide state has `BFD/EFL = 1.3952`, so it satisfies the project's numerical definition of a retrofocus state.

G3 is a compact positive group consisting of three positive elements followed by one negative element, exactly the arrangement described in claim 8 and ¶0034. The stop moves with this group. The patent associates this configuration with control of lower-side coma at wide angle while also contributing to tele-end spherical correction.

G4 is a negative cemented pair. It participates in zooming and is the stabilization-capable group. The patent identifies its power as important to intermediate-focal-length spherical correction and states that part or all of this group may shift for image stabilization (¶0035).

G5 is the rear positive relay group. It contains five physical production elements, including a cemented pair and the hybrid aspherical element. Its axial motion contributes to the rear-subsystem magnification change represented by the patent's `Z5` condition.

The final zoom model preserves all five published variable intervals while removing one inactive source datum. Patent S27 is a flat air-to-air bookkeeping plane with no refractive action. It is omitted, and its spacing is added to the fixed 2.1000 mm air distance after source S26. This changes no axial station or optical power.

The D23 reversal is retained explicitly. Linear interpolation is therefore piecewise across the three patent states rather than imposing an artificial monotonic G3–G4 separation.

The patent does not publish clear apertures. All surface semi-diameters in the data file are modeling inferences derived from paraxial ray envelopes, the production mechanical envelope, and the patent optical section. They are not presented as patent dimensions. The stop location is source-published, but its physical semi-diameter is not; the authored base `STO.sd = 9.3585 mm` is the wide-state value inferred from the published FNO. Paraxial inversion of the three source FNO values gives state-effective stop radii of approximately 9.3585, 14.2439, and 9.3270 mm at wide, middle, and tele respectively; these are modeled effective apertures, not patent-published mechanical iris dimensions.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal length quoted on each element line is the standalone thick-element focal length in air stored by the final data file. It must not be confused with the equivalent power of a cemented stack or the in-situ power of a functional zoom group.

### G1 — Front positive group

#### D1: L1 + L2 — Cemented front pair

**L1:** `nd = 2.00100`, `νd = 29.13`. Glass: TAFD55-W (HOYA). Standalone `f = −150.498 mm`.<br>
**L2:** `nd = 1.49700`, `νd = 81.61`. Glass: FCD1 (HOYA). Standalone `f = +143.013 mm`.

L1 is a negative meniscus and L2 a biconvex positive element. Their cemented net equivalent focal length is +2883.970 mm, so the pair is nearly afocal compared with the complete G1 power of +91.527 mm. The pair therefore supplies strong internal index/dispersion contrast without itself carrying most of the first group's positive power.

This pairing follows the patent's explicit first-group strategy. L1 satisfies the high-index/high-dispersion condition `Nd1 > 1.84`, `νd1 < 35`, while L2 satisfies `Nd2 < 1.60`, `νd2 > 60`. The patent connects this material ordering to compactness and control of tele-end chromatic and spherical aberration (¶0025–¶0029).

#### L3 — Positive Meniscus

`nd = 1.59282`, `νd = 68.62`. Glass: FCD515 (HOYA). Standalone `f = +151.391 mm`.

L3 is the second air-separated positive member of G1. It satisfies the patent's third-material requirement `Nd3 < 1.65`, `νd3 > 60`. In combination with L2 it keeps two low-dispersion-coordinate positive elements near the front of the lens while leaving L4 available to raise index and complete the first-group power distribution.

#### L4 — Positive Meniscus

`nd = 1.72916`, `νd = 54.67`. Glass: TAC8 (HOYA). Standalone `f = +246.858 mm`.

L4 is the rearmost positive member of G1. Its index exceeds L3 by 0.13634, satisfying both `Nd4 − Nd3 > 0.02` and `Nd4 > Nd3 > Nd2`. Paragraph ¶0029 states that increasing the index toward the image side of the three positive elements is favorable for reducing first-group diameter while balancing tele-end spherical and chromatic correction.

### G2 — Negative variator group

#### L5 — Negative Meniscus, two aspherical surfaces

`nd = 1.85135`, `νd = 40.10`. Glass: M-TAFD305 (HOYA). Standalone `f = −21.949 mm`.

L5 is the object-side element of G2 and carries both aspherical surfaces 8A and 9A. The patent specifically states that an asphere at the most object-side position in G2 is favorable for distortion control (¶0036). Its strong negative standalone power and the curvature of 9A make it a principal contributor to the negative variator action.

#### L6 — Biconcave Negative

`nd = 1.77250`, `νd = 49.60`. Glass: TAF1 (HOYA) — 773496 class proxy, Δνd=+0.03. Standalone `f = −28.722 mm`.

L6 is the second negative component in G2. Its glass label is deliberately qualified: the final data uses a close HOYA TAF1 spectral proxy for the patent coordinate pair rather than claiming an exact procurement identity. Its negative power reinforces the compact G2 power level required by condition (6).

#### L7 — Biconvex Positive

`nd = 1.84666`, `νd = 23.78`. Glass: FDS90-SG (HOYA). Standalone `f = +19.210 mm`.

L7 is the only positive element inside G2 and has the strongest positive standalone power in that group. Its high index and low Abbe number provide a strong counter-term between the surrounding negative elements. The group nevertheless remains strongly negative at −12.9668 mm equivalent focal length.

#### L8 — Biconcave Negative

`nd = 1.77250`, `νd = 49.60`. Glass: TAF1 (HOYA) — 773496 class proxy, Δνd=+0.03. Standalone `f = −23.628 mm`.

L8 closes the variator with another negative element using the same qualified spectral proxy as L6. The four-element negative-positive-negative distribution after L5 allows the group to maintain high negative power while distributing curvature among several surfaces rather than concentrating it at a single interface.

### G3 — Positive group and stop-following group

#### L9 — Biconvex Positive

`nd = 1.72916`, `νd = 54.67`. Glass: TAC8 (HOYA). Standalone `f = +51.991 mm`.

L9 is the first of the three positive elements prescribed for G3. The stop sits 1.0000 mm ahead of L9 at every published zoom state, so the aperture and G3 translate together in the model as described by the patent.

#### L10 — Biconvex Positive

`nd = 1.49700`, `νd = 81.54`. Glass: S-FPL51 (OHARA). Standalone `f = +43.224 mm`.

L10 is a low-index, high-Abbe positive element in the high-flux region immediately behind the stop. Its catalog-resolved line indices provide a higher-quality spectral model than an Abbe-only approximation. It is not, by itself, evidence for an apochromatic designation.

#### D2: L11 + L12 — Cemented positive/negative pair

**L11:** `nd = 1.62263`, `νd = 58.16`. Glass: M-BACD15 (HOYA) — 623582 class proxy, Δνd=+0.01. Standalone `f = +37.360 mm`.<br>
**L12:** `nd = 1.92286`, `νd = 20.88`. Glass: E-FDS1-W (HOYA). Standalone `f = −49.336 mm`.

The D2 cemented pair has a net equivalent focal length of +147.407 mm. L11 carries aspherical surface 21A, while L12 supplies a very high-index, high-dispersion negative partner at the cemented interface.

The complete G3 remains much stronger than the D2 pair alone, with `f = +21.0544 mm`. Patent ¶0034 attributes the three-positive/one-negative arrangement to simultaneous control of wide-end lower coma and tele-end spherical aberration; L11/L12 forms the negative-ended part of that sequence.

### G4 — Negative stabilization-capable group

#### D3: L13 + L14 — Cemented negative/positive pair

**L13:** `nd = 1.82080`, `νd = 42.71`. Glass: M-TAFD51 (HOYA). Standalone `f = −19.479 mm`.<br>
**L14:** `nd = 1.80809`, `νd = 22.76`. Glass: FD225 (HOYA). Standalone `f = +40.605 mm`.

G4 is exactly this cemented pair, so its cemented net and functional-group equivalent focal lengths are both −35.9313 mm. Surface 24A on L13 is aspherical.

Patent ¶0035 identifies G4 as important to intermediate-focal-length spherical correction and states that, when used for image stabilization, an appropriate G4 focal length is needed to limit stabilization-induced image degradation. The data model represents the centered prescription only; it does not invent a lateral decenter amplitude.

### G5 — Rear positive group

#### L15 — Biconvex Positive

`nd = 1.49700`, `νd = 81.54`. Glass: S-FPL51 (OHARA). Standalone `f = +65.179 mm`.

L15 begins G5 with the same low-index, high-Abbe catalog glass used at L10. It provides positive rear-group power ahead of the more strongly powered cemented D4 pair.

#### D4: L16 + L17 — Cemented negative/positive pair

**L16:** `nd = 1.80610`, `νd = 33.27`. Glass: NBFD15-W (HOYA). Standalone `f = −29.196 mm`.<br>
**L17:** `nd = 1.51742`, `νd = 52.43`. Glass: S-NSL36 (OHARA). Standalone `f = +19.596 mm`.

D4 has a cemented net equivalent focal length of +46.231 mm. The index and dispersion contrast between L16 and L17 produces a positive pair even though the front member is strongly negative in isolation. This is a cemented-stack property; it should not be confused with the +66.7974 mm equivalent focal length of the complete G5.

#### H1: L18r + L18 — Hybrid aspherical production element 18

**L18r resin layer:** `nd = 1.51460`, `νd = 49.96`. Glass: Unmatched (hybrid asphere resin layer, nd=1.51460, νd=49.96). Standalone `f = +495.788 mm`.<br>
**L18 substrate:** `nd = 1.79952`, `νd = 42.22`. Glass: S-LAH52 (OHARA). Standalone `f = −23.578 mm`.

The 0.2000 mm L18r medium is modeled separately because the data schema must trace its refractive index and aspherical outer surface 33A. Physically, the resin layer and L18 glass substrate are counted as one production hybrid aspherical element. Their cemented net equivalent focal length is −24.693 mm.

The resin is intentionally left `Unmatched`; no `nC`, `nF`, `ng`, or `dPgF` values are invented for it. The identification of this thin layer as the resin portion of the manufacturer's hybrid element is a modeling inference supported by its thickness, cemented geometry, aspherical surface, and the production construction count.

#### L19 — Positive Meniscus

`nd = 1.80518`, `νd = 25.46`. Glass: FD60-W (HOYA). Standalone `f = +63.632 mm`.

L19 is the final positive element and completes the rear positive relay. The final air distance after surface 37 is the variable back focal distance, increasing from 40.1487 mm at wide to 89.5105 mm at tele in the published infinity states.

## Focus Mechanism

The focus status is `NO_INTERNAL_RECONSTRUCTION`. Example 7 publishes only infinity-focus zoom spacings and infinity-focus aberration plots. It does not provide a close-focus spacing table, focus-group travel, object-distance series, or another mechanism-constrained set of data from which a unique internal focusing solution could be recovered.

Accordingly, every focus pair in `var` is identical at each zoom state. The model therefore represents zoom motion only and does not simulate internal close-focus motion. No focus type—unit, inner, rear, or floating—is assigned from the patent.

The required `closeFocusM` field retains the manufacturer's 0.49 m minimum object distance as product metadata. That number is not converted into an assumed object distance from surface 1 or a reconstructed patent focus state. The product's PZD designation identifies its autofocus drive technology but does not resolve the missing internal optical motion.

## Aspherical Surfaces

Example 7 has five aspherical surfaces: 8A and 9A on L5, 21A on L11, 24A on L13, and 33A on the hybrid resin layer L18r. The patent equation is

`X(y) = (y²/R) / [1 + sqrt(1 − ε·y²/R²)] + A4·y⁴ + A6·y⁶ + A8·y⁸ + A10·y¹⁰`.

The patent's `ε` is not the standard LensVisualizer conic constant. Comparison with the standard denominator gives `K = ε − 1`. Every Example 7 asphere has `ε = 1.0000`, so all five surfaces are stored with `K = 0`. Because no scale factor is applied, the polynomial coefficients are copied without dimensional transformation.

## Chromatic Correction Strategy

The patent's principal chromatic argument is concentrated in G1. Rather than using the conventional three-element first group that the patent regards as limiting for a compact approximately 10× zoom, Example 7 uses one negative element followed by three positive elements and constrains their material coordinates explicitly.

The final data reproduce those coordinates exactly:

- L1: `nd = 2.00100`, `νd = 29.13` — very high index and high dispersion.
- L2: `nd = 1.49700`, `νd = 81.61` — low index and very high Abbe number.
- L3: `nd = 1.59282`, `νd = 68.62` — low-to-moderate index and high Abbe number.
- L4: `nd = 1.72916`, `νd = 54.67` — higher index than L3 and L2.

The resulting ordering satisfies the patent's material inequalities while distributing positive power across L2–L4. The catalog-derived line data also show substantial partial-dispersion contrast: L2 has stored `dPgF = +0.032056`, L3 `+0.017389`, while L4 has `−0.006868`. Those values are catalog-derived properties of the chosen coordinate matches, not patent-published glass identities.

Additional low-dispersion-coordinate elements appear at L10 and L15, both modeled as S-FPL51 with `νd = 81.54` and stored `dPgF = +0.031938`. High-index/high-dispersion partners occur in several cemented pairs, including L12 (`nd = 1.92286`, `νd = 20.88`) and L14 (`nd = 1.80809`, `νd = 22.76`). This provides the spectral model with real C/F/g-line contrast rather than relying on Abbe interpolation alone.

The availability of line-index data supports quantitative chromatic tracing for the catalog-resolved elements, but it does not justify labeling the lens APO. The patent and manufacturer sources used here do not make such a designation, and the unmatched hybrid resin layer remains an explicit spectral limitation.

## Image Stabilization

The production name includes VC, and the patent's five-group embodiment explicitly provides an optical stabilization mechanism. Claim 5 and ¶0016 state that part or all of G4 may move during shake correction; ¶0035 discusses the G4 focal-length range in terms of both intermediate-focal-length correction and stabilized image quality.

In the final data, G4 is the cemented L13/L14 pair with equivalent focal length −35.9313 mm and an asphere at 24A. The model is centered and sequential: it represents the G4 prescription and zoom spacing but does not add a decentered stabilization state because the patent does not publish a lateral travel table for Example 7.

Tamron's production metadata distinguishes the mounts mechanically. Canon EF and Nikon F versions use lens VC, while the Sony A version omits lens VC. All three mount IDs remain associated with the same production optical family in the data file; the mechanical stabilization difference does not alter the centered Example 7 prescription represented here.

## Aberration Correction Strategy

The patent's correction strategy is tied closely to the power distribution rather than to any single special element.

At the front, the four-element G1 is used to shorten the first-group focal length while avoiding the tele-end chromatic and spherical penalties that the patent associates with a compact three-element front group (¶0010–¶0011, ¶0030). The material-index progression across L2–L4 is part of that compactness strategy rather than a separate marketing classification.

G2 is deliberately strong and negative. Paragraph ¶0031 explains the trade: weakening G2 increases movement and front diameter, while making it too strong increases wide distortion, Petzval/astigmatic burden, zoom-dependent lower coma, and tele spherical aberration. The computed `f2/ft = −0.044519` lies inside the stated range.

G3 combines three positive elements with a final negative element. Paragraph ¶0034 identifies this sequence as favorable for wide-end lower-coma correction while retaining tele-end spherical correction. The image-side G3 asphere at 21A adds a local surface-shape degree of freedom where the F-number ray bundle remains substantial.

G4 provides a separate negative correction group after G3. Its spacing relative to G3 reverses between the middle and tele states, so the model preserves the non-monotonic D23 path rather than treating G4 as a simple monotonic compensator. The patent links G4 power particularly to intermediate-focal-length spherical correction and stabilization performance.

The rear G5 then returns the system to positive power and carries the final chromatic/aspherical correction resources, including an S-FPL51 element, a positive cemented D4 pair, and the hybrid aspherical element. The five-group system therefore distributes correction and zoom compensation through several moving subsystems instead of assigning all compensation to one rear group.
