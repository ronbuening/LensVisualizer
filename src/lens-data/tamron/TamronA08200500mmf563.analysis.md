## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP 2003-344768 A  
**Application Number:** JP 2002-155993  
**Filed:** 2002-05-29  
**Published:** 2003-12-03  
**Inventor:** Akio Arakawa  
**Applicant:** Tamron Co., Ltd.  
**Title:** 望遠ズームレンズ (Telephoto zoom lens)  
**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 1

The analyzed prescription is Example 1 of JP 2003-344768 A. The patent describes a six-functional-group zoom architecture arranged, from object to image, as positive L1, negative L2, positive L3, positive L4, aperture-stop group L5, and negative L6 (¶0010-¶0012; Fig. 1). Example 1 is the fixed source selected for the TAMRON SP AF 200-500mm f/5-6.3 Di LD [IF] production correlation represented by the data file.

The production correlation is supported by several convergent points rather than by an explicit Tamron statement that Model A08 uses this patent example:

1. The patent example spans 200.00, 300.32, and 490.00 mm with published FNO values of 5.160, 5.722, and 6.250 (¶0011; Table 2; Figs. 2-4), closely corresponding to the marketed 200-500mm f/5-6.3 range.
2. The prescription contains 13 glass elements in 10 air-separated physical groups, matching Tamron's published optical-construction count for Model A08.
3. Two elements, E3 and E8, share the unusually low-dispersion coordinate pair nd = 1.49700 and νd = 81.6. Tamron states that the production A08 uses two LD elements. The data therefore treats this as correlation evidence, not as a unique vendor-glass identification.
4. The patent assigns focusing to the rear negative L6 group (¶0020). That architecture is consistent with the production lens's [IF] designation, although the manufacturer does not publish the internal spacing table.
5. The patent was published in December 2003; Tamron lists Model A08 as a 2004 release.

Manufacturer specifications and patent design values are kept separate. Tamron markets the lens as 200-500mm f/5-6.3, with 2.5 m minimum object distance throughout the zoom range and 1:5.0 maximum magnification. The modeled prescription itself computes to 199.998212 mm and 489.988146 mm at the endpoints, and its common physical stop produces modeled wide-open f-numbers of 5.165511, 5.726846, and 6.238032 at the three authored zoom stations.

No scale factor is applied. The prescription remains at the patent's native dimensions, so no dimensional rescaling or aspheric-coefficient transformation is present.

## Optical Architecture

The data file contains 13 elements in 10 physical glass groups. Those physical groups are organized by the patent into five powered functional lens groups plus the aperture-stop group: L1 positive, L2 negative, L3 positive, L4 positive, L5 stop, and L6 negative. Independent subassembly tracing gives functional-group focal lengths of approximately +260.000 mm, -140.000 mm, +157.383 mm, +140.000 mm, and -70.000 mm for L1, L2, L3, L4, and L6 respectively. These are group-level paraxial descriptors; they are not interchangeable with the standalone focal lengths of individual elements or with each group's exact in-situ contribution to the complete zoom system.

The zoom action is accomplished by relative translation of the powered groups and stop while L2 is used as the fixed reference in the verification model. From 200 to 490 mm, L1 moves 93.459 mm objectward relative to L2, L3 moves 37.203 mm objectward, L4 moves 23.365 mm objectward, the stop moves 40.889 mm objectward, and L6 moves 58.412 mm objectward. The resulting movement ratio m1/m6 = 1.599997 reproduces the patent's Example 1 value of 1.6.

The D14 air spacing is non-monotonic across the three source stations: 5.551 mm, 20.159 mm, and 19.389 mm. This is a reversal of the inter-group gap rather than a reversal of L4 travel. With L2 fixed, the absolute L4 position continues monotonically objectward from wide to tele.

The strict project definition of a telephoto configuration is based on total lens track divided by effective focal length. Under that definition, the modeled TL/EFL ratios are approximately 1.335 at 200 mm, 1.011 at 300.32 mm, and 0.736 at 490 mm. Thus only the tele endpoint satisfies TL/EFL < 1, even though the patent title uses the general category “telephoto zoom lens.” No zoom station is retrofocus under the project criterion BFD > EFL; the corresponding BFD/EFL ratios are approximately 0.374, 0.325, and 0.272.

### Modeled extent, image plane, and omitted source planes

Two source planes are intentionally excluded from the ordinary sequential model. Source surface 1 is an infinite-radius air-air reference plane and contributes no refractive power. Source surface 26 is labeled FS and functions as a flare-stop/cutter reference rather than as an active refracting surface in the modeled path. Both remain part of the source interpretation but not of the authored active prescription.

Table 1 does not publish an IMG row. Its D25 spacing runs from the last refracting surface to FS26, not to the paraxial image plane (¶0012; Table 1). With L2 fixed, source FS26 lies at 159.910, 159.909, and 159.909 mm at the three zoom stations, while independent paraxial tracing places the corresponding raw image planes at 206.407882, 206.408513, and 206.403824 mm. Their mean, 206.406739 mm, is used as the common normalized image plane. The resulting authored FS26-to-image continuations are therefore state-specific—46.496739, 46.497739, and 46.497739 mm—rather than one identical continuation added separately to each D25 value. The infinity rear spacings in the data file are 74.884739, 97.665739, and 133.296739 mm at 200, 300.32, and 490 mm.

### Stop and inferred clear apertures

The aperture stop is source-published as surface 20, represented in the data as the single surface labeled `STO` (¶0012; Table 1). The patent publishes FNO values but no stop diameter. The physical stop semi-diameter of 12.168448 mm is consequently a modeling inference obtained by reconciling the three published FNO states with the computed entrance-pupil magnification. A single physical radius is used at all zoom positions; this produces the modeled `nominalFno` values recorded in the data rather than forcing the rounded patent labels onto a common stop.

The patent also does not publish usable semi-diameters for the glass surfaces. The authored clear apertures are therefore inferred geometry, not source facts. The front pair is modeled at 41.0-40.5 mm semi-diameter, intermediate groups near 20 mm, and L6 at 14.5 mm on surface 21 and 13.6 mm thereafter. These values are constrained by Tamron's 86 mm filter diameter, the proportions visible in patent Figure 1, marginal-ray envelopes, edge thickness, actual rim slope, and shared-band cross-gap clearance. The narrow 2.0 mm gap from surface 22 to 23 is the limiting rear-group geometry case.

All active refracting surfaces are spherical. There are no aspheric coefficients or conic conversions in this embodiment.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The element focal lengths below are the standalone isolated-element EFLs carried by the validated data file. They describe each glass element by itself in air. Cemented-pair powers and functional-group powers are separate quantities, and the behavior of any element inside the assembled zoom cannot be inferred by simply adding standalone dioptric powers.

### E1 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.2. Glass: 487702 class (vendor underdetermined). f = +569.736878 mm.

E1 is the front positive collector of functional group L1. Its standalone power is modest compared with the complete L1 subassembly, which verifies at approximately +260.000 mm. The relatively high Abbe number places E1 on the low-dispersion side of the front-group glass palette, but the patent supplies only nd and νd, so no anomalous-partial-dispersion behavior is assigned.

E1 is air-spaced from the cemented E2-E3 pair. This separation lets the front group distribute positive power across a weak positive meniscus and a cemented correcting pair rather than concentrating all convergence in a single steep element.

### E2 — Negative Meniscus, J1 Front Component

nd = 1.83400, νd = 37.2. Glass: 834372 class (vendor underdetermined). f = -318.781694 mm.

E2 is the negative, high-index component of the front cemented pair J1. Its standalone negative power is countered by E3, whose much lower dispersion and positive power dominate the pair's net sign. The E2-E3 cemented combination verifies at approximately +474.323 mm as an isolated doublet.

The distinction between the negative standalone power of E2 and the positive net power of J1 is important. E2 does not make L1 negative; it provides a negative contribution inside a front group whose total first-order power remains positive.

### E3 — Biconvex Positive, J1 Rear Component

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6. Glass: 497816 class (vendor underdetermined). f = +189.545095 mm.

E3 completes J1 and is the stronger positive member of the cemented pair. Its νd = 81.6 coordinate is one of two repeated low-dispersion-class positions in the prescription, the other being E8. This supports the selected production correlation to Tamron's statement that Model A08 uses two LD elements, but it does not uniquely identify a catalog melt.

At the cemented interface, the optical medium changes directly from E2 to E3; there is no synthetic cement layer in the data model. The pair contributes net positive power while combining markedly different index and dispersion coordinates.

### E4 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.6. Glass: 773496 class (vendor underdetermined). f = -68.140128 mm.

E4 is the strongest standalone negative element in functional group L2. Together with E5 it forms a net-negative zoom group whose verified group focal length is approximately -140.000 mm.

The negative L2 group is central to the focal-length change. In the normalized movement description it is held fixed while the other functional groups translate around it, making the wide-to-tele motion easy to compare without implying that L2 is mechanically immobile in every physical reference frame.

### E5 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: 805254 class (vendor underdetermined). f = +139.697408 mm.

E5 is the positive member of L2. Despite its positive standalone focal length, the E4-E5 subassembly remains net negative. Its low Abbe number contrasts with E4's more moderate dispersion and provides an ordinary first-order achromatizing degree of freedom inside the variator group.

No stronger chromatic interpretation is justified from νd alone. The data does not contain line indices or a uniquely resolved Sellmeier glass for this class.

### E6 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: 805254 class (vendor underdetermined). f = -226.378023 mm.

E6 is the negative front component of functional group L3. It uses the same nd/νd coordinate as E5 but with the opposite standalone sign because of its surface curvatures.

L3 as a complete air-spaced two-element group is positive, verifying at approximately +157.383 mm. E6 therefore acts as a correcting negative component within a positive compensating subassembly rather than as a negative functional group by itself.

### E7 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.58913, νd = 61.2. Glass: 589612 class (vendor underdetermined). f = +94.635907 mm.

E7 supplies the dominant positive standalone power in L3. Its higher Abbe number relative to E6 gives L3 a broad dispersion contrast while preserving the group's net positive power.

The E6-E7 spacing is an air gap, so L3 is not a cemented doublet. Its internal air space participates in the subassembly's first-order behavior and should not be collapsed into a simple thin-lens sum of the two standalone powers.

### E8 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6. Glass: 497816 class (vendor underdetermined). f = +234.175222 mm.

E8 is the second element in the prescription with the 1.49700/81.6 low-dispersion-class coordinate pair. It stands at the front of functional group L4 and provides a positive, relatively low-dispersion contribution before the J2 cemented pair.

The repeated coordinate at E3 and E8 is consistent with the production lens's two-LD-element specification. The correspondence remains a product-to-patent inference: neither the patent nor Tamron's product page publishes a vendor glass name for these positions.

### E9 — Biconvex Positive, J2 Front Component

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Glass: 516641 class (vendor underdetermined). f = +90.319736 mm.

E9 begins the cemented J2 pair and provides strong positive standalone power. Its comparatively high Abbe number is paired directly with E10's higher index and lower νd.

The isolated J2 cemented combination verifies at approximately +347.135 mm. That net positive doublet then works with E8 so that the complete L4 functional group verifies at approximately +140.000 mm.

### E10 — Negative Meniscus, J2 Rear Component

nd = 1.83400, νd = 37.2. Glass: 834372 class (vendor underdetermined). f = -120.501937 mm.

E10 is the negative member of J2. The same 834372 class also appears in E2, again demonstrating that equal material coordinates do not imply equal optical function: E2 participates in the front L1 cemented pair, whereas E10 corrects the rear side of the positive L4 doublet.

Its negative standalone power reduces the much stronger positive contribution of E9 without reversing the sign of the cemented pair.

### E11 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.6. Glass: 773496 class (vendor underdetermined). f = -91.692975 mm.

E11 is the first element of the rear focusing group L6. It is air-spaced from the J3 cemented pair and contributes negative standalone power to a functional group whose verified net focal length is approximately -70.000 mm.

Because L6 is the focusing group named by the patent, the optical role of E11 is inseparable from group translation in use. The standalone focal length describes its isolated power only; finite-focus behavior is governed by the motion of the complete L6 assembly.

### E12 — Negative Meniscus, J3 Front Component

nd = 1.69680, νd = 55.5. Glass: 697555 class (vendor underdetermined). f = -66.042211 mm.

E12 is the negative front member of the rear cemented pair J3. It has the strongest negative standalone power of that pair.

Together with E13, J3 verifies as a net-negative cemented doublet with an isolated EFL of approximately -320.563 mm. The full L6 group is substantially stronger in magnitude because J3 operates together with the separate negative E11 and the intervening air spacing.

### E13 — Positive Meniscus, J3 Rear Component

nd = 1.75520, νd = 27.5. Glass: 755275 class (vendor underdetermined). f = +81.582368 mm.

E13 completes the final cemented pair and provides positive standalone power against E12's stronger negative contribution. Its lower Abbe number relative to E12 supplies a substantial dispersion contrast at the back of the optical system.

The J3 pair remains net negative despite E13's positive sign, and the complete L6 assembly remains negative. During the constrained focus reconstruction, E13 therefore moves with E11 and E12 as part of one rigid rear group rather than as an independently floating correction element.

## Glass Identification / Selection

The patent provides d-line refractive index and Abbe number but does not name glass vendors or catalog designations. The validated data consequently stores derived six-digit coordinate classes instead of assigning a specific OHARA, HOYA, SCHOTT, HIKARI, SUMITA, or CDGM glass. A fresh current-catalog check finds an OHARA S-family candidate for every one of the nine coordinate classes with |Δn| no greater than 0.000003 and no difference at the patent's one-decimal νd precision; current HIKARI candidates are also close. HOYA's official six-vendor cross-reference places competing SCHOTT, OHARA, HIKARI, SUMITA, and CDGM types at the same or neighboring six-digit coordinates. A vendor-specific label would therefore overstate what the patent establishes.

No element carries nC, nF, ng, or dPgF because the patent does not publish those values and no unique catalog identity is established. The chromatic interpretation is therefore limited to nd/νd relationships. The data does not support an APO classification, a quantitative anomalous-partial-dispersion claim, or a vendor-specific Sellmeier model.

The strongest production-linked glass inference concerns E3 and E8. Tamron states that A08 contains two LD elements, and these are the only two elements sharing the very high νd = 81.6 coordinate. This is a plausible correspondence in the selected production correlation, but it is not a manufacturer-published mapping of element numbers to glass names.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent assigns focusing to the rear negative L6 group (¶0020) and presents close-distance aberration plots at magnification 1/40 (¶0023; Figs. 5-7), but it does not publish a close-focus spacing table for Example 1. The data therefore uses the focus status `CONSTRAINED_RECONSTRUCTION`, not `PUBLISHED`.

The reconstruction translates L6 as a rigid group while preserving the normalized image plane. Only the two air spaces adjacent to L6 are changed: the stop-to-L6 gap represented by `STO`, and the final L6-to-image gap represented by surface 25. Their sum remains constant at each zoom station. All other zoom-variable gaps retain identical infinity and close values.

Tamron's 2.5 m production minimum object distance is used as the finite-object calibration target. The solved L6 shifts and authored spacing endpoints are:

| Zoom station | L6 shift from infinity | `STO` gap: infinity → 2.5 m | Surface 25 BF: infinity → 2.5 m | Computed paraxial magnification at 2.5 m |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 200.00 mm | 5.162785 mm imageward | 19.704000 → 24.866785 mm | 74.884739 → 69.721955 mm | 1:11.8351 |
| 300.32 mm | 8.215044 mm imageward | 12.870000 → 21.085044 mm | 97.665739 → 89.450695 mm | 1:7.9146 |
| 490.00 mm | 13.985911 mm imageward | 2.181000 → 16.166911 mm | 133.296739 → 119.310829 mm | 1:4.9695 |

The reconstructed tele-end magnification at the patent's 490 mm station is close to Tamron's marketed 1:5.0 maximum magnification at 500 mm and 2.5 m. That agreement supports the constrained mechanism model, but it does not convert the reconstructed spacings into patent-published data.

The patent's 1/40 aberration figures describe a different finite-object state. Solving the same L6-only mechanism for β = -1/40 requires only about 1.48-1.62 mm of imageward L6 shift across the zoom range and corresponds to much longer subject distances than 2.5 m. Those values remain verification outputs rather than authored focus-control points.

## Chromatic Correction Strategy

At the level supported by the data, the design uses strong dispersion contrasts inside several functional groups rather than relying on a single front achromat. The front J1 pair combines the high-index 834372 class with the low-dispersion 497816 class; L2 juxtaposes 773496 and 805254 classes; L3 pairs the high-dispersion 805254 class against the higher-Abbe 589612 class; and L4 combines the second 497816 low-dispersion element with the J2 pair. The rear L6 group then introduces another set of index/dispersion contrasts in E11-E13.

Tamron specifically attributes reduced axial chromatic aberration in the production A08 to two LD elements. In the selected patent correlation, E3 and E8 are the natural candidates because both have nd = 1.49700 and νd = 81.6. The analysis does not extend that manufacturer statement into an apochromatic claim: no g-line data, anomalous partial-dispersion value, or unique Sellmeier glass is available for these elements.

The dispersion strategy is therefore described as a multi-group achromatizing allocation visible in the nd/νd coordinates, with two particularly low-dispersion positions. More specific secondary-spectrum conclusions would require spectral information absent from this embodiment.

## Conditional Expressions

The patent gives five inequalities governing the relationship between functional-group powers and zoom movement. Independent tracing of the model reproduces the Example 1 values printed in ¶0016:

| Condition | Patent bound | Computed from validated data | Example 1 printed value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| f1 / ft | 0.45-0.58 | 0.530612 | 0.531 |
| \|f2\| / ft | 0.25-0.35 | 0.285714 | 0.286 |
| f3 / ft | 0.30-0.35 | 0.321190 | 0.321 |
| \|f6\| / ft | 0.12-0.18 | 0.142857 | 0.143 |
| m1 / m6 | 1.2-2.0 | 1.599997 | 1.6 |

Here ft is the tele-end focal length used by the patent, while f1, f2, f3, and f6 are the paraxial focal lengths of the corresponding functional groups. The fifth expression compares wide-to-tele movement of L1 and L6. All five computed values lie within the specified intervals and round to the values printed for Example 1.

These conditions explain the broad first-order allocation more directly than the standalone element focal lengths. L1 is positive but relatively weak compared with the tele focal length; L2 provides the principal negative zoom-group power; L3 is a moderate positive compensator; and L6 is a comparatively strong negative rear group whose motion can serve both zoom compensation and focusing without requiring a large rear lens diameter.

## Verification Summary

Independent reduced-angle y-ν tracing and an ABCD matrix calculation recover effective focal lengths of 199.998212, 300.316300, and 489.988146 mm at the three patent zoom stations. Matrix determinants remain unity to numerical precision, providing a first-order cross-check on the sequential model.

The common inferred stop radius of 12.168448 mm gives modeled f-numbers of 5.165511, 5.726846, and 6.238032. These differ slightly from the patent labels 5.160, 5.722, and 6.250 because the latter are source-rounded states and the data enforces a single physical iris size across zoom.

The signed Petzval sum, evaluated surface by surface as φ/(n·n′), is +0.0003146496 mm⁻¹. This is a computed prescription property, not a manufacturer specification.

The inferred semi-diameter model satisfies the independent geometry checks applied to all authored zoom/focus endpoints. The minimum computed element edge thickness is about 0.909 mm, the maximum spherical rim-slope angle is about 23.923°, and the worst shared-band cross-gap intrusion is at surfaces 22→23, where the 1.754953 mm sag intrusion occupies about 0.8775 of the 2.0 mm gap. Representative off-axis diagnostics show one tele-end boundary vignette first occurring at the front surface rather than at a cemented junction.

The tele D6 value in Table 2 is 135.377 mm. A plausible low-resolution reading of 136.377 mm would move the computed tele EFL to approximately 484.412 mm and is therefore inconsistent with the source's 490.00 mm state. The data retains 135.377 mm; the rejected alternative is a low-resolution transcription/OCR ambiguity, not a correction to the patent itself.

The model contains exactly one active stop and no aspheric surfaces. Source surface 1 and FS26 are omitted only because they are inactive in the ordinary sequential path, while the optical effect of the rear reference-plane normalization is preserved in the authored surface-25 spacing. No sensor cover glass, filter plate, folded path, or scaling operation is introduced.
