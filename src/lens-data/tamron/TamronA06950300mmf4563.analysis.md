**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 1

This prescription transcribes Example 1 of US 2024/0295723 A1 and correlates it with the **TAMRON 50-300mm f/4.5-6.3 Di III VC VXD (A069)**. The patent does not identify Model A069 by name, so the link is a modeling correlation rather than manufacturer confirmation.

The correlation is supported by several independent points that converge on the same production lens:

1. Example 1 contains 19 physical lens elements in 14 air-separated groups, matching Tamron's published 19-element / 14-group construction for A069.
2. The patent design covers 51.5094-290.8673 mm at F/4.635-6.489, close to the marketed 50-300 mm f/4.5-6.3 range without applying any scale factor.
3. Example 1 contains two elements at `nd = 1.437001, νd = 95.10` and two at `nd = 1.496997, νd = 81.61`. Tamron describes the production lens as containing two XLD and two LD elements, and its published optical-construction diagram places those two XLD members in the front-group positions corresponding to L2/L3 and the two LD members in positions corresponding to L5/L10. This positional and numerical correspondence strengthens the correlation, but it does not identify any individual patent glass as a particular production XLD or LD material.
4. The patent provides a laterally movable vibration-compensation subgroup G2V and an internally moving fourth group G4 for focusing. Tamron specifies VC stabilization and a VXD linear-motor autofocus system for A069.
5. The patent's February 2023 priority predates the production lens's June 27, 2024 release.

The patent prescription is retained at **scale `s = 1.000000`**. Marketing and design values are therefore kept separate rather than forcing the patent endpoints to exactly 50 and 300 mm. The patent's Example 1 full fields are 45.8330°, 15.6744°, and 8.2302° from the published half-angles; Tamron markets a diagonal angle-of-view range of 46°48′ to 8°15′ for the production lens.

Two source/modeling qualifications are important. First, the searchable Example 1 prose at ¶0123 contains an apparent element-number corruption, calling the positive meniscus in G2 “lens 16” and the positive member of the cemented G2V pair “lens 18.” Figure 1 and the numerical surface sequence require **L6** and **L8**, respectively; the prescription therefore follows those figure/table-consistent labels, with the source corruption documented separately. Second, Example 1 publishes no surface semi-diameters and no physical stop diameter. Those apertures are modeled in the data file and are not patent-listed dimensions.

## Optical Architecture

Example 1 is a five-major-group zoom with paraxial power sequence **positive - negative - positive - negative - negative**. The 19 elements are divided into 14 air-separated physical groups but are organized by the patent into G1 through G5, with G3 subdivided into G3A and G3B and G2 containing the transverse vibration-compensation subgroup G2V. The aperture stop lies inside the G3 assembly between L9 and the cemented L10+L11 pair (¶0121, ¶0124).

Independent paraxial computation from the final data file gives the following major-group powers:

| Group | Construction | Paraxial focal length | Function in the model |
|---|---|---:|---|
| G1 | L1+L2 cemented pair, then L3 | +136.357 mm | Positive front collector |
| G2 | L4, L5, L6, cemented L7+L8 | -27.786 mm | Principal negative variator; contains G2V |
| G3 | G3A + G3B | +31.671 mm | Positive relay/converging group around the stop |
| G4 | Cemented L15+L16 | -41.622 mm | Negative internal focusing group |
| G5 | L17, L18, L19 | -200.779 mm | Weak net-negative rear group |

G3 is itself split into two positive subassemblies. G3A, containing L9, the stop, and cemented L10+L11, computes to +56.562 mm; G3B, containing L12 and cemented L13+L14, computes to +43.026 mm. The 11.700 mm air interval between the two portions is the largest fixed internal gap in G3 and is one of the quantities used by the patent's conditional expressions.

Zooming is accomplished through changes in five axial spacings. From wide to telephoto, the patent states that G1 moves toward the object, G2 toward the image, and G3, G4, and G5 toward the object (¶0128). The final data reproduce those endpoint directions. Computed relative to a fixed image plane, the wide-to-tele endpoint shifts are approximately 57.238 mm objectward for G1, 8.872 mm imageward for G2, 21.189 mm objectward for G3, 21.268 mm objectward for G4, and 20.379 mm objectward for G5.

The motion is not everywhere monotonic. G2 moves imageward from the wide state to the intermediate state and then partially reverses objectward toward the telephoto state. The published D25 and D28 gaps around G4 likewise reverse at the intermediate zoom position. These reversals are preserved explicitly by the three-point zoom table rather than approximated by a single endpoint interpolation.

Using the project's geometric definition, the design is **telephoto only at the telephoto endpoint**: the air-equivalent first-surface-to-image track divided by EFL is approximately 3.202 at wide, 1.279 at the intermediate state, and 0.764 at telephoto. No state is retrofocus because the paraxial back focal distance remains shorter than the EFL throughout the zoom range.

The selected embodiment is entirely spherical. No aspheric surface, conic constant, or polynomial coefficient is present in Example 1, so no asphere conversion or scaling transform is applicable.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 + L2 — Front Cemented Pair D1

**L1:** `nd = 1.804198, νd = 46.50`. Glass: `804465 class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = -151.404 mm`.

L1 is a negative meniscus and the negative member of the first cemented pair. Its relatively high refractive index allows appreciable bending with moderate curvature. In isolation its power is negative; it should not be described as a positive front element merely because it belongs to positive G1.

**L2:** `nd = 1.437001, νd = 95.10`. Glass: `437951 class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = +145.365 mm`.

L2 is a positive meniscus with an exceptionally high Abbe number. It nearly balances L1's standalone negative power at the cemented interface. The computed **L1+L2 cemented net focal length is +4150.845 mm**, so the pair is almost afocal in first order even though the individual members are strong. This distinction between standalone and cemented net power is important: the front group's useful positive power is supplied primarily by the combined action of the pair with L3, not by treating either member independently.

### L3 — Biconvex Positive

`nd = 1.437001, νd = 95.10`. Glass: `437951 class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = +140.124 mm`.

L3 repeats the same very-low-dispersion coordinate as L2 but is air-spaced behind the front cemented pair. Its positive power converts the nearly neutral L1+L2 pair into the net-positive G1 assembly. The complete G1 focal length is +136.357 mm.

The repeated `νd = 95.10` coordinate in L2 and L3 is consistent with a design that places very low dispersion in the front positive group. Because the patent provides only d-line index and Abbe number, no claim is made here about anomalous partial dispersion or apochromatic correction.

### L4 — Negative Meniscus

`nd = 1.696802, νd = 55.46`. Glass: `697555 class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = -50.817 mm`.

L4 begins G2 and supplies strong negative power immediately after the wide-ranging D5 zoom gap. Its position at the front of the negative variator means that the large change in G1-G2 separation directly changes the system's overall magnification.

### L5 — Biconcave Negative

`nd = 1.496997, νd = 81.61`. Glass: `497816 class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = -102.335 mm`.

L5 is a lower-index, high-Abbe negative element placed behind L4. It adds negative power while using a substantially less dispersive coordinate than L4. The same `497816` coordinate reappears in the positive L10 element after the stop, but the data do not identify either as a specific vendor melt.

### L6 — Positive Meniscus

`nd = 1.647690, νd = 33.84`. Glass: `648338 class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = +66.027 mm`.

L6 supplies positive counterpower inside the otherwise negative G2 assembly. The rendered patent prose at ¶0123 is the location of the “lens 16” numbering corruption; Figure 1 and the surface table identify this element unambiguously as **L6**.

Its relatively low Abbe number compared with L5 also makes the sequence more than a simple stack of same-dispersion negative elements. The exact chromatic consequence is not inferred beyond the d-line/Abbe information actually stored in the data file.

### L7 + L8 — Cemented Vibration-Compensation Group G2V

**L7:** `nd = 1.729160, νd = 54.10`. Glass: `S-LAL19 (OHARA catalog-equivalent; production supplier unspecified)`. Standalone `f = -26.112 mm`.

L7 is a biconcave negative element and the stronger standalone member of the vibration-compensation pair.

**L8:** `nd = 1.858833, νd = 30.00`. Glass: `859300 class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = +49.341 mm`.

L8 is a positive meniscus cemented directly to L7. The patent's prose corruption calling this “lens 18” is resolved to L8 from Figure 1 and the numerical sequence.

Together L7+L8 compute to **-54.871 mm** and constitute G2V. The pair remains part of the net-negative G2 group in the centered prescription. During vibration correction the patent specifies that G2V moves perpendicular to the optical axis; no axial zoom spacing is assigned to that transverse stabilization motion.

### L9 — Biconvex Positive

`nd = 1.691002, νd = 54.82`. Glass: `691548 class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = +49.217 mm`.

L9 begins the positive G3 assembly and lies immediately ahead of the aperture stop. It is the only standalone element in G3A ahead of the stop; the remainder of G3A is the cemented L10+L11 pair behind it.

The stop location is directly published by the patent as surface 17. Its physical diameter is not published, however, so the stop semi-diameter in the data file is a modeling inference rather than a source dimension.

### L10 + L11 — Post-Stop Cemented Pair D3

**L10:** `nd = 1.496997, νd = 81.61`. Glass: `497816 class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = +35.216 mm`.

L10 is a biconvex positive element using the second occurrence of the `497816` low-dispersion coordinate.

**L11:** `nd = 1.903658, νd = 31.31`. Glass: `904313 class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = -29.229 mm`.

L11 is a high-index biconcave negative element cemented to L10. Although both members are individually strong, the cemented pair computes to a much weaker **-231.209 mm**. In situ, the pair follows the stop and combines with positive L9 so that G3A remains net positive at +56.562 mm.

This is another case where standalone element powers are not a substitute for the net behavior of the cemented unit or the complete functional group.

### L12 — Biconvex Positive

`nd = 1.720467, νd = 34.71`. Glass: `720347 class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = +40.038 mm`.

L12 begins G3B. Its surface-table Abbe value is rounded to 34.71; the patent's Table 1 carries 34.707 for the corresponding conditional-expression quantity `νd3B`.

A current catalog coordinate close to this pair can reproduce the patent's partial-dispersion condition, but the patent does not name a vendor and the data therefore retain the neutral `720347` class rather than a named catalog melt. No `nC`, `nF`, `ng`, or `dPgF` values are authored for L12.

### L13 + L14 — Rear G3B Cemented Pair D4

**L13:** `nd = 1.540720, νd = 47.20`. Glass: `541472 class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = +29.064 mm`.

L13 is a biconvex positive element and the positive member of the rear G3B cemented pair.

**L14:** `nd = 1.950000, νd = 29.37`. Glass: `950294 class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = -25.182 mm`.

L14 is a very high-index biconcave negative element. The cemented L13+L14 pair computes to **-291.245 mm**, substantially weaker than either standalone member. Together with positive L12, G3B remains net positive at +43.026 mm.

The G3A/G3B combination therefore illustrates the design's repeated use of strong positive and negative element powers to build a moderate positive group. The interpretation here is first-order: higher-order aberration roles are not assigned without corresponding patent statements or dedicated computation.

### L15 + L16 — Cemented Focusing Group G4

**L15:** `nd = 1.921189, νd = 23.96`. Glass: `921240 class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = +36.654 mm`.

L15 is a high-index, low-Abbe biconvex positive element.

**L16:** `nd = 1.799520, νd = 42.24`. Glass: `800422 class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = -19.352 mm`.

L16 is the stronger negative member of the pair. Cementing L15 to L16 produces a **net negative group of -41.622 mm**, which is the complete G4 focusing group.

The patent explicitly assigns focus to G4 rather than to either element individually. From infinity toward a nearer object, the complete cemented pair moves toward the image side (¶0128). The selected Example 1 does not publish the corresponding close-focus D25/D28 values, so the data file does not invent them.

### L17 — Biconvex Positive

`nd = 1.575006, νd = 41.51`. Glass: `575415 class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = +30.643 mm`.

L17 begins G5 with substantial positive standalone power. Its placement after the focusing group means that it participates in the final relay to the image plane rather than in the axial focus translation assigned to G4.

### L18 — Biconcave Negative

`nd = 1.900433, νd = 37.37`. Glass: `900374 class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = -38.680 mm`.

L18 is a high-index biconcave negative element. Together with L17 and L19 it contributes to a G5 whose total first-order power is much weaker than any one of the three standalone elements.

### L19 — Negative Meniscus

`nd = 1.870705, νd = 40.73`. Glass: `871407 class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = -57.600 mm`.

L19 is the final powered element before the image-side clearance. The complete L17-L19 rear group computes to **-200.779 mm**, so G5 is weakly negative in first order despite L17's strong positive standalone power.

The original patent table places a 2.500 mm cover-glass plate (`nd = 1.516798`) after L19, followed by 1.000 mm of air to the image plane. LensVisualizer excludes sensor cover glass from the ordinary lens prescription; its first-order effect is retained through the documented air-equivalent rear-spacing normalization described below.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent identifies **G4, the cemented L15+L16 negative group**, as the focusing group. During focus from infinity toward a near-distance object, G4 moves toward the image side (¶0128). Tamron identifies the production autofocus drive as VXD, but the patent prescription itself describes the optical group motion rather than the production actuator implementation.

For Example 1, the patent publishes only the three **infinity-focus zoom states**. It does not provide close-focus values for the two gaps surrounding G4. The focus status is therefore **`NO_INTERNAL_RECONSTRUCTION`**. All `[d_infinity, d_close]` pairs in the data file are identical, and the modeled maximum focus-induced gap change is exactly 0.000 mm at all three zoom positions.

This means the focus control is deliberately non-physical in the present data set: it does not simulate near-focus optical travel. The top-level `closeFocusM = 0.22` is production metadata representing the shortest published MOD, not a conjugate used to solve G4 motion. Tamron publishes 0.22 m at 50 mm and 0.9 m at 300 mm, with maximum magnifications of 1:2 and 1:3.1 respectively. Those product values are not inverted into internal D25/D28 spacings.

The mechanism constraint is nevertheless clear. If G3 and G5 are held fixed at a selected zoom position, a pure axial translation of G4 must change D25 and D28 by equal and opposite amounts, conserving their sum. Without a published displacement or a sufficiently constrained internal conjugate, that relationship does not determine a unique close-focus state.

## Image Stabilization

The patent defines the cemented L7+L8 pair as vibration-compensation subgroup **G2V** within the negative G2 variator. During image-blur correction, G2V moves in a direction perpendicular to the optical axis (¶0128). Its centered paraxial focal length is -54.871 mm, compared with -27.786 mm for the complete G2 group; the patent's conditional Expression (5) uses the ratio `f2V/f2` to constrain this relationship.

The production A069 is specified by Tamron as having VC stabilization. This functional agreement is one of the production-correlation criteria, but the patent does not provide a transverse decenter range for G2V in Example 1. The data file therefore models the centered prescription only and does not add a synthetic stabilization displacement.

## Verification Summary

The final TypeScript object was independently re-read and traced rather than relying on the Stage 1 transcription arrays. Sequential reduced-angle propagation and a separate ABCD chain agree to a maximum absolute matrix/basis-ray difference of `1.421 × 10^-14`.

| State | Patent focal length | Computed EFL | Residual | Patent FNO | Air-equivalent track |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Wide | 51.5094 mm | 51.509479 mm | +0.000079 mm | 4.635 | 164.928909 mm |
| Intermediate | 149.9986 mm | 149.998966 mm | +0.000366 mm | 5.567 | 191.906109 mm |
| Telephoto | 290.8673 mm | 290.864262 mm | -0.003038 mm | 6.489 | 222.166809 mm |

The largest focal-length residual is about `1.0 × 10^-5` relative at the telephoto endpoint and is consistent with the precision of the printed radii, indices, and spacings. The computed Petzval sum is `+0.0007052290 mm^-1`, using the project convention `φ/(n·n′)` surface by surface.

Example 1's source surfaces 35-36 form a sensor cover plate: 2.500 mm at `nd = 1.516798`, followed by 1.000 mm of air to the image plane. Sensor cover glass is excluded from the LensVisualizer lens prescription. Its first-order axial effect is retained as `2.500/1.516798 = 1.648208924 mm` of air-equivalent distance, so the authored surface-34-to-image distances are 20.648208924, 25.425708924, and 41.027408924 mm across the three zoom states. The small residual differences from the independently traced paraxial BFD are not “corrected” by modifying the patent prescription.

The stop **position** is a source fact: patent surface 17 is the aperture stop. Its **diameter is inferred** because Example 1 gives FNO but no physical aperture size. The current LensVisualizer runtime uses each state's `nominalFno` to set the nominal entrance-pupil semi-diameter as `EFL/(2·FNO)` and then traces that finite on-axis ray with exact Snell refraction to obtain the physical stop radius. The resulting modeled stop semi-diameters are approximately 10.2014, 10.1968, and 10.3381 mm across the three zoom states; the stored wide-state seed is `STO.sd = 10.201416 mm`. Because FNO is an input to this normalization rather than an independently published stop dimension, these figures verify runtime consistency rather than independently re-derive the patent's FNO values.

All ordinary surface semi-diameters are likewise inferred rather than patent-published. After the Figure 1 silhouette refinement, the final geometry audit gives a minimum element edge thickness of 0.225 mm, a maximum actual spherical rim-slope angle of 43.714°, a worst positive shared-gap intrusion of 0.8825 of the available gap against the 0.90 policy limit, and zero required render trim. At the project's default 0.6-field off-axis sampling, wide and intermediate states pass all sampled pupil rays; at telephoto the most negative `-0.75` sample is vignetted at surface 1, an ordinary physical aperture boundary rather than a numerical failure.

No scale factor is applied, no close-focus state is reconstructed, and no aspheric coefficient transform exists because Example 1 is all-spherical. The only deliberate prescription normalization is the documented sensor-cover-glass omission and air-equivalent rear spacing.
