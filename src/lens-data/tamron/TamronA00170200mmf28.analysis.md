## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2008/0212200 A1  
**Application Number:** 12/071,968  
**Priority:** March 1, 2007 (JP 2007-051816)  
**Filed:** February 28, 2008  
**Published:** September 4, 2008  
**Inventors:** Dayong Li; Akio Arakawa  
**Assignee / Applicant:** None identified on the selected US publication front page  
**Title:** “Telephoto Zoom Lens”  
**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 1 / Embodiment 1  
**Production correlation:** TAMRON SP AF 70-200mm f/2.8 Di LD [IF] MACRO (A001), treated as the fixed correlation for this prescription; the patent itself does not identify the production lens.

US 2008/0212200 A1 describes a four-functional-group, large-aperture inner-focusing zoom in which the first group is positive, the second negative, the third positive, and the fourth positive. The first group is divided into a stationary foremost subgroup G1F and a rear subgroup G1R that translates for focusing; the second and third groups move for zooming while the first and fourth groups remain stationary (¶¶0013–0024, ¶0041). Example 1 is the prescription transcribed in the companion data file.

The production correlation is inferential rather than manufacturer-confirmed. Several independent features nevertheless converge on the selected A001 model:

1. The patent Example 1 and the production lens both use 18 elements in 13 air-separated physical groups.
2. The production specification is 70–200 mm at f/2.8, while the selected numerical example is published at 71.5 / 117.4 / 194.0 mm with Fno = 2.88. The final model keeps these marketing and design quantities separate.
3. The patent specifies foremost-group inner focusing and a 0.95 m close-focus target; Tamron specifies 0.95 m minimum focus throughout the A001 zoom range.
4. At the reconstructed 194 mm close state, the final data trace gives a paraxial magnification of −0.318785, or approximately 1:3.1369, consistent with Tamron's rounded 1:3.1 maximum-magnification specification at 200 mm and 0.95 m.
5. Tamron's construction diagram identifies three LD positions. The patent prescription contains three high-Abbe elements at L5, L12, and L14, each with nd = 1.49700 and νd = 81.6, in corresponding regions of the optical train. This positional agreement supports the correlation without establishing a vendor glass identity.

The data file therefore uses the normalized product name `TAMRON SP AF 70-200mm f/2.8 Di LD [IF] MACRO`, the production focal range `[70, 200]`, and production aperture `2.8`, while retaining independently computed design endpoints of 71.499670 and 194.001215 mm and the modeled F-number of 2.88. The production variants represented by the same optical formula are recorded as Canon EF, Nikon F, Pentax K, and Sony A, with `135-full-frame` as the intended image-format classification.

No uniform scaling is applied. The numerical prescription is modeled at the patent's native dimensions.

## Optical Architecture

The selected numerical example is best described, under the project's first-order terminology, as a four-functional-group **positive–negative–positive–positive zoom** with a subdivided positive first group and a stationary positive rear group. The patent's title uses the term “Telephoto Zoom Lens,” but the final paraxial model does not satisfy the project's strict `TL/EFL < 1` telephoto criterion at any of the three authored zoom states. Source terminology and computed architectural classification are therefore kept distinct.

The 18 glass elements form 13 air-separated physical groups. Five of those groups are cemented doublets: D1 = L1+L2, D2 = L3+L4, D3 = L6+L7, D4 = L8+L9, and D5 = L12+L13. These physical groups are organized into four functional groups:

| Functional group | Elements | Verified paraxial focal length | Motion and function |
|---|---|---:|---|
| G1 | L1–L5 | +106.2269 mm | Stationary during zoom; split into stationary G1F and moving G1R for focus |
| G2 | L6–L10 | −31.8824 mm | Negative variator; translates imageward as focal length increases |
| G3 | L11–L13 | +85.9052 mm | Positive compensating group; moves non-monotonically during zoom |
| G4 | L14–L18 | +107.8650 mm | Stationary rear group containing the aperture stop |

The first group's internal division is the principal architectural feature. D1 forms G1F and has a verified cemented net focal length of −1031.5928 mm. The remainder, G1R, consists of D2 plus the air-spaced L5 and has a verified net focal length of +99.4041 mm. Their combination makes G1 positive while allowing only the rear subgroup to move for focus. The patent explicitly associates the negative G1F with reduced focusing displacement and reduced eclipse risk at short distance (¶0027).

The patent prose contains one direct contradiction that matters to the physical grouping. Paragraph 0026 calls G1R a three-piece cemented composite, but the numerical prescription and Fig. 1 show a real 0.200 mm air gap between L4 and L5. The data file follows the numerical table and drawing: L3+L4 are cemented as D2, while L5 is a separate singlet. The 0.200 mm air gap is retained.

Zooming is controlled by the four published variable gaps D3, D8, D16, and D21. At infinity, G2 shifts by 0.000 / +22.698 / +36.610 mm from the wide position at the three patent control points. G3 shifts by 0.000 / +11.404 / +6.512 mm, so it reverses direction between the middle and 194 mm states. The sum `d8 + d16 + d21` remains 46.147 mm at all three infinity states, confirming internal group motion within a constant 236.303 mm first-surface-to-image optical track.

The aperture stop is source-published as surface 24 inside G4, between L14 and L15 (¶0020, ¶0032, Example 1 table). Its location is therefore not inferred. The patent does not publish its physical diameter; the data file uses an inferred stop semi-diameter of 16.69756 mm, solved from the published constant F/2.88 and independently verified entrance-pupil geometry.

The patent does not publish clear semi-diameters. All authored surface semi-diameters are modeling inferences constrained by the patent section, Tamron's construction drawing, the F/2.88 marginal bundle, field rays, edge thickness, actual rim slope, cross-gap clearance, and off-axis containment. The largest modeled optical semi-diameter is 37.0 mm. The declared `gapSagFrac = 0.97` is a geometry-policy override used because the default 0.90 shared-gap threshold would reject a physically clear region required by the 194 mm F/2.88 marginal bundle; the narrowest verified physical shared-gap clearance remains +0.100443 mm.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are verified **standalone element focal lengths in air** from the final data file. Cemented net powers and functional-group powers are stated separately where useful. Standalone values do not by themselves describe an element's in-situ contribution inside the complete zoom.

### D1 — L1 + L2: Stationary G1F Cemented Pair

**L1 — Negative Meniscus.** nd = 1.78590, νd = 43.9. Glass: `786439 coordinate class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone f = −130.5806 mm.  
**L2 — Biconvex Positive.** nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.4. Glass: `487704 coordinate class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone f = +149.7056 mm.

L1 and L2 are cemented and together form D1, which is also the complete G1F subgroup. Their verified cemented net focal length is −1031.5928 mm: far weaker in magnitude than either element considered alone. The pair therefore demonstrates why the project distinguishes standalone element power from cemented net power.

The patent requires G1F to have negative refractive power and specifically assigns the high-Abbe convex member a condition on νn1 (¶¶0015, 0018, 0027, 0030). At the d-line/Abbe level, the pair places the lower-Abbe negative meniscus against the higher-Abbe positive element. The source attributes this arrangement to chromatic control during focusing, but the present data contains no line indices or partial-dispersion data from which a stronger secondary-spectrum or apochromatic claim could be computed.

D1 remains fixed during both zooming and focusing. Its weak negative net power precedes the positive moving G1R subgroup and contributes to the positive net power of G1 only in combination with the rest of that group.

### D2 — L3 + L4: Front Pair of the Moving G1R Subgroup

**L3 — Negative Meniscus.** nd = 1.69895, νd = 30.1. Glass: `699301 coordinate class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone f = −420.2808 mm.  
**L4 — Biconvex Positive.** nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.4. Glass: `487704 coordinate class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone f = +132.7216 mm.

L3 and L4 form cemented doublet D2 with a verified net focal length of +196.5274 mm. The pair combines a comparatively low-Abbe negative meniscus with a high-Abbe positive element, producing a weak positive cemented assembly before L5.

D2 moves as part of G1R during focusing. The front radius of L3 is R1R = +87.2384 mm, the curvature quantity used by patent condition (3). The data preserves this value directly from surface 4.

Although ¶0026 describes all three G1R elements as cemented, D2 terminates at surface 6 and is followed by 0.200 mm of air before L5. The final model follows the numerical prescription rather than altering it to match that prose sentence.

### L5 — Positive G1R Singlet

**L5 — Biconvex Positive.** nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6. Glass: `497816 coordinate class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone f = +190.5956 mm.

L5 is the second positive component of the moving G1R subgroup and is air-spaced from D2. Together D2 and L5 form a G1R subgroup with verified in-situ group focal length +99.4041 mm. G1R is consequently much stronger than D2 or L5 considered alone, illustrating the importance of their separation and combined ray transfer.

L5's νd = 81.6 is the same high-Abbe coordinate used by L12 and L14, the G3/G4 positive elements that satisfy the patent's high-Abbe condition; L5 itself lies in G1. In the production correlation, its position is also consistent with one of Tamron's three marked LD elements. Because the patent does not supply a vendor, line indices, or partial-dispersion data, the data file retains only the coordinate class rather than naming a specific ED glass.

### D3 — L6 + L7: Front Cemented Pair of G2

**L6 — Positive Meniscus.** nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.8. Glass: `847238 coordinate class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone f = +91.3468 mm.  
**L7 — Biconcave Negative.** nd = 1.64000, νd = 60.2. Glass: `640602 coordinate class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone f = −59.3417 mm.

D3 has a verified cemented net focal length of −169.1715 mm. Its sign is negative even though L6 alone is positive, because the stronger standalone negative power of L7 and the cemented interface determine the pair's net result.

This doublet begins the negative G2 zoom group. The large difference in νd between its members provides strong first-order dispersion opposition, but no vendor-specific dispersion curves are authored. The pair's role in the model is therefore described in terms of its verified net negative power and placement rather than an unverified secondary-spectrum claim.

### D4 — L8 + L9: Central Cemented Pair of G2

**L8 — Biconcave Negative.** nd = 1.64000, νd = 60.2. Glass: `640602 coordinate class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone f = −66.3229 mm.  
**L9 — Positive Meniscus.** nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.8. Glass: `847238 coordinate class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone f = +127.4787 mm.

D4 has a verified cemented net focal length of −134.5329 mm. Its material pairing reverses the sign order used by D3: the higher-Abbe element is negative and the lower-Abbe element positive. The net result remains negative.

D3 and D4 together form most of the front portion of G2. Their alternating positive and negative components allow the functional group to reach a substantially stronger verified net power of −31.8824 mm once L10 and the intervening air gaps are included.

### L10 — Rear Negative Singlet of G2

**L10 — Biconcave Negative.** nd = 1.78590, νd = 43.9. Glass: `786439 coordinate class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone f = −61.4594 mm.

L10 completes G2. It is a relatively strong negative singlet and reinforces the group's net negative power. G2 is the principal negative variator: its first surface moves +22.698 mm by the middle position and +36.610 mm by the 194 mm position relative to wide, with positive displacement defined imageward.

The patent's raw scalar block prints `f2 = +31.88`, but the architecture requires G2 to be negative and the final TypeScript arrays independently give −31.8824 mm. The negative sign is therefore treated as a calculation-backed source correction rather than a change to the surface prescription.

### L11 — Front Positive Singlet of G3

**L11 — Biconvex Positive.** nd = 1.80610, νd = 40.7. Glass: `806407 coordinate class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone f = +119.9557 mm.

L11 begins the positive G3 group. Its standalone positive power is followed by the weaker positive cemented D5 assembly. Together they yield a verified G3 focal length of +85.9052 mm.

G3 is the compensating moving group. Its displacement is non-monotonic: +11.404 mm at the middle state relative to wide, then +6.512 mm at 194 mm. The reversal is explicitly preserved by the three zoom control points rather than approximated by a monotonic motion law.

### D5 — L12 + L13: Rear Cemented Pair of G3

**L12 — Biconvex Positive.** nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6. Glass: `497816 coordinate class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone f = +85.0896 mm.  
**L13 — Negative Meniscus.** nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.8. Glass: `847238 coordinate class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone f = −117.5152 mm.

D5 has a verified cemented net focal length of +300.5651 mm. The cemented pair is therefore only weakly positive compared with the standalone power of L12. L13 supplies negative power and a strongly different Abbe number within the pair.

The patent specifically places a high-Abbe requirement on at least one positive element in G3 or G4 (¶0019, ¶0031). L12 has νd = 81.6 and satisfies that criterion. This supports a patent-grounded statement about the selected Abbe-number strategy, but not a claim about anomalous partial dispersion.

### L14 — Positive Pre-Stop Singlet in G4

**L14 — Positive Meniscus.** nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6. Glass: `497816 coordinate class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone f = +76.0265 mm.

L14 is the only G4 element ahead of the aperture stop and is one of the strongest positive singlets in the rear half of the lens. Its νd = 81.6 again meets patent condition (6) for a high-Abbe positive element in G3/G4.

The stop follows L14 by the source-published 1.927 mm air spacing. Paragraph 0032 explains the placement of the stop in G4 in terms of limiting beam shading and permitting a smaller aperture diameter because the rear-group optics converge the beam. The final model preserves the stop's axial position exactly while inferring only its clear radius.

### L15 — First Post-Stop Negative Singlet

**L15 — Biconcave Negative.** nd = 1.62004, νd = 36.3. Glass: `620363 coordinate class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone f = −122.0471 mm.

L15 begins the post-stop portion of the stationary G4 group. It provides negative standalone power immediately after the stop and is followed by a long 22.815 mm air space before L16.

In first-order terms, L15 participates in the alternating negative-positive-negative-positive sequence that makes the complete rear group positive without requiring every constituent to be positive. Its precise aberration contribution is not separately stated by the patent and is not inferred beyond the verified paraxial power distribution.

### L16 — Positive Rear-Group Singlet

**L16 — Biconvex Positive.** nd = 1.83400, νd = 37.3. Glass: `834373 coordinate class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone f = +87.2864 mm.

L16 supplies substantial positive standalone power after the long L15–L16 air gap. Its high refractive index allows that power with the authored curvatures, but the patent does not identify a maker or glass family, so the coordinate class remains the only defensible material label.

### L17 — Negative Rear-Group Meniscus

**L17 — Negative Meniscus.** nd = 1.80610, νd = 33.3. Glass: `806333 coordinate class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone f = −52.3682 mm.

L17 is the strongest negative standalone element in G4. Its coordinate pair is the least universally matched of the ten patent classes: the six-vendor catalog comparison found defensible coordinate matches in the HOYA, HIKARI, and CDGM catalogs, while the nearest checked OHARA and SCHOTT rows fell outside the project's round-trip window and no current SUMITA identity was defended. This still leaves vendor identity unresolved.

The element is separated from L18 by only 0.433 mm of air. Its strong negative power is therefore closely followed by the final positive singlet.

### L18 — Final Positive Singlet

**L18 — Biconvex Positive.** nd = 1.62004, νd = 36.3. Glass: `620363 coordinate class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone f = +101.8547 mm.

L18 is the final refracting element. Together with L15–L17 and the pre-stop L14, it completes G4 at a verified functional-group focal length of +107.8650 mm.

The final surface is followed by 46.696 mm to the image plane in the source table. Independent tracing gives 46.695643 / 46.695524 / 46.696297 mm back focal distance at the three zoom states, showing that the published final spacing is consistent with the actual paraxial image location to source precision.

The three 497816 elements are important to the source's chromatic strategy because νd = 81.6 lies well above the patent's lower bound of 62 for selected positive elements. Tamron also marks three LD elements in the production construction diagram. That correspondence is useful for production identification, but it does not establish that the patent elements are a particular OHARA, HOYA, SCHOTT, CDGM, HIKARI, or SUMITA glass.

The neutral class labels permit the resolver to use coordinate-compatible catalog curves for spectral tracing while leaving the production supplier unspecified. `nC`, `nF`, `ng`, and `dPgF` are intentionally absent from the authored prescription. Consequently, the analysis does not classify the prescription as APO and does not claim anomalous-partial-dispersion behavior; those claims would require source line data or a uniquely identified production glass.

## Focus Mechanism

The source mechanism is foremost-group inner focusing. G1F = L1–L2 remains stationary, while G1R = L3–L5 translates along the optical axis (¶¶0024–0027). Zoom movement and focus movement are separated in the data model: D16 and D21 are zoom-only gaps, while D3 and D8 carry the G1R focus translation in addition to D8's zoom dependence.

The close-focus spacing is a **CONSTRAINED_RECONSTRUCTION**, not a published spacing table. Example 1 prints a “9.124-mm displacement” at a 0.95 m object distance. Applying 9.124 mm to the source-stated G1R mechanism gives approximately 1.591 m object-to-image distance at all three zoom states, so the printed travel and distance cannot both be correct.

Independent conjugate solving of the final prescription gives 19.1228–19.1238 mm travel for exactly 0.95 m across the three control points. The authored source-precision travel is therefore 19.124 mm objectward. This reconstruction is additionally consistent with Example 2, whose separately printed 17.062 mm focus displacement reproduces its stated 1.0 m reference convention. The correction is treated as a missing leading `1` in the Example 1 scalar line; the published surface prescription itself is not altered.

The mechanism constraint is simple and explicit: moving G1R objectward decreases D3 by 19.124 mm and increases D8 by 19.124 mm. Their sum is conserved independently at each zoom position.

| Zoom header | D3 infinity → close (mm) | D8 infinity → close (mm) | Computed object-to-image distance (mm) | Paraxial magnification |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 71.5 | 20.101 → 0.977 | 1.196 → 20.320 | 949.964 | −0.117494 (1:8.511) |
| 117.4 | 20.101 → 0.977 | 23.894 → 43.018 | 949.978 | −0.192833 (1:5.186) |
| 194.0 | 20.101 → 0.977 | 37.806 → 56.930 | 949.993 | −0.318785 (1:3.1369) |

The reconstructed 194 mm magnification is consistent with Tamron's rounded 1:3.1 production specification, but the manufacturer value is not used to tune the prescription. The 0.95 m conjugate and mechanism constraints determine the authored focus state.

No internal-focus movement beyond this single G1R translation is invented. G1F remains fixed, and the two zoom-only gaps retain identical infinity/close values at each control point.

## Chromatic Correction Strategy

The patent explicitly frames several design conditions around chromatic aberration rather than leaving material selection unexplained. It requires the convex member of G1F to have νn1 greater than 62 (condition 5) and requires at least one positive element in G3/G4 to have ν greater than 62 (condition 6). Paragraphs 0030 and 0031 associate those limits with suppression of secondary chromatic aberration in G1 and in the G3/G4 region respectively.

Example 1 uses νd = 70.4 for L2 in G1F and νd = 81.6 for L12 and L14 in G3/G4. Those values satisfy the patent's stated Abbe-number constraints. The cemented pairs also repeatedly combine elements with markedly different νd values: D1 uses 43.9/70.4, D2 uses 30.1/70.4, D3 uses 23.8/60.2, D4 uses 60.2/23.8, and D5 uses 81.6/23.8.

These pairings support a first-order description of dispersion balancing, but nd/νd alone does not determine secondary-spectrum behavior. Because the selected source provides no per-element C/F/g indices or partial-dispersion deviations, the data cannot distinguish ordinary from anomalous partial dispersion within a coordinate class. The chromatic strategy is therefore described only at the patent's Abbe-number level.

## Verification Summary

Independent verification reads the authored TypeScript arrays directly and reconstructs the system using an independent sequential height/reduced-angle trace and a separately assembled ABCD matrix. The maximum discrepancy between the two methods over the three infinity zoom states is `3.33 × 10⁻16`.

| Patent zoom header | Verified EFL (mm) | Verified BFD (mm) | Verified F-number | First surface → image (mm) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 71.5 | 71.499670 | 46.695643 | 2.879994 | 236.303 |
| 117.4 | 117.348540 | 46.695524 | 2.879991 | 236.303 |
| 194.0 | 194.001215 | 46.696297 | 2.880015 | 236.303 |

The 117.4 mm header differs from the computed middle-state EFL by −0.05146 mm. Because the source variable gaps are printed only to 0.001 mm, this residual is treated as source-precision consistent rather than corrected.

The constant stop radius reconstruction is also internally consistent. The single authored `STO.sd = 16.69756 mm` produces the three F-numbers above while the entrance-pupil radius expands from 12.413161 mm at wide to 33.680594 mm at 194 mm.

Petzval is computed surface by surface as `φ/(n·n′)`. The final sum is +0.002110829156 mm⁻¹, corresponding to a signed Petzval radius of approximately +473.7475 mm. The neutral stop contributes zero.

Under the project's strict first-order terminology, the example is neither telephoto nor retrofocus at any authored zoom state:

| State | TL/EFL | BFD/EFL | Project classification |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 71.5 mm | 3.30495 | 0.65309 | Neither strict telephoto nor retrofocus |
| 117.4 mm | 2.01369 | 0.39792 | Neither strict telephoto nor retrofocus |
| 194.0 mm | 1.21805 | 0.24070 | Neither strict telephoto nor retrofocus |

This classification does not alter the patent's source title; it only prevents the title from being converted into an unsupported first-order architectural claim.

The inferred semi-diameters pass the available independent geometry checks over every defined infinity and close state and dense interpolation between zoom control points. The minimum verified element edge thickness is 0.623681 mm, the maximum actual spherical rim-slope angle is 33.234929°, and the minimum full-field chief-ray semi-diameter margin is +3.073965 mm. No tested off-axis pupil-edge bundle first clips at a cemented interface. The narrowest physical shared-gap clearance is +0.100443 mm at surfaces 11→12; under the declared `gapSagFrac = 0.97` policy, the remaining policy clearance is +0.017823 mm.

The prescription is entirely spherical and `asph` is empty. No aspheric conic convention or polynomial coefficient transformation is applicable. No uniform scaling is applied, so there are likewise no scaled radii, spacings, image-plane coordinates, or transformed aspheric coefficients.

No sensor cover glass, filter, inactive dummy plane, flare-cutter plane, or mechanical component is included in the modeled prescription. No omitted optical plate requires an air-equivalent rear-spacing correction for this selected example.
