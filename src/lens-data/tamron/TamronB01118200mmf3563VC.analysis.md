# TAMRON 18-200mm f/3.5-6.3 Di III VC (B011) — Patent Analysis

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP 2012-181525 A  
**Application Number:** JP 2012-42215  
**Priority:** 2011-02-28 (CN)  
**Filed:** 2012-02-28  
**Published:** 2012-09-20  
**Inventors:** Nobuyuki Adachi; Hisayuki Yamanaka  
**Applicant:** Tamron Optical Foshan Co., Ltd.  
**Title:** Zoom Lens  
**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 1 / 第1実施形態

The prescription is the unscaled Example 1 design from JP 2012-181525 A. The selected production correlation is the TAMRON 18-200mm f/3.5-6.3 Di III VC, Model B011. The correlation is not presented as a manufacturer confirmation that the commercial lens uses this exact numerical example; it rests on convergent patent, prescription, and product evidence.

1. Tamron specifies the production B011 as an APS-C mirrorless 18-200mm f/3.5-6.3 zoom with 17 elements in 13 groups, Sony E and Canon EF-M variants, VC stabilization, and a 0.5 m minimum object distance. The data file preserves those items as product metadata while keeping the patent's numerical prescription separate.
2. Example 1 computes to 18.4998, 59.9957, and 193.9984 mm at the three published infinity states, with source f-numbers 3.56, 5.50, and 6.47. These are close to, but not substituted for, the marketed 18-200mm f/3.5-6.3 specification.
3. The physical prescription contains 17 lenses in 13 air-spaced groups. The hybrid asphere is modeled as a thin resin layer bonded to a glass substrate, so the data file necessarily has 18 optical-media entries while retaining `elementCount: 17`.
4. Tamron states that the production B011 uses three glass-molded aspherical lenses and one hybrid aspherical lens. Example 1 has five aspherical surfaces distributed across four physical lenses: one hybrid element and three other aspherical lenses, one of which is aspherical on both faces.
5. The patent architecture is a positive-leading high-ratio mirrorless zoom with a single positive inner-focus element immediately behind the stop and a laterally shifted VC group in the rear positive assembly. That architecture agrees with the product's mirrorless, stepping-motor focus, and VC identity.

The manufacturer also describes two LD elements and one XR glass in the production lens. Those product categories are not assigned one-for-one to specific patent rows because the patent gives only d-line refractive index and Abbe number and does not name Tamron's production glass designations.

## Optical Architecture

Patent ¶0039 defines the optical sequence, from object to image, as G1(+) – G2(−) – aperture stop S – F(+) – M(+). The rear M assembly is divided into MA, MVC, and MC, with MVC translated laterally for vibration compensation. The final data file follows that sequence directly as G1, G2, `STO`, F, MA, MVC, and MC.

Independent paraxial recomputation from the final TypeScript arrays gives the following fixed-group powers:

| Functional block | Computed focal length | Sign / role |
|---|---:|---|
| G1 | +93.4423 mm | Positive front group |
| G2 | −13.0910 mm | Negative variator group |
| F | +34.7126 mm | Positive inner-focus singlet |
| MA | +33.7154 mm | Positive front subassembly of M |
| MVC | −21.1368 mm | Net-negative VC cemented pair |
| MC | +54.8832 mm | Positive rear relay |
| Complete M | +88.1411 mm | Net-positive rear group |

These are in-situ group results from the authored surface blocks. They are distinct from the standalone air focal lengths stored on individual elements and from the net powers of cemented pairs.

The zoom is driven by five authored variable axial spacings: D6, D15, D16, D18, and the cover-normalized rear image gap. At the three published infinity states:

- D6 grows from 0.800 to 25.131 to 55.897 mm, strongly increasing the G1-G2 separation.
- The G2-to-F cavity, D15 + D16, contracts from 22.109 to 10.470 to 2.948 mm.
- D16 changes 4.056 → 5.655 → 1.898 mm, while D18 changes 5.652 → 4.054 → 7.810 mm. The F element therefore reverses its relative motion between the middle and telephoto states.
- The stop-to-M distance remains essentially fixed: 12.308, 12.309, and 12.308 mm. This reproduces the patent's statement that the stop moves integrally with the M group.

The data model uses the first active surface as its front reference, so these spacing changes should not be read as a complete mechanical barrel-motion diagram. The patent separately states that G1 advances objectward during zooming; the normalized LensVisualizer coordinates preserve the optical separations rather than an absolute housing datum.

The final sequential model omits patent surfaces 34-35, a 2.000 mm plane-parallel plate with nd = 1.51680 and νd = 64.2. Under the current project rule that plate is treated as a sensor-cover/filter plane rather than a lens element. Its paraxial effect is preserved by replacing the raw rear spacing with an air-equivalent Gaussian image distance. No optical prescription scaling was applied.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths quoted below are the standalone air focal lengths stored in the data file and independently recomputed from the final surface arrays. A cemented pair can have a very different net power from either standalone component, and the complete functional group can differ again once the remaining elements and internal spacings are included.

### J1 — L1 + L2, G1 front cemented pair

**L1:** nd = 1.90366, νd = 31.3. Glass: 904313 — lanthanum-flint class. Standalone f = −118.7 mm.  
**L2:** nd = 1.43500, νd = 95.0. Glass: Unmatched (1.43500/95.0 ultra-low-dispersion crown). Standalone f = +120.8 mm.

L1 is a negative meniscus and L2 is a biconvex positive lens. Treated separately in air they have substantial opposing powers; cemented together, J1 is nearly afocal, with a computed net focal length of about −6589 mm. The complete G1 block is nevertheless strongly positive at +93.44 mm because L3 supplies the principal positive contribution and the group operates as a three-lens assembly.

The very high νd of L2 marks it as the lowest-dispersion medium in the front group. Its exact catalog identity remains unresolved, so the analysis does not equate it with a specific manufacturer's fluorophosphate or fluorite-family glass.

### L3 — G1 positive singlet

**nd = 1.72916, νd = 54.7. Glass: 729547 — lanthanum-crown class. Standalone f = +92.2 mm.**

L3 is a positive meniscus that completes G1. In the paraxial decomposition it converts the nearly neutral J1 cemented pair into the net-positive front group required by the patent's positive-leading zoom architecture.

Its role is primarily architectural in the source description: G1 supplies positive front power and participates in the large change of separation relative to G2 during zooming. More detailed aberration allocation to L3 would be an inference rather than a patent statement.

### H1 — L4r + L4g, hybrid aspherical element in G2

**L4r:** nd = 1.51460, νd = 50.0. Glass: Unmatched (hybrid asphere resin/polymer). Standalone f = −304.6 mm.  
**L4g:** nd = 1.88300, νd = 40.8. Glass: 883408 — high-index lanthanum-flint class. Standalone f = −20.4 mm.

The thin L4r resin layer is bonded directly to the L4g glass substrate and carries aspherical surface 7A. LensVisualizer must represent these as two optical media because the refractive index changes across their cemented interface, but they constitute one physical hybrid lens for the 17-element count.

The computed cemented net focal length of H1 is −19.07 mm, so the substrate supplies most of the negative power while the resin layer mainly provides a manufacturable aspherical departure. This division between substrate power and thin molded asphere is consistent with the hybrid construction identified by the production-lens literature, without asserting that the commercial resin chemistry is identical to the patent medium.

### L5 — G2 negative singlet

**nd = 1.80420, νd = 46.5. Glass: 804465 — lanthanum-flint class. Standalone f = −29.8 mm.**

L5 is a biconcave negative lens following the hybrid element. Together with H1, L6, and L7 it forms the strongly negative G2 variator whose computed focal length is −13.09 mm.

The element's position in the negative zoom group is more securely established than a specific aberration assignment. The patent does not isolate L5 as a dedicated distortion, coma, or color corrector.

### L6 — G2 positive singlet

**nd = 1.92286, νd = 20.9. Glass: 923209 — dense-flint class. Standalone f = +21.4 mm.**

L6 is a strong biconvex positive element embedded between net-negative components. Its low Abbe number and high index give G2 a large dispersion and power contrast without requiring the whole group to be built from same-sign elements.

The final G2 remains negative despite L6's positive standalone power. This is an example of why element power should not be read as the sign of the functional group.

### L7 — G2 rear aspherical singlet

**nd = 1.80139, νd = 45.4. Glass: M-TAF31 (Hoya catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). Standalone f = −31.7 mm.**

L7 is a negative meniscus whose rear face, surface 15A, is aspherical. It terminates G2 immediately in front of the long stop cavity at the wide state and the much shorter cavity at telephoto.

The asphere gives the rear of the variator higher-order shape freedom close to the stop. The patent does not attribute a single named aberration exclusively to this surface, so the analysis treats that function as design inference rather than source fact.

### L8 — F focusing element

**nd = 1.69350, νd = 53.2. Glass: 694532 — low-Tg molded lanthanum-crown class. Standalone f = +34.7 mm.**

L8 is the entire F group: a single biconvex positive lens with aspherical surfaces 17A and 18A. Its standalone focal length and functional-group focal length are therefore the same, +34.7126 mm.

This element is central to the patent's inner-focus concept. Patent ¶0014 states that the F group is preferably a single lens element and that at least one aspherical face can be used to control spherical aberration and off-axis coma. Example 1 uses aspheres on both faces, giving the moving focus element substantial correction freedom without increasing its moving mass.

### J2 — L9 + L10, first MA cemented pair

**L9:** nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6. Glass: 497816 — low-dispersion crown class. Standalone f = +20.7 mm.  
**L10:** nd = 1.80610, νd = 33.3. Glass: 806333 — high-index flint class. Standalone f = −50.9 mm.

L9 is biconvex positive and L10 is a negative meniscus. Cemented together, J2 has a net focal length of +33.86 mm.

Patent ¶0040 describes MA as two cemented pairs and explicitly associates that construction with correction of longitudinal chromatic aberration from wide to telephoto. J2 supplies the stronger positive part of that MA strategy through a high-Abbe positive crown paired to a substantially lower-Abbe negative flint.

### J3 — L11 + L12, second MA cemented pair

**L11:** nd = 1.90366, νd = 31.3. Glass: 904313 — lanthanum-flint class. Standalone f = −22.7 mm.  
**L12:** nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6. Glass: 497816 — low-dispersion crown class. Standalone f = +22.5 mm.

J3 reverses the sign order of J2: a negative high-index, low-Abbe element is cemented to a positive high-Abbe element. Their standalone powers nearly cancel, and the cemented pair has only weak net negative power, about −877 mm focal length.

Taken together, J2 and J3 produce the complete MA subassembly at +33.72 mm. The two cemented achromatizing pairs distribute color correction across opposite sign combinations rather than relying on a single doublet.

### J4 — L13 + L14, MVC stabilization pair

**L13:** nd = 1.68893, νd = 31.1. Glass: 689311 — low-Tg molded flint class. Standalone f = +16.4 mm.  
**L14:** nd = 1.83481, νd = 42.7. Glass: 835427 — high-index lanthanum-flint class. Standalone f = −9.1 mm.

L13 is a biconvex positive lens with aspherical front surface 25A; L14 is biconcave and negative. Cemented together they form J4, whose net focal length is −21.1368 mm. In this case the cemented-pair power and the MVC functional-group power are the same because MVC consists only of this pair.

Patent ¶0021 specifies that the laterally shifted stabilization group should contain at least one positive and one negative lens so that color degradation during decentering can be controlled. The authored d-line data give an Abbe-number separation of 11.6 between the two elements. The patent discusses an Abbe separation of roughly seven as desirable, but no anomalous-partial-dispersion claim is made here.

### L15 — MC positive singlet

**nd = 1.54072, νd = 47.2. Glass: 541472 — flint class. Standalone f = +26.8 mm.**

L15 is a biconvex positive singlet at the front of MC. Along with L16 and L17 it forms the final positive relay, whose computed focal length is +54.88 mm.

The patent does not assign a separate named correction task to L15; its secure role is as the first positive contributor in the rear MC block.

### L16 — MC negative singlet

**nd = 1.88300, νd = 40.8. Glass: 883408 — high-index lanthanum-flint class. Standalone f = −22.6 mm.**

L16 is a negative meniscus between two positive MC singlets. The high index allows substantial negative bending in a compact axial space while the complete MC block remains positive.

Its glass coordinate is the same 883408 class used for the hybrid substrate L4g, but that equality of d-line coordinates does not establish a specific vendor or melt.

### L17 — MC rear positive singlet

**nd = 1.67270, νd = 32.2. Glass: 673322 — dense-flint class. Standalone f = +47.2 mm.**

L17 is the final biconvex positive singlet before the normalized image-space gap. Its rear surface is the last powered surface retained in the LensVisualizer model.

The model's post-L17 distance is not the raw patent D33. It is the cover-normalized Gaussian image distance required after omission of the 2 mm rear plate.

The strongest chromatic structure visible directly from the d-line prescription is the repeated pairing of high-Abbe positive media with lower-Abbe negative media, especially in MA. The 1.49700/81.6 crown appears in both cemented MA pairs, while the partners are 1.80610/33.3 and 1.90366/31.3. Patent ¶0040 explicitly links the two MA cemented pairs to longitudinal color correction across the zoom range.

The front group also contains the unusually high-νd L2 medium, while MVC deliberately combines opposite power signs and separated Abbe numbers. These observations support an achromatizing strategy, but they do not support an APO or anomalous-dispersion designation. The patent publishes no per-element `nC`, `nF`, `ng`, `PgF`, or `dPgF`, and the data file intentionally carries none of those fields.

Tamron's production literature states that the B011 uses two LD elements and one XR glass. Because the patent is vendor- and trade-name-silent, the analysis does not relabel any individual numerical-example element as the production LD or XR component solely from `nd` and `νd`.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent uses inner focusing by moving the single positive F group, L8, along the optical axis. The stop lies immediately in front of F, and the patent states that F moves toward the image side when focusing from infinity to a nearer object.

Example 1 publishes only one finite-distance row, at the telephoto setting. Relative to telephoto infinity, D16 changes from 1.898 to 8.486 mm and D18 from 7.810 to 1.223 mm. The source therefore shows F moving 6.588 mm imageward while the adjacent D16 + D18 cavity remains constant to 0.001 mm source rounding.

The patent does not publish corresponding wide- or middle-zoom close-focus rows. After normalization of the omitted rear plate and image plane, the telephoto finite-conjugate row is also insufficient to determine a unique continuous focus mechanism without imposing additional reference-plane assumptions. The final data file therefore uses the focus status **NO_INTERNAL_RECONSTRUCTION**: every `[infinity, close]` pair in `var` is identical, so LensVisualizer models only the three published infinity zoom states.

`closeFocusM: 0.5` is product metadata from Tamron, not a reconstructed optical state. Tamron also states that the production B011 drives focus with a stepping motor; that mechanical fact supports the product identity but does not supply missing patent spacings.

## Aspherical Surfaces

Patent ¶0036 uses the standard rotationally symmetric conic-plus-polynomial form

$$
z(h)=\frac{c h^2}{1+\sqrt{1-(1+K)c^2h^2}}+A_4h^4+A_6h^6+A_8h^8+A_{10}h^{10}+\cdots,
$$

with `c = 1/R`. The patent's `K` is already the standard conic constant; no κ-to-K offset is required. No prescription scaling was applied, so the aspheric coefficients are transcribed without dimensional rescaling. The data file carries A12 and A14 as zero because no higher nonzero terms are published for Example 1.

| Surface | Element | K | A4 | A6 | A8 | A10 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 7A | L4r hybrid resin | 0 | +2.11773e-5 | −7.42565e-8 | +2.76094e-10 | +4.23754e-13 |
| 15A | L7 | 0 | −2.56488e-6 | −1.77205e-8 | −1.29711e-9 | +1.69949e-11 |
| 17A | L8 front | +2.50125 | −9.11521e-6 | −7.20102e-7 | +1.32972e-8 | −1.24641e-10 |
| 18A | L8 rear | 0 | +1.01753e-5 | −8.28466e-7 | +1.46868e-8 | −1.30385e-10 |
| 25A | L13 | 0 | +4.27471e-5 | −5.26048e-7 | +1.52615e-8 | −1.07325e-10 |

At the final inferred and validated semi-diameters, the polynomial departure from the conic base is +0.3002 mm at 7A, −0.0116 mm at 15A, −0.3047 mm at 17A, −0.2421 mm at 18A, and +0.1340 mm at 25A. These departure values are computed model results at the authored clear radii, not patent-published aperture departures.

Surface 17A is the only Example 1 asphere with positive K. Its authored semi-diameter is 9.0 mm, comfortably inside the project's 0.98 conic-domain limit of 20.3373 mm. The two F-group aspheres are especially significant because the patent explicitly identifies aspherization of the lightweight single-element focus group as a means of maintaining spherical-aberration and off-axis coma correction while limiting moving mass.

## Chromatic Correction Strategy

The numerical example distributes chromatic correction across several widely separated power zones rather than concentrating it in one front doublet. G1 combines a low-Abbe high-index negative element with an extremely high-Abbe positive partner; MA uses two cemented pairs with large Abbe contrasts; and MVC combines positive and negative power in a cemented stabilizing pair so lateral displacement need not be borne by a single dispersive singlet.

The strongest source statement is patent ¶0040, which says the two MA cemented pairs correct longitudinal chromatic aberration over the zoom range. Patent ¶0021 separately states that the VC group should contain positive and negative lenses with an Abbe-number separation to suppress axial-color degradation during stabilization.

The data support an achromatizing interpretation at the d line. They do not support an apochromatic or anomalous-partial-dispersion claim because the source supplies no line-index or partial-dispersion data. The M-TAF31 curve improves spectral tracing for L7 but is explicitly an optical equivalent, not proof of the production melt.

## Conditional Expressions

The patent gives five design inequalities. Recalculation from the final TypeScript arrays reproduces all five Example 1 tabulated values to the source's three-decimal precision.

| Condition | Patent interval | Computed | Published Example 1 |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| FF / FT | 0.03 < FF/FT < 0.50 | 0.178932 | 0.179 |
| FVC / FM | −1.0 < FVC/FM < −0.1 | −0.239806 | −0.240 |
| \|F1 / FT\| | 0.18 < \|F1/FT\| < 2.10 | 0.481665 | 0.482 |
| \|ΔT1 / F1\| | 0.70 < \|ΔT1/F1\| < 1.10 | 0.802078 | 0.802 |
| ΔT3 / F3 | 0.025 < ΔT3/F3 < 0.160 | 0.062168 | 0.062 |

Condition (5) requires an explicit reference-plane note. The patent's own 0.062 value is reproduced when ΔT3 is taken as the F group's zoom motion relative to the co-moving stop/M carrier, numerically `D16_wide − D16_tele = 2.158 mm`. Treating ΔT3 as an absolute image-referenced F translation does not reproduce the published condition. The analysis therefore preserves the carrier-relative interpretation rather than silently redefining the source quantity.

## Image Stabilization

MVC is the only group displaced laterally for vibration compensation. In the final centered prescription it is the cemented L13-L14 pair with net focal length −21.1368 mm. Patent ¶0020-¶0022 requires the VC group to be net negative and explains that a negative group can increase the shake-correction coefficient, reducing the lateral travel required for a given correction.

Figure 5 of the patent labels the Example 1 telephoto VC positions at +0.35 mm, 0 mm, and −0.35 mm lateral shift. The tiny axial differences in the patent's separate tele-VC table are not treated as an axial stabilization mechanism because the source explicitly defines VC as motion perpendicular to the optical axis.

The LensVisualizer data file represents the centered optical state only; it does not add a decentered VC control. Tamron's production description separately states that B011 uses a three-coil electromagnetic VC mechanism with the moving coils adjacent to the compensating optics. That is product-mechanical context, not a prescription parameter.

## Verification Summary

The final data file was checked from its actual TypeScript arrays rather than by assuming the extraction tables had been copied correctly.

| State | Source focal length | Recomputed EFL | Source F/# | Cover-normalized BFL | Active track |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Wide | 18.50 mm | 18.4997908 mm | 3.56 | 14.8252829 mm | 109.7932829 mm |
| Middle | 60.00 mm | 59.9956598 mm | 5.50 | 42.2719339 mm | 148.3339339 mm |
| Telephoto | 194.00 mm | 193.9984455 mm | 6.47 | 51.6793317 mm | 184.7413317 mm |

Under the project definitions, only the telephoto endpoint satisfies the telephoto-form test (`TL/EFL = 0.9523 < 1`); the wide and middle states have `TL/EFL = 5.9348` and `2.4724`. None of the three sampled states is retrofocus because the cover-normalized BFL remains below the EFL throughout.

The surface-by-surface Petzval sum, using `φ/(n·n′)`, is +0.003138001446 mm⁻¹, corresponding to a signed Petzval radius of −318.6742 mm under the project's convention.

The semi-diameters are modeling inferences because the patent publishes no clear-aperture table. They were derived from wide-open marginal and chief-ray envelopes and then checked against the current geometry rules. The final set has a minimum computed element edge thickness of 0.2548 mm, a maximum actual rim-slope angle of 48.659°, and a tightest positive shared-band cross-gap margin of 0.2942 mm. The sampled wide-open on-axis and off-axis bundles are contained in all three defined infinity zoom states.

The aperture stop position is source-published, but its physical diameter is not. The model uses a mechanical stop semi-diameter of 6.8270 mm, large enough for all three states, while the modeled f-number array 3.56 / 5.50 / 6.47 governs the state-dependent wide-open pupil requirement.

No numerical patent correction was applied to the radii, thicknesses, indices, Abbe numbers, or asphere coefficients. The patent's separate “lens total length” row remains offset by about 3.823 mm from the independently reconstructed physical surface-2-to-image track when the source plate is retained at the infinity states; because the source does not define that datum, it is retained as a reference ambiguity and is not used to set the LensVisualizer image plane.

No scaling was applied. The marketed 18-200mm f/3.5-6.3 values remain product metadata, while the design fields and zoom control points preserve the Example 1 optical values.
