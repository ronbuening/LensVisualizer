## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 6,437,923 B1  
**Priority:** February 9, 2001 (JP 2001-034421)  
**Filed:** July 2, 2001  
**Granted:** August 20, 2002  
**Inventor:** Yasuharu Yamada  
**Assignee:** Tamron Co., Ltd.  
**Title:** *High Zoom Ratio Lens*  
**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 1 / Table 1

The selected production correlation is the TAMRON AF 28-200mm SUPER XR f/3.8-5.6 Aspherical [IF] MACRO, Model A03. The correlation is treated as fixed for this entry, but not as a manufacturer statement that US 6,437,923 B1 is the production prescription. The identification rests on convergent source evidence:

1. Tamron identifies Model A03 as a 28-200 mm F/3.8-5.6 lens with 14 groups and 15 lens pieces, 0.49 m minimum focus distance, and 1:4 maximum magnification at 200 mm.
2. Tamron states that A03 uses three composite aspherical lenses. Example 1 contains three 0.2 mm, $n_d=1.53610$, $\nu_d=41.2$ bonded layers aligned with the three patent aspheres at surfaces 7, 17, and 30.
3. The patent example spans 29.07-193.0 mm at F/3.69-F/5.82, close to the marketed 28-200 mm F/3.8-5.6 range without requiring a uniform prescription scale.
4. The patent specifies a four-functional-group positive-negative-positive-positive zoom and G2-only inner focusing. Tamron markets A03 as an [IF] lens.
5. Tamron's archived A03 specification dates the lens to April 2001, and Tamron's lens history lists Model A03 under 2001. That timing is consistent with the patent's February 2001 priority and July 2001 filing dates.

Marketing and design values remain separate in the data. The marketed focal-length range is 28-200 mm; the corrected computed design endpoints are 29.071407664 mm and 192.988185473 mm. The patent's three authored zoom stations remain 29.07, 74.76, and 193.0 mm. The marketed maximum-aperture designation is F/3.8-F/5.6, while the modeled stop produces F/3.696024, F/5.021170, and F/5.820483 at the three patent stations.

A source correction is necessary at patent surface 31. Table 1 visibly prints $n=1.53610$, $\nu=48.9$. That value does not reproduce the patent focal lengths. Solving the surface-31 index independently at the wide, intermediate, and tele stations gives 1.53173124, 1.53171576, and 1.53171208; the data therefore uses $n_d=1.53172$, $\nu_d=48.9$ while preserving the raw printed value in the data header and audit. With the correction, the three computed EFL residuals relative to the printed focal lengths are +0.001408 mm, -0.002057 mm, and -0.011815 mm.

No uniform scaling is applied. The prescription scale factor is therefore $s=1.0$.

## Optical Architecture

Example 1 is a four-functional-group high-ratio zoom with group powers in the sequence positive, negative, positive, positive. For the corrected prescription, the intrinsic paraxial powers are:

| Functional group | Surface range | Power (mm$^{-1}$) | Group EFL (mm) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| G1 | 1-6 | +0.012388867 | +80.717632 |
| G2 | 7A-15 | -0.065869051 | -15.181637 |
| G3 | 17A-23 | +0.023657299 | +42.270253 |
| G4 | 24-33 | +0.017022799 | +58.744746 |

These are functional moving groups, not the same quantity as the physical air-separated group count. The production specification and the authored `groupCount` record 14 air-separated physical groups and 15 physical lens pieces. The data contains 18 element entries because each of the three composite aspherical physical lens pieces is represented by a substrate and a bonded resin layer.

At infinity, the published zoom variables are $D6=1.768/23.261/42.131$ mm, $D15=19.553/10.101/0.969$ mm, and $D23=5.785/3.360/1.913$ mm from wide through intermediate to tele. Thus the G1-G2 gap expands, while the G2-G3 and G3-G4 separations contract. After each station is normalized to its own paraxial image plane, G1, G3, and G4 move toward the subject from wide to tele, matching the patent description. The patent also states that G2 moves reciprocally during continuous zooming. The three tabulated stations do not identify the turning point, so the authored three-station interpolation does not claim to reconstruct that reversal between the published states.

The aperture stop is source-published as patent surface 16, immediately before G3. Its axial placement is therefore not inferred. Its physical radius is inferred. The three published focal-length/F-number pairs, combined with the computed stop-to-entrance-pupil magnifications, imply stop radii of 8.379714, 8.352005, and 8.367669 mm; their arithmetic mean, 8.366462573 mm, is retained as the single physical stop radius for all zoom positions. The resulting modeled f-numbers are the `nominalFno` values used by the viewer.

The design should not be assigned a global "telephoto" or "retrofocus" label. Applying the definitions $TL/EFL<1$ and $BFD/EFL>1$, respectively, only the tele station is telephoto (0.882247), while only the wide station is retrofocus (1.414600). These are state-specific properties of the zoom.

The selected patent prescription contains no sensor cover, filter, inactive dummy plane, flare-cutter plane, folded path, or mechanical surface. No such plate is omitted from the sequential optical model, and no air-equivalent plate correction is required. The patent leaves the image plane implicit after surface 33; the authored last spacing is the independently solved paraxial BFD at each zoom station.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The `fl` values stored on individual data entries are isolated-in-air first-order focal lengths of those modeling entries. For the three hybrid composite lenses, the physical lens-piece focal length is the independently recomputed net power of the resin-plus-substrate stack. For the cemented L14/L15 rear pair, the separate L14 and L15 focal lengths are hypothetical isolated-in-air values; the cemented net value is the physically relevant pair quantity.

### L1 — Negative Meniscus

$n_d=1.84666$, $\nu_d=23.8$. Glass: **847238 (vendor-neutral d-line class)**. Isolated $f=-110.584526$ mm.

L1 is the front member of positive functional group G1. Its isolated power is negative despite G1's positive net power. The high refractive index and low Abbe number are consistent with the front-group high-index strategy that Tamron associates with the A03 XR design, but the patent does not identify a glass supplier.

Within G1, L1's weak negative power is followed by two positive members. The resulting three-piece group has a net EFL of +80.717632 mm.

### L2 — Biconvex Positive

$n_d=1.69680$, $\nu_d=55.5$. Glass: **697555 (vendor-neutral d-line class)**. Isolated $f=+77.694653$ mm.

L2 supplies positive power in G1 and counterbalances the front negative meniscus. Its lower refractive index and substantially higher Abbe number than L1 provide a different power/dispersion coordinate within the front group. No anomalous-partial-dispersion claim is made because the patent publishes no line indices or partial-dispersion data.

### L3 — Positive Meniscus

$n_d=1.77250$, $\nu_d=49.6$. Glass: **773496 (vendor-neutral d-line class)**. Isolated $f=+118.924033$ mm.

L3 is the rear positive member of G1. It completes the positive front functional group and precedes the large variable gap D6. D6 is one of the two spacings that also changes in the constrained focus model because G2 translates relative to fixed G1.

### L4 — Hybrid Composite Negative Meniscus (H1)

**Resin entry L4r:** $n_d=1.53610$, $\nu_d=41.2$; Glass: **Unmatched (composite-asphere resin; nd=1.53610, νd=41.2)**; isolated modeling-entry $f=-571.028259$ mm.  
**Substrate entry L4:** $n_d=1.83481$, $\nu_d=42.7$; Glass: **835427 (vendor-neutral d-line class)**; isolated modeling-entry $f=-23.185503$ mm.  
**Physical H1 composite:** independently recomputed isolated net $f=-22.280636$ mm.

H1 begins negative focusing group G2. Surface 7A is the air-to-resin asphere, followed by a 0.2 mm resin layer and the glass substrate. Treating the thin resin as a separate modeling medium preserves the patent's refractive index and asphere while retaining Tamron's physical count of one composite lens piece.

The resin layer contributes only weak standalone power; the substrate supplies most of the composite's negative power. The aspheric departure is therefore principally a shape-correction degree of freedom rather than a large independent paraxial-power contribution.

### L5 — Biconcave Negative

$n_d=1.72000$, $\nu_d=50.2$. Glass: **720502 (vendor-neutral d-line class)**. Isolated $f=-30.510771$ mm.

L5 continues the negative power of G2. It follows the strongly curved rear surface of H1 and is separated from L6 by only 0.1 mm of air. Its role is established geometrically as part of the moving negative focus group; the patent does not assign it a separate named aberration function.

### L6 — Biconvex Positive

$n_d=1.84666$, $\nu_d=23.8$. Glass: **847238 (vendor-neutral d-line class)**. Isolated $f=+20.000234$ mm.

L6 is a strong positive lens within the otherwise negative G2 group. The positive-negative alternation inside G2 distributes the large net negative functional power across several refracting surfaces rather than concentrating it in one lens.

Its glass coordinate is the same 847238 class used at L1 and L10. This repetition is a source-level numerical fact; it is not evidence that the production lens necessarily used one particular vendor's named glass.

### L7 — Negative Meniscus

$n_d=1.83481$, $\nu_d=42.7$. Glass: **835427 (vendor-neutral d-line class)**. Isolated $f=-29.027707$ mm.

L7 is the rear negative member of G2. Its rear surface 15 bounds D15, the second focus-sensitive spacing. In the constrained close-focus model, G2 moves toward the subject, D6 decreases, and D15 increases by exactly the same amount, preserving the G1-to-stop axial span at each zoom station.

### L8 — Hybrid Composite Positive Lens (H2)

**Resin entry L8r:** $n_d=1.53610$, $\nu_d=41.2$; Glass: **Unmatched (composite-asphere resin; nd=1.53610, νd=41.2)**; isolated modeling-entry $f=+32270.633934$ mm.  
**Substrate entry L8:** $n_d=1.79952$, $\nu_d=42.2$; Glass: **800422 (vendor-neutral d-line class)**; isolated modeling-entry $f=+42.835133$ mm.  
**Physical H2 composite:** independently recomputed isolated net $f=+42.864364$ mm.

H2 is the first physical lens piece of positive group G3 and lies directly behind the aperture stop. Surface 17A is the aspheric air-to-resin face. Because the resin interface and substrate front surface share the same paraxial radius, the thin resin entry has negligible standalone paraxial power; its significance is in the non-spherical departure carried by 17A.

This is the asphere for which the patent gives the clearest functional explanation. The patent states that the front aspheric surface of G3 increases positive refractive action toward the edge to compensate spherical-aberration variation associated with moving the strong negative G2 toward the subject in the intermediate-to-tele zoom region. That statement is source-derived; it is not inferred from the coefficient signs alone.

### L9 — Positive Meniscus

$n_d=1.72000$, $\nu_d=50.2$. Glass: **720502 (vendor-neutral d-line class)**. Isolated $f=+42.957701$ mm.

L9 is the second positive physical lens piece in G3. Together with H2 and the following negative L10, it forms a net positive group with independently recomputed EFL +42.270253 mm.

### L10 — Biconcave Negative

$n_d=1.84666$, $\nu_d=23.8$. Glass: **847238 (vendor-neutral d-line class)**. Isolated $f=-36.974022$ mm.

L10 is the negative rear member of G3. Its negative contribution reduces the net positive power of the two preceding G3 members and precedes the zoom-variable D23 gap to G4.

The patent's condition (5) constrains G3 imaging behavior across the zoom range. The independently computed values of $1/\beta_{3W}$ and $1/\beta_{3T}$ remain within the stated interval, indicating that the final TypeScript transcription preserves the first-order group behavior intended by the patent.

### L11 — Biconvex Positive

$n_d=1.51633$, $\nu_d=64.1$. Glass: **516641 (vendor-neutral d-line class)**. Isolated $f=+33.429674$ mm.

L11 is the front positive member of G4 and has the highest Abbe number in the prescription. That makes it the least dispersive d-line coordinate in the modeled glass set, but no stronger chromatic claim is justified without line-index or partial-dispersion data.

### L12 — Negative Meniscus

$n_d=1.80610$, $\nu_d=40.9$. Glass: **806409 (vendor-neutral d-line class)**. Isolated $f=-31.225697$ mm.

L12 provides negative power inside positive G4. Its combination with the positive lenses before and after it distributes the rear-group power while maintaining the G4 net value of +0.017022799 mm$^{-1}$.

### L13 — Hybrid Composite Positive Lens (H3)

**Substrate entry L13:** $n_d=1.51823$, $\nu_d=59.0$; Glass: **518590 (vendor-neutral d-line class)**; isolated modeling-entry $f=+28.949353$ mm.  
**Resin entry L13r:** $n_d=1.53610$, $\nu_d=41.2$; Glass: **Unmatched (composite-asphere resin; nd=1.53610, νd=41.2)**; isolated modeling-entry $f=+34653.261974$ mm.  
**Physical H3 composite:** independently recomputed isolated net $f=+28.982586$ mm.

H3 differs in orientation from H1 and H2: the resin layer is bonded to the rear of the glass substrate. Surface 30A is the aspheric resin-to-air surface. As with H2, the thin layer carries little standalone paraxial power because its bounding base radii are equal; the aspheric polynomial provides the meaningful departure from that base geometry.

The patent does not assign surface 30 a separate named aberration function, so any more specific attribution would be modeling interpretation rather than a published fact.

### L14 — Positive Front Member of Cemented Rear Pair D1

$n_d=1.53172$, $\nu_d=48.9$. Glass: **532489 (vendor-neutral d-line class; source-corrected nd)**. Hypothetical isolated-in-air $f=+18.203627$ mm.

L14 begins the true cemented pair at the rear of G4. Its refractive index is the documented surface-31 correction. The separate isolated focal length is useful for power bookkeeping, but L14 does not operate in air at its rear surface in the actual design.

### L15 — Negative Rear Member of Cemented Pair D1

$n_d=1.77250$, $\nu_d=49.6$. Glass: **773496 (vendor-neutral d-line class)**. Hypothetical isolated-in-air $f=-12.937963$ mm.

L15 is cemented directly to L14 at surface 32. The independently recomputed L14+L15 cemented net power is $-0.021297753$ mm$^{-1}$, corresponding to $f=-46.953311$ mm. This cemented-net value is the physically relevant power of the pair; it must not be confused with either isolated component value.

The pair is nevertheless embedded within an overall positive G4. In situ, the full G4 surface stack has power +0.017022799 mm$^{-1}$ and EFL +58.744746 mm. This distinction between isolated elements, the cemented pair, and the complete functional group is essential when interpreting the rear section.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent explicitly specifies inner focusing by movement of G2 alone. It does not publish close-focus spacing rows. Tamron publishes a 0.49 m minimum focus distance across the zoom range and a maximum magnification of 1:4 at 200 mm. The data therefore marks the close state as `CONSTRAINED_RECONSTRUCTION`, not as a patent-transcribed focus table.

The reconstruction imposes four optical constraints: the paraxial image plane is fixed at each zoom station; G1, G3, and G4 remain fixed; only G2 translates; and $D6+D15$ remains constant. The manufacturer MFD is normalized as the distance from the object plane to the fixed paraxial image/focal plane because the archived product page does not restate a different reference convention.

| Zoom station | G2 shift toward subject (mm) | D6 infinity → close (mm) | D15 infinity → close (mm) | Close paraxial magnification |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 29.07 mm | -1.163412 | 1.768000 → 0.604588 | 19.553000 → 20.716412 | -0.070324 |
| 74.76 mm | -2.795733 | 23.261000 → 20.465267 | 10.101000 → 12.896733 | -0.160559 |
| 193.0 mm | -9.239504 | 42.131000 → 32.891496 | 0.969000 → 10.208504 | -0.254030 |

The independently re-solved close states reproduce the authored G2 shifts to numerical precision while conserving $D6+D15$. At tele, $|m|=0.25403$, equivalent to approximately 1:3.94, which is consistent with Tamron's rounded 1:4 maximum-magnification specification. This agreement supports the constrained model but does not turn it into a published patent focus state.

No focus motion is assigned to G1, G3, G4, D23, or the rear BFD in the close-focus model.

## Aspherical Surfaces

The patent uses the standard conic-plus-even-polynomial sag form

$$
X(H)=\frac{H^2/R}{1+\sqrt{1-(1+K)H^2/R^2}}+A_4H^4+A_6H^6+A_8H^8+A_{10}H^{10}.
$$

The patent's printed $\varepsilon$ is already the standard conic constant $K$. No $K=\kappa-1$ or similar conversion is applied. The patent provides no $A_{12}$ or $A_{14}$ terms; the data stores those schema fields as zero.

| Surface | Physical composite | $K$ | $A_4$ | $A_6$ | $A_8$ | $A_{10}$ |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 7A | H1 / L4 front | +1.8366 | +9.765170e-06 | +8.272330e-09 | +1.821910e-11 | +6.338150e-13 |
| 17A | H2 / L8 front | -0.1979 | +3.725280e-06 | +3.393830e-08 | +1.625800e-11 | -1.739540e-12 |
| 30A | H3 / L13 rear | +1.3716 | +6.110410e-07 | +1.083190e-07 | -2.766560e-10 | +1.517680e-11 |

The patent does not publish clear-aperture semi-diameters. The data therefore uses modeled semi-diameters derived from paraxial marginal/chief-ray envelopes, the inferred physical stop, Fig. 1 proportions, and the current geometry constraints. These semi-diameters satisfy the edge-thickness, actual-rim-slope, conic-domain, cross-gap, containment, and trim-proxy checks used for this model. The aspheric departures from a same-radius spherical base are:

| Surface | Authored semi-diameter (mm) | Verified departure at that radius (mm) |
|---|---:|---:|
| 7A | 12.5 | +0.358958 |
| 17A | 9.6 | +0.042439 |
| 30A | 7.9 | +0.023320 |

These departures are model-dependent because their evaluation radii are inferred, not patent-published clear apertures. The coefficients themselves are patent-transcribed.

Tamron identifies three composite aspherical lenses in A03. The correspondence between that statement and the three patent 0.2 mm $n_d=1.53610$ layers is therefore the basis for modeling H1, H2, and H3 as bonded resin/substrate composites. The patent explicitly discusses the front asphere of G3 as a means of controlling spherical-aberration variation during the G2 floating behavior. More specific aberration roles for 7A and 30A are not assigned because the patent does not state them separately.

No uniform scale is applied: $s=1.0$. Consequently all radii, thicknesses, image-plane coordinates, and semi-diameters remain at patent/model scale, $K$ is unchanged, and the prescribed asphere scaling law $A_p\rightarrow A_p/s^{p-1}$ reduces to the identity. No aspheric coefficient transformation was numerically required.

Surface-by-surface Petzval curvature, evaluated as $\phi/(n n')$, sums to +0.002433200439 mm$^{-1}$, corresponding to a Petzval radius of +410.981350 mm. This is a paraxial computed quantity, not a patent-published field-curvature radius.

The modeled semi-diameters pass the current geometry policy at all three zoom positions and both focus endpoints. The minimum modeled edge thickness is 0.106064 mm; the maximum actual rim-slope angle is 61.232266°; the minimum shared-band cross-gap margin is +0.029022 mm after the 0.90 gap-sag allowance; and all on-axis stop-edge rays remain contained. The default off-axis test produces controlled first clipping only at physical surfaces 5 and 28, with no first clip at the internal cemented interfaces. These are validation results of the authored model, not patent aperture dimensions.

The surface-31 correction remains the only numerical source correction incorporated into the prescription. No scaling correction, omitted sensor plate, dummy-surface removal, or folded-path normalization is involved in this embodiment.
