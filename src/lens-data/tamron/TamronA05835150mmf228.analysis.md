The prescription represents the selected construction correlation for the TAMRON 35-150mm f/2-2.8 Di III VXD
(Model A058). The controlling numerical source is the Japanese A publication, with its original PAJ abstract
wrapper. The optical example is retained at its native scale; the marketed focal range does not replace the
tabulated design stations.

The production association rests on several convergent features:
1. The patent describes 21 glass elements in 15 air-separated components, matching the manufacturer construction count.
2. The four low-dispersion positions correlate with L2, L3, L7 and L11; the construction contains the distinctive middle cemented triplet.
3. L15, L16 and L21 are molded-glass aspheric elements. Their five aspheric surfaces agree with the source arrangement and the three molded-element production description.
4. The native zoom range is 36.0267–145.5296 mm, with source maximum apertures f/2.0604–2.9089, close to the marketed 35–150 mm f/2–2.8 range.
5. A small negative internal group performs focus. Tamron documents the production VXD linear focus drive, although the patent prescription does not encode that actuator.
These criteria support the selected correlation; they do not establish that the published example is an
unchanged factory prescription. The production Sony E and Nikon Z variants use the full-frame format. Their
published minimum focus distances of 0.33 m at wide and 0.85 m at tele differ from the approximately 0.8 m
object-to-image test configurations in this example. Those production distances are not substituted into the
source spacing table.

Source locators are ¶0064–¶0071 for construction and motion; ¶0081–¶0085 for numerical data; and Tables 1–2

## Optical Architecture
Five functional zoom groups form a positive–negative–positive–negative–negative sequence. These five moving
units are distinct from the 15 air-separated optical components. The physical camera-side cover glass is
additional sensor-side optics and is excluded from the 21-element photographic-lens count.

| Functional group | Elements | Source focal length (mm) | Recomputed focal length (mm) | Construction role |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| G1 | L1–L3 | +142.9320 | +142.930427 | Positive front unit |
| G2 | L4–L9 | -30.6227 | -30.623083 | Negative second zoom unit |
| G3 | L10–L16 | +32.3890 | +32.388899 | Positive intermediate group M |
| G4 | L17–L18 | -55.4052 | -55.405081 | Negative internal-focus group F |
| G5 | L19–L21 | -136.9750 | -136.975265 | Negative rear group R |

The intermediate group M is strongly positive as a complete unit, but it contains a negative cemented
triplet and a negative aspheric meniscus. Two cemented interfaces within the triplet are explicitly
divergent. The patent uses those interfaces, together with the negative air-lens region between L14 and L15,
as part of its spherical-aberration and field-curvature strategy (¶0019–¶0025, ¶0037–¶0038 and ¶0068). The
power signs alone do not quantify any individual aberration coefficient.

The aperture stop is source surface 17, immediately before G3. It is represented by STO without moving its
source location. G4 is a cemented positive/negative doublet whose net power is negative. The final G5
combines a positive cemented pair with a negative rear asphere; therefore its complete negative power cannot
be inferred from the L19/L20 doublet alone.

aberration correction. Glass labels reproduce the data file and denote coordinate-equivalent catalog
classes; no label establishes the actual supplier or melt.

field would use the wrong baseline.

The source-first calculation using the three-decimal θgF gives dPgF = 0.03032016. This small difference
reflects the two printed precisions; it is not treated as a patent correction. The implemented value uses

the patent-listed tag, because ¶0054 states that condition (5) governs the anomalous dispersion of positive
lens P. L17 repeats L10's coordinates but is not lens P and stays untagged.

Current spectral resolution can use a compatible catalog coefficient entry before falling back to dPgF-
corrected or plain Abbe approximations. Consequently, an authored source dispersion datum and a runtime
catalog hypothesis remain distinct pieces of evidence. Every element now resolves to a coordinate-compatible
catalog curve, including L14 (TAFD25L) and L15 (L-LAH53); those curves remain class approximations. No
overall APO performance, secondary-spectrum residual or measured spectral equivalence is claimed.

The source status is PUBLISHED. All three infinity stations and all three near stations are retained. G4,
consisting of L17 and L18, moves toward the image to focus closer while the other functional groups stay
fixed at the selected zoom position, apart from the printed rounding drift discussed below (¶0065 and
¶0083).

The increase in d29 and decrease in d32 encode G4 travel. At tele, the printed pair sum differs by −0.0001
mm between the infinity and near rows. That difference is preserved; it is not silently redistributed to
force exact conservation. It lies within the combined last-place rounding range of the four gap entries.

The near distances are measured from different current first vertices but sum with the physical track to
approximately the same 800 mm object-to-image distance. finiteConjugates records the three actual source
first-vertex distances. The separate zoomCloseFocusM labels use the corresponding image-plane distances.
Neither field is a reconstruction of the production minimum-focus specification.

Existing continuous interpolation stays available between the source stations. The gap values are
interpolated piecewise linearly; the focus distance label follows the existing inverse-distance convention.
These continuous states form an illustrative modeled family. Their geometry can be sampled, but the patent
does not certify those intermediate mechanical positions, distances or best focus.

At the unchanged source image plane, the computed paraxial near best-focus shifts are +0.052315, +0.094345
and +0.150178 mm at wide, middle and tele. The image plane is not moved to eliminate these residuals. They
should not be presented as an independently measured production focus error or assumed to be entirely
explained by decimal rounding.

Tamron describes the production drive as VXD. The prescription supports an internal negative focusing
doublet but does not encode motor dynamics, autofocus software, breathing compensation or real mechanical
travel limits.

## Verification, Aperture Modeling and Limitations
### Literal prescription and source precision
Sequential reduced-angle and independently implemented ABCD calculations agree. The computed infinity EFLs
differ from the final printed decimals of the source targets. Those tight comparisons remain failed
observations; independent source-last-place sensitivity envelopes contain the discrepancies. No radius,
glass coordinate, zoom spacing or focal-length scale is adjusted to force a printed match.

The paraxial infinity best-focus shifts relative to the source image plane are +0.043948, +0.021690 and
−0.005810 mm at wide, middle and tele. Those offsets are retained alongside the finite-state residuals. The
physical cover plate and source image plane remain unchanged throughout.

### Estimated rims and narrow nominal margins
Clear semi-diameters are inferred from source construction, pupil geometry and ray clearance. The source
does not publish them. The outer element rims agree with the Figure 1 wide-end silhouette within about 3%.
Two concave faces that the figure ends at a flat mounting annulus are modeled at that optical extent
instead of at the outer blank: source7 at 19.2 mm (figure about 19.0 mm) and source36A at 15.2 mm (figure
about 14.7–15.0 mm). Both lie outside every transmitted ray sampled up to the corner field, whose largest
heights there are about 18.30 mm and 14.90 mm. These two rims set the engine's chief-ray half-field
estimate at the wide and middle stations, 31.11° and 17.86°, which stay above the source half-fields of
30.97° and 15.37°; the traced corner chief ray and the computed vignetting curve are unaffected.

The L19/L20 doublet follows the figure's L19 blank, 15.2 mm on source33, with source34 and source35 at
14.7 mm, just inside the 14.9 mm radius at which the two L20 faces meet. Where Figure 1 draws a flat
annulus and a cylindrical edge (L4, L7, L9, L12, L14, L15, L19 and L21), the rendered element shows one
straight chamfer between its front and rear rims.

The implemented source15 radius is 14.86 mm and source26A radius is 14.41 mm, with gapSagFrac = 0.932. Every
source physical gap is unchanged; this parameter changes the modeled geometric clearance policy, not the
optical prescription.

The minimum nominal air separation is 0.130802 mm at source14→15. The minimum glass edge is 0.228449 mm at
source2→3. The smallest sampled non-stop radial headroom is 0.003451 mm, approximately 3.451 µm, at source15
and tele/infinity. Native rendering requires no hidden material trim over the tested grid.

This is not a manufacturing tolerance guarantee. A conservative printed-input sensitivity exercise leaves
about +3.365 µm in an executed adverse corner when the exact candidate iris is frozen. When the iris is re-
inferred from each perturbed EFL/f-number, an executed adverse corner instead overruns source15 by 2.638 µm.
The latter negative headroom remains a failed robustness diagnostic; it is not edited out of the record or
incorporated as a new prescription.

### Physical iris targeting and exact-edge numerical accuracy
A 41 × 41 grid of zoom/focus positions covers 1,681 states. Independently authored tight physical-iris
aiming passes all 3,362 exact axial-edge probes, both signs, and all 1,681 stop-centred corner chiefs at the
published 21.633 mm image semi-height. Inset probes at 99.9999% iris radius also pass. The original six
source states are distinguished from modeled intermediate states.

A coarser aiming run retains 1,066 raw exact-STO clips. Its stopping residual was 1e−8 mm, larger than the
unchanged engine aperture tolerance of 1e−9 mm. The separate tight calculation reaches stop residuals below
2e−12 mm and passes the original aperture check. The coarse failures remain recorded; they are not relabeled
as transmission or solved by inflating the iris.

Corner-chief passage is coverage of a central field ray, not a fully unvignetted corner bundle. In an
independent source-state skew-ring stress test, 288 rays divide into 132 clear, 72 physically clipped, 30
trace failures and 54 aims not certified on the available forward branch or requested tolerance. Unvignetted full-pupil
off-axis transmission is not claimed. A larger interpolated ring sweep retains its own separate failures.

### Current viewer behavior and interpolation
Current finite UI rays use entrance-pupil offsets with an approximate convergence slope. At finite distance,
stop height depends on both input height and slope; keeping the infinity-style pupil intercept can overfill
the physical iris. Re-aimed physical-source rays support the prescription, while some default UI fans still
clip. No shared viewer correction is part of this model.

The current infinitesimal-pupil readout gives infinity f-numbers 2.027637, 2.551235 and 2.849188, whereas
exact marginal-iris inversion reproduces the source calibration targets 2.0604, 2.6090 and 2.9089. These
calculations use different pupil definitions. The matching marginal values are calibration results, not
independent measurements of the iris or a certificate of full-field throughput.

Continuous interpolation remains available as an approximate visualization. Dense samples do not prove
clearance or focus accuracy at every real-valued control position, and the source does not supply a
production cam law. The nominal source model is useful within these documented limits; it does not claim a
corrected UI, verified full-field throughput, production tolerances or exact factory performance.
