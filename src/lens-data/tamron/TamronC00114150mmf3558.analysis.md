## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2014/0347522 A1  
**Application Number:** 14/283,680  
**Priority:** May 22, 2013 (JP 2013-108445)  
**Filed:** May 21, 2014  
**Published:** November 27, 2014  
**Inventor:** Yoshito Iwasawa  
**Applicant / Assignee:** Tamron Co., Ltd.  
**Title:** *Zoom Lens and Imaging Apparatus*  
**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 3

The prescription represented here is Example 3 of US 2014/0347522 A1, specifically Tables 9–12 and the corresponding optical section and aberration figures in Figs. 9–12. The patent describes a high-ratio zoom organized as six functional lens groups, G1 through G6, with a positive–negative–positive–negative–negative–positive power sequence. Example 3 contains 17 physical lens elements. In the LensVisualizer model, the thin optically active material layer on L4 is represented separately as `L4r`, so the authored `elements` array contains 18 material entries while `elementCount` remains 17.

The project correlation identifies this embodiment with the TAMRON 14–150mm f/3.5–5.8 Di III (Model C001). The patent itself does not name C001, so this is a production correlation rather than a manufacturer-confirmed patent attribution. The correlation is supported by several independent points:

1. The production lens and Example 3 both use 17 physical lens elements in 13 air-separated optical groups.
2. The patent's 14.43–145.40 mm focal-length range closely brackets the marketed 14–150 mm range without requiring uniform scaling.
3. The patent gives f/3.6708, f/5.4085, and f/5.9148 at its three zoom stations, while the product is marketed as f/3.5–5.8. The model deliberately keeps the marketing and design apertures separate.
4. Example 3 has aspheric surfaces on three physical lens elements: the L4 composite, both faces of L8, and the front of L10. Tamron states that C001 uses one hybrid aspherical element and two molded-glass aspherical elements. That agreement is correlation evidence; the patent does not itself label L8 and L10 by the production manufacturing terminology.
5. Example 3 focuses by translating only G4 toward the image side. Tamron states a production minimum object distance of 0.5 m and identifies C001 as a Micro Four Thirds lens with stepping-motor autofocus.
6. The timing is compatible: the patent claims May 2013 priority, was filed in May 2014, and Tamron gives a June 26, 2014 release date for C001.

The production metadata in the data file therefore uses the marketed 14–150 mm range, Micro Four Thirds mount, Four Thirds image format, seven aperture blades, and 0.5 m minimum object distance. Tamron's published product specification gives 17 elements in 13 groups, 1:3.8 maximum magnification at 150 mm and 0.5 m, a 52 mm filter thread, and a 63.5 mm maximum barrel diameter. These product facts are not substituted for the exact patent prescription.

No uniform scaling is applied: `s = 1.0`. Consequently all published radii and axial dimensions retained in the normalized model remain at source scale, and the aspheric transformation `A_p,scaled = A_p,patent / s^(p-1)` leaves every coefficient unchanged. `K` is likewise unchanged.

Several normalization choices are explicit rather than silent. Four 0.0100 mm same-radius generic cement/adhesive media at source surfaces 2, 22, 26, and 30 are collapsed into the downstream material while preserving the corresponding axial station. The separate rear plane-parallel plate at source surfaces 37–38 is excluded under the project rules; its propagation is replaced by an air-equivalent distance of 14.7689873418 mm from source surface 36 to the image plane. The stop plane itself is published as source surface 16, but the patent does not publish an absolute stop diameter.

## Optical Architecture

Example 3 is a six-functional-group zoom with power sequence `+ − + − − +`:

| Functional group | Elements | Final normalized paraxial EFL | Function in the published mechanism |
|---|---|---:|---|
| G1 | L1–L3 | +70.1691 mm | Positive front group; moves objectward during zooming |
| G2 | L4–L7 | −10.9977 mm | Strong negative variator group |
| G3 | L8–L13 | +16.0420 mm | Positive central group; moves objectward |
| G4 | L14–L15 | −28.7314 mm | Negative translating focus group |
| G5 | L16 | −68.8178 mm | Negative group moving on the same zoom track as G3 |
| G6 | L17 | +70.1747 mm | Fixed positive rear group |

These are functional zoom groups, not the same thing as the production construction count of 13 air-separated optical groups. The latter counts cemented assemblies as single air-separated groups; the former describes the six moving/fixed power units used by the zoom mechanism.

The group motion is unusually constrained for a zoom of this ratio. G3 and G5 move on the same track. G4 lies between them and changes its relative position: from wide to the intermediate station it moves imageward relative to G3, then reverses that relative motion toward telephoto. G6 remains fixed with respect to the image plane. This arrangement lets the focus group remain small while the larger surrounding groups provide the principal zooming motion.

The patent calls the design a telephoto type. The final normalized model satisfies the project's strict `TL/EFL < 1` criterion at the telephoto endpoint: the first-surface-to-image track is 141.3810 mm against a computed EFL of 145.3850 mm, giving `TL/EFL = 0.97246`. The criterion is not satisfied at the wide or intermediate stations, so “telephoto” is used here in the patent's tele-end architectural sense rather than as a global property of every zoom setting. The independently computed paraxial BFDs are 14.8198, 14.8172, and 14.7510 mm at the wide, intermediate, and telephoto stations. BFD exceeds EFL only at the wide endpoint, so the system is not described globally as retrofocus.

The final modeled focal lengths are 14.43237, 57.84746, and 145.38500 mm. They reproduce the patent's 14.43, 57.85, and 145.40 mm control points within the precision expected after the documented cement and rear-plate normalization. The fixed modeled stop produces f/3.69094, f/5.38753, and f/5.90528. Those values are stored in `nominalFno`; the marketed f/3.5–5.8 range remains separately represented as product metadata.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths in this section are standalone air-space paraxial EFLs computed from each final normalized material entry. They are not the same as the net power of a cemented assembly or the in-situ power of a functional zoom group. Where a cemented or composite assembly is important, its net EFL is stated separately.

### L1–L2 — Front Cemented Pair in G1

**L1:** `nd = 1.90366`, `νd = 31.31`. Glass: `904313 class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = −113.7493 mm`.  
**L2:** `nd = 1.49700`, `νd = 81.61`. Glass: `497816 fluorophosphate / ED-crown class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = +78.5358 mm`.

L1 is a negative meniscus followed immediately by the strongly positive, high-Abbe L2. Their normalized cemented assembly D1 has a weak positive net EFL of +251.8883 mm. The large Abbe-number contrast is consistent with ordinary chromatic balancing in the front group, but no anomalous-partial-dispersion claim is made because the patent provides no per-element line indices or `dPgF`.

The pair is followed by another positive element, so G1 as a whole is substantially more positive than D1 alone. This distinction matters: the pair's net power is weak, while the complete G1 has a computed EFL of +70.1691 mm in the final model.

### L3 — Positive Rear Element of G1

`nd = 1.61800`, `νd = 63.39`. Glass: `618634 phosphate-crown class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = +96.8386 mm`.

L3 is a positive meniscus separated from D1 by a 0.2 mm air gap. Its additional positive power brings G1 to the group power required for the wide-to-tele front-group translation. Because the element remains within G1 at all zoom positions, its standalone power does not change with zoom; only the inter-group air spacing changes.

### L4r + L4 — Hybrid Composite Negative Meniscus in G2

**L4r:** `nd = 1.51460`, `νd = 49.96`. Glass: `Unmatched (hybrid-asphere resin, nd=1.51460, vd=49.96)`. Standalone `f = −719.4751 mm`.  
**L4 substrate:** `nd = 1.91082`, `νd = 35.25`. Glass: `911353 class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = −16.9097 mm`.

The L4 model is deliberately split into the thin aspherical material layer and the high-index substrate. The layer is only 0.2 mm thick and is very weak in standalone first-order power; the substrate supplies nearly all of the composite's negative paraxial power. The complete H1 composite has a net EFL of −16.5221 mm.

This is the clearest case in the design where aspheric form and paraxial power should not be conflated. The resin layer materially changes the outer surface geometry while contributing little standalone first-order power. The identification as a hybrid-asphere layer is a production-correlation/modeling inference from the thin separate material and Tamron's statement that C001 contains one hybrid aspherical element; the patent itself does not use that manufacturing label.

### L5 — Strong Biconcave Negative in G2

`nd = 1.91082`, `νd = 35.25`. Glass: `911353 class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = −16.6783 mm`.

L5 continues the strong negative action of G2 after the L4 composite. Its high refractive index permits substantial negative power in a physically compact element. No vendor-specific glass designation is asserted because the patent gives only the `nd`/`νd` coordinates.

### L6 — Positive Element Embedded in the Negative G2 Group

`nd = 1.92286`, `νd = 20.88`. Glass: `923209 dense-flint class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = +14.7852 mm`.

L6 is a strong positive biconvex element placed between negative members of G2. The group nevertheless remains strongly negative, with a final normalized EFL of −10.9977 mm. The alternating power is therefore an in-situ balancing structure rather than a simple stack of negative singlets.

Its very low Abbe number relative to several neighboring elements contributes a large dispersion contrast in the d-line/Abbe representation. The available data do not establish anomalous partial dispersion.

### L7 — Rear Negative Meniscus of G2

`nd = 1.77250`, `νd = 49.62`. Glass: `773496 class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = −27.7273 mm`.

L7 closes G2 with negative power before the large variable spacing D15 and the aperture stop. Its placement near the stop separates the strongly powered variator from the central positive group G3.

### L8 — Double-Aspheric Positive Element at the Front of G3

`nd = 1.58313`, `νd = 59.46`. Glass: `583595 crown class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = +16.5580 mm`.

L8 is a strongly positive biconvex element with aspherical surfaces on both faces, `17A` and `18A`. It immediately follows the stop and supplies a large fraction of the local positive power at the front of G3. Because both faces are aspheric, the element can alter peripheral ray bending on both entry and exit without requiring an additional material interface.

The production source states that C001 contains two molded-glass aspherical elements. L8 is structurally compatible with one of them, but that manufacturing assignment is not stated by the patent and is not encoded as a glass-catalog fact.

### L9 — Negative Meniscus within G3

`nd = 1.86188`, `νd = 42.08`. Glass: `LASFN13 (Sumita catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified)`. Standalone `f = −24.7855 mm`.

L9 supplies a negative counter-term inside the net-positive G3 group. SUMITA LASFN13 (`1.86300 / 41.60`) is the unique compatible curve recovered from the vendor's discontinued-inclusive catalog. It is used as an optical equivalent for tracing, not as a production-supplier identification.

### L10–L11 — First Cemented Pair in the Rear Half of G3

**L10:** `nd = 1.58313`, `νd = 59.46`. Glass: `583595 crown class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = +21.2342 mm`.  
**L11:** `nd = 1.90766`, `νd = 33.41`. Glass: `908334 class (catalog unresolved)`. Standalone `f = −41.8873 mm`.

L10 carries the front asphere `21A` and is cemented to the negative L11. The final normalized D2 assembly has a net EFL of +43.2936 mm. The contrast between the positive crown-class member and the higher-index, lower-Abbe negative member provides both power balancing and ordinary achromatizing leverage in the Abbe-number model.

The normalized L11 thickness includes the collapsed 0.0100 mm adhesive layer from the source table. Its standalone EFL is therefore computed from the final authored geometry rather than the pre-collapse source thickness.

### L12–L13 — Second Cemented Pair in G3

**L12:** `nd = 1.91082`, `νd = 35.25`. Glass: `911353 class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = −27.1068 mm`.  
**L13:** `nd = 1.61882`, `νd = 64.32`. Glass: `M-PCD4 (Hoya catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified)`. Standalone `f = +15.0987 mm`.

The D3 pair is net positive, with a final normalized EFL of +30.8477 mm. It combines a high-index negative element with a lower-index, higher-Abbe positive partner. Together with L8, L9, and D2, it forms the complete G3 group, whose net EFL is +16.0420 mm.

As with L11, the L13 standalone focal length is based on the final normalized thickness that includes the collapsed 0.0100 mm source adhesive layer.

### L14–L15 — Negative Cemented Focus Group G4

**L14:** `nd = 1.80518`, `νd = 25.46`. Glass: `805255 dense-flint class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = +38.2268 mm`.  
**L15:** `nd = 1.80420`, `νd = 46.50`. Glass: `804465 class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = −16.0431 mm`.

G4 is a cemented doublet whose front member is individually positive and rear member strongly negative. The assembly is nevertheless net negative, with a final normalized EFL of −28.7314 mm. That net assembly power, not either standalone singlet power, governs the focusing action.

The patent explicitly assigns focus to G4 alone. Its small physical size relative to the front groups is consistent with the patent's stated motivation for reducing focus-group movement and moving mass. The data model preserves this single-group focus mechanism.

### L16 — Negative G5 Element

`nd = 1.80518`, `νd = 25.46`. Glass: `805255 dense-flint class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = −68.8178 mm`.

L16 is the complete G5 group. It moves on exactly the same zoom track as G3 while remaining separated from G4 by the variable D32 gap. This same-track relationship is a published feature of the patent and is reproduced numerically by the final data arrays.

### L17 — Fixed Positive Rear Group G6

`nd = 1.72916`, `νd = 54.67`. Glass: `729547 class (vendor unresolved)`. Standalone `f = +70.1747 mm`.

L17 forms the fixed positive G6 rear group. Its axial position relative to the image plane is constant across the three authored zoom stations. The rear sensor-side plane-parallel plate shown in the patent is not included as a LensVisualizer element; its propagation is folded into the final air-equivalent spacing after L17.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent states that focus from infinity toward a near object is performed by moving only G4 toward the image side. Table 12 gives proximity-object spacings whose published `D(0)` plus source physical track is 1000.005, 1000.149, and 999.962 mm at the wide, intermediate, and telephoto stations. Because the rounded Table 12 rows do not retrace as exact finite-conjugate solutions, they are treated as a nominal approximately 1.0 m source state rather than an exact reconstructed state. The production C001 minimum object distance is 0.5 m.

The data file therefore uses the focus status `CONSTRAINED_RECONSTRUCTION`. It does not treat the 0.5 m state as patent-published. At each zoom station, the reconstruction preserves the published one-degree-of-freedom mechanism by translating only G4 and enforcing a constant `D28 + D32` sum.

| Zoom position | Infinity D28 | Reconstructed close D28 | Infinity D32 | Reconstructed close D32 | G4 shift imageward | Paraxial magnification |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 14.43 mm | 1.4374 mm | 1.61054 mm | 12.1029 mm | 11.92976 mm | 0.17314 mm | 0.03392 |
| 57.85 mm | 6.0872 mm | 7.50382 mm | 7.4531 mm | 6.03648 mm | 1.41662 mm | 0.12710 |
| 145.40 mm | 3.6419 mm | 9.49816 mm | 9.8984 mm | 4.04214 mm | 5.85626 mm | 0.25713 |

The telephoto reconstruction gives a paraxial magnification of about 1:3.89. Tamron publishes 1:3.8 at 150 mm and 0.5 m for the production lens. The model is not force-fitted to that marketed figure; the difference is retained as part of the distinction between the patent embodiment and the released lens.

Tamron states that the production C001 uses a stepping motor for autofocus. The patent specifies the optical focus-group motion but does not specify that actuator, so the stepping-motor statement is treated as product metadata rather than a patent mechanism claim.

## Aspherical Surfaces

Example 3 has four aspherical surfaces: source surfaces 7, 17, 18, and 21, represented as `7A`, `17A`, `18A`, and `21A`. The patent writes the base term with a coefficient `ε` in the square-root expression. In LensVisualizer's standard conic form, the conversion is `K = ε − 1`. Every Example 3 row has `ε = 1.0000`, so every modeled asphere has `K = 0`.

With radial height `h` in millimeters, the modeled form is

`Z(h) = (h²/R) / [1 + sqrt(1 − (1+K)(h/R)²)] + A4 h⁴ + A6 h⁶ + A8 h⁸ + A10 h¹⁰`.

The source coefficients are:

| Surface | K | A4 | A6 | A8 | A10 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `7A` | 0 | +1.19556e-5 | −5.12224e-8 | +4.21707e-10 | +2.89639e-12 |
| `17A` | 0 | −4.81203e-5 | −6.04617e-7 | +2.40398e-8 | −4.15344e-10 |
| `18A` | 0 | +1.17843e-4 | −9.32847e-7 | +2.61092e-8 | −4.24829e-10 |
| `21A` | 0 | −5.75515e-6 | −1.80638e-7 | +2.44731e-9 | −5.43340e-12 |

The patent supplies coefficients only through A10. `A12` and `A14` are therefore zero in the data file. Because `s = 1.0`, no asphere coefficient rescaling is required; the general project transform `A_p / s^(p-1)` leaves these values unchanged.

Example 3 does not publish clear-aperture heights. The semi-diameters used to evaluate the following departures are modeled values that were separately checked against the current geometry rules; they are not patent aperture data.

| Surface | Verified modeled semi-diameter | Departure from K=0 base conic | Rim angle |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `7A` | 7.5 mm | +0.034565 mm | 6.55° |
| `17A` | 7.3 mm | −0.212780 mm | 25.40° |
| `18A` | 7.3 mm | +0.221466 mm | 10.51° |
| `21A` | 7.4 mm | −0.027589 mm | 6.14° |

The paired L8 surfaces carry by far the largest modeled departures of the four aspheres. Their opposite departure signs modify the element's entry and exit surface profiles in complementary directions. The thin `7A` resin layer has much smaller first-order power than its L4 substrate, so its aspheric function is predominantly a surface-form correction rather than a separate paraxial power group.

## Chromatic Correction Strategy

The design's chromatic strategy can be described only to the level supported by its d-line coordinates. The front D1 pair combines a high-index, low-Abbe negative element with a very high-Abbe positive element. G2 mixes the very low-Abbe L6 with higher-Abbe negative partners. G3 uses two positive/negative cemented pairs with substantial Abbe-number contrast. G4 likewise combines nearly equal refractive indices with markedly different Abbe numbers.

These combinations provide conventional degrees of freedom for longitudinal and lateral color correction across a 10× zoom ratio. They do not, by themselves, establish secondary-spectrum or apochromatic performance. No element is marked `apd`, and no `nC`, `nF`, `ng`, or `dPgF` field is authored.

## Verification Summary

The final prescription was re-evaluated directly from the TypeScript arrays using both ABCD multiplication and an independent sequential reduced-angle trace with state `[y, ν]`, where `ν = nθ`. Across the three infinity zoom stations, the two methods agree at numerical precision. The computed EFLs are 14.43237, 57.84746, and 145.38500 mm.

The modeled aperture is also an inference rather than a published stop size. The patent publishes F-number but not the absolute diaphragm diameter. In the normalized model, the three published F-numbers independently imply stop semi-diameters of 5.53950, 5.48791, and 5.50041 mm. The authored fixed `STO` semi-diameter, 5.50927 mm, is their arithmetic mean; it produces modeled apertures of f/3.69094, f/5.38753, and f/5.90528. The axial stop plane is source-defined, while its semi-diameter is model-derived.

The patent likewise publishes no surface semi-diameters. The authored values were inferred from ray envelopes, the patent optical section, the production construction envelope, and geometry limits. Their independent checks give a minimum element edge thickness of 0.24241 mm, a maximum actual rim angle of 37.32°, and a worst shared-band cross-gap intrusion of 0.86672 of the available gap, below the configured 0.90 limit. No positive-K conic-limit case exists because all four aspheres have `K = 0`.

The final normalized Petzval sum, evaluated surface by surface as `φ/(n·n′)`, is +0.000368262 mm⁻¹, corresponding to a simple reciprocal radius of about +2715.46 mm. This number is a first-order curvature diagnostic; it is not used here as a substitute for the patent's full aberration plots.

Two field-angle discrepancies remain explicitly unresolved in the source. Table 11 lists 37.102° at wide and 3.671° at telephoto, while the rendered aberration figures read 37.012° in Fig. 10 and 4.279° in Fig. 12. The data file preserves the raw Table 11 zoom prescription; the figure readings are treated only as documented source corrections for interpretation and are not silently written back into the table values.

There is also a direct motion-direction contradiction. Paragraph 0096 says that G2 moves toward the image side from wide to telephoto, but the Table 11 spacings, when normalized to a fixed image plane, move the G2 front 3.7597 mm toward the object side. The quantitative model follows the table and records the prose contradiction rather than reconciling it by assumption.
