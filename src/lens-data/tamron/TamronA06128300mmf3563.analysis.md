# TAMRON AF 28-300mm f/3.5-6.3 XR Di LD Aspherical [IF] MACRO

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2003/0156333 A1  
**Filed:** July 1, 2002  
**Priority:** February 20, 2002 (JP 2002-43151)  
**Published:** August 21, 2003  
**Inventor:** Yasuharu Yamada  
**Assignee / applicant in source publication:** none identified  
**Title:** *High Zoom Ratio Lens*  
**Embodiment analyzed:** Job-card Example 1, normalized to the patent's sole preferred numerical embodiment / Table 1

The prescription is the sole numerical embodiment published in US 2003/0156333 A1. The patent describes a four-functional-group zoom with positive, negative, positive, and positive group powers, approximately 75° wide-angle coverage, approximately 10× zoom, a wide-end aperture in the f/3–4 range, and a telephoto aperture in the f/6–7 range. It further specifies that only the second functional group moves for focusing. [1, ¶0009–¶0020, ¶0036–¶0042]

The production correlation remains fixed to the Tamron AF 28–300mm f/3.5–6.3 XR Di LD Aspherical [IF] Macro, Model A061, but the strongest manufacturer evidence runs through its immediate predecessor, Model A06. The patent does not name either model, so the attribution remains an inference rather than a manufacturer-confirmed patent mapping. The following evidence is convergent:

1. Tamron released Model A06 in 2002 and describes it as a completely new optical system for a 28–300 mm f/3.5–6.3 zoom. Its published construction is 13 groups / 15 pieces with a 62 mm filter, three composite aspherical lenses, two LD lenses, 0.49 m minimum focus throughout the zoom range, and 1:2.9 maximum magnification at 300 mm. [12]
2. The patent claims the same compact four-functional-group problem class, specifically contrasting the earlier 185D and targeting an approximately 62 mm filter class. Its sole numerical embodiment is 29.00–289.53 mm, uses three 0.2 mm bonded aspheric media, and resolves to 15 physical lens pieces in 13 air-separated component groups. [1, ¶0003–¶0008, Table 1]
3. Tamron's own history states that the 2004 A061 was released **based on Model A06** as the digital-era Di version. [13]
4. A061 retains the same marketed 28–300 mm f/3.5–6.3 range, 13-group/15-piece count, 0.49 m all-range MFD, 1:2.9 telephoto magnification, 83.7 mm length, 73 mm maximum diameter, 62 mm filter, and nine-blade aperture published for A06. [2; 12]
5. The patent specifies that G2 alone moves for focusing. Both A06 and A061 carry Tamron's `[IF]` designation, and the data model preserves the patent's G2-only focus constraint. [1, ¶0020; 2; 12]

This establishes A06 as the chronologically direct production correlate and A061 as the fixed job-card lens through Tamron's documented A06-to-A061 lineage. It does not establish that Tamron used the patent prescription unchanged in production; that remains a modeling inference.

The marketing and design values are kept separate. Tamron's product specification is 28–300 mm at f/3.5–6.3. The final prescription computes 28.995813 mm at wide and 289.528364 mm at tele. Its single fixed physical stop yields modeled wide-open f-numbers of 3.500000, 5.354420, and 6.512208 at the three defined zoom states. The patent itself prints 3.50, 5.37, and 6.50. None of these exact design quantities is replaced by the rounded marketing label.

No uniform scaling is applied. Radii, thicknesses, variable spacings, refractive indices, and aspheric coefficients remain at the patent's source scale. The data file also contains no sensor-cover glass, filter plate, inactive dummy plane, flare-cutter plane, or mechanical component. The 62 mm filter remains a mechanical/product specification rather than a prescription surface.

The patent's rendered Table 1 and asphere table are the numerical authority where OCR is unreliable. In particular, surface 23 is `R = 19.39 mm`, and the scientific exponents of the asphere coefficients are taken from the rendered page. These are OCR recoveries, not substantive corrections to the patent. The rendered condition (5) lower bound is read as `2.7`; parsed OCR can render it as `2.74`. No prescription value has been altered to force agreement with the patent's evaluated condition (10), discussed below.

## Optical Architecture

The design is a high-ratio four-functional-group zoom with power sequence G1(+), G2(−), G3(+), G4(+). The data file contains 15 physical lens pieces in 13 air-separated component groups, represented by 18 optical-media entries because three bonded aspheric layers are modeled as distinct media. Those three layers are R6, R16, and R29. The distinction is important: the 18-entry `elements` array is a computational representation, while `elementCount: 15` and `groupCount: 13` preserve the physical/manufacturer count.

Independent paraxial calculation from the final data gives the functional-group focal lengths:

| Functional group | Surfaces | EFL (mm) | First-order character |
|---|---|---:|---|
| G1 | 1–5 | +96.961750 | positive front group |
| G2 | 6A–14 | −15.076243 | strong negative variator / focus group |
| G3 | 16A–22 | +42.913655 | positive relay group |
| G4 | 23–32 | +68.916622 | positive rear group |

These are complete air-to-air functional-group powers computed from the final prescription; they are not the same quantities as the standalone focal lengths of individual glass pieces.

Zooming from wide to tele changes three internal air gaps in the patent prescription. D5 increases from 1.698 to 57.566 mm, while D14 decreases from 20.845 to 0.991 mm and D22 decreases from 6.685 to 1.117 mm. This reproduces the patent's qualitative kinematics: G1–G2 separation widens, G2–G3 separation narrows, and G3–G4 separation narrows. [1, ¶0009]

The final air spacing after surface 32 is a derived Gaussian back focal distance, not a published Table 1 row. It is 40.055462 mm, 71.203877 mm, and 89.648898 mm at wide, middle, and tele. This provides the sequential model's image plane without adding an optical cover plate or filter that the patent does not prescribe.

The aperture stop is published at patent surface 15 and is therefore not positionally inferred. Its modeled semi-diameter, 9.512269 mm, is inferred from the wide-end f/3.50 state and then held fixed. The resulting modeled f-numbers at middle and tele differ slightly from the patent's rounded tabulation, while remaining consistent with a single physical iris.

Semi-diameters are not published in the patent. They are modeling values inferred from meridional ray containment over every defined zoom/focus state and then checked against edge thickness, actual rim slope, conic-domain limits, cross-gap intrusion, and off-axis containment. They must not be read as patent dimensions.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The subsections follow the 13 physical air-separated component groups. For hybrid groups, the thin bonded layer and its glass substrate are discussed together. Standalone focal lengths are isolated-in-air quantities computed from each element's two bounding radii and center thickness; compound focal lengths are independently computed for the bonded or cemented assembly. Neither should be confused with the complete functional-group behavior listed above.

### D1 — L1 + L2 front cemented pair

**L1:** nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.8. Glass: S-TIH53 (OHARA equivalent). Standalone f = −174.363 mm.  
**L2:** nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6. Glass: S-FPL51 (OHARA equivalent). Standalone f = +123.060 mm.  
**Cemented D1:** net f = +414.966 mm.

L1 is a negative meniscus and L2 is a biconvex positive member. Although the isolated members have opposite signs, the cemented pair is only weakly positive as a compound. This is a useful example of why standalone element power cannot be substituted for cemented net power.

The pair combines a high-index, low-Abbe member with a very high-Abbe positive crown equivalent. At the model level, the large dispersion contrast supplies chromatic balancing at the front of G1. The patent does not name the glass families, so S-TIH53 and S-FPL51 are current coordinate-compatible catalog equivalents rather than source-stated production glasses.

### L3 — rear positive member of G1

**L3:** nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.6. Glass: S-LAH66N (OHARA equivalent). Standalone f = +126.181 mm.

L3 is a positive meniscus separated from D1 by a 0.2 mm air gap. Together with the weakly positive D1 compound it raises the complete G1 power to f = +96.962 mm. The element therefore contributes materially to the positive front-group convergence without requiring the cemented pair itself to carry strong net power.

The S-LAH66N annotation is catalog-derived. Its current OHARA coordinates, nd = 1.772499 and νd = 49.55, round to the patent's 1.77250 / 49.6 entry. [5]

### H1 — R6 bonded aspheric layer + L4

**R6:** nd = 1.53610, νd = 41.2. Glass: Unmatched bonded aspheric resin. Standalone base-surface f = −326.972 mm.  
**L4:** nd = 1.83481, νd = 42.7. Glass: S-LAH55VS (OHARA equivalent). Standalone f = −24.993 mm.  
**Hybrid H1:** net paraxial f = −23.194 mm.

H1 begins the strong negative G2. The 0.2 mm R6 medium is modeled as a distinct bonded layer because the patent gives it its own refractive index and thickness before the L4 substrate. The patent does not identify a resin trade name; the `Unmatched` label is therefore intentional.

The layer's aspheric outer surface is 6A. Its spherical-base power is weak relative to L4, while the bonded compound remains strongly negative. The main design significance of R6 is therefore not a new high-power lens piece, but an aspheric correction surface placed directly on a strongly powered negative substrate.

### L5 — biconcave negative member of G2

**L5:** nd = 1.69680, νd = 55.5. Glass: S-LAL14 (OHARA equivalent). Standalone f = −33.679 mm.

L5 adds negative power within G2 after the H1 hybrid. Its biconcave form and moderate dispersion place another independent degree of freedom inside the variator/focus group. The patent's functional description assigns focusing to G2 as a whole rather than to any one glass piece, so no separate focusing role is attributed to L5 alone.

### L6 — strong positive member inside G2

**L6:** nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.8. Glass: S-TIH53 (OHARA equivalent). Standalone f = +20.113 mm.

L6 is a strongly positive biconvex element inside the net-negative G2. Its positive standalone power is larger in magnitude than the complete G2 power, but it is embedded between substantial negative members. The functional group therefore remains negative at f = −15.076 mm.

This internal alternation of strong positive and negative powers provides more aberration-control freedom than a simple thin negative variator. The patent discusses the need to balance compactness, group power, and aberration variation across the approximately 10× zoom range, but does not assign a specific named aberration to L6 individually. [1, ¶0021–¶0024]

### L7 — rear negative member of G2

**L7:** nd = 1.83481, νd = 42.7. Glass: S-LAH55VS (OHARA equivalent). Standalone f = −25.594 mm.

L7 is the rear biconcave negative member of G2. It completes the negative focusing group immediately ahead of the stop. Because G2 alone moves during focusing, every element from R6 through L7 translates together in the constrained model; no internal floating motion within G2 is introduced.

### H2 — R16 bonded aspheric layer + L8

**R16:** nd = 1.53610, νd = 41.2. Glass: Unmatched bonded aspheric resin. Standalone base-surface f = +25,252.571 mm.  
**L8:** nd = 1.61800, νd = 63.4. Glass: S-PHM52 (OHARA equivalent). Standalone f = +50.945 mm.  
**Hybrid H2:** net paraxial f = +50.958 mm.

H2 begins G3 immediately behind the stop. The front and rear base radii of the 0.2 mm R16 layer are both +30.74 mm, so its isolated spherical-base power is almost zero. The compound's first-order power is consequently almost identical to L8 alone.

That first-order result clarifies the purpose of the bonded layer: the aspheric surface 16A can modify higher-order ray behavior with negligible change to the paraxial power of the substrate assembly. This is an inference from the verified geometry and powers, not a manufacturing statement in the patent.

### L9 — low-dispersion positive member of G3

**L9:** nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6. Glass: S-FPL51 (OHARA equivalent). Standalone f = +59.283 mm.

L9 is a positive element following H2. The second use of the S-FPL51 coordinate class places a very high-Abbe positive member in the rear half of the optical system as well as in G1. In combination with the surrounding higher-dispersion members, this supplies distributed chromatic balancing rather than concentrating all low-dispersion glass in the front group.

### L10 — rear negative member of G3

**L10:** nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.8. Glass: S-TIH53 (OHARA equivalent). Standalone f = −66.407 mm.

L10 is a biconcave negative element following the two positive members of G3. The complete G3 remains positive at f = +42.914 mm. The use of the same high-index, low-Abbe coordinate class as L1 and L6 provides a strong dispersion contrast against L9's S-FPL51-class positive glass.

### L11 — front positive member of G4

**L11:** nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.2. Glass: S-FSL5 (OHARA equivalent). Standalone f = +34.113 mm.

L11 is the first element of G4 and has substantial positive power. G4 is the final positive functional group and moves during zooming according to the patent's four-group kinematics. [1, ¶0009]

The S-FSL5 equivalent has relatively high Abbe number but is less extreme than S-FPL51. In the modeled palette it provides a positive low-dispersion contribution before the stronger negative and positive powers farther back in G4.

### L12 — negative meniscus in G4

**L12:** nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.6. Glass: S-LAH66N (OHARA equivalent). Standalone f = −28.108 mm.

L12 follows L11 as a strongly powered negative meniscus. The sign reversal between L11 and L12 gives the rear functional group significant internal power distribution while preserving a positive net group focal length.

### H3 — L13 + R29 rear bonded asphere

**L13:** nd = 1.51823, νd = 59.0. Glass: S-NSL3 (OHARA equivalent). Standalone f = +31.418 mm.  
**R29:** nd = 1.53610, νd = 41.2. Glass: Unmatched bonded aspheric resin. Standalone base-surface f = +123,570.756 mm.  
**Hybrid H3:** net paraxial f = +31.443 mm.

L13 is a biconvex positive substrate. The 0.2 mm R29 layer is bonded to its rear surface, and the layer's rear surface 29A is aspherical. As with R16, the equal base radii on the two resin boundaries make the layer nearly neutral in first-order power; the compound focal length remains essentially that of L13.

This arrangement again separates paraxial power from higher-order correction: L13 supplies the converging power, while the thin bonded layer provides an independently shaped rear surface without materially changing the compound's Gaussian focal length.

### D2 — L14 + L15 rear cemented pair

**L14:** nd = 1.54072, νd = 47.2. Glass: S-TIL2 (OHARA equivalent). Standalone f = +18.470 mm.  
**L15:** nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.6. Glass: S-LAH66N (OHARA equivalent). Standalone f = −13.133 mm.  
**Cemented D2:** net f = −52.878 mm.

The final cemented pair contains two very strong standalone elements of opposite sign and is net negative as a compound. G4 nevertheless remains positive because L11, H3, and the other members precede D2. This is another case where the sign of a cemented subassembly cannot be used as a shortcut for the sign of the full functional group.

The final surface is followed only by the derived air back-focus spacing to the image plane. No sensor-cover or filter glass is appended.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent publishes only d-line refractive index and Abbe number; it does not name vendors or glass trade names. The data file therefore preserves the patent's nd / νd coordinates while using selected current OHARA glasses as modeling representatives for the corresponding coordinate classes. Cross-vendor tables map several of these six-digit classes to HOYA, Schott, HIKARI, Sumita, and CDGM counterparts and explicitly caution that equivalent codes do not imply identical composition. The OHARA representatives supply the stored nC, nF, ng, and dPgF values; they are not asserted as the actual 2002 production melts. [3–11; 14]

| Catalog-equivalent glass | Patent nd / νd used in data | dPgF stored | Elements | Modeling note |

| S-FPL51 (OHARA) | 1.49700 / 81.6 | +0.0280 | L2, L9 | very high-Abbe positive glass |
| S-LAH66N (OHARA) | 1.77250 / 49.6 | −0.0094 | L3, L12, L15 | lanthanum high-index class |

| S-LAL14 (OHARA) | 1.69680 / 55.5 | −0.0082 | L5 | moderate-dispersion negative member |
| S-PHM52 (OHARA) | 1.61800 / 63.4 | +0.0051 | L8 | positive G3 substrate |
| S-FSL5 (OHARA) | 1.48749 / 70.2 | +0.0022 | L11 | high-Abbe rear-group positive member |

The residuals between patent coordinates and the selected OHARA representatives are small: all catalog-resolvable bulk elements remain well inside the project's round-trip compatibility window. The mapping is not unique at the vendor level. For example, the 835427 coordinate class around L4/L7 has multiple current cross-vendor equivalents, and even OHARA lists closely related S-LAH55V/S-LAH55VS variants. The stored S-LAH55VS line data are therefore a representative spectral model, not a source-proven glass identity. [14]

The two S-FPL51-class elements provide the strongest high-Abbe entries in the model, while S-TIH53-class elements provide the opposite high-index/low-Abbe extreme. Tamron's two-LD production count corresponds to those two positions, so L2 and L9 carry `apd: "inferred"` diagram tags; the tags describe that production correlation, not a proven production melt. The presence of explicit line indices and dPgF values permits line-aware chromatic modeling for the mapped bulk glasses. It does not justify calling the lens apochromatic, and the three unmatched bonded media remain limited to their patent nd / νd values.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent specifies an inner-focusing system in which G2 alone moves. It notes the practical advantage of avoiding motion of the relatively large first group and also states that, toward the closest object, G2 approaches G1. [1, ¶0020, ¶0028]

The patent does not publish finite-distance spacing rows. The final data therefore labels the focus model `CONSTRAINED_RECONSTRUCTION`, not `PUBLISHED`. The reconstruction uses four constraints:

- G2 is the only moving focus group.
- The image plane remains fixed at each zoom state.
- D5 + D14 is conserved at each zoom state, so G2 translation changes only its two adjacent gaps.
- Tamron's official 0.49 m minimum focusing distance is interpreted as image-plane-to-object distance. [2]

The resulting close-focus states are:

| Zoom state | D5 infinity (mm) | D5 close (mm) | D14 infinity (mm) | D14 close (mm) | G2 motion toward object (mm) | Computed magnification |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Wide | 1.698000 | 0.676762 | 20.845000 | 21.866238 | 1.021238 | 1:13.8219 |
| Middle | 32.301000 | 29.563470 | 10.646000 | 13.383530 | 2.737530 | 1:4.94188 |
| Tele | 57.566000 | 47.468713 | 0.991000 | 11.088287 | 10.097287 | 1:2.89177 |

D22 and the Gaussian back-focus spacing remain unchanged during focusing. No additional floating motion is introduced.

The telephoto reconstruction provides an external consistency check: its computed 1:2.89177 ratio agrees with Tamron's rounded 1:2.9 maximum-magnification specification at 300 mm. [2] This agreement supports the constrained reconstruction, but it does not convert the reconstructed spacings into patent-published data.

## Aspherical Surfaces

The patent marks three aspherical surfaces: 6, 16, and 29. The data file labels them `6A`, `16A`, and `29A` according to the current surface-label convention. [1, ¶0037–¶0038, Table 1]

The patent's sag equation is

$$
X(H)=\frac{H^2/R}{1+\sqrt{1-(1+e)(H/R)^2}}+A_4H^4+A_6H^6+A_8H^8+A_{10}H^{10}.
$$

Because the conic term explicitly uses `1 + e`, the published `e` is already the standard conic constant K used by the data schema. No `K = e - 1` conversion is required. No uniform scaling is applied, so none of the polynomial coefficients is rescaled.

| Surface | Bonded layer | K | A4 | A6 | A8 | A10 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 6A | R6 on L4 | +33.6256 | +5.42142e-6 | +9.41353e-9 | −7.17372e-11 | +5.53524e-13 |
| 16A | R16 on L8 | −2.1180 | −2.13533e-7 | +4.22277e-10 | +1.10635e-10 | −8.53798e-13 |
| 29A | R29 on L13 | +27.3693 | +1.56165e-5 | −5.46433e-9 | +2.26112e-10 | −7.28883e-12 |

The data stores A12 and A14 as zero because the current schema provides those standard slots and the patent gives no nonzero terms beyond A10.

Surface 6A lies on the front of the strongly negative H1 hybrid. The R6 layer has weak standalone base-surface power compared with L4, so its aspheric profile primarily adds shape freedom to an already strong negative compound.

Surface 16A lies on R16, whose equal +30.74 mm base radii make its isolated first-order power nearly zero. H2 therefore has essentially the same paraxial focal length as L8 alone. The asphere can change higher-order ray behavior without materially changing the compound's Gaussian power.

Surface 29A behaves similarly. R29's equal −68.00 mm base radii make its isolated first-order power negligible, while H3 retains the approximately +31.4 mm focal length of L13. The rear aspheric surface is therefore a corrective degree of freedom superimposed on L13's positive power.

The patent publishes no aperture heights or semi-diameters, so the following departures are model results rather than source values. At the validated authored semi-diameters, the full asphere relative to a sphere of the same paraxial radius departs by +0.197315 mm at 6A (h = 11.7 mm), −0.105854 mm at 16A (h = 10.4 mm), and −0.014803 mm at 29A (h = 9.0 mm). The semi-diameters are independently validated geometry values and are not treated as patent apertures.

## Chromatic Correction Strategy

The chromatic strategy is inferred from the final glass map rather than described explicitly by the patent. The palette spans νd from 23.8 to 81.6 and repeats the extreme S-TIH53 / S-FPL51 coordinate classes in more than one functional group. This permits strong dispersion balancing without forcing a single cemented pair to carry all longitudinal color correction.

In G1, the weakly positive D1 compound combines L1's high-index, low-Abbe coordinate with L2's very high-Abbe positive coordinate. In G3, L9 supplies the same S-FPL51-class high-Abbe coordinate while L10 returns to the S-TIH53-class low-Abbe extreme. G4 uses a less extreme set of S-FSL5-, S-LAH66N-, S-NSL3-, and S-TIL2-class coordinates.

The OHARA-derived nC, nF, ng, and dPgF values make this interpretation line-aware for the matched bulk glasses. The three bonded layers remain Abbe-only because no authoritative line data has been identified for their 1.53610 / 41.2 medium. Accordingly, the model supports discussion of distributed chromatic balancing but not an APO designation or a claim that the patent specified anomalous-dispersion glass by trade name.

## Verification Summary

The final data file was re-evaluated numerically before analysis authoring. Sequential height/reduced-angle tracing and an independent height/slope ABCD construction agree to floating-point precision at all three infinity zoom states.

| State | Computed EFL (mm) | Patent f (mm) | Derived BFD (mm) | Modeled wide-open f-number |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Wide | 28.995813 | 29.00 | 40.055462 | 3.500000 |
| Middle | 92.212091 | 92.21 | 71.203877 | 5.354420 |
| Tele | 289.528364 | 289.53 | 89.648898 | 6.512208 |

The reference-plane tests change character across the zoom. At wide, BFD/EFL = 1.38142, so the wide state meets the project's retrofocus criterion, while TL/EFL = 4.41386 and is not telephoto. The middle state meets neither criterion. At tele, TL/EFL = 0.718489, so the tele state meets the telephoto criterion, while BFD/EFL = 0.309638 and is not retrofocus. These labels are state-specific rather than names for the whole zoom.

The full-system principal-plane locations relative to the first vertex (H1) and last optical surface (H2) are +41.704409 / +11.059649 mm at wide, +92.966748 / −21.008215 mm at middle, and −246.724147 / −199.879466 mm at tele. The sign changes are a consequence of the strongly varying equivalent system and are evaluated in the same normalized front-to-rear reference convention as the BFD and track values.

The surface-by-surface Petzval sum, using `φ/(n·n′)` at every refracting surface, is +0.001856106583 mm⁻¹, with reciprocal magnitude 538.762 mm. This small net results from cancellation among much larger positive and negative surface contributions; it is not inferred from a thin-lens shortcut.

The inferred geometry was also tested over all three zoom positions at both infinity and reconstructed close focus. The minimum conservative edge thickness is 0.143227 mm, the largest actual rim angle is 48.7246°, the tightest margin to the 90% cross-gap intrusion boundary is 0.070061 mm, and the smallest tested-ray semi-diameter containment margin outside the stop is 0.140172 mm. These are validation results for the modeled semi-diameters, not patent dimensions.

No project runtime fields are added to conceal invalid geometry. The patent-published stop location is retained, the three positive-K conic domains remain comfortably outside the authored semi-diameters, and the data does not use the obsolete universal `sd/|R| < 0.90` criterion.
