# SCHACHT TRAVEGON 35mm f/3.5

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [DE 1,020,809 B, _Objektiv_](https://patents.google.com/patent/DE1020809B/)

**Inventors:** Ludwig Bertele

**Assignee:** None; personal application by Ludwig Bertele, Heerbrugg

**Filed:** 12 January 1955

**Published:** 12 December 1957

**Priority:** Switzerland, 18 June 1954

**Example details:** Beispiel 2, table on PDF p. 2 and claim 5 on p. 3

Beispiel 2's radii and axial distances are scaled by 0.35 from 100 to 35 mm. It may be the Travegon's design: [AllPhotoLenses](https://allphotolenses.com/lenses/item/c_3226.html) credits Bertele, lists 35 mm f/3.5 and six elements in three groups, and its [diagram](http://allphotolenses.com/public/files/optical/b361f4c9a9c43e7ab72b9a1b1571b10c.png) shows Beispiel 2's flat interface and thick L4. No Schacht factory prescription confirms the identification.

## Optical Architecture

Three cemented doublets form an all-spherical negative–positive–positive wide-angle objective. The patent calls them Glieder A, B and C: A is a diverging meniscus convex toward the object; B and C collect the beam around the iris. Their focal lengths are −51.26, +83.05 and +23.65 mm.

The first air gap follows the table's 18.26 mm over the text's inconsistent 0.1836 F. Group A's printed −1.47 F approximates the calculated −1.465 F.

L1/L2 provide net negative power; L3/L4 share plane surface 5. L5 is the more dispersive partner of positive L6. The listed individual powers are in air, distinct from assembly powers.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The indices refer to helium d. For L1, L2, L4, L5 and L6, Δnd = 0; Δνd is +0.08, −0.09, +0.05, 0 and 0 respectively. L5 matches Sumita LF7, the light flint of Schott's LF7 type, exactly. Sumita LAK6 is discontinued. Historical suppliers and melts remain unknown. L3 has no close match. Unlisted partial dispersion and apochromatic performance remain unestablished.

## Focus Mechanism

Only infinity focus is modeled; no finite-focus spacings are printed. The 0.5 m minimum comes from the [AllPhotoLenses M42 entry](https://allphotolenses.com/lenses/item/c_3226.html). An [MFlenses forum thread](https://forum.mflenses.com/schacht-travegon-vs-other-retrofocus-wide-angles-t52837.html) describes an automatic Exakta-mount version, also listed by [Kamerastore](https://kamerastore.com/en-us/products/a-schacht-ulm-35mm-f3-5-r-travegon-exakta); [Flashback Camera](https://flashbackcamera.jp/en/products/024698-leica-copy-travegon-35mm-f35-l) lists a rangefinder-coupled Leica-thread version with a 1 m minimum.

## Image Plane, Iris and Field Limits

Computed EFL is 35.003 mm at f/3.5; paraxial BFD is 33.656 mm, total track 62.85 mm, and infinity defocus negligible. [US 2,772,601](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2772601A/en) Example 2 rounds the same design at about 1.0055×. Its back-focus convention is paraxial: US Example 1's 98.5 mm agrees with the computed 98.54 mm of German Beispiel 1.

The iris has 5.06 before and 2.90 after it in patent units, reversing the printed order. That order would put it 1.015 mm behind surface 6, inside L4's rear rim, which reaches 1.18 mm behind the vertex where its curve ends at 5.7 mm. Both drawings support placement near C. Its modeled position is 23.10 mm from the first vertex, with an inferred radius of 4.571 mm.

AllPhotoLenses lists eight iris blades and an f/3.5–16 range, likely for the early chrome version. The later black version has six blades and reaches f/22.

No rim is printed, so all are inferred. The patent's only drawing shows Beispiel 1, which is drawn to scale along the axis, so it guides shapes and proportions rather than sizes. As drawn there, group C is square-cut, here at 6.7 mm. L4's rear curve ends short of its edge, here at 5.7 mm with a flat step out to the 7 mm edge it shares with L3. L2's rear curve ends in a flat step, here from 8.5 to 9.4 mm, before the chamfer to the 12.5 mm front rim. The drawing makes groups B and C smaller still relative to the front group, but at those sizes they would cut the f/3.5 beam or corner light, so the larger rims stay. The stated 32° half-field reaches 21.54 of the 21.65 mm film half-diagonal, and the 32.1° corner chief ray clears. At f/3.5 the rims pass about 70% of the beam at 29° and 59% at 32°, with group C clipping one side of the corner beam. Measured production coverage is unknown.
