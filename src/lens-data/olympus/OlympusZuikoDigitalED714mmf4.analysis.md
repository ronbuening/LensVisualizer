# OLYMPUS ZUIKO DIGITAL ED 7-14mm f/4.0 — US 7,280,284 B2, Example 3

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 7,280,284 B2.  
**Inventor:** Atsujiro Ishii.  
**Assignee:** Olympus Corporation.  
**Filed:** September 7, 2005.  
**Granted:** October 9, 2007.  
**Priority:** JP 2004-259416, September 7, 2004.  
**Example details:** Example 3, numerical prescription on US grant page 16; section Figure 3; infinity aberrations Figure 7; finite aberrations Figure 8. US 2006/0050406 A1 and JP 4632724 B2, publications of the same application, print the same prescription and the same asphere equation.

**Scope:** The data file transcribes Example 3. Its identification with the production lens is an inference from the construction, the aspherical and low-dispersion element positions, the focal-length range and the close-focus distance; Olympus has not published a prescription.

The September 28, 2004 Olympus announcement and September 2010 manufacturer catalog identify the Four Thirds DSLR ZUIKO DIGITAL ED 7–14 mm F4.0: 18 elements/12 groups, two double-sided aspheric elements (including a rear ED element), two Super ED plus two ED elements, 0.25 m minimum focus distance, seven diaphragm blades, and 86.5×119.5 mm/780 g. The announcement expected spring 2005 availability. This is not the Micro Four Thirds M.Zuiko 7–14 mm f/2.8 or Panasonic 7–14 mm.

The association rests on the 18/12 construction; two double-sided aspheric elements at L2 and L16, the positions the catalog drawing marks as aspherical; low-dispersion glass (nd 1.497, νd 81.54) at L4, L6, L14 and L16, the positions the drawing marks Super ED, Super ED, ED and ED glass-molded aspherical; the 7.1/9.6/13.7 mm focal lengths at F4; the negative-front/positive-rear retrofocus layout; and the printed 250 mm object-to-image close-focus state, which equals the production 0.25 m minimum focus distance. Examples 1 (9.5–18.3 mm) and 2 (17 elements in 11 groups) fit the product less well. These similarities do not prove that Example 3 was manufactured unchanged, and the patent's single low-dispersion glass does not distinguish Olympus's Super ED from ED elements.

## Optical Architecture

The source has 31 optical surfaces including the aperture stop at S19, with 18 glass elements in 12 air-separated components. The negative front group contains L1–L10; the positive rear group contains L11–L18. Six cemented interfaces form four doublets and one triplet. There is no camera-side plate in this example. The group labels describe optical power blocks, not twelve independent zoom-moving groups.

At infinity the source varies D18 = 16.7/8.6/1.1 mm, D19 = 3.2/1.6/0.6 mm and D31 = 33.6/40.4/51.8 mm. All other internal dimensions keep their printed values. Relative to the fixed image plane, the front group moves on a concave path and returns to its wide-end position at tele, while the rear group advances toward the object. The stop-to-rear-group gap narrows.

The model has 33 equally spaced zoom stations; stations 0, 16 and 32 are the printed rows. Between them D18 and D19 interpolate linearly, and D31 is solved so that the paraxial focus offset varies linearly between the printed stations, which are left as printed. These stations are a smooth interpolation, not the production cam. With 33 stations, linear interpolation between neighbouring stations adds at most 0.0067 mm of defocus.

## Glass Identification / Selection

The patent names no supplier. The model uses the printed d-line indices and Abbe numbers; fifteen elements carry the Ohara catalog glass whose nd/νd equal the printed pair (S-LAH51, L-LAL13, S-LAH58, S-FPL51, S-TIL1, S-NSL36, S-TIM2, S-LAM3, S-FSL5), which supplies their dispersion curves as proxies, not production identity. L7, L9 and L10 have no catalog equivalent.

L4, L6, L14 and L16 each have nd 1.49700, νd 81.54 and printed θgF 0.54, stored as ΔPgF = θgF − (0.6438 − 0.001682 νd) = +0.03335. The Olympus drawing marks L4 and L6 as Super ED and L14 and L16 as ED; the patent prints one glass for all four, so the Super ED/ED distinction is not in the source. L18 (nd 1.48749, νd 70.23) has no printed partial dispersion, so colour MTF is shown only at the reference wavelength; a d-line MTF result is not broadband colour performance.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent prints a close-focus state at an object-to-image distance (IO) of 250 mm at all three zoom stations: D4 opens from 11.6 to 17.3 mm and D15 closes from 6.8 to 1.1 mm, so the L3–L8 block (L3–L6 and doublet D1) moves 5.7 mm toward the image while L1/L2, doublet D2 and the rear group stay fixed, as the patent's description of Example 3 states. The focus spacings are the same at every zoom position, so the model applies them at all 33 stations; the wide, middle and tele states are marked as published and carry the patent's 250 mm IO for finite-distance MTF. Olympus describes inner focus with a floating mechanism; Example 3 moves a single unit, so the production focusing may differ in detail.

The printed image planes lie +0.069 / +0.057 / −0.054 mm from the paraxial focus at wide / middle / tele and are kept as printed. With the image plane held at that infinity-focus offset, the printed close spacings focus at about 249.5 / 246.6 / 249.6 mm object-to-image, within the rounding of the patent's IO row, at magnifications of about −0.055 / −0.074 / −0.107 against Olympus's 0.11× maximum. The 250 mm IO equals the production 0.25 m minimum focus distance, which is measured from the image plane; Olympus's 10 cm working distance is measured from the front of the lens.

## Aspherical Surfaces

The printed equation uses the standard conic term with (1+K), so K is the conic constant as the data file stores it. The US application, US grant and Japanese grant all print no operator between the conic term and A4·y⁴ (in the Japanese text the break falls at a line wrap); the later terms are joined by plus signs. The model reads the missing operator as the usual plus. With it, the full-aperture longitudinal spherical aberration is −0.090 / −0.103 / −0.037 mm at wide / middle / tele, with a positive zone of about +0.055 mm at tele, matching the curves of Figure 7 on their ±0.20 mm scale. Reading the S27/S28 A4 terms as subtracted instead gives −7.4 / −13.1 / −27.8 mm, and doing so at S3/S4 gives a geometry the model cannot build. The July 8, 2008 certificate of correction changes only the claims.

## Aperture, Field and Model Limits

Semi-diameters are estimated from the wide-end section of Figure 3(a); the patent prints no clear apertures. The drawing shows stepped front-element rims. S2 is 24.2 mm, inside the r2 rim the figure draws at about 24.5–25 mm; the surface slope there is 66.5°, so the file raises the rim-slope limit to 67°. S4 follows the drawing out to 18.2 mm, where L3's front rim (S5, 17.1 mm) meets it; the two faces come within about 0.2 mm without crossing, so the file allows the air gap between them to be 99 % filled. S6 is 14.7 mm. No printed radius, glass, thickness or coefficient was changed to fit the apertures. The stop diameter is not printed; the model sets it for F/4 at each station.

The patent plots image heights of 10.59 mm at wide and 11.45 mm at middle and tele. With the S2 aperture above, the chief ray reaches the 10.82 mm Four Thirds corner at every zoom station, at a half-field of about 56.7° at wide. The model traces full fields of 112.2° at the 10.59 mm wide image height and 99.4° and 78.4° at 11.45 mm in the middle and at tele, against the printed 2ω of 114.7°, 97.9° and 78.2° and Olympus's 114°; the printed wide and middle values do not follow from the patent's own image heights and distortion plots, so they read as nominal.

Because the printed image planes are kept, the MTF tab evaluates them as printed and reports the axial best-focus shift separately.
