## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 3,838,911
**Filed:** 1973-04-24
**Published:** 1974-10-01
**Inventor:** Yoshitsugi Ikeda
**Assignee:** Olympus Optical Co., Ltd.
**Title:** Telephoto Lens System
**Example details:** Embodiment 2 table (columns 2–3, repeated in claim 2); Fig. 1, shared by both embodiments; aberration plots Figs. 3a–3d. Printed at f = 100; every length is scaled ×2 to 200 mm.

Embodiment 2 is matched to the Olympus E.Zuiko Auto-T 200mm f/4 by specification:

1. Five elements in four groups, as Olympus lists for the 200mm f/4.
2. F/4 and a 12° full field, the lens's marketed aperture and angle of view.
3. A plano-convex front element, one cemented pair, a negative meniscus and a positive rear meniscus, as in the optical section of an Olympus dealer binder (drawn with the object on the right). The flat r2 of Embodiment 2 fits that drawing better than the meniscus front of Embodiment 1.
4. A 1972 Japanese priority, the period of the OM system's launch.

The match identifies the design, not a confirmed factory prescription or production revision. The later Zuiko (MC) Auto-T 200mm f/4 is listed with the same construction. Embodiment 1 of the same patent (F/3.5, 2ω = 18°) is matched to the E.Zuiko Auto-T 135mm f/3.5; scaled to 200 mm it would not match this lens. Fig. 1 is one schematic for both embodiments, not a scale drawing of Embodiment 2, so the flat r2 and the nearly flat cemented interface (r4 = −551.36 at f = 100) are kept as printed.

## Optical Architecture

A positive–positive–negative–positive telephoto of five spherical elements in four groups: a plano-convex front element, a positive/negative cemented doublet, a negative meniscus and a positive rear meniscus. The nine faces include one cemented interface (S4). The stop sits in d5 behind the doublet. The patent prints a telephoto ratio ζ = 0.85; the model gives 0.847 (169.43 mm from the front vertex to the image plane).

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Plano-Convex Positive

nd = 1.62041, νd = 60.2. Glass: S-BSM16 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +163.5 mm.

Positive front collector with a flat image-side face; it carries the largest clear aperture in the lens.

### L2 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.48749, νd = 69.8. Glass: traced at the printed pair (S-FSL5 matches nd but not νd). f = +118.4 mm in air.

Low-dispersion positive front member of the cemented pair. The doublet as a whole is weakly positive (f = +412.5 mm).

### L3 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.76182, νd = 26.5. Glass: S-TIH14 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = −154.1 mm in air.

Dense-flint negative partner. The shared interface is nearly flat (R = −1102.72 mm), so the doublet's large index step (n3 − n2 = 0.274) acts there only weakly; patent condition (4) requires n3 − n2 > 0.05 to keep zonal spherical aberration small and coma correctable.

### L4 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.67790, νd = 55.3. Glass: S-LAL12 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = −79.0 mm.

Negative meniscus, convex to the object, behind the stop: the main negative member that shortens the lens. Patent condition (2), 0.25f < 2r7 < r6 < 1.2f (here 34.74 < 51.53 at f = 100), sets its shape for a short telephoto ratio with corrected spherical and off-axis aberrations.

### L5 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.78472, νd = 25.6. Glass: S-TIH11 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +205.5 mm.

Positive meniscus, convex to the object. Patent condition (3), 0.25f < 1.3r8 < r9 < 2f (here 41.99 < 52.33 at f = 100), governs its shape: with 1.3r8 > r9 lateral colour becomes unbalanced at a short telephoto ratio, and with r9 > 2f coma flare is hard to correct.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent lists refractive index and Abbe number without naming a spectral line. Read as d-line values, every pair matches an OHARA catalog glass in nd: S-BSM16 (L1), S-FSL5 (L2), S-TIH14 (L3), S-LAL12 (L4) and S-TIH11 (L5). The data file uses four of them as catalog equivalents for dispersion; they are not a production identification, and the original supplier is not known. S-FSL5's catalog νd (70.2) is 0.44 above the printed 69.8, enough to move the colour correction of a 200 mm telephoto, so L2 traces at the printed pair instead. The doublet pairs a low-dispersion crown (νd 69.8) with a dense flint (νd 26.5), and the rear meniscus L5 is a second dense flint. No anomalous partial dispersion is claimed.

## Focus Mechanism

Unit focus: the whole five-element block moves forward on a straight helicoid to a minimum focus distance of 2.5 m. The patent prints the infinity state only. The model's close state is derived, not published: the back focus grows from 63.99 mm to 83.56 mm (19.57 mm of travel), which puts the object 2.5 m from the image plane at a magnification of about 1:10.2 (β = −0.098).

## Geometry, Aperture and Image Plane

The patent gives no clear apertures. The front semi-diameter (27.5 mm) is the f/4 entrance-pupil radius plus 10%, rounded to 0.1 mm. The four groups stand at 1.00 : 0.88 : 0.54 : 0.62, between the outline ratios of Fig. 1 (1 : 0.88 : 0.57 : 0.63) and of the maker's section (1 : 0.87 : 0.51 : 0.63). Fig. 1 is drawn to Embodiment 1's spacing. The maker's section matches Embodiment 2's spacing and radii, and its rims fall within 0.9 mm of the model's. Both drawings chamfer the image-side rims of L3 and L4, so faces 5 and 7 end short of their elements' front faces. These are inferred clear apertures, not filter-thread or barrel dimensions.

The stop is placed at 0.75 of d5 behind the doublet (61.06 mm from the front vertex), read from the stop mark in Fig. 1; the total gap is unchanged. Its wide-open radius, 13.37 mm, follows from the f/4 design aperture. The maker lists f/4–32; the blade count is not known.

The back focus is not printed. The image plane is the paraxial focus, 63.99 mm behind the last surface and 169.43 mm from the front vertex, at EFL 200.05 mm. No focal-length normalization or refocus is applied.

## Model Scope and Limitations

At the paraxial image plane the prescription reproduces the patent's plots for Embodiment 2: longitudinal spherical aberration stays within 0.05 mm at f/4 (Fig. 3a shows it near zero), the sagittal and tangential fields stay within 0.08 mm of the image plane to 6° (Fig. 3c), and distortion reaches +1.4% pincushion at 6° (Fig. 3d).

The patent's 6° half-field reaches an image height of 21.3 mm, slightly short of the 21.6 mm corner of the 36 × 24 mm frame. With the inferred rims, the 6° field keeps about 84% of the on-axis light. Rims, stop position and the close-focus state are inferred or derived, as described above; they are not production measurements.
