# OLYMPUS M.ZUIKO DIGITAL ED 30mm f/3.5 Macro — Audit Log

and Olympus Corporation as assignee. The US application was filed on 17 November 2015 with priority from

## Transcription Check

All 16 surface rows, six sets of aspheric constants and both variable gaps at each printed position agree with
the patent page images. The prescription retains the radii, thicknesses, spaces and glass pairs at the printed
millimetre scale and the helium d line. The sag equation uses the printed conic constant directly. The equation
allows an $A_{12}$ term, and Example 6 lists coefficients through $A_{10}$. Surface 5 is the stop. Surface 7 is
the cemented interface between L3 and L4.

The plane-parallel plate at surfaces 15–16 is 6.08 mm thick with $n_d$ 1.51633 and $\nu_d$ 64.14. The air gaps
before and after it are 16.04 mm and 0.75 mm, respectively. The image plane is 73.69 mm behind the front vertex.

The calculated iris radius is 4.686 mm at infinity. The printed close-range apertures imply about 4.13 mm at
0.5× and 3.30 mm at 1×. The model uses the infinity iris throughout. At 1× the axial ray through its edge clears
surface 6. Its heights at surfaces 7, 8, 9A and 10A exceed the clear radii by 0.04–0.14 mm. Surface 10A is the
tightest and passes about 96.5% of the iris area.

The printed gaps place the 0.5× and 1× objects 54.80 mm and 23.92 mm ahead of the first surface at paraxial
focus on the fixed image plane. Interpolating between the three printed gap pairs alone gives midpoint
defocus of 3.01 mm and 2.00 mm at the corresponding reciprocal object-to-image distances. With the 33
calculated intermediate positions, the largest segment-midpoint defocus is 0.018 mm. The calculated positions
focus within 0.0002 mm of the image plane after rounding the gaps and minimum focus distance.

## Product Identification

The Olympus section agrees with the seven-element, six-component layout, the L3–L4 cemented pair, the stop
gap and the shallow front of L7. The DSA, EDA and aspherical positions correspond to L1, L5 and L6, which have
aspheres on both faces. L6 occupies the moving G3 position in Fig. 6A–C. Olympus specifies inner focus with
a screw drive. The section supports Example 5 or 6. Examples 2–4 differ in element count or length, and
Example 1 has a rear meniscus and a 41.6° field. Example 6's 39.89° field is 0.24° closer to the maker's 40°
than Example 5's 39.65°. [Patent, Examples 1–6](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9791677B2/en)
[Olympus construction drawing](https://jp.omsystem.com/content/000083238.jpg)
[OM SYSTEM specifications](https://explore.omsystem.com/us/en/m-zuiko-ed-30mm-f3-5-macro)

Olympus specifies a 30 mm f/3.5 Micro Four Thirds lens with seven elements in six groups and a seven-blade
circular diaphragm that closes to f/22. The production lens reaches 1.25× at 95 mm from the image plane,
with about 14 mm from the front of the lens to the subject. Example 6 has a printed infinity focal length
and aperture of 30.43 mm and f/3.56 and ends at 1×. That state corresponds to 97.61 mm from object to image
in the model. [Olympus specifications](https://jp.omsystem.com/product/lens/macro/30_35macro/spec.html)
[OM SYSTEM close-focus specifications](https://explore.omsystem.com/ee/en/m-zuiko-ed-30mm-f3-5-macro-black)

## Corrections, 2026-11-24

- The model is identified by the production lens name, with Example 5 or 6 as the match and Example 6 as the
  source of the prescription. The patent number uses its grant form. The maker's mount, format, construction,
  aperture range and minimum focus distance accompany the production identification.
- The infinity aperture opened from f/5.6 to the printed f/3.56. The aperture settings follow the standard
  f/4–22 scale from the marketed f/3.5 maximum, with seven rounded blades as specified by Olympus.
  [Olympus specifications](https://jp.omsystem.com/product/lens/macro/30_35macro/spec.html)
- The 3° half-field restriction was lifted and the estimated rims were replaced with the Fig. 6C readings
  above. The surface 6 land preserves the drawn outer edge. The 10.82 mm Four Thirds corner sets the coverage
  target because the printed image height of 11.15 mm extends past it.
- The three printed gap pairs were retained and 33 calculated positions added to keep focus near the fixed
  image plane between them. G3 travels 6.16 mm toward the image at 0.5× and 13.76 mm at 1×. The two gaps
  always sum to 21.72 mm. The model stops at the last printed position.
- L1–L7 follow the patent's element labels. Shape descriptions, optical roles and individual paraxial focal
  lengths identify each element, and a bracket identifies the cemented pair. The plate and image plane stay
  at the printed positions.
- The following catalog curves give dispersion estimates for the seven elements using the printed d-line glass
  values. Differences are catalog minus patent. L5's Abbe number supports the maker's EDA designation at
  that position. [Olympus construction drawing](https://jp.omsystem.com/content/000083238.jpg)

## Remaining Questions

- The Example 6 table gives IH 11.15 mm, while Fig. 12 labels the maximum image height FIY 11.45 mm.
- The exact clear diameters and the production lens's close-range iris behavior remain unknown.
- The production spacing at 1.25×, the choice between Examples 5 and 6 and the production glass suppliers
  and melts remain unknown.
