# OLYMPUS E.ZUIKO AUTO-T 100mm f/2.8 — Patent Optical Analysis

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 3,749,478  
**Filed:** 7 April 1972  
**Granted:** 31 July 1973  
**Priority:** 10 April 1971 (Japan, 46/22087)  
**Inventors:** Jihei Nakagawa; Sumio Nakamura  
**Assignee:** Olympus Optical Co., Ltd.  
**Title:** Lens system having a large aperture and long focal length  
**Example details:** Numerical table in column 3, repeated in claim 3; aberration curves in Figs. 3a–3c; normalized to f = 1.0 and scaled uniformly by 100.

Example 2 is a patent design associated with the Olympus E.Zuiko Auto-T 100mm f/2.8 for the OM system, not a confirmed factory prescription. The association rests on these points:

1. **Construction.** The five air-spaced singlets match the 5 elements in 5 groups listed in Olympus's *The Zuiko Interchangeable Lens Group* catalog (p. 12), which also gives f/2.8–22, 1 m minimum focus, 230 g and a 49 mm filter.
2. **Field.** Figs. 3b and 3c plot Example 2 to a half-field of 12°10′, which reaches the 135-format corner at f ≈ 100 mm. Example 1 is plotted to 9°, which would suit about 137 mm.
3. **Aperture and telephoto ratio.** The patent aims at an aperture ratio of about 2.8 and a telephoto ratio of about 0.93. Example 2 computes to 0.928.
4. **Section drawing.** Olympus's drawing of this lens has Example 2's spacing, including the thicker L5 and shorter d8 that separate it from Example 1.
5. **Timing.** The April 1971 priority and July 1973 grant bracket the lens's production start in about 1972.

The catalog prints the 85mm f/2 and 100mm f/2.8 drawings under each other's text: the five-singlet section printed under the 85mm f/2 entry is this lens.

## Optical Architecture

The design is a telephoto of five spherical singlets in five air-spaced groups, with no aspheres, cemented interfaces or filter plates. The power sequence is positive–positive–positive–negative–positive, and all ten radii are printed positive. The first three menisci form a converging collector, L4 supplies the negative telephoto action, and L5 restores positive power behind a long air space.

The patent starts from the Ernostar type. It splits the composite second component into two lenses and turns the weak negative third component into a weak positive meniscus, which is what allows the telephoto ratio to fall to about 0.93 at this aperture.

All printed radii and thicknesses are multiplied by exactly 100, giving a track of 49.97 mm from S1 to S10. The factor comes from the marketed focal length and is not fitted to the computed EFL of 100.047 mm.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Positive Meniscus, convex to object

nd = 1.58913, νd = 61.11. Glass: S-BAL35 (OHARA) equivalent. f = +81.5 mm.

L1 begins the converging collector and carries its strongest power.

### L2 — Positive Meniscus, convex to object

nd = 1.56873, νd = 63.16. Glass: BAL22 (OHARA) equivalent. f = +147.2 mm.

L2 adds positive power across a 0.42 mm air gap. With L1 it forms the low-dispersion side of the collector that condition 5 calls for.

### L3 — Positive Meniscus, convex to object

nd = 1.70154, νd = 41.1. Glass: BASF7 (SUMITA) equivalent. f = +198.7 mm.

L3 closes the collector. The patent keeps it weak under condition 2 and sets its Abbe number below 48 under condition 5, which it credits with correcting axial and lateral color together.

### L4 — Negative Meniscus, convex to object

nd = 1.7847, νd = 26.22. Glass: PBH23 (OHARA) equivalent. f = −33.4 mm.

The strongly curved rear surface, R = +19.60 mm, makes L4 negative and bows toward the image into the long gap. The patent requires n4 > 1.7: below that, it says, the Petzval sum is overcorrected, sagittal resolution falls at the picture edge, and r8 grows, which degrades off-axis correction.

### L5 — Positive Meniscus, convex to object

nd = 1.7847, νd = 26.22. Glass: PBH23 (OHARA) equivalent. f = +128.0 mm.

L5 uses the same glass pair as L4 but has positive power. It sits behind the printed 25.78 mm air gap that holds the stop. Its rim is the one semi-diameter raised toward Olympus's section drawing, as described under Stop Position and Clear Apertures.

The table prints plain n and ν with no wavelength subscript. Every pair matches a catalog nd / νd, and none matches a catalog ne / νe, so n is read at the d line. Fig. 3a supports this by plotting d and g curves, with the d curve at zero on axis.

The labels are present-day catalog equivalents that supply the model's dispersion curves. The patent names no glass, and the supplier of the production glass is not established.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent prints the infinity state only, so the model is static: all spacings are fixed and no focus travel is authored. The 1 m minimum focus is Olympus's catalog figure. The catalog also says the lens has an automatic correction mechanism for close distances. The model does not carry that mechanism and assumes no unit-focus law in its place.

## Image Plane

The patent prints no back focus. A paraxial trace of the scaled table gives EFL = 100.047 mm and back focus = 42.869 mm, which places the image plane 92.839 mm from S1. The back focus is therefore derived, not printed, and it gives the telephoto ratio of 0.928.

The patent's aberration curves agree with this plane. On it, d-line spherical aberration is −0.074 mm at the 0.7 zone and −0.002 mm at full aperture, where Fig. 3a plots about −0.073 mm and about zero after scaling by 100. Distortion at 12°10′ is +1.15 %, against a plotted +1.18 %.

## Stop Position and Clear Apertures

The patent marks no stop: Fig. 1 draws none and the text gives no position. Fig. 3b's astigmatism curves place it, because the tangential curve near the field edge moves quickly as the stop shifts. Fitting the plotted curves over the d8 air space puts the stop 30.4 mm from S1, 8.8 mm behind S8 and 17.0 mm ahead of S9. Olympus's section drawing shows the break in the axis about there, some 29 % of the way from S8 to S9 against the model's 34 %. The printed d8 of 25.78 mm is unchanged and only divided around the stop.

At 12°10′ the model puts the tangential focus at +0.053 mm and the sagittal focus at +0.003 mm, against about +0.06 mm and about zero in Fig. 3b. The iris radius of 9.93 mm follows from the paraxial entrance pupil at f/2.8.

No semi-diameters are printed. L1 to L4 use 1.10 times the paraxial axial marginal height at f/2.8, rounded up to 0.01 mm and shared by both faces of each element: 19.66, 18.70, 17.38 and 13.37 mm. Olympus's drawing reads L1 : L2 : L3 as 1 : 0.95 : 0.90, against 1 : 0.95 : 0.88 here. It draws L4 larger, at 0.77 of L1 against 0.68 here.

L5 is raised to 11.0 mm toward Olympus's section drawing, which reads it at about 71 % of L1, or about 13.4 mm at the drawing's axial scale. The patent's Fig. 1 is drawn at Example 1's spacing, with that example's thinner L5, so it is not a guide to this rim. The model stays below the drawn height: a drawn outline shows the glass rather than the clear aperture, and 13.4 mm would pass L4's 13.37 mm and reverse the step the drawing shows.

With these rims at f/2.8, the transmitted share of the off-axis bundle is 93.8 % at 6.0°, 81.4 % at 8.1°, 65.3 % at 10.1° and 47.5 % at the 12.1° format corner, with L5 and L1 setting the limit. Example 2's 12°10′ edge lands at 21.82 mm, 0.17 mm beyond the 21.65 mm corner used for this comparison.

## Conditional Expressions

| Condition | Example 2 |
|---|---|
| (n1 + n2 + n3)/3 > 1.6 | 1.620 |
| 3f > f3 > 4f1,2,3 | 3.00 > 1.987 > 1.621 |
| n4 > 1.7 | 1.7847 |
| 0.24f > d1 + … + d6 > 0.12f | 0.177f |
| ν1 > 50, ν2 > 50, ν3 < 48 | 61.11, 63.16, 41.1 |

All five hold. At the patent's f = 1 scale the table computes to f = 1.0005, f1,2,3 = 0.4052 and f3 = 1.987, against the printed 1.0, 0.407 and 2.0. The f3 value rounds to the print. Example 1's printed group values are similarly loose (0.392 against a computed 0.3913), and no single-digit change to Example 2's table reaches 0.407 without spoiling the fit to Fig. 3a, so the table stands as printed.
