# OLYMPUS ZUIKO DIGITAL ED 90–250mm f/2.8: Example 1 reconstruction

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP 2006-201524 A  
**Application Number:** JP 2005-013429  
**Filed:** January 21, 2005  
**Published:** August 3, 2006  
**Inventor:** Tomoki Kono (the original PAJ cover prints KONO TOMOKI)  
**Assignee:** Sigma Corporation  
**Title:** 大口径望遠ズームレンズ / Large-diameter Telephoto Zoom Lens  
**Example details:** Figure 1, paragraphs 0021–0024, three infinity and three finite object-distance states.

Example 1 has a probable production correlation with the original Four Thirds DSLR **Zuiko Digital ED 90–250mm F2.8**. Sigma Corporation is the patent assignee; no public source confirms Sigma's design or manufacturing role for the Olympus-branded lens. The prescription is not authenticated as the factory design. The Olympus manual and September 27, 2005 announcement support the correlation through several converging facts:

1. The manual's optical section on PDF page 5/printed page 18 shows 17 elements in 12 components, including three separated front singlets. That distinguishes Example 1 from the alternative front assembly of Example 2; it does not establish every numerical radius.
2. The 90–250 mm range and constant marketing f/2.8 correspond approximately to the patent's 90.95–248.91 mm and f/2.86. These design and product labels differ; the prescription is not rescaled.
3. Three maker ED elements are qualitatively consistent with the source's two low-dispersion front positives and the low-dispersion positive in G4. ED is a product class, not an identified melt or full dispersion curve.
4. The positive–negative–positive–positive architecture, internal zoom and internal G3 focus are compatible with the maker's inner-zoom/inner-focus description and 2.5 m minimum focus distance.
5. The Four Thirds manual gives a 14–5° field, f/2.8–22, 105 mm filter and 276 mm length. The announcement adds nine diaphragm blades. The modeled iris is circular; it does not represent nine-blade diffraction.

The manual lists 3250 g and 123 mm diameter, while the announcement gives 3270 g and 124 mm; both give 276 mm length. These documentary differences are not assigned to a particular variant. The manual's removable protection filter has no published prescription; the model omits it, along with any sensor cover or other unprescribed plane-parallel plate.

## Optical Architecture

The prescription has 17 glass elements, 12 air-separated components, 29 spherical refracting interfaces and one separate stop at row 23. The four functional groups are positive G1 (L1–L3), negative G2 (L4–L8), positive G3 (L9–L10), and positive G4 (L11–L17). Their isolated focal lengths are +184.917954, −45.357889, +137.783362 and +112.794491 mm. The diagram uses the patent's G1–G4 group names and editorial L1–L17 element names; D1–D5 identify the five cemented doublets. G2 is the zoom variator and moves imageward; G3 compensates with a reversing zoom path and moves imageward for close focus. The patent describes G1/G4 as fixed during zoom, and G1/G2/G4 as fixed during focusing. The numerical endpoints contain a small fixed-span discrepancy discussed below.

G1 combines a high-index negative lead with two low-dispersion positive singlets. G2 contains two cemented doublets and a negative singlet. G3 is a positive/negative cemented focusing doublet. G4 combines a positive singlet, a low-dispersion positive/negative doublet, the stop in air, a negative singlet, a strongly opposed positive/negative doublet, and a final positive singlet. The patent describes a telephoto zoom with a compact internal focusing group. Its first-vertex-to-printed-image track/EFL ratios are 3.34178/2.01882/1.22084 at the three infinity settings, exceeding unity. The Petzval sum using the actual refracting interfaces is +0.0006680351280313203/mm.

Paragraph 0020 explicitly defines d-line refractive index and Abbe number. The model retains the printed radii, thicknesses, indices, Abbe values, the stop's following 10.9938 mm interval, and all six gap/BF combinations. All surfaces are spherical. The element roles below are qualitative power/dispersion interpretations; a complete non-Petzval Seidel decomposition and measured production performance are unavailable.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Isolated element focal lengths below use the two vertex curvatures, center thickness and air boundaries on both sides. Cemented-system power uses the actual downstream media. Catalog equivalents below are optical proxies; the production supplier and melt are not identified.

### L1 — Negative Meniscus, convex to object

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: FD60 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). Isolated f = -415.855713 mm.

The negative lead has a gentler object-side curvature than its rear curvature. Its high index and relatively low Abbe number provide negative power ahead of the two low-dispersion positives. Its role is not inferred from the word ED.

### L2 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.49700, νd = 79.3. Glass: Unmatched (fluor-crown; inferred ED; production melt unidentified). Isolated f = +226.296659 mm.

The first low-dispersion front positive supplies strong positive collecting power in G1. It is an air-spaced singlet. Its printed 1.49700/79.3 pair has no compatible entry in the catalog search described below.

### L3 — Positive Meniscus, convex to object

nd = 1.49700, νd = 79.3. Glass: Unmatched (fluor-crown; inferred ED; production melt unidentified). Isolated f = +293.754673 mm.

The second low-dispersion front positive is a positive meniscus. Together L2/L3 supply most of the positive front-group power, with L1 providing opposite power and dispersion. The model retains its printed 1.49700/79.3 coordinates.

### L4 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.5. Glass: FD60 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). Isolated f = +69.343916 mm.

The positive half of the first G2 cemented doublet shares r8 with L5. Its interface power depends on the difference between the two printed indices; the isolated focal length above assumes air boundaries.

### L5 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.58144, νd = 40.9. Glass: E-FL5 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). Isolated f = -83.088438 mm.

The negative partner of the first G2 doublet opposes L4. Both share an inferred 23 mm clear radius estimated from the drawing; the patent does not tabulate diameters.

### L6 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.83481, νd = 42.7. Glass: TAFD5G (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). Isolated f = -50.581610 mm.

The negative half of the second G2 cemented doublet begins with the very weak r10 = −1000 mm surface and ends at the strongly positive r11 curvature. Its printed index is 1.83481, distinct from the recurring 1.80518 glass.

### L7 — Positive Meniscus, convex to object

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.5. Glass: FD60 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). Isolated f = +101.221300 mm.

The positive meniscus partner shares r11 with L6. Both faces have a 20.8 mm clear radius, matching the square rim drawn in Figure 1. Its rear face comes within 0.13 mm of L8 at L8's 19 mm rim, consistent with the figure's near-contact outline.

### L8 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.80610, νd = 40.7. Glass: NBFD13 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). Isolated f = -63.627936 mm.

This negative singlet completes G2, reinforcing its negative variator power. Its printed center thickness is 1.800 mm; the drawn section does not establish a manufactured edge thickness.

### L9 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.4. Glass: FC5 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). Isolated f = +71.444038 mm.

The low-dispersion positive half of G3 is cemented to L10. The small two-element group is the actual source-defined internal focus unit; the positive/negative pair moves together without changing its internal thicknesses.

### L10 — Negative Meniscus, concave to object

nd = 1.80610, νd = 40.7. Glass: NBFD13 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). Isolated f = -150.135027 mm.

The negative meniscus partner supplies opposite dispersion and power within G3. Its isolated negative focal length does not contradict the combined positive group focal length. All source focus motion is assigned to this complete cemented pair.

### L11 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.69680, νd = 55.5. Glass: LAC14 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). Isolated f = +90.758051 mm.

The positive singlet starts G4. The following air interval is only 0.3614 mm, as printed.

### L12 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6. Glass: FCD1 (HOYA catalog equivalent; inferred ED; production melt unidentified). Isolated f = +74.268803 mm.

The low-dispersion positive in G4 is cemented to L13. Its table coordinate is 1.49700/81.6, while the separate condition summary says ν4 = 79.3. The model retains the table's 81.6; the source disagreement remains unresolved.

### L13 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.80610, νd = 33.3. Glass: NBFD15 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). Isolated f = -82.507551 mm.

The high-index negative partner shares r21 with L12. This low-/high-dispersion positive/negative pair supplies chromatic balancing within the fixed rear group, without establishing a particular commercial glass pair.

### L14 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.6. Glass: TAF1 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). Isolated f = -103.846640 mm.

This biconcave negative singlet lies after the stop. Its power opposes the following strong doublet and contributes to the rear-group balance; no individual aberration coefficient is asserted without a separate calculation.

### L15 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.8. Glass: FDS90 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). Isolated f = +23.983179 mm.

The strong positive half of the rear G4 doublet is cemented to L16 at r27. Its high index and low Abbe number differ from those of the negative partner; the common interface must use the actual source indices.

### L16 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.5. Glass: FD60 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). Isolated f = -16.780114 mm.

The strong biconcave negative partner opposes L15. Both faces have a 13.8 mm clear radius. An independent reading of Figure 1 places the rear and cemented-face rim ends at the same height, so the second review removes the earlier artificial rear bevel. These are figure estimates, not mechanical barrel measurements.

### L17 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.70154, νd = 41.1. Glass: BAFD7 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). Isolated f = +58.914195 mm.

The final positive singlet completes G4 and the 29-surface refracting system. Its printed rear radius is −250 mm, followed by the printed BF. The model includes no rear filter or digital sensor plate.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The model uses these HOYA catalog equivalents: FD60 (L1/L4/L7/L16), E-FL5 (L5), TAFD5G (L6), NBFD13 (L8/L10), FC5 (L9), LAC14 (L11), FCD1 (L12), NBFD15 (L13), TAF1 (L14), FDS90 (L15) and BAFD7 (L17). They are qualified dispersion proxies, not identified production melts or suppliers. L2/L3 remain Unmatched: K-PFK80's Abbe residual of +2.0504 falls outside the ±0.003 index/±2 Abbe comparison window. L12 retains its printed 81.6 rather than the condition summary's 79.3.

The patent requires at least one positive element in G1/G4 to have an Abbe number above 70/75 respectively. L2/L3 satisfy 79.3 > 70 and L12 satisfies 81.6 > 75; either value in the disputed G4 summary satisfies the second inequality. Olympus lists three ED elements; their assignment to the fluor-crowns L2/L3/L12 is inferred. ED markings and nd/νd coordinates alone do not establish anomalous partial dispersion or a complete spectral curve. Catalog curves are proxies; d-line calculations retain the printed indices, and complete photopic production performance is unknown.

## Focus Mechanism and Continuous Zoom Reconstruction

Example 1 tabulates three finite configurations. The first-vertex object distances are 2196.0763, 2196.1327 and 2196.1142 mm. Adding the corresponding optical track to the printed image plane gives 2500.0025, 2499.9989 and 2499.9991 mm: approximately the maker's 2.5 m minimum focus distance. The 2196 mm distances are measured from the first surface rather than the image plane.

| Printed zoom | Infinity D6 / D14 / D17, mm | Finite D6 / D14 / D17, mm | G3 imageward travel, mm |
|---|---|---|---:|
| 90.95 | 19.6268 / 45.2255 / 43.8896 | 19.6268 / 49.0058 / 40.1093 | 3.7803 |
| 150.52 | 53.7367 / 31.2025 / 23.8027 | 53.7367 / 40.2551 / 14.7501 | 9.0526 |
| 248.91 | 75.4497 / 6.3585 / 26.9324 | 75.4497 / 27.9014 / 5.3895 | 21.5429 |

The model contains 65 connected zoom stations and 17 focus keyframes, preserving the three published infinity and three published near-focus configurations. Intermediate positions are reconstructed. D6 and total intergroup track are interpolated in zoom; G3's position follows the finite-imaging quadratic while G1/G2/G4 remain fixed during each focus sweep. The interpolation uses image-plane diopters: the close image-plane distance divided by the focus fraction, minus the current lens-to-image track, gives first-vertex object distance.

The current grid retains every fourth station and keyframe of the earlier reconstruction. At infinity, the printed wide/middle/tele G3 front vertices lie at 120.2775/140.3644/137.2334 mm from the first surface: G3 first moves imageward, then returns toward the object. The reconstructed path reaches its maximum at station 46 (182.4584 mm), at 141.976365 mm. The paraxial defocus change at omitted nodes is at most 0.011 mm. These interpolated positions do not establish the factory cam, drive law, smooth-velocity trajectory or manufacturing tolerances.

The patent's fixed-body description coexists with a 0.0013 mm tele vertex-span difference and a 0.06 mm printed BF range; the model retains both variations. The viewer anchors the drawing to its wide-infinity image plane without changing the optical gaps. Its focus-tracking fan uses real-ray sensitivity and differs from a physical bundle launched from the printed finite object point. Finite-conjugate MTF is available only at the three published near-focus states; intermediate finite states are unsupported.

## Source Conditions and Model Scope

Using the printed tele focal-length label gives f1/fT = 0.742910906; the calculated tele G3 slope magnification is 7.454786646 and the finite image-plane object-distance ratio is 1.000001360. These satisfy the patent's bounds. Continuing the chief-ray branch from the axis over complete spherical caps to 7°, 4.1° and 2.5° gives exit slopes of 0.059657475, 0.058341503 and 0.058789791. The patent's (image height − last-surface ray height)/vertex BF expression gives 0.059864997, 0.058532703 and 0.058986408 because the last intersection lies on a sagged cap. All are below 0.23, but neither sequence reproduces the printed 0.052/0.054/0.029. The maximum-height/field datum behind those printed values is unknown. Complete-cap continuation does not establish aperture admission or global uniqueness of the chief-ray solution.

All clear radii are inferred because the patent tabulates no diameters. Figure 1 at wide/infinity supports the 23 mm L4/L5 rims, 21.6 mm at r10 and 20.8 mm at r11/r12, 14.5 mm on L14, a square 13.8 mm rim across L15/L16, and 16 mm on L17. The second review retains L1/L2 at 49 mm and lowers L3 to 47 mm to restore the drawn approximately 2 mm step. These estimates also follow the maker diagram's overall outline recorded in the earlier audit. The f/2.86 axial beam clears the rims at all 65 zoom stations and all 17 focus keyframes. Facing surfaces at L5/L6 and L7/L8 approach within 0.065 mm and 0.13 mm, consistent with the figure; their sag/gap ratios reach 0.976 and 0.974, below the model's 0.98 limit. The sampled minimum positive glass thickness is 1.056 mm at L15, minimum air clearance is 0.049780745 mm at r2/r3, and maximum rim slope is 32.2502313°. These geometric estimates are not manufacturing tolerances. G1 remains approximately 5–6% larger than the independent figure readings, and r10 cannot reach the drawn 23.5 mm rim without crossing L5.

The exact 17.3 × 13 mm format has a half-diagonal slightly beyond the rounded 10.82 mm reference. Finite-object bundles and the viewer's sensitivity-based focus fan use different launch conventions. Off-axis clipping and exterior misses remain distinct from transmitted rays; clear axial beams do not imply an unvignetted field.

Reference-wavelength MTF calculations at the printed image planes have frequency limits of 40 or 80 lp/mm, depending on the field. The published angular fields are 7°/4.1°/2.5°; reference calculations include f/2.86 and f/8 at infinity and the published finite states. Those results do not establish complete photopic performance, uniform 80 lp/mm convergence, or factory performance. Intermediate finite-conjugate MTF remains unavailable.
