# OLYMPUS ZUIKO DIGITAL ED 50mm f/2.0 Macro

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2003/0103269 A1\
**Title:** Macro lens, and camera comprising the same\
**Inventors:** Hideyasu Takato\
**Assignee:** Olympus Optical Co., Ltd.\
**Application Number:** 10/304,076\
**Filed:** November 26, 2002\
**Priority:** JP 2001-361096, November 27, 2001\
**Published:** June 5, 2003\
**Example details:** ¶¶0093–0095 and ¶0102, with sections in Fig. 2 and aberration plots in Fig. 5.

The patent is a close match for the Four Thirds macro lens Olympus announced on June 24, 2003. Olympus's section shows the same 11 elements in 10 air-separated groups, the stop between the fifth and sixth elements, the ED glass in the seventh position, and the cemented pair at the rear. It marks no aspheres, in agreement with the spherical prescription. Olympus also describes a floating focus mechanism. The patent's 11.1 mm image height is close to the 10.82 mm Four Thirds corner, and the patent was filed before the announcement.

All three examples use the same 11-element layout and glass values. Examples 1 and 2 divide the optics into two moving groups. Example 3 splits the front assembly and varies a third gap. Example 2 fits the production drawing most closely. The drawn air space between the fifth and sixth elements is about 9 mm. Example 2 gives 9.09 mm around the stop, compared with 6.21 mm in Examples 1 and 3. The axial positions differ from the drawing by about 0.39 mm RMS for Example 2 and about 0.78 mm for the other two. The drawing's eighth-element thickness is slightly nearer Example 1. At the closest printed setting, Example 2 gives −0.5191×, which agrees in magnitude with Olympus's 0.52×. The factory prescription remains unpublished.

Olympus specifies 50 mm, f/2–f/22, seven diaphragm blades, a 24° diagonal field and 0.24 m minimum focus. Example 2 prints 51.009 mm and f/1.83 and reaches 0.2291 m at the closest printed setting. The calculated infinity focal length is 51.007 mm, and a chief ray at 12.04° reaches the Four Thirds corner, corresponding to a 24.08° full diagonal field.

## Optical Architecture

Two positive groups move independently. The front group G1, about +66.40 mm on its own, contains eight singlets and carries the iris. Its negative front meniscus helps provide back focus for the reflex mirror (¶¶0040–0045). Two biconcave elements flank the iris. Behind them, a low-dispersion positive and a stronger biconvex positive bring the first group to net positive power.

The rear group G2 has an isolated focal length of +124.88 mm. It consists of a negative singlet followed by a positive cemented doublet. The patent uses the rear group's power and its relative movement to limit changes in spherical aberration and field curvature at close focus (¶¶0054–0058). It also describes the cemented positive and negative members as a means of controlling chromatic aberration (¶0037).

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The element focal lengths below are thick-lens values in air. The cemented pair itself has an isolated focal length of +47.63 mm.

### L1 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.64769, νd = 33.79. Glass match S-TIM22 (Ohara). f = −115.93 mm.

The concave front face supplies the initial divergence. The patent links the power and bending of this meniscus to the balance between back focus and aberration correction (¶¶0040–0045).

### L2 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.60. Glass match S-LAH66 (Ohara). f = +70.87 mm.

A strongly curved rear face supplies positive power just behind the front negative. The signs and magnitudes of the printed radii give a positive element.

### L3 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.72916, νd = 54.68. Glass match S-LAL18 (Ohara). f = +59.93 mm.

This singlet supplies further convergence before the stop. Its very large positive rear radius makes the back face nearly plane.

### L4 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.52249, νd = 59.84. Glass match S-NSL5 (Ohara). f = +243.36 mm.

The more weakly powered meniscus completes the positive run ahead of the two negative elements around the iris.

### L5 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.59551, νd = 39.24. Glass match S-TIM8 (Ohara). f = −32.08 mm.

The first of the two negative elements around the iris has its stronger curvature on the iris side.

### L6 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.58144, νd = 40.75. Glass match S-TIL25 (Ohara). f = −29.07 mm.

This negative element faces L5 across the iris, with its stronger concave face toward the stop. The adjacent edges of L6 and L7 lie close together.

### L7 — Positive Meniscus, ED

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.54. Glass match S-FPL51 (Ohara). f = +65.18 mm.

Olympus marks this position as ED. Its low dispersion supplies positive power behind the iris with comparatively little primary color. The patent describes the use of anomalous-dispersion glass behind the stop to control both longitudinal and lateral color at macro distances (¶0068).

### L8 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.60. Glass match S-LAH66 (Ohara). f = +37.27 mm.

The strongest positive singlet in G1 closes the front group immediately ahead of the variable space.

### L9 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.51742, νd = 52.43. Glass match S-NSL36 (Ohara). f = −70.21 mm.

The front element of G2 is close to plano-concave because its object-side radius is very large. Its negative power partly offsets the positive cemented pair.

### L10 and L11 — Cemented Doublet

**L10 — Negative Meniscus.** nd = 1.76182, νd = 26.52. Glass match S-TIH14 (Ohara). f = −94.43 mm.\
**L11 — Biconvex Positive.** nd = 1.74100, νd = 52.64. Glass match S-LAL61 (Ohara). f = +31.84 mm.

The more dispersive negative member and the less dispersive positive member share surface 21. The pair finishes the lens with net converging power and contributes to chromatic correction in the rear group.

## Glass Identification and Selection

All eleven printed d-line index and Abbe-number pairs match Ohara catalog entries. Their dispersion curves provide the wavelength dependence used here. The patent gives the index and dispersion pairs, while the glass names are catalog matches. Olympus identifies the seventh element as ED, and its values agree with S-FPL51. The production supplier and melt are undocumented.

## Focus Mechanism

Both groups move toward the object as the lens focuses closer. At the nearest printed setting, G2 advances 23.69 mm relative to the image plane. G1 and its iris advance 29.98 mm because the intergroup space also grows by 6.29 mm. The distance from the last glass surface to the image plane changes with focus.

| Printed magnification | G1–G2 gap (mm) | Last surface to image (mm) | Object to first surface (mm) | Object to image (mm) | Calculated magnification |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Infinity | 0.50 | 35.06 | ∞ | ∞ | — |
| −0.1× | 1.71 | 39.46 | 525.75 | 620.08 | −0.09945× |
| −0.52× | 6.79 | 58.75 | 110.36 | 229.06 | −0.51909× |

The patent prints the magnifications and gaps. The distances are paraxial conjugates calculated at the corresponding image plane. Between the printed positions, the group separation follows a straight-line relation with back focus within each interval. Intermediate positions keep the displayed distance close to paraxial focus. The patent gives no mechanical cam profile.

## Clear Apertures and Field

Figure 2(a) gives the rim proportions, while the surface table gives the radii, axial spacings and iris location. The clear radii are estimated from the drawing and bounded by the available air space near L6 and L7. The 10.02 mm iris radius comes from the printed infinity f/1.83.

The corner chief ray reaches 10.82 mm at each printed focus setting. At the closest setting, the full f/1.83 iris admits an axial bundle wider than the L6 rear rim can pass. The narrower f/2 setting passes the axial bundle at all three positions. The patent specifies f/1.83 at infinity and gives no iris schedule through focus.

## Conditional Expressions

The principal ratios from ¶0105 follow the prescription. The first-element focal length divided by system focal length is −2.27. The first-element bending ratio is −3.08. The group focal lengths divided by system focal length are 1.30 and 2.45. The front group's closest-focus travel is 0.59 times the infinity focal length.

The patent gives a 13.3° half-field for condition (6). At its stated image height of 11.1 mm, the prescription gives an object-side chief-ray angle of 12.34° and an image-side angle of 7.94°. Both the traced object-side angle and the printed value are within the patent's 7° to 16° range.
