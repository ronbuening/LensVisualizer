# OLYMPUS ZUIKO DIGITAL ED 50mm f/2.0 Macro — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2003/0103269 A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20030103269A1/en), *Macro lens, and camera comprising the same*, names Hideyasu Takato on the front page. US application 10/304,076 was filed November 26, 2002 and published June 5, 2003. ## Product Identification

Olympus announced this Four Thirds lens on June 24, 2003. The product has 11 elements in 10 groups, one ED element, a 50 mm focal length, a seven-blade diaphragm covering f/2–f/22, a 24° diagonal field, and a maximum magnification of 0.52×. Olympus specifies 0.24 m minimum focus, measured from the image plane. The model reaches 0.229 m at −0.51909×. [Olympus announcement](https://www.olympus.co.jp/jp/news/2003a/nr030624zuikoj.html), [Olympus instruction manual](https://learnandsupport.getolympus.com/sites/default/files/media/files/2018/03/Zuiko%20Digital%2050mm%20f2.0%20Macro%20Manual.pdf), [Imaging Resource review](https://www.imaging-resource.com/lenses/olympus-50mm-f-2-zuiko-digital-macro/)

Olympus's drawing shows the stop between L5 and L6, ED glass at L7 and the cemented rear pair. Its roughly 9 mm L5–L6 air space agrees with Example 2's 9.09 mm. Examples 1 and 3 give 6.21 mm. Example 2 fits the section to about 0.39 mm RMS axially, compared with 0.78 mm for the other two. L8's thickness alone is nearer Example 1. The closest magnification and the drawing together make Example 2 the closest public match. [LensTip construction diagram](https://www.lenstip.com/185.3-Lens_review-Olympus_Zuiko_Digital_50_mm_f_2.0_Macro_ED_Build_quality.html)

The patent's two moving groups change separation through focus. Olympus also specifies floating focus for the production lens, which has an extending barrel and a separately moving rear group. ## Remaining Questions

- The factory prescription, exact clear apertures, glass supplier and mechanical focus cam remain undocumented. The rim readings from Fig. 2(a) have about ±0.2 mm reading uncertainty. Olympus specifies seven blades and leaves their curvature unspecified.
- At −0.52×, the fully open iris requires 11.53 mm at surface 13 and 11.52 mm at surface 14. The two spherical faces meet at about 11.42 mm under the printed spacing. The patent gives no finite-distance iris opening. Its close-range aberration plot is drawn for an object-side numerical aperture of 0.101, whose marginal ray needs 10.97 mm at surface 13 and passes the 11.30 mm rim.
- Condition (6) lists a 13.3° half-field. The 11.1 mm image height gives 12.28° by the paraxial estimate and 12.34° by chief-ray tracing. Condition (10) lists 3.15, while 35.06/11.1 is 3.159. Its inequality at 2.8 points the other way from the accompanying prose.
- Several shape descriptions in ¶0093 disagree with the numerical radii. The table gives L2 positive power, an image-facing convex surface on L7 and two concave faces on L9, with surface 18 nearly plane.
