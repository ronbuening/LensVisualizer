# OLYMPUS ZUIKO DIGITAL ED 50mm f/2.0 Macro — audit

## Source

[US 2003/0103269 A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20030103269A1/en), *Macro lens, and camera comprising the same*, by Hideyasu Takato. US application 10/304,076 was filed on November 26, 2002 and published on June 5, 2003. It claims priority to JP 2001-361096 of November 27, 2001, published as JP 2003-161877 A. The US application's front page gives the inventor. The assignee, Olympus Optical Co., Ltd., is recorded in the assignment associated with [US 6,747,817 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6747817B2/en).

## Transcription

Example 2 is in ¶0102 on printed page 6 (PDF page 15). The model reproduces the 22 numbered surface rows, including the flat stop at surface 11, all center thicknesses and air spaces, and all eleven d-line glass pairs. The flat image plane is surface 23. Radii 6 and 18 remain the printed +3,929,264.108 mm and −17,636,653.385 mm. All refracting surfaces are spherical. The cemented interface is surface 21. The example table has no sensor-side plate.

The three printed pairs of variable gaps, d17 and d22, are 0.50 and 35.06 mm, 1.71 and 39.46 mm, and 6.79 and 58.75 mm. G1 carries the iris and moves 29.98 mm toward the object at the closest position. G2 moves 23.69 mm in the same direction. Each printed gap is used at its own setting.

The figure shows lands on surfaces 8, 10 and 12. Their cap-to-edge ratios are about 0.932, 0.889 and 0.871, each with about ±0.008 uncertainty from a one-pixel reading. The inferred cut depths are 3.32, 3.23 and 2.90 mm. Surface 10 ends 0.08 mm before the iris plane. The surface 12 cap retains its estimated clear radius after the sixth element's outer edge is reduced.

The drawn iris opening is roughly 10.8 mm with the axial scale, or 9.7 mm with the radial reference. The model uses a 10.02 mm radius calculated from the paraxial entrance pupil for f/1.83. The same physical opening is used through focus at the widest setting.

The patent gives no object distances or mechanical cam profile. Finite object distances are solved from the printed gaps at the printed image plane. With only the three printed positions, linear gap interpolation differs from the interpolated distance by as much as 0.25 mm and 4.02 mm of axial image shift in the two intervals. The 24 calculated intermediate positions solve for back focus at intermediate object distances, with d17 linear in d22 within each span. Between those positions the largest sampled image shift is 0.019 mm.

## Product Identification

The [Olympus announcement](https://www.olympus.co.jp/jp/news/2003a/nr030624zuikoj.html) gives 50 mm, f/2–f/22, seven blades, 24°, 0.24 m minimum focus, 0.52× maximum magnification, and 11 elements in 10 groups. The [instruction manual](https://learnandsupport.getolympus.com/sites/default/files/media/files/2018/03/Zuiko%20Digital%2050mm%20f2.0%20Macro%20Manual.pdf) identifies the Four Thirds mount. The [Olympus America specification sheet](https://biofos.com/esystem/_img/elenses/50mm.pdf) describes floating focus. The [Olympus section reproduced by LensTip](https://www.lenstip.com/upload3/2967_olympus_50mm_bud.jpg) shows the seventh-element ED glass and the rear cemented pair in the same positions as the patent.

The section's roughly 9 mm air space between L5 and L6 favors Example 2 at 9.09 mm. The other two examples give 6.21 mm. Example 2 fits the drawing at about 0.39 mm RMS axially versus about 0.78 mm for Examples 1 and 3. The closest Example 2 magnification is also consistent with the product's 0.52×. The patent was filed before the June 2003 announcement, and Example 2 gives the closest public match.

## Corrections

### 2026-11-24

The surface table and all three gap pairs are reproduced as printed. The model retains the exact infinity gap even though the paraxial back focus lies another 0.009 mm from the last surface. The optical design values and the Olympus product ratings are recorded separately.

The sixth element's 12.05 mm drawn semi-diameter would make surfaces 13 and 14 intersect. Both faces use 11.36 mm, the largest hundredth-millimetre value within 99% of the printed 0.91 mm gap at their shared rim. This retains about 0.009 mm of edge clearance. A 10% clearance allowance would limit this radius to 10.83 mm. The front and outer rims of the other elements follow the section.

## Remaining Questions

- The production prescription, exact clear apertures, glass supplier and focus cam remain undocumented. The differing scales in Fig. 2(a) limit the precision of its rim readings.
- At −0.52×, a fully open iris requires 11.53 mm at surface 13, and the corresponding beam needs 11.52 mm at surface 14. The two spherical profiles meet at about 11.42 mm under the printed spacing. The patent gives no finite-distance iris opening.
- Example 2's condition (6) tabulates a 13.3° half-field. The table gives 12.28° by the paraxial image-height estimate and 12.34° by tracing the chief ray to 11.1 mm. Condition (10) tabulates 3.15, while 35.06/11.1 is 3.1586. The printed inequality also points the wrong way at 2.8 relative to the accompanying prose.
- The shape descriptions in ¶0093 disagree with several radius pairs. The numerical table gives L2 positive power, L7 convex to the image, and L9 two concave faces with surface 18 nearly plane.
