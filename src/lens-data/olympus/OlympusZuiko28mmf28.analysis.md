# Olympus Zuiko MC Auto-W 28 mm f/2.8

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 4,352,544  
**Title:** Wide-angle photographic camera lens system  
**Application Number:** 173751  
**Priority:** 1 August 1979, Japan 54-98785  
**Filed:** 30 July 1980  
**Published:** 5 October 1982  
**Inventor:** Toshihiro Imai  
**Assignee:** Olympus Optical Co., Ltd.  
**Example details:** repeated claim 1 table, six air-separated singlets.

The association with the **six-element/six-group 28 mm f/2.8 catalog lens** remains probable; exact factory coordinates and identity across every version are unconfirmed. The source gives normalized f = 1, f/2.8 and 37.5° half-field. Its twelve spherical surfaces, eleven vertex intervals and six glass pairs are transcribed unchanged and scaled uniformly by the conditional factor 28. The result is 27.973859264 mm, not a prescription adjusted to exactly 28 mm.

The product association rests on several convergent but limited witnesses:

1. The 1985 Olympus system catalog describes the compact 28 mm f/2.8 as retrofocus on PDF page 24 / printed page 23. Its specification table on PDF page 28 / printed page 27 gives 6/6, 75°, f/2.8–22, 0.3 m minimum focus, 170 g and 32 mm length. The final page has the literal imprint C20E-1285D.
2. The patent is a compact retrofocus design with exactly six separate elements and the corresponding focal length, aperture and field when conditionally scaled.
3. A separate MC Zuiko four-language leaflet with literal 0181 imprint also says 6/6. Its illustrated section, however, is Gauss-like and does not reliably support this retrofocus topology. That illustration is not used to infer the clear apertures.
4. An undated Zuiko four-language leaflet says **7/6 in all four languages**. This conflict is retained. Neither a typographical error nor an undocumented production revision is inferred to make the sources agree.

The MW2828 exploded-parts sheet with literal 1181 imprint supports mechanical identity, not an optical prescription or chronology resolving the conflicting leaflets. The 32 mm catalog barrel length is not the model's 37.828 mm first-to-last optical vertex span. Likewise, the 49 mm screw filter does not establish a clear-aperture schedule.

## Optical Architecture

The system has six elements in six groups, twelve refracting glass/air interfaces and one separately inferred iris. Every optical face is spherical; there are no cemented interfaces, synthetic glass splits, aspheres or rear cover plates.

The element-power sequence is positive, negative, positive, negative, positive, positive. The first two singlets form the patent's front diverging lens group, with focal length −32.454727 mm. The remaining four form its rear converging lens group, with focal length +24.491003 mm. The diagram brackets them as FRONT GROUP (−) and REAR GROUP (+). Their focal-length ratios to the computed complete-system EFL are −1.160180531 and +0.875496029. These are close to, but not exactly equal to, the source prose values −1.161 f and +0.88 f. They are not additional constraints used to repair the table.

The large 15.064 mm air separation after L2 gives the retrofocus arrangement. The back focal distance is 36.701321864 mm, longer than the effective focal length. The first vertex to the Gaussian infinity image is 74.529321864 mm. Dividing that distance by the literal EFL gives 2.664248832, compared with the source's rounded telephoto-ratio statement 2.66. This ratio does not describe the mechanical catalog barrel length.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1: Positive meniscus

nd = 1.72000, νd = 43.7. Glass: OHARA S-LAM52 coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed. f = +142.192 mm.

The weak front positive meniscus is followed by the substantially stronger negative L2. It contributes positive power without changing the net-divergent character of their combined component. Its two clear caps share the inferred 16.7 mm radius. The source does not assign a unique aberration budget to this individual element.

### L2: Negative meniscus

nd = 1.62041, νd = 60.27. Glass: OHARA S-BSM16 coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed. f = −25.721 mm.

L2 supplies the dominant negative power in the front component. Its rear radius is only 10.836 mm after scaling. The source section visibly distinguishes the smaller rear powered arc from the stepped mechanical shoulder. The model therefore uses 14.5 mm at the front and 9.7 mm at the rear, rather than extending the rear sphere to the outer body height. The renderer's straight rim join is an inferred closed-solid representation, not a manufacturing drawing of the shoulder.

### L3: Biconvex positive

nd = 1.80440, νd = 39.62. Glass: OHARA S-LAH63 coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed. f = +19.079 mm.

This is the strongest positive singlet and the first element of the convergent rear component. Its 8.4 mm axial thickness and high source index are retained. Its 10 mm clear radii are schematic hypotheses. The inferred stop lies after this element, within the source's 2.548 mm air interval to L4.

### L4: Biconcave negative

nd = 1.78472, νd = 25.71. Glass: HOYA FD110 coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed. f = −16.233 mm.

The relatively dispersive negative element is opposed to the neighboring positive elements. Its thin 1.176 mm center and two negative-power faces are literal source values. The inferred clear radii are 6.68 and 6.64 mm. Their slightly unequal rims give a tapered side boundary rather than a cylinder of arbitrary diameter.

### L5: Positive meniscus

nd = 1.62041, νd = 60.27. Glass: OHARA S-BSM16 coordinate equivalent used for dispersion, as in L2; production melt unconfirmed. f = +41.414 mm.

The positive meniscus adds rear-component convergence with lower dispersion than L4. Its 7.5 mm shared clear radius follows the square-cut rim drawn in the source figure, above the 6.85 mm nominal axial-beam height at its rear face. The small neighboring source air intervals are unchanged; L4's smaller rim sits inside L5's front edge, so the two faces never meet.

### L6: Biconvex positive

nd = 1.61272, νd = 58.75. Glass: OHARA BSM4 coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed. f = +41.639 mm.

The rear positive singlet completes the convergent component. Its front face is weakly curved and its rear face carries most of its positive surface power. Both clear caps use an inferred 9.5 mm radius. It is air-separated from L5; there is no added cemented seventh glass to reconcile the undated leaflet.

## Glass Identification and Spectral Scope

The table prints n and ν without a wavelength subscript. Figure 2 labels d/F in the spherical-aberration plot and g/F/C in the lateral-chromatic plot. This context supports the nominal d-line design convention used here. The six source n/ν pairs are retained exactly; the known contextual reference is not labeled as an unspecified spectral line.

Catalog coordinate equivalents supply dispersion curves for all six elements. Catalog-minus-patent residuals (Δnd, Δνd) are: OHARA S-LAM52 for L1 (0, −0.010188); OHARA S-BSM16 for L2/L5 (+0.000001, +0.019582); OHARA S-LAH63 for L3 (−0.000002, −0.033800); HOYA FD110 for L4 (0, +0.010000); and OHARA BSM4 for L6 (−0.000004, −0.000871), an exact match at printed precision. The patent's printed nd/νd remain the design coordinates; catalog curves provide spectral behavior away from d. Production suppliers and melts remain unconfirmed.

Reference MTF uses the native d-line wavelength, 587.5618 nm. The quoted approximate-photopic results use the nd/Abbe approximation; catalog curves now support secondary-spectrum and chromatic calculations. Neither represents measured factory melt spectra or establishes historical anomalous partial dispersion. Coating transmission remains unspecified.

## Focus Mechanism

The MC leaflet names the six-element/six-group lens ZUIKO MC AUTO-W and specifies a straight helicoid and 0.3 m minimum focus. Neither leaflet nor the 1985 catalog describes floating elements for this lens. The model therefore uses inferred unit focus: all six elements move together, with internal spacings fixed. The patent prints only the infinity prescription.

At a 300 mm object-to-film distance, the paraxial conjugate gives a 40.233865110 mm rear image distance, compared with 36.701321864 mm at infinity. The resulting extension is 3.532543246 mm, with the object 221.938134890 mm ahead of the first vertex. This is calculated travel, not a published close-focus prescription. Finite-distance MTF remains unavailable because no source-published finite optical configuration is supplied; intermediate travel is interpolated.

## Iris, Clear Apertures and Physical Geometry

No source numerical iris position or clear-diameter table is available. The computational stop is inferred at the midpoint of d6: 1.274 mm after L3 and 1.274 mm before L4. Its wide-open radius is 6.762649473 mm, calibrated by the native exact axial marginal ray for the nominal f/2.8 beam radius 4.995332011 mm. This calibration is a modeling convention, not a measured factory iris.

The paraxial entrance-pupil radius is instead 5.247817884 mm, giving Gaussian EFL/(2 EP) = 2.665284875. The pupil is 17.000374993 mm imageward of the first vertex; the exit pupil is 8.229978341 mm objectward of the last vertex. The marginal-calibrated and paraxial pupil conventions are not silently equated. The principal planes are +27.557938745 mm from the first vertex and +8.727462600 mm from the last vertex.

The final surface clear radii, in order, are 16.7, 16.7, 14.5, 9.7, 10, 10, 6.68, 6.64, 7.5, 7.5, 9.5 and 9.5 mm. They are explicitly inferred and measured against the source figure. L4's caps are nominal axial-beam floors; they agree with the height at which its drawn curves meet a chamfered 7.5 mm blank, which is mechanical rather than optical. The other caps follow the drawn rims. No off-axis MTF fitting is used to enlarge an aperture. At the prescription's curvatures the R8 and R9 faces would meet near 7.0 mm, so L4 cannot reach the drawn blank height; the radii are modeling inferences rather than published clear apertures.

Analytic geometry gives a minimum shared-band glass thickness of 0.723773517 mm and maximum spherical rim angle of 63.529537209°. The largest physical gap-sag fraction is 0.899935540 across R8–R9, leaving 0.145693854 mm clearance on their shared radial band. This is below the unchanged 90% default. **No gap-sag override is used.** The full inferred element outlines fit without hidden trimming. Their conical or cylindrical rim joins remain part of the physical geometry beyond the shared-band cap clearance.

## First-Order Properties, Coverage and MTF

The normalized focal length is 0.999066402289, a residual of −0.000933597711 from the printed f = 1. Exact point equality is explicitly false. Source cells are not changed to remove that small residual. Conservative printed-precision interval overlap is only a numerical enclosure, not proof that some simultaneous hidden prescription exactly reproduces every rounded prose value. There is no printed Seidel table to claim as independently reproduced.

Field references are kept distinct. The source coma plot is labeled 27.8°, while the heading and other field plots use 37.5°. Their exact modeled chief heights at the Gaussian image are about 14.493208082 and 21.079143689 mm. The actual 36×24 mm diagonal has radius 21.633307653 mm and requires approximately 38.191373063°. The native rounded 43.3 mm image-circle reference uses radius 21.65 mm and approximately 38.211876642°. Both lie beyond the source 37.5° and the runtime heuristic half-field 35.463665620°. The heuristic is not substituted for the source or format boundary.

At infinity, the nominal axial beam passes at every stop. Some off-axis bundles vignette, including the source 37.5° field and the full-frame corners; the sampled 15° bundle vignettes while the 27.8° bundle is fully admitted, so vignetting is not necessarily monotonic with field angle. Surviving paths remain inside the inferred glass outlines, including tapered rims, and in the appropriate surrounding air regions. Finite sampling does not prove continuous-pupil transmission or universal coverage.

Three aperture conventions remain distinct: exact marginal recalibration at each f-number; linear iris scaling with the nominal EFL/(2f) beam; and the viewer's paraxial entrance pupil, with both pupil and iris scaled by the wide-open-to-current f-number ratio. The wide-open viewer pupil radius is 5.247817884 mm, compared with the nominal 4.995332011 mm beam. The larger viewer pupil is partially clipped at the iris even on axis at f/2.8. Thus nominal-beam admission does not imply full transmission of the viewer pupil. The rounded-format corner is approximately 38.2118764445°, with one admitted chief-ray branch; the tiny difference from 38.211876642° reflects numerical solver tolerance.

The infinity reference MTF spans f/2.8, 4, 5.6, 8, 11, 16 and 22, three format fractions and the source field heights, on both Gaussian and best-axial image planes. Reference and approximate-photopic results converge through 50 lp/mm without failed rays. Frequencies are 0, 20, 40 and 50 lp/mm; the convergence band ends at 50. Best-axial focus remains a separate analysis plane and does not replace the Gaussian infinity image distance.

For example, at infinity and f/2.8 the viewer-pupil on-axis design-plane MTF is approximately 0.26046, 0.08774 and 0.19333 at 20, 40 and 50 lp/mm. Its best-axial result is about 0.89578, 0.77115 and 0.69653. The nonmonotonic design-plane values and the large focus sensitivity remain visible; they are not a promise of commercial performance. The patent's plotted curves depend on source conventions and an unprinted stop, so these reconstructed-iris results are not represented as a numerical reproduction of those curves or of a factory lens.
