## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 3,796,481
**Title:** Varifocal Lens Systems
**Inventor:** Toru Fujii
**Assignee:** Olympus Optical Co., Ltd.
**Priority:** 1 May 1971 (Japan 46-29159) and 14 January 1972 (Japan 47-6465)
**Filed:** 28 April 1972
**Granted:** 12 March 1974
**Example details:** Example 4, section in Fig. 2, aberration curves in Figs. 6a–6i at f = 76.5, 108.18 and 153 mm; the prescription is repeated in claim 4. Native patent scale.

The patent describes a mechanically compensated zoom for 35 mm still cameras and gives four numerical examples. Example 4 is the one transcribed, and the model is associated with the Olympus Zuiko Auto-Zoom 75-150mm f/4 for the OM system, which MIR describes as Olympus's first zoom lens. Olympus has not published the prescription, and the patent does not name the product, so the identification rests on construction and specification:

1. The production lens has 15 elements in 11 groups. Example 4 is the only example in the patent with that count: Examples 1 and 2 have 14 elements in 10 groups, and Example 3 has 14 elements in 11 groups. Example 4's text says it differs from Example 3 only in the fourth group, whose front component is split into two lenses (Fig. 2) and whose middle pair is cemented.
2. The example covers f = 76.5–153 mm at a constant F/4, against the marketed 75–150 mm f/4. At the 21.63 mm corner of the 35 mm frame the model's angle of view is 31.6° to 16.1°; the production specification quotes 32° to 16°.
3. The assignee is Olympus Optical, and the priority dates (1971–72) and grant (1974) fit a lens of the early OM system. Toru Fujii is also named as co-inventor of the later Zuiko Auto-Zoom 65-200mm f/4 (US 4,568,150), a four-group zoom of the same power sequence.
4. The production construction diagram reproduced by MIR shows, front to rear, a single lens and a cemented pair, a four-element group of a cemented pair and two negative lenses whose deep concave faces meet across an air space, a cemented pair, the iris, two positive lenses and a cemented pair, a long air space, and two separate rear lenses. That is the layout of Fig. 2 and Example 4. The model's first vertex lies 112.5 mm ahead of the OM mount flange (46 mm flange distance), against a quoted barrel length of 115 mm.

The diagram is a small, uncredited image and cannot confirm radii, thicknesses or glass. The model is therefore a strong construction match, not a confirmed factory prescription, and the production lens may differ from the patent table in detail.

## Optical Architecture

Fifteen spherical elements form a four-group zoom of positive, negative, positive and positive power. The patent divides it into a varifocal system I (groups LA, LB and LC, here G1–G3) and a prime system II (LD, here G4). The printed group focal lengths are fA = 100.023, fB = −34.432, fC = 88.145 and fD = 95.369 mm; the table reproduces them as 100.022, −34.432, 88.145 and 95.369 mm.

- **G1 (L1–L3)** is a positive singlet followed by a cemented achromat, fixed during zooming.
- **G2 (L4–L7)** is the variator: the cemented pair L4/L5 and two negative lenses L6 and L7. It moves 24.347 mm toward the image from wide to tele.
- **G3 (L8/L9)** is a cemented positive doublet, the compensator. It moves toward G4 and back as G2 travels, returning to the same position at both ends.
- **G4 (L10–L15)** is a fixed relay: two positive menisci, a cemented pair, a 26 mm air space, and a positive and a negative singlet.

G1–G3 form an afocal system at every setting: a collimated beam from infinity leaves G3 still collimated, with an angular magnification of 0.802× at wide and 1.605× at tele. G4 then focuses it, so the focal length of the whole lens is fD times that magnification, the image always lies at G4's back focus, and an iris placed in the collimated space ahead of G4 gives the same f-number at every focal length. The paraxial back focus is 44.393 mm and the first vertex lies 158.54 mm from the image, a telephoto ratio of 1.04 at 153 mm.

The patent prints the three variable gaps dA5, dB7 and dC3 at the two ends only. Their sum, 34.164 mm, is the same at both ends, and dC3 is 6.707 mm at both, which is the signature of a compensator that moves out and back. The model adds 13 solved stations between the ends: at each, dA5 sets the focal length, the gap sum stays 34.164 mm, and dC3 places the paraxial image on the fixed image plane. The solved stations are first-order states, not Olympus's cam.

G3 comes closest to G4 near 108 mm, where dC3 is 2.53 mm, 4.18 mm less than at the ends. Between neighbouring stations the gaps are interpolated linearly, which leaves at most 0.033 mm of defocus (near 103 mm). Interpolating between the two printed rows alone would hold G3 still and defocus the image by up to 5.2 mm.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Focal lengths are for each element alone in air.

### L1 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.15. Glass: S-BSL7 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +185.1 mm.

The front element, convex to the object, carries about half of G1's positive power. The patent leaves the first group's form open (one cemented component, or a singlet and a cemented pair, depending on field angle and zoom ratio) and asks only that it correct axial spherical and chromatic aberration.

### L2 and L3 — Cemented Achromat (D1)

L2: nd = 1.74077, νd = 27.79. Glass: S-TIH13 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = −149.5 mm.

L3: nd = 1.51821, νd = 65.04. Glass: BSL22 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +87.2 mm.

A dense-flint negative meniscus cemented to a crown positive meniscus that is nearly flat at the rear (R = 1742.45 mm). The pair is weakly positive (f = +215.3 mm) and achromatizes G1.

### L4 and L5 — Cemented Variator Pair (D2)

L4: nd = 1.61800, νd = 63.38. Glass: PCD4 (HOYA catalog equivalent). f = −122.2 mm.

L5: nd = 1.78470, νd = 26.22. Glass: PBH23 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +64.5 mm.

The patent's key change to the variator is this pair: a biconcave crown cemented to a biconvex dense flint, weakly positive overall (f = +132.9 mm). Earlier variators of this type used two components and three lenses, with the front pair negative. Here the chromatic coma of L5 opposes that of L4, and the weak concave front surface (rB1 = −175.362 mm, conditions 1 and 2) controls coma and distortion at the wide end. The patent asks for a weak positive power at L5's front, the cemented face.

### L6 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.57250, νd = 57.65. Glass: S-BAL11 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = −58.8 mm.

### L7 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.61800, νd = 63.38. Glass: PCD4 (HOYA catalog equivalent). f = −53.7 mm.

L6 and L7 carry the variator's negative power (G2: f = −34.43 mm). Their deep concave surfaces, L6's rear (39.98 mm) and L7's front (−35.068 mm), face each other across a 5.26 mm air space. Condition 4 bounds their mean radius against fB; the patent uses them, with rB1, to balance the coma of L5's rear surface.

### L8 and L9 — Cemented Compensator (D3)

L8: nd = 1.74077, νd = 27.79. Glass: S-TIH13 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = −86.7 mm.

L9: nd = 1.62230, νd = 53.20. Glass: S-BSM22 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +43.8 mm.

A negative flint meniscus cemented to a biconvex crown, f = +88.15 mm. The patent notes that the compensator works over a small magnification range, so its chromatic change is minor and it may be a single lens or a doublet.

### L10 and L11 — Positive Menisci

L10: nd = 1.50378, νd = 66.81. Glass: BSL21 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +66.7 mm.

L11: nd = 1.50048, νd = 65.99. Glass: BK4 (SUMITA catalog equivalent). f = +176.1 mm.

The relay's front component LDF, here split into two menisci convex to the object (f = +48.7 mm together). Condition 1 keeps it strong, |fDF / fD| < 0.7 (0.511 here), to shorten the relay's telephoto ratio; the patent warns that a strong front lens also brings negative spherical aberration and chromatic coma, which the middle pair must cancel.

### L12 and L13 — Cemented Middle Pair (D4)

L12: nd = 1.50378, νd = 66.81. Glass: BSL21 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +41.1 mm.

L13: nd = 1.74400, νd = 44.78. Glass: S-LAM2 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = −19.7 mm.

The middle component LDM is negative (f = −47.2 mm). Its index step from the low-index crown L12 to the lanthanum glass L13, 0.240 (condition 2 asks for 0.1 to 0.3), supplies the strongly diverging action that corrects LDF's spherical aberration and chromatic coma. L13's rear surface, R = 16.95 mm, faces the 26 mm air space.

### L14 and L15 — Rear Pair

L14: nd = 1.60717, νd = 40.34. Glass: BAFD3 (HOYA catalog equivalent). f = +31.0 mm.

L15: nd = 1.66755, νd = 41.93. Glass: J-BASF6 (HIKARI catalog equivalent). f = −36.4 mm.

A biconvex lens and a biconcave lens 1.44 mm apart (LDB, f = +134.1 mm). Their nearly cancelling powers (condition 4: 0.449 against a limit of 0.8) and L14's rear radius, −28.103 mm (condition 3: between 0.2 fD and 0.4 fD), remove the asymmetry of off-axis coma and hold the relay's Petzval sum in range.

The patent gives refractive index and Abbe number without naming a spectral line. All twelve distinct pairs equal a catalog nd/νd within 0.00001 in index and 0.12 in Abbe number, so the table is read as d-line data. The variator pair spans 37.2 in Abbe number (condition 6 asks for more than 20) and 0.167 in index (condition 5: more than 0.08). The relay is built mostly from low-index crowns, with flint-like barium glasses in the rear pair; the patent's Petzval condition, 0.4 < ΣPD < 1.0 for the relay (0.510 here), offsets the large negative Petzval sum of the varifocal section. No element is an ED or anomalous-dispersion glass.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent prints the infinity state only and names no focusing group, so the model has no focus movement. The production lens focuses by a rotating helicoid to 1.6 m, and its specification lists an automatic correction mechanism for close-distance aberrations; neither the moving group nor that mechanism is described in the patent, and neither is modeled.

## Source Erratum

Example 4 prints the thickness dB2 of L5 as 1.96 mm. The model uses 2.96 mm. Example 4's text states that it differs from Example 3 only in the fourth group, and Example 3, Example 1 and claim 4 all print 2.96 mm. The traced table confirms it. With 2.96 mm the variator's focal length is −34.432 mm, as printed, and the two printed zoom rows put the image on the same plane (back focus 44.3930 and 44.3927 mm), as a compensated zoom must. With 1.96 mm the variator becomes −34.471 mm, the image moves 0.74 mm between the ends, and L5's edge would vanish at a 14 mm radius.

## Stop, Clear Apertures and Model Limits

The patent neither prints nor draws a stop. The model places the iris 1.0 mm ahead of L10, fixed with G4, where the production diagram draws it, just behind the compensator's path. G3 comes no closer than 1.54 mm. Because the beam there is collimated, the stop radius, 11.92 mm, set from F/4 at the wide end, gives F/4 at every station, and its exact position within the air space does not change the f-number. A stop placed farther forward would be crossed by the compensator in the middle of the zoom range.

No clear aperture is printed. The front rims, 22.5 mm, sit inside the 49 mm filter thread; the others are read from Fig. 2, which draws the compensator and the relay's two front menisci at about the same height, the relay's cemented pair smaller, and L13's concave rear face ending inside a bevel. The f/4 axial beam passes every rim at every station, and the chief ray to the 21.63 mm corner clears every rim from 76.5 to 153 mm. Off-axis bundles are partly vignetted, mostly by the front group and the variator; wide open, the corner keeps about 53% of its light at 76.5 mm, 61% near 90–95 mm and 48% at 153 mm (geometric transmission). That depends on the inferred rims and is not a measured production value.

The back focus is not printed. The model's image distance, 44.3928 mm, is the paraxial back focus of the printed rows. The model's diaphragm range, f/4 to f/22, follows the production specification.
