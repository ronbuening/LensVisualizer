## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2008/0062534 A1  
**Application Number:** 11/900,379  
**Filed:** 10 September 2007  
**Published:** 13 March 2008  
**Inventors:** Atsujiro Ishii; Hirohiko Kimata  
**Title:** Zoom Lens  
**Example details:** Paragraph 0127, PDF pages 23–24; Figures 1, 6 and 11. The prescription is used at the patent's own scale.

The production lens is the Four Thirds DSLR ZUIKO DIGITAL ED 40–150mm F4.0–5.6, with 12 elements in 9 groups. It is not the older F3.5–4.5, the Micro Four Thirds 13/10 M.ZUIKO / R, or the F2.8 PRO. Examples 1 and 3 of the patent both match it equally well, and the published evidence does not show which of the two went into production. Example 1 is modeled here as the probable production design.

The original A1 front page names both inventors but no applicant or assignee, so the file records no assignee. The later grant US 7,450,319 B2 names Olympus Imaging Corp. and explicitly identifies this A1 as its prior publication. The two originals carry the same application and Japanese priority 2006-246348, dated 12 September 2006. Every Example 1 numerical row matches the grant's first unnumbered block in columns 11–12; the A1 explicitly prints the Example 1 heading.

The manufacturer connection rests on several independent architectural features:

1. Olympus announced the lens on 13 November 2006 (on sale in Japan from 23 November 2006). That announcement and the September 2010 Four Thirds joint lens catalogue give 12 elements in 9 groups, one ED element, internal focusing, 0.9 m closest focus throughout the zoom, 0.14× maximum magnification and a 72 mm barrel length. The model's telephoto close-focus magnification is 0.143×, and its front vertex sits 72.25 mm ahead of the Four Thirds flange at the wide end.
2. The catalogue's construction drawing has cemented L1–L2, L4–L5 and L8–L9 pairs and the same three separated rear elements as Examples 1 and 3. It colours the biconvex front element of the third-group doublet as the ED element, which is L8 (νd 81.5) in both examples.
3. Examples 1 and 3 use the same glass at every position and share every radius sign; their radii differ by about 1 % or less. That is too small to separate at the scale of the catalogue drawing.
4. The 12 September 2006 priority date is two months before the announcement. The timing is consistent with the lens but does not prove which prescription went into production.

Example 2 moves the low-dispersion glass to the singlet L7, so it does not match the drawing. Examples 4 and 5 have an air-spaced front pair and 12 elements in 10 groups. Example 1 is preferred over Example 3 because only Example 1 prints the OD = 900 mm close-focus rows, which agree with the 0.9 m minimum focus. That makes Example 1 the better-documented choice; it does not prove that Example 1 rather than Example 3 is the production prescription.

## Optical Architecture

The design is a three-unit positive–negative–positive zoom. G1 comprises L1–L2; G2 comprises L3–L6; the iris is source surface 11; G3 comprises L7–L12. The specification's 9 groups are air-separated components; the 3 zoom groups are units that move together. All powered surfaces are spherical.

G1 and G3 move objectward from wide to telephoto. G2 follows a reversing locus: it initially moves slightly imageward, then ends objectward of its wide position. The iris travels with G3. G2 is the internal focusing unit, moving objectward for close focus (paragraph 0106). At the three printed infinity states, effective focal lengths are 40.925618, 77.513796 and 146.715301 mm, compared with the patent's stated 40.9, 77.5 and 146.7 mm. The design is not rescaled to marketed 40–150 mm.

Source surfaces 23–24 are the camera-side filter/cover-glass block F, not a thirteenth production-lens element. Its physical thickness is 4.63 mm, n_d = 1.5163 and νd = 64.1, followed by 2.50 mm of air. The model traces the plate at its physical thickness, with no air-equivalent back distance added on top. The source's physical last-lens-to-image distances are 35.56, 40.94 and 61.66 mm.

The patent's aberration plots label f/4.080, f/4.800 and f/5.811, while the one-decimal table prints 4.1, 4.8 and 5.8. The more precise plot values set the model's iris. They remain distinct from marketed f/4–5.6. The source publishes no stop-diameter schedule or invariant-iris statement.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Biconvex Positive

n_d = 1.6031, νd = 60.6. Glass: N-SK14 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). Isolated-in-air thick-element f = +80.53528 mm.

The positive member of the front cemented collector. Its moderate dispersion is paired with the much higher-index negative member to control primary axial color in G1.

### L2 — Negative Meniscus

n_d = 1.8467, νd = 23.8. Glass: S-TIH53 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). Isolated-in-air thick-element f = -221.32364 mm.

The front doublet negative member shares source surface 2 with L1. The small rear curvature and high dispersion balance the positive collector without adding an air-separated component.

### L3 — Negative Meniscus

n_d = 1.8052, νd = 25.4. Glass: S-TIH6 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). Isolated-in-air thick-element f = -56.30493 mm.

The object-facing negative meniscus begins the negative variator/focus group. Its source signs establish the orientation; the element is not biconcave.

### L4 — Biconcave Negative

n_d = 1.5168, νd = 64.2. Glass: N-BK7 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). Isolated-in-air thick-element f = -28.64791 mm.

The biconcave member of the G2 cemented doublet. The relatively low-index crown is paired with the high-index positive meniscus at the shared surface 7.

### L5 — Positive Meniscus

n_d = 1.8467, νd = 23.8. Glass: S-TIH53 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). Isolated-in-air thick-element f = +25.97940 mm.

The positive member in the negative G2 assembly. Its dense flint coordinate helps control the group chromatic balance; its individual positive power does not make G2 positive overall.

### L6 — Negative Meniscus

n_d = 1.7725, νd = 49.6. Glass: S-LAH66 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). Isolated-in-air thick-element f = -38.64930 mm.

The image-facing negative meniscus ends G2. The thin air gap between source surfaces 8 and 9 constrains the shared clear radius and is explicitly checked for physical clearance.

### L7 — Biconvex Positive

n_d = 1.4875, νd = 70.2. Glass: S-FSL5 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). Isolated-in-air thick-element f = +48.25981 mm.

The first G3 positive singleton lies just behind the iris. Its low-index, low-dispersion coordinate is distinct from the stronger ED coordinate of L8.

### L8 — Biconvex Positive

n_d = 1.4970, νd = 81.5. Glass: S-FPL51 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). Isolated-in-air thick-element f = +25.19387 mm.

The biconvex low-dispersion member of the G3 doublet. At νd = 81.5 it has the highest Abbe number in the design. It sits where the Olympus catalogue drawing colours the lens's single ED element, in both Example 1 and Example 3, so the model tags it as the inferred ED element.

### L9 — Negative Meniscus

n_d = 1.8010, νd = 35.0. Glass: S-LAM66 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). Isolated-in-air thick-element f = -31.82185 mm.

The negative member cemented to L8 has the higher-index, more dispersive coordinate needed for an achromatizing pair. The source gives optical constants, not a production supplier or partial-dispersion certificate.

### L10 — Positive Meniscus

n_d = 1.5750, νd = 41.5. Glass: S-TIL27 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). Isolated-in-air thick-element f = +80.88848 mm.

The positive meniscus begins the separated rear subgroup after the 13.90 mm air interval. Its convex face points imageward.

### L11 — Biconvex Positive

n_d = 1.5168, νd = 64.2. Glass: N-BK7 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). Isolated-in-air thick-element f = +94.29032 mm.

The thin biconvex positive element follows L10 across a 0.30 mm central air gap. That gap opens away from the axis with the retained surface signs.

### L12 — Negative Meniscus

n_d = 1.7725, νd = 49.6. Glass: S-LAH66 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). Isolated-in-air thick-element f = -37.67657 mm.

The final image-facing negative meniscus completes the rear subgroup. Its source material repeats L6; the camera filter block remains downstream and separate.

## Focus Mechanism

Focusing is internal: G2 moves objectward for close focus (¶0106) while G1, the iris and G3 remain fixed. The patent prints gaps at infinity and at OD = 900 mm, measured from the image plane (¶0126), for WE, ST and TE only.

| Zoom position | D3 infinity / 900 mm | D10 infinity / 900 mm | D22, both states |
| ------------- | -------------------: | --------------------: | ---------------: |
| WE            |          2.31 / 1.00 |         17.47 / 18.77 |            28.43 |
| ST            |        37.66 / 34.33 |         10.74 / 14.07 |            33.81 |
| TE            |        48.35 / 43.37 |           2.10 / 7.07 |            54.53 |

The printed rows give 1.30, 3.33 and 4.97 mm of objectward G2 travel. At WE and TE the sum D3 + D10 differs by 0.01 mm between infinity and close focus. The model keeps the printed values rather than redistributing that difference to hold G1 exactly still. The 0.9 m close focus agrees with Olympus's minimum focusing distance, which applies throughout the zoom.

The model uses 65 zoom positions and 18 focus positions: 17 evenly spaced focus steps plus one step just off infinity. Only the six printed states are source values. At intermediate zoom positions G1 and G3 are interpolated between the printed states, and G2 is solved so the image-plane offset follows the printed values smoothly. At intermediate focus positions G2 is solved for the object distance. This is a reconstruction, not Olympus's focus cam, so distance labels between the printed states are approximate: the first finite step of the focus control reads 225 m but corresponds paraxially to about 246 m (+9.4 %). Finite-distance MTF is available only at the three printed 900 mm states.

The printed prescription carries small paraxial image-plane offsets: +0.025 / +0.026 / +0.032 mm at infinity and +0.034 / +0.026 / +0.040 mm at 900 mm. The model keeps them, and the interpolation adds at most 0.008 mm. At the printed image plane the 900 mm rows focus paraxially at object-to-image distances of 914.7, 903.9 and 902.0 mm.

## Clear Apertures and Physical Geometry

Clear semi-diameters are inferred, because the source has no effective-diameter column. They were scaled from the optical outlines in Figure 1(a), using the 75.36 mm span from the first to the last powered surface as the scale. The figure draws no separate mounting flanges: L2, L3, the cemented L4–L5, L6, the L8–L9 doublet, L11 and L12 are drawn with squared rims, and the model gives each of them one rim height across its faces, except that L2 shares its cemented front surface with the slightly taller L1. The uncaptioned Olympus section confirms the layout and relative proportions, but not millimetre apertures.

In G2 the drawing gives about 7.8 mm for the cemented L4–L5 and 8.0 mm for L6, but r8 and r9 meet at about 7.67 mm. L4–L5 therefore use 7.4 mm and L6 7.6 mm, just above the telephoto axial beam, which needs 7.33 mm at r8 and r9 and 7.57 mm at r10.

The 1.27 mm air gap between r8 and r9 is the tightest in the design. With these rims the two surfaces' sags take up about 93 % of it, leaving 0.089 mm of air at the rim. Across all zoom and focus positions the thinnest glass is 0.94 mm and nothing is hidden or trimmed in the drawing.

## Source Field, Image Plane and Simulated MTF

The patent's aberration plots use an image height of 11.60 mm, which is not a published image-circle diameter. The model uses the Four Thirds format: 17.3 × 13.0 mm in Olympus's E-410 specifications, a half-diagonal of 10.82 mm.

The patent also gives full field angles of 30.5°, 16.4° and 8.7°. In the model, chief rays at those half-angles reach image heights of 10.89, 11.32 and 11.29 mm. Reaching the plotted 11.60 mm height takes about 16.25°, 8.39° and 4.47°. The patent's two field definitions therefore do not describe identical endpoints, and neither is corrected to match the other.

The patent's spherical-aberration curves and an exact d-line trace both show a positive paraxial image offset, while the outer pupil zones cross the axis short of the plane. The RMS best-focus plane therefore lies about 0.093, 0.094 and 0.107 mm in front of the printed image plane at WE, ST and TE; photopic wide-open axial best focus is about 0.107, 0.109 and 0.109 mm in front. The model keeps the printed image plane, indices and filter spacing.

At the printed image plane, wide-open photopic axial MTF at 40 lp/mm is about 0.21 / 0.34 / 0.38 at WE / ST / TE, and telephoto corner tangential MTF is about 0.09. Proxy glass dispersion, estimated clear rims, the choice of image plane and the Example 1 / Example 3 ambiguity all limit how far these figures describe the production lens. They are not a manufacturer MTF chart or a measurement.
