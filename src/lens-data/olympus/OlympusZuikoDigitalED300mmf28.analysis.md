## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 6,927,926 B2  
**Filed:** April 15, 2003  
**Granted:** August 9, 2005  
**Inventors:** Takashi Kasahara; Masahiro Imamura  
**Assignee:** Olympus Corporation  
**Title:** Telephoto Lens and Telephoto Lens Apparatus Having the Same  
**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 3, Numerical data 3, Figure 7

The prescription represents the native third patent embodiment correlated with the OLYMPUS ZUIKO DIGITAL ED 300mm f/2.8. The correlation is supported by several convergent features, but is not a manufacturer-confirmed attribution:

1. The patent has twelve powered elements in ten air-separated components. The production manual specifies thirteen elements in eleven groups and requires a rear SCF-43 filter. The manufacturer catalog sectional drawing shows the corresponding powered topology plus a rear plane element. [C] Counting that filter explains the difference by inference; the manufacturer does not explicitly define the counting convention.
2. The manufacturer's three ED positions align with the three native high-Abbe elements L12, L13 and L32. The cemented pairs and the four-unit distribution also correspond. [P, M, C]
3. The nominal 300mm, f/2.8 specification and Four Thirds association agree with the general design scale. The native patent close state is distinct from the production minimum-focus specification of 2.4m and maximum magnification of 0.15×. [M, O]
4. The native finite state, variable gaps and optical configuration, rather than marketing focal length alone, distinguish the selected embodiment from the other two examples. [P, R]

The original grant has two appended USPTO certificates. The July 18, 2006 certificate corrects the selected surface-5 index from printed 51.49700 to 1.49700; that correction is applied. The November 28, 2006 certificate corrects claim typography. Its imperfectly reproduced Abbe inequality is not reconstructed into a new optical value. The specification's inequalities govern the calculations. [P, PDF 19–20]

All dimensions retain the patent scale. No filter, cover glass, asphere or unpublished optical surface is added. The production filter is optically consequential, but the patent supplies no thickness, index or axial position from which to construct it. This model therefore describes the native twelve-element embodiment rather than a complete commercial lens-plus-filter system.

## Optical Architecture

The all-spherical design has four functional units in positive–negative–positive–negative order: G1 contains L11–L14, G2 contains L21–L23, G3 contains L31–L32, and G4 contains L41–L43. These four functional units are distinct from the ten air-separated components. The only cemented pairs are L21/L22 and L41/L42. Source plane 18 is the stop, between G3 and G4. [P, col. 9–10; Fig. 7]

The calculated standalone unit focal lengths are G1 +207.489339mm, G2 -137.162493mm, G3 +155.113804mm and G4 -319.329662mm. The two cemented pairs have positive net focal lengths, 1179.532214mm and 433.520413mm respectively; neither pair alone determines the sign of its full negative unit.

G1 collects the incoming beam with two positive low-dispersion constituents bracketed by negative elements. G2 and G3 move differentially for internal floating focus, while G1 and G4 are nominally fixed. The patent attributes reduced focus-dependent field-curvature variation to coordinated moving units, and relates the rear negative unit and its cemented index contrast to off-axis correction. These are group-level statements; an isolated element-power sign does not establish a unique aberration contribution. [P, col. 3–5, 9]

At infinity, corrected d-line EFL is 299.995876mm, first-to-last vertex length is 218.503600mm, and derived last-vertex BFD is 96.493536mm. First vertex to Gaussian image is 314.997136mm, giving track/EFL 1.050005. Thus the patent's telephoto designation does not establish a first-vertex track shorter than focal length. The printed EFL 299.9986mm differs beyond its last displayed digit, but lies within the propagated source-rounding interval; the native radii and gaps are retained.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The following focal lengths are calculated for each isolated thick element in air at the d-line, not assigned in-situ powers. OHARA names identify coordinate-compatible dispersion proxies; the factory supplier and melt are unconfirmed. Patent element labels appear in parentheses after the model labels.

### L1 (L11) — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.74950, νd = 35.28. Glass: S-LAM7 (OHARA coordinate-compatible proxy; supplier unconfirmed). f = -515.777082mm.

The front negative meniscus precedes both positive high-Abbe elements. It is one of the two negative constituents of G1; the patent specifically favors this negative–positive–positive–negative ordering for controlling front-unit axial and off-axis aberrations. A unique individual correction coefficient is not established. [P, col. 4, 9]

### L2 (L12) — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.54. Glass: S-FPL51 (OHARA coordinate-compatible proxy; supplier unconfirmed). f = +191.772685mm.

The first positive element supplies part of the converging front-unit power while satisfying the patent's high-Abbe requirement. Its dispersion contrast with the adjacent negative constituents implements the patent's stated front-unit chromatic strategy, without by itself proving apochromatic performance. [P, col. 3–5, 9]

### L3 (L13) — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.54. Glass: S-FPL51 (OHARA coordinate-compatible proxy; supplier unconfirmed). f = +304.404790mm.

The second positive element shares the high-Abbe coordinate of L12. Splitting the front positive power across these two elements supplies the additional correction freedom described in the specification. Its entrance index is the value corrected by the July certificate. [P, col. 3–4, 9–10; PDF 19]

### L4 (L14) — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.83400, νd = 37.16. Glass: S-LAH60 (OHARA coordinate-compatible proxy; supplier unconfirmed). f = -514.523631mm.

This weakly powered negative meniscus closes G1 before the large variable gap. It completes the preferred four-element front-unit ordering; its relatively high index does not independently establish a particular spherical-aberration correction. [P, col. 4, 9]

### L5 (L21) — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.78. Glass: S-TIH53 (OHARA coordinate-compatible proxy; supplier unconfirmed). f = +186.061272mm.

The positive constituent of the moving G2 cemented pair shares source surface 10 with L22. Its much lower Abbe number than the negative partner provides a dispersion contrast. The patent discusses this doublet together with L23 as a means of controlling chromatic aberration of magnification and other second-unit aberrations. [P, col. 3, 9]

### L6 (L22) — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.63930, νd = 44.87. Glass: S-BAM12 (OHARA coordinate-compatible proxy; supplier unconfirmed). f = -221.770712mm.

The negative constituent completes the L21/L22 doublet. The downstream medium at the common source surface belongs to this element. The doublet is net positive in air, whereas the complete G2 including L23 is negative; those two powers must not be conflated. [P, col. 9–10]

### L7 (L23) — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.69680, νd = 55.53. Glass: S-LAL14 (OHARA coordinate-compatible proxy; supplier unconfirmed). f = -122.797406mm.

This air-separated biconcave element closes G2 and supplies its stronger negative constituent. Its position before the variable G2–G3 separation makes the full unit part of the floating-focus system. The source does not isolate this element's aberration budget. [P, col. 9–10]

### L8 (L31) — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.80100, νd = 34.97. Glass: S-LAM66 (OHARA coordinate-compatible proxy; supplier unconfirmed). f = -221.337480mm.

The negative meniscus opens the moving positive G3. The strongly curved negative constituent and following high-Abbe positive element form an air-spaced pair, not a cemented doublet. Their shared motion follows the native gap endpoints. [P, col. 9–10]

### L9 (L32) — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.54. Glass: S-FPL51 (OHARA coordinate-compatible proxy; supplier unconfirmed). f = +90.988644mm.

The positive high-Abbe element makes the complete G3 positive despite L31. It supplies the third low-dispersion position that matches the production sectional evidence. Its presence supports a chromatic-design interpretation, but the front-unit inequalities do not formally constrain this third ED position. [P, col. 9–10; C]

### L10 (L41) — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.56732, νd = 42.82. Glass: S-TIL26 (OHARA coordinate-compatible proxy; supplier unconfirmed). f = -85.524764mm.

The negative constituent opens G4 immediately after the stop interval and shares source surface 20 with L42. The patent's rear-doublet condition concerns the positive-minus-negative refractive-index difference, not an Abbe-number difference. [P, col. 3–4, 9–10]

### L11 (L42) — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.80610, νd = 40.92. Glass: S-LAH53 (OHARA coordinate-compatible proxy; supplier unconfirmed). f = +71.091310mm.

The positive high-index constituent completes the rear cemented pair. It produces the required positive-minus-negative index contrast. The patent connects this doublet and the rear unit power to field-curvature correction; it does not identify the selected proxy as an actual production material. [P, col. 3–4, 9–10]

### L12 (L43) — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.69680, νd = 55.53. Glass: S-LAL14 (OHARA coordinate-compatible proxy; supplier unconfirmed). f = -175.549509mm.

The final negative meniscus closes G4. It makes the complete rear unit negative although the preceding cemented pair is positive. Its native rear surface is followed by a calculated image-space distance, not a patent-published back-focus number. [P, col. 9–10]

The retained patent nd/νd coordinates were compared with original CDGM, HIKARI, HOYA, OHARA, SCHOTT and SUMITA catalog sources. All nine distinct coordinate pairs have matching displayed-coordinate OHARA entries, with additional equivalent or nearby candidates across the catalog set. This supports reproducible proxy selection, not historical supplier identification. [G]

Displayed catalog νd and νd recomputed from published dispersion coefficients are distinct evidence. For example, the OHARA worksheet displays the native S-FPL51 and S-LAH53 coordinates, while coefficient-derived values can round differently at the last displayed decimal. The native patent nd/νd remain unchanged; the runtime resolves compatible Sellmeier data as proxies. No measured spectral line indices or anomalous partial-dispersion offsets are authored, and no APO performance is asserted.

The patent's stated strategy uses multiple high-Abbe positive elements in G1 to reduce axial chromatic generation before subsequent groups magnify it. The moving second-unit cemented pair and rear-unit doublet offer additional chromatic and off-axis control. Quantitative production secondary-spectrum performance cannot be inferred from those structural features alone. [P, col. 3–5]

The three S-FPL51 proxies at L12, L13 and L32 give catalog PgF ≈ 0.53746 and ΔPgF ≈ +0.03081 relative to the Schott normal line. Their APD highlights are inferred from these curves and are consistent with the production lens's three ED elements [O]; they are not patent-listed anomalous-glass identities.

## Focus Mechanism

Example 3 uses differential internal focus: G2 moves toward the image and G3 toward the object. G1 and G4 are nominally fixed. The two published gap states are retained exactly; linear interpolation between them is illustrative and is not a recovered production cam law or a set of validated finite conjugates. The source does not supply a motor prescription. [P, col. 9–10]

Relative to G1, the native gaps give G2 travel +17.1374mm and G3 travel -16.4990mm. The rounded sum of the three gaps changes by +0.0020mm; that small discrepancy is retained rather than silently adjusted to impose exact fixed-unit coordinates.

At the native proximity state, object-to-first-vertex distance is 1685.0494mm and calculated transverse magnification is -0.194274775, agreeing with printed −0.1943 after rounding. The state has cardinal EFL 249.056898mm; its finite-conjugate BFD is 96.492363mm, distinct from its infinity-conjugate BFD 48.106890mm.

With the authored last gap fixed at the infinity BFD, the near authored image coordinate is 314.999135978mm from the first vertex. The computed near Gaussian image is 314.997962727mm, or -0.001173251mm relative to that authored plane. Separately, the near Gaussian image differs from the infinity Gaussian image by +0.000826749mm. These are different reference-plane comparisons. The model's object-to-authored-image distance is 2.000048536m, not the production 2.4m minimum focus.

## Conditional Expressions

The calculations use the corrected native source. Conditions (1)–(3) have printed example values; (4)–(5) and preferred ranges occur in the general specification. [P, col. 3–5, 10]

1. Positive G1 Abbe number: νd > 80; both L12 and L13 have 81.54, matching the example.
2. Rear-unit power ratio: −1.2 < f4/f < −0.5. The computed ratio is -1.064446837, consistent with printed −1.06 and the preferred −1.1 to −0.6 range.
3. Rear cemented positive-minus-negative index: 1.80610 − 1.56732 = 0.23878, consistent with printed 0.239. It exceeds 0.2 and remains below the amended claim's 0.5 upper bound.
4. Image extent: 10mm < Y < 13mm; the plots mark Y = 12.25mm. The prose calls Y a diagonal length whereas the plot treats it as an axis-to-edge coordinate. The model uses the plot semiheight interpretation and preserves this terminology ambiguity.
5. Positive G1 dispersion: 85 > νd > 80, with preferred 83 > νd > 81.5. Both 81.54 values meet these intervals. The native 2.33° half-field also lies within the specification's preferred 0.5°–10° interval.

## Aperture, Field and Reconstruction Limits

The patent gives no physical clear apertures. All semi-diameters are inferred from Figure 7 and ray requirements. The stop radius is calibrated at infinity using an exact spherical d-line axial ray and the table aperture, not measured from a manufactured iris. Individual rim steps in the drawing are uncertain; an independent estimate of the rear-doublet positive rim differs from the adopted value by about 4.8%. This does not constitute a measured optical aperture tolerance.

The model uses table f/2.80 and inferred stop radius 24.553818069mm. The corresponding exact on-axis entrance radius is 53.570692212mm. Figures 8A/9A instead print FNO 2.850; that conflict remains unresolved. The physical iris is fixed between native focus states, and some near full-aperture rays are front-rim limited. No undocumented iris schedule or enlarged aperture is introduced to remove that behavior.

The patent half-field 2.33° and plot Y = 12.25mm are native design annotations. Production sources give full fields of 4.2° in the manual and 4.1° in the later official specification; these are not silently substituted for the patent values. The model's Four Thirds format-limited field and its larger raw aperture-limited chief-ray bound are different quantities and do not redefine the source field. [P, M, O]

All spherical surfaces and native cemented interfaces are retained. The inferred geometry passes the default gap-sag allowance without a geometry override. Sampled rays demonstrate a clear chief path and the stated infinity axial beam; they do not establish complete finite-pupil clearance, area-weighted transmission or production image quality. The engine's public rejection boolean agrees with an independent physical tracer, but some outside-front-rim trials have raw noBracket status rather than an explicit aperture-clip category. That classification distinction is not hidden as a claim of identical engine failure reasons.
