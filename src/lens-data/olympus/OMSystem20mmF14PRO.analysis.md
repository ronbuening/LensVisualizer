## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [JP 2023-32222 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP2023032222A/ja)  
**Filed:** 26 August 2021  
**Published:** 9 March 2023  
**Inventors:** Jumpei Nakamoto; Takehisa Koyama  
**Applicant:** Sigma Corporation  
**Title:** Inner-focus optical system  
**Example details:** 数値実施例1, printed pages 13–14 (surface, aspheric, variable-spacing and group data); Figure 1 on printed page 25. No scale factor is applied.

The production lens is the OM SYSTEM M.Zuiko Digital ED 20mm F1.4 PRO, a Micro Four Thirds prime that OM Digital Solutions announced on 4 November 2021 and released on 10 December 2021, the first product under the OM SYSTEM brand. OM lists 11 elements in 10 groups with one Super ED, three ED, two Super HR and two aspherical lenses, a 0.25 m minimum focus (0.11×), a nine-blade circular diaphragm and an F1.4–F16 range ([product page](https://explore.omsystem.com/us/en/m-zuiko-ed-20mm-f1-4-pro); [press release](https://jp.omsystem.com/cms/content/000116719.pdf), in Japanese).

The applicant is Sigma Corporation, not OM Digital Solutions or Olympus. The patent states its aim as an F1.4 inner-focus lens with the angle of view of a 40 mm lens on 35 mm film, whose focusing group can wobble during video autofocus with little change in image size (¶0001). That angle of view and the 11.15 mm image height put Example 1 in the Four Thirds format. Sigma's own small-format primes, the DC DN series, are APS-C designs, and none is a 20 mm f/1.4. The most direct reading is that Sigma designed this optical system for OM Digital Solutions. Neither company has said so, so the attribution rests on the optical evidence, which is strong. [Asobinet](https://asobinet.com/info-patent-sigma-20mm-f1-4/) reached the same identification from the patent's lens layout.

1. **Construction.** Example 1 has 11 elements in 10 groups, the count OM publishes.
2. **Maker drawing.** OM's construction drawing (press release, p. 2) matches Figure 1 element by element: a thin positive front meniscus; a deep negative meniscus with a thin biconcave element nested behind it; a small biconvex element; the stop; a cemented biconcave–biconvex pair; two large biconvex elements; a thin negative meniscus; a biconvex element; and a rear negative meniscus. Its glass colours fall on the same positions as the printed glasses. Super HR is on L1 and L4 (nd 2.00069 and 2.05090), ED on L2 (νd 81.61), L7 and L8 (νd 68.62), and Super ED on L3 (νd 95.10). The aspherical lenses are L6 and L10, the two elements that carry Example 1's three aspheric surfaces.
3. **Specifications.** f = 20.27 mm, F1.43, 2ω = 59.01° and Y = 11.15 mm fit a 20 mm f/1.4 for Four Thirds. OM's 57° angle of view corresponds to the slightly smaller sensor diagonal.
4. **Focus.** A single thin negative element focuses, consistent with OM's statement that the focusing lens was made light for fast autofocus.
5. **Timing.** The application was filed on 26 August 2021, about ten weeks before the announcement.
6. **Other examples.** Examples 2–6 share the layout but not the glass and asphere map. Examples 2 and 6 use two νd 95.10 elements in G1b, Example 2 puts its aspheric surfaces on L8 and L10, and Examples 3–5 have aspheric surfaces on three elements.

[Photography Blog's tests](https://www.photographyblog.com/reviews/olympus_om_system_m_zuiko_digital_ed_20mm_f1_4_pro_review/sharpness_1) report excellent central sharpness from f/1.4 to f/11. [LensTip's summary](https://www.lenstip.com/623.11-Lens_review-OM_System_M.Zuiko_Digital_20_mm_f_1.4_PRO_Summary.html) cautions against extending the strong central performance to the whole frame. Both concern the production lens, not this model.

## Optical Architecture

Example 1 is a negative-lead inner-focus design with four groups of power negative, positive, negative and positive: G1 −77.32, G2 +18.43, G3 −34.61 and G4 +52.51 mm as printed, all reproduced by the model. G1 (S1–S8) divides into a positive single lens G1a, a negative pair G1b and a positive single lens G1c (printed +133.27, −22.91 and +54.47 mm). The stop S9 follows G1. G2 (S10–S16) is a cemented negative–positive pair and two biconvex elements. G3 is the single focusing meniscus L9. G4 (S19–S22) is a double-aspherical biconvex element and a negative meniscus. The negative front group gives a retrofocus-like separation, and the positive G2 supplies most of the convergence.

The patent's aim is a focusing group that can wobble during video autofocus with little change in image size (¶0003, ¶0030). A cited earlier design placed its focus group beside the stop, which made that change large (¶0007). Here the light focusing element sits well behind the stop, and the stop-to-image length is set against the image height (condition (9), ¶0068). The negative focusing element sits where the off-axis chief ray is high, so it generates astigmatism, lateral colour and distortion; G4 is placed in the same region to correct them (¶0029). G1b also serves as an adjustment group: decentering it in assembly corrects decentering coma from manufacturing variation (¶0026, ¶0048).

The 4.14 mm optical filter FL (S23–S24, nd 1.51633, νd 64.14) stands for the camera's sensor-side filter stack. It is traced as a rear plate with the printed 15 mm gap ahead of it and the printed 1.3929 mm BF behind it, and is not drawn. Front vertex to image is 80.2629 mm (printed 80.26 mm).

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Focal lengths are computed from the printed prescription. The patent names no glass: the glass names below are catalog rows whose nd and νd equal the printed values, and the production supplier is not known. The Super HR, ED, Super ED and aspherical designations come from OM's construction drawing.

### L1 — Positive Meniscus, convex to object (S1–S2)

nd = 2.00069, νd = 25.46. Glass: TAFD40 (HOYA catalog equivalent) — Super HR in OM's drawing. f = +133.3 mm.

G1a. The patent makes the front of the negative first group a positive single lens so that the retrofocus-type power split does not produce excessive negative distortion and positive field curvature (¶0025). The very high index keeps both curvatures shallow.

### L2 — Negative Meniscus, convex to object (S3–S4)

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.61. Glass: FCD1 (HOYA catalog equivalent) — ED in OM's drawing. f = −42.4 mm.

First member of G1b. Its strongly curved rear face (R = 12.80 mm) carries the element's negative power. The patent builds G1b from two negative lenses so that its spherical aberration and its decentering behaviour as an adjustment group stay controllable (¶0026).

### L3 — Biconcave Negative (S5–S6)

nd = 1.43700, νd = 95.10. Glass: FCD100 (HOYA catalog equivalent) — Super ED in OM's drawing. f = −57.1 mm.

Nested close behind L2. With L2 it gives G1b an average Abbe number of 88.35, well above the lower limit of 55 in condition (10). The patent uses that condition to limit lateral colour from the strongly negative subgroup and the colour error that decentering it during adjustment would add (¶0073).

### L4 — Biconvex Positive (S7–S8)

nd = 2.05090, νd = 26.94. Glass: TAFD65 (HOYA catalog equivalent) — Super HR in OM's drawing. f = +54.5 mm.

G1c, just ahead of the stop. It receives the beam diverged by G1b, and its positive power lowers the axial beam height at the stop and in the following groups (¶0027).

### L5–L6 — Cemented Doublet D1 (S10–S12, aspherical S12)

- **L5**, Biconcave Negative: nd = 1.77047, νd = 29.74. Glass: NBFD29 (HOYA catalog equivalent). f = −15.8 mm.
- **L6**, Biconvex Positive: nd = 1.76450, νd = 49.10. Glass: L-LAH91 (OHARA catalog equivalent) — aspherical lens in OM's drawing. f = +22.0 mm.

The pair (combined f ≈ −79.0 mm) opens G2 right behind the stop, where the axial beam is still growing toward its widest at S14. The flint–lanthanum crown pairing corrects axial colour, and the aspherical rear face S12 corrects the spherical aberration of the F1.43 beam.

### L7 — Biconvex Positive (S13–S14)

nd = 1.59282, νd = 68.62. Glass: FCD515 (HOYA catalog equivalent) — ED in OM's drawing. f = +32.9 mm.

The thickest element (7.25 mm). A weak front face and a strong rear face (R = −21.48 mm) carry G2's positive power where the axial beam is widest.

### L8 — Biconvex Positive (S15–S16)

nd = 1.59282, νd = 68.62. Glass: FCD515 (HOYA catalog equivalent) — ED in OM's drawing. f = +37.1 mm.

Closes the fixed positive G2. L7 and L8 give the group most of its positive power in low-dispersion glass, balanced by the flint L5.

### L9 — Negative Meniscus, convex to object (S17–S18)

nd = 1.73037, νd = 32.23. Glass: NBFD32 (HOYA catalog equivalent). f = −34.6 mm, also the printed G3 focal length.

The focusing group: a single 0.8 mm meniscus. The patent prefers a single lens for G3 to keep the focus group light (claim 5, ¶0029).

### L10 — Biconvex Positive, both faces aspherical (S19–S20)

nd = 1.80610, νd = 40.73. Glass: M-NBFD130 (HOYA catalog equivalent; HOYA NBFD13 has the same coordinates) — aspherical lens in OM's drawing. f = +32.1 mm.

G4 sits where the off-axis chief ray is high. There it corrects the positive astigmatism, lateral colour and positive distortion that the negative focusing element generates (¶0029). The two aspherical faces give that correction its higher-order freedom.

### L11 — Negative Meniscus, convex to object (S21–S22)

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.44. Glass: FC5 (HOYA catalog equivalent). f = −73.3 mm.

A weak low-dispersion negative meniscus closing G4 (printed f = +52.51 mm), ahead of the filter.

## Focus Mechanism

Inner focus by the single element L9 (G3), which moves toward the image as the lens focuses closer: 0.3647 mm from infinity to the printed near state, 1.619 mm to OM's 0.25 m minimum focus. Only d16 and d18 change in the printed table, so G1, the stop, G2, G4, the filter and the image plane stay fixed (claim 1, ¶0092).

| Spacing (mm) | Infinity | 0.025× (printed) | 0.109× (derived) |
|---|---|---|---|
| d0, object to S1 | ∞ | 794.6816 | 169.74 |
| d16 | 1.4000 | 1.7647 | 3.019 |
| d18 | 6.0800 | 5.7153 | 4.461 |
| BF | 1.3929 | 1.3929 | 1.3929 |

The printed near state is 874.94 mm object to image, with a paraxial magnification of −0.0250. The patent prints only this one finite state, alongside its aberration plots (Figs. 3 and 5); it is not the close limit. OM specifies a 0.25 m minimum focus and 0.11× (0.22× in 35 mm terms). The model's close end is that 0.25 m: with the patent's own focusing group, L9 is moved until the paraxial image of an object 250 mm from the image plane lands on the fixed image plane, which needs d16 3.019 mm and gives −0.109×, in line with OM's figure. That state is derived, not printed, and finite-distance analyses certify only the printed 0.025× state. Positions between states interpolate the gaps linearly.

## Aspherical Surfaces

The patent's sag equation (¶0081) is `z = (y²/r) / [1 + √(1 − (1 + K)(y/r)²)] + A4·y⁴ + A6·y⁶ + … + A16·y¹⁶`. All three aspheric surfaces have K = 0, so each has a spherical base. The coefficients are used as printed, including the printed zeros:

| Surface | A4 | A6 | A8 | A10 | A12 | A14 | A16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | 3.33950e-5 | 2.82730e-8 | −7.13200e-11 | 1.47300e-14 | −6.10360e-16 | 0 | 0 |
| 19 | 1.88440e-5 | −1.27440e-8 | 5.31540e-10 | 9.63830e-13 | −1.51470e-14 | 1.88150e-17 | 0 |
| 20 | 1.63120e-5 | −1.20380e-7 | 1.84370e-9 | −9.72690e-12 | 3.52060e-14 | −1.80610e-16 | 4.73790e-19 |

S12 is the rear face of L6 near the stop, where it acts mainly on spherical aberration. S19 and S20 are on L10, where the chief ray is high, so they act mainly on field aberrations. Each surface combines a positive fourth-order term with alternating higher orders, so the net departure depends on height and cannot be read from A4 alone. OM marks L6 and L10 as aspherical lenses. Their catalog equivalents, L-LAH91 and M-NBFD130, are mouldable glasses, which is consistent with glass-moulded aspheres. This is an inference from the glass coordinates, not a statement in the patent.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The patent prints no clear apertures and no iris diameter. Semi-diameters are estimated from Figure 1 and are not factory apertures. The figure ends the curved faces of L2, L3, L5, L9 and L11 short of the element edge with a flat land; the model draws these lands too, and the L5, L9 and L11 lands sit where they leave the wide-open beams untouched. The iris diameter, about 17.40 mm, is derived from the design F1.43. The computed paraxial back focus at infinity is 1.3933 mm against the printed BF of 1.3929 mm. The printed value is kept, which leaves a 0.0004 mm defocus.

At full aperture the rear face of L2, ahead of the stop, and the rim of L10, behind it, clip the oblique beam toward the field edge, and the rim of L9 trims it at mid field. At F1.43 and infinity about 60% of the axial beam's rays reach the 29.5° field edge (71% at 26.6°). This mechanical vignetting is set by the estimated apertures. It is a geometric ray fraction, not a measured T-stop or relative illumination. Coatings, tolerances, measured melt dispersion and production aperture dimensions are not modeled.
