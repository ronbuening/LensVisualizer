# OLYMPUS LENS 35mm f/2.8 (Olympus μ[mju:]-II) — Audit Log

## Source

[JP 3610160 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP3610160B2/en), Example 1, supplies the prescription at its printed millimetre scale. The application was filed on 22 March 1996, published as JP H9-258098 A on 3 October 1997, and granted as JP 3610160 B2 on 12 January 2005. The inventors are Toshio Takahashi, Seiji Shimizu and Hideyasu Takato; the assignee is Olympus Corporation.

The numerical prescription is on p. 8, its section is Figure 1, and Figures 7 and 8 show infinity and 35 cm aberrations. Paragraph 0055 gives the stop position; paragraphs 0063–0064 give the asphere equation. Figure 7 labels d/g/F/C, supporting the helium d-line reference.

## Transcription Check

Comparison with the patent page at 400 dpi found no mismatch in the eight radii, seven spacings, four index/Abbe pairs, stop position, conic constant, A2, A4–A10 or back focal distance. The printed 2.49 mm L2-to-L3 space is split into 1.60 mm before the stop and 0.89 mm after it, following ¶0055.

The asphere equation uses K + 1, so the printed K = 0 needs no conversion. The printed A2 = 0 can be omitted without changing the surface. The other coefficients, source indices and Abbe numbers are unchanged. No additional spectral-line indices were copied from glass catalogs.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The stop radius is inferred from f/2.89; the drawing suggests roughly 5.3 mm. Clear radii combine drawing measurements with surface geometry. Figure 1 draws all four elements square-cut, at about 6.9, 6.1, 6.6 and 8.7 mm. L1's rear clear radius is 5.9 mm because its rear surface and L2's front surface cross near 6.25 mm height; a flat land carries L1's edge out to 6.9 mm just ahead of L2's rim, where Figure 1 draws the two elements touching. L2 and L3 keep clear radii of 6.1 and 6.5 mm. L4's front curve ends at 7.3 mm with a flat land to 8.7 mm, and its aspheric rear face runs to the 8.7 mm edge. None of the rims was enlarged to pass rays.

The patent describes whole-unit focus to 35 cm but prints no finite-focus spacings. The rear gap varies from the printed 14.6687 mm to an inferred 18.87 mm, extending all four elements and the stop toward the object by 4.20 mm. Internal separations and stop-to-element distances stay fixed. The intermediate rear gaps are 15.535, 16.506 and 17.607 mm, corresponding approximately to object-to-image distances of 1.40, 0.70 and 0.467 m.

A direct paraxial solution for exactly 350 mm object-to-image distance gives a rear gap near 18.875 mm. The 18.87 mm endpoint corresponds to about 350.3 mm object-to-image distance. Two endpoint positions alone would give 0.264 mm defocus at the inverse-distance midpoint and a maximum of 0.266 mm. The three intermediate positions reduce the four interval-midpoint residuals to 0.012, 0.014, 0.018 and 0.023 mm; the maximum is 0.023 mm between positions 4 and 5. These are inferred spacings, not patent-tabulated finite conjugates or measurements of the camera's focus travel.

The in-air element focal lengths are calculated as +20.69, −16.21, +20.50 and −62.97 mm. The Ohara names below are catalog equivalents for the printed index/Abbe pairs; they do not identify the production supplier or melt. The rear resin grade remains unknown. The patent recommends a plastic rear element and names low-moisture-absorption ZEONEX in ¶0030. It supplies no measured multi-line resin dispersion. No anomalous-dispersion designation was added.

## Product Identification

The 1997 Olympus μ[mju:]-II, also called Stylus Epic, has a fixed 35 mm f/2.8 lens with four elements in four groups and one aspherical element. The [Olympus specifications of 18 February 1997](https://www.olympus.co.jp/jp/news/1997a/nr970218mju2spj.html) confirm the fixed-lens camera, 135 film, 24 × 36 mm frame, 0.35 m minimum focus and programmed exposure endpoints of f/2.8 at 4 seconds and f/11 at 1/1000 second. The [Olympus introduction reproduced by Wanafoto](https://wanafoto.blogspot.com/1997/02/olympus-mju-ii-compact-camera.html) identifies the aspherical element. [135compact](https://135compact.com/135_olympus_mjuII.htm) and [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympus_Stylus_Epic) corroborate the product specifications and alternate name.

The Olympus filing precedes the camera's introduction by about a year. Timing, construction, aperture, asphere and 0.35 m focus give strong circumstantial evidence of an association with this patent family. All five examples share those broad features, however. Examples 1–3 place the stop before L3; Examples 4–5 place it after L3. No maker construction diagram identifies the exact production example. Example 1 remains a plausible design for the camera rather than a proven factory prescription.

[KJ Vogelius's account](https://gear.vogelius.se/-editorials/olympus-mju-ii/index.html) gives three diaphragm blades. This count describes the camera; it does not identify a particular patent example.

### 2026-11-20 second review on the local site

- The live section was compared side by side with Figure 1 at the same millimetre scale, and Figure 1 was measured again on the patent's own scan: 23.4 px/mm along the axis from the 21.0641 mm first-to-last vertex span, with the drawn stop 1.56 mm behind L2 (printed 1.60 mm). Circle fits to r1, r7 and r8 give 23.5–24.3 px/mm across the axis, so drawn heights carry about 2% uncertainty. Read on both sides of the local axis line, which tilts by 2 px across the scan, the drawn edges are L1 6.85–6.98 mm, L2 6.02–6.13 mm, L3 6.53–6.66 mm and L4 8.63–8.79 mm. From the axial position of L4's front land, its curve ends at 7.32 mm. The drawn L4 rear outline follows the printed asphere within about 0.08 mm up to 8.6 mm. Every stored radius lies inside these ranges and the drawn order of heights (L4, L1, L3, L2) is kept, so no semi-diameter was changed.
- Lands: L1's rear land (5.9 to 6.9 mm) and L4's front land (7.3 to 8.7 mm) are both drawn in Figure 1 and cost no light. Wide open at infinity every field receives as much light as with the rims before the rim review, or more (70.3% against 44.5% at 32°), and returning to those rims gains nothing. Both lands stay. The 0.94 sag allowance for the L1–L2 air space is the smallest two-decimal value that admits L1's land; at 0.93 the land would reach past the allowed depth of the 0.92 mm space.
- No surface clips the f/2.89 axial beam or the chief ray to the 21.6 mm image height. Corner bundles lose about 4%, 14% and 10% on one side at surfaces 1–3 at infinity, and 2%, 11% and 7% with the 0.35 m spacing. The 32° half-field, the 28.1° tracing estimate and f/2.89 are unchanged.
- Labels: L1–L4 and the stop between L2 and L3 match Figure 1's L1, L2, S, L3 and L4. The element types agree with the radius signs: L1 is a meniscus convex to the object (+12.0391 / +50.0294), L2 biconcave, L3 biconvex and L4 a meniscus concave to the object (−8.3616 / −11.8346). The patent marks only r8 as aspherical (p. 8), and only surface 8A carries the A suffix. The element focal lengths shown, +20.69, −16.21, +20.5 and −62.97 mm, agree with a paraxial calculation. The patent names no special-dispersion glass, and no element carries such a tag.
- Movement: the focus control runs from infinity on the left to 35 cm on the right. The rear gap grows steadily through 14.6687, 15.535, 16.506, 17.6069 and 18.87 mm, so all four elements and the iris move together 4.20 mm toward the object, as the patent's whole-unit focusing describes; the movement view shows the same 4.20 mm for every element. The last position focuses at 0.350 m from object to image, matching Olympus's 0.35 m minimum focus.
- Glass and colour: L1–L3 resolve to Ohara catalog curves and L4 uses an estimate from its Abbe number, so no element is shown with constant dispersion. Figure 7's paraxial C, F and g foci lie +0.053, −0.082 and −0.082 mm from d; the model gives +0.062, −0.078 and −0.073 mm, within the reading error. The resin values nd 1.52542 / νd 55.78 recur in Examples 2 and 3 and in the reference example; no vendor material matches them.
- The patentee printed on the front page, オリンパス株式会社, is Olympus Corporation, as recorded. Olympus's 18 February 1997 specifications list the lens as "オリンパスレンズ35mm F2.8 4群4枚", which supports the display name.

## Remaining Questions

- The exact production example, glass suppliers, melts and rear resin grade remain unidentified.
- The inferred finite-focus spacings do not establish the camera's mechanical focus law or agreement with Figure 8's near-focus aberrations.
- The retained 64° field falls slightly inside the patent's 21.6 mm image height. Corner bundles remain partly vignetted despite clear chief rays.
- Blade curvature is undocumented.
