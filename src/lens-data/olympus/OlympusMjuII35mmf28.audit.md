# OLYMPUS 35mm f/2.8 (Olympus μ[mju:]-II) — Audit Log

## Source

[JP 3610160 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP3610160B2/en), Example 1, supplies the prescription at its printed millimetre scale. The application was filed on 22 March 1996, published as JP H9-258098 A on 3 October 1997, and granted as JP 3610160 B2 on 12 January 2005. The inventors are Toshio Takahashi, Seiji Shimizu and Hideyasu Takato; the assignee is Olympus Corporation.

The numerical prescription is on p. 8, its section is Figure 1, and Figures 7 and 8 show infinity and 35 cm aberrations. Paragraph 0055 gives the stop position; paragraphs 0063–0064 give the asphere equation. Figure 7 labels d/g/F/C, supporting the helium d-line reference.

## Transcription Check

Comparison with the patent page at 400 dpi found no mismatch in the eight radii, seven spacings, four index/Abbe pairs, stop position, conic constant, A2, A4–A10 or back focal distance. The printed 2.49 mm L2-to-L3 space is split into 1.60 mm before the stop and 0.89 mm after it, following ¶0055.

The asphere equation uses K + 1, so the printed K = 0 needs no conversion. The printed A2 = 0 can be omitted without changing the surface. The other coefficients, source indices and Abbe numbers are unchanged. No additional spectral-line indices were copied from glass catalogs.

A check of the surface geometry finds no edge-thickness or overlap errors. The aperture remains within 3% of the stated f-number, with no rim, tracing or iris limits. The image circle covers the stated format. At 32.0° the chief ray reaches 21.51 mm, about 99% of the 21.65 mm full-frame half-diagonal; the 32.2° corner chief ray is clear. The patent's 21.6 mm image height is reached near 32.1°. At that height there is no axial clipping or blocked chief ray, but one-sided corner-bundle clipping is about 4%, 14% and 10% at surfaces 1–3.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The stop radius is inferred from f/2.89; the drawing suggests roughly 5.3 mm. Clear radii combine drawing measurements with surface geometry. Figure 1 suggests outer radii near 6.9, 6.1, 6.5 and 8.7 mm, with roughly 0.1–0.3 mm uncertainty. L1's rear clear radius is 5.9 mm because its rear surface and L2's front surface cross near 6.25 mm height, despite the overlap depicted in Figure 1. L2 and L3 use their drawn outer boundaries because separate curve endpoints are indistinct. L4 has inferred clear radii of 7.1 and 7.8 mm and opaque flat lands to 8.7 mm, following the drawn step. Its rear reading spans 7.7–7.9 mm; the bevel shape is unknown. None of the rims was enlarged to pass rays.

The patent describes whole-unit focus to 35 cm but prints no finite-focus spacings. The rear gap varies from the printed 14.6687 mm to an inferred 18.87 mm, extending all four elements and the stop toward the object by 4.20 mm. Internal separations and stop-to-element distances stay fixed. The intermediate rear gaps are 15.535, 16.506 and 17.607 mm, corresponding approximately to object-to-image distances of 1.40, 0.70 and 0.467 m.

A direct paraxial solution for exactly 350 mm object-to-image distance gives a rear gap near 18.875 mm. The stored 18.87 mm endpoint gives about 350.30 mm, or 350.33 mm when the infinity rounding offset is held fixed. Two endpoint positions alone gave 0.264 mm defocus at the inverse-distance midpoint and a maximum of 0.266 mm. The three intermediate positions reduce the four interval-midpoint residuals to 0.012, 0.014, 0.018 and 0.023 mm; the maximum is 0.023 mm near relative focus setting 0.878, below 0.05 mm. These are inferred spacings, not patent-tabulated finite conjugates or measurements of the camera's focus travel.

The in-air element focal lengths are calculated as +20.69, −16.21, +20.50 and −62.97 mm. The Ohara names below are catalog equivalents for the printed index/Abbe pairs; they do not identify the production supplier or melt. The rear resin grade remains unknown. The patent recommends a plastic rear element and names low-moisture-absorption ZEONEX in ¶0030. It supplies no measured multi-line resin dispersion. No anomalous-dispersion designation was added.

## Product Identification

The 1997 Olympus μ[mju:]-II, also called Stylus Epic, has a fixed 35 mm f/2.8 lens with four elements in four groups and one aspherical element. The [Olympus specifications of 18 February 1997](https://www.olympus.co.jp/jp/news/1997a/nr970218mju2spj.html) confirm the fixed-lens camera, 135 film, 24 × 36 mm frame, 0.35 m minimum focus and programmed exposure endpoints of f/2.8 at 4 seconds and f/11 at 1/1000 second. The [Olympus introduction reproduced by Wanafoto](https://wanafoto.blogspot.com/1997/02/olympus-mju-ii-compact-camera.html) identifies the aspherical element. [135compact](https://135compact.com/135_olympus_mjuII.htm) and [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympus_Stylus_Epic) corroborate the product specifications and alternate name.

The Olympus filing precedes the camera's introduction by about a year. Timing, construction, aperture, asphere and 0.35 m focus give moderate-to-strong evidence of an association with this patent family. All five examples share those broad features, however. Examples 1–3 place the stop before L3; Examples 4–5 place it after L3. No maker construction diagram identifies the exact production example. Example 1 remains a plausible design for the camera rather than a proven factory prescription.

[Raymond Yee's teardown](https://lostlenscaps.com/project-02-olympus-mju-ii/) documents one set of three blades shared by the shutter and aperture, forming a triangular opening. [KJ Vogelius's account](https://gear.vogelius.se/-editorials/olympus-mju-ii/index.html) also gives three diaphragm blades. This count describes the camera; it does not identify a particular patent example.

## Corrections, 2026-11-20

- The prescription was made visible under the product name, OLYMPUS 35mm f/2.8 (Olympus μ[mju:]-II), and its name and key were replaced by the product identity. The data declaration and default export now share the name `LENS_DATA`.
- Compressed notes were replaced by plain explanations of the source, scale, stop, rims, image plane and focus. The subtitle now reads “JP 3610160 B2, Example 1 — Olympus / Takahashi, Shimizu & Takato.” The specifications show “4 ELEMENTS / 4 GROUPS,” “DESIGN f = 33.91 mm,” “DESIGN F/2.89” and “1 ASPHERICAL SURFACE,” replacing statements about the file's status with optical facts.
- The fixed-camera mount and 0.35 m marketed minimum focus were added from Olympus's specifications. Olympus, the 135 full-frame format, 4/4 counts, marketed 35 mm f/2.8, design 33.91 mm f/2.89, f/11 endpoint and 0.35 m focus limit were already correct. The stop choices remain f/2.89, f/4, f/5.6, f/8 and f/11; they illustrate apertures within the camera's programmed range rather than documented manual detents.
- The four calculated element focal lengths and descriptions of their optical roles were added. L4's type was simplified to Negative Meniscus, with its rear asphere described separately. Four unsupported index-reference notes were removed because Figure 7 provides the d-line evidence. No cemented pairs exist.
- Previously unidentified glass labels were replaced by the following catalog equivalents. The rear label was expanded to identify a cyclo-olefin polymer of Zeonex type while leaving its exact grade unknown. All printed index/Abbe values remain unchanged.

All three matches lie within 0.0001 in index and 0.15 in Abbe number. L3's index difference is −0.000004, correcting an earlier upper bound of 0.000002.

- The fixed final gap was replaced by five whole-unit focus positions, preserving the printed infinity gap and using the inferred finite-focus values above. The two original focus settings, 0 and 1, became 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1 to reduce interpolation defocus. The focus description now explains the real 35 cm focus limit and inferred spacings; its stated extension is rounded to 4.20 mm.
- Source identification, element roles, glass uncertainty, focus, asphere and optical limits were documented. The Wanafoto citation now points directly to the article rather than its monthly archive; obsolete access statements and unrelated commentary were removed.
- Three aperture blades were added from the teardown, with corroboration from Vogelius. Blade roundedness remains unspecified. Component brackets L1–L4 were added using the names in patent ¶0054 and the corresponding surface pairs, following the four air-separated elements.
- The analysis and this record were rewritten in plain technical language, and calculated prose values were rounded to sensible precision. The full audit record now uses the source, transcription, first-order, inferred-value, product, correction and remaining-question sections.

No change was made to scale, radii, thicknesses, indices, Abbe numbers, asphere coefficients, stop position, internal gaps, rims, field, focus positions or image plane during the wording and metadata changes. The earlier focus additions also preserved all infinity values. After all corrections, EFL remains 33.91 mm, aperture f/2.89 and paraxial back focal distance 14.668 mm, with 21.06 mm first-to-last surface length and 35.73 mm total infinity track.

## Remaining Questions

- The exact production example, glass suppliers, melts and rear resin grade remain unidentified.
- L1's unequal clear radii and the L1/L2 overlap in Figure 1 need closer drawing comparison, as do the indistinct L2/L3 endpoints, L4's 7.7–7.9 mm rear clear-radius reading and its bevel shape.
- The inferred finite-focus spacings do not establish the camera's mechanical focus law or agreement with Figure 8's near-focus aberrations.
- The retained 64° field falls slightly inside the patent's 21.6 mm image height. Corner bundles remain partly vignetted despite clear chief rays.
- The cited blade descriptions do not supply a numerical blade-roundedness value. No separate production image-circle diameter, teleconverter compatibility or additional optical attachment is documented by these sources; those optional values remain absent.
