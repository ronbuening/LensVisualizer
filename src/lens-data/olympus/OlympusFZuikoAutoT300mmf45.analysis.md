## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 3,819,253  
**Filed:** 1973-05-11  
**Granted:** 1974-06-25  
**Priority:** 1972-05-15 (JP 47-47166)  
**Inventor:** Keiichi Itoh  
**Assignee:** Olympus Optical Co., Ltd.  
**Title:** Telephoto lens system  
**Example details:** The patent's only numerical example, columns 1–2, repeated unchanged in claim 1; section in Fig. 1, aberration plots in Figs. 2A–2D. The example is printed at f = 300, so no scaling is applied.

This is a patent design associated with the Olympus OM Zuiko Auto-T 300mm f/4.5, not a confirmed factory prescription. The lens was sold first as the F.Zuiko Auto-T, the F marking its six elements, and later, multicoated, as the Zuiko Auto-T. Both versions are listed at six elements in four groups, and the example fits either. The association is strong and rests on six points:

1. The patent's four components, a biconvex lens, a biconcave lens and a cemented positive meniscus in front and a cemented negative meniscus behind, make six elements in four groups, the marketed construction.
2. The example is printed at f = 300 and F 1:4.5, and its astigmatism and distortion plots run to 4°, half of the lens's 8° diagonal angle of view.
3. The construction drawing on the mir.com.my Zuiko pages shows the same four groups with the same shapes and proportions. Scaled to the patent's 100 mm space between the groups, the drawing's front group is about 36 mm long against the model's 34.1 mm and its front element about 70 mm across against 71.5 mm, and its rear doublet is about 0.42 times the front element's diameter, against 0.415 in the model.
4. The model's front vertex lies 224.9 mm ahead of the image. The lens's 181 mm barrel plus the 46 mm OM flange distance gives about 227 mm.
5. Extended as a unit to the 3.5 m minimum focus, the example reaches 0.112×, a 321 × 214 mm field. The lens's specifications give a 21 × 32 cm minimum field.
6. The May 1972 priority falls at the launch of the OM system in 1972, and reference listings date the lens to the system's first years.

The PhotonsToPhotos Optical Bench makes the same identification in the title of its transcription of this example. Olympus published no prescription, so the production radii cannot be confirmed, and the stop position and clear apertures come from that transcription and the patent's drawing rather than from its table.

Retail and reference listings give f/4.5 to f/32, focusing by straight helicoid to 3.5 m, 72 mm filters, a built-in hood, a tripod collar and a weight of about 1.0–1.1 kg.

## Optical Architecture

A telephoto of six spherical elements in four groups. The positive front group, L1 to L3b (f = +180.75 mm), is separated by a 100 mm air space from the negative rear group, the cemented pair L4a–L4b (f = −142.79 mm), which lengthens the front group's focal length by a factor of 1.66. The power sequence is positive, negative, positive (cemented), then negative (cemented). There are two cemented interfaces, S6 and S9, no aspheric surfaces and no cover plate.

The patent numbers components rather than elements, L1 to L4, and Fig. 1 brackets the two cemented ones. The single lenses L1 and L2 keep those names; the elements of the cemented component L3 are L3a and L3b, and those of the rear component L4 are L4a and L4b, front to rear.

The patent's aim is a telephoto ratio, the distance from the first surface to the image divided by the focal length, within 0.8, against 0.85 to 0.9 for most telephoto lenses of the time. The example's ratio is 0.750: the image lies 224.89 mm behind S1 for a focal length of 299.85 mm. The patent explains that a short telephoto lens needs a strongly positive front group and a strongly negative rear group, which raises the Petzval sum and the secondary spectrum. It answers the first with a small air space between L1 and L2 and the second with a special glass in L3b. The model's Petzval sum is 1.23 × 10⁻⁴ mm⁻¹, a Petzval radius of about 8,160 mm, or 27 times the focal length.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.1. Glass: S-FSL5 (OHARA) equivalent, a fluor crown (catalog νd 70.24). f = +136.0 mm.

The front element and the main positive power of the front group, 13 mm thick, with its stronger curve (R1 = 89.944 mm) toward the object. A low-dispersion crown keeps down the primary colour generated by the lens's strongest positive component.

### L2 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.69350, νd = 53.3. Glass: LAC13 (HOYA) equivalent, a lanthanum crown. f = −217.8 mm.

L2 sits 4 mm behind L1, and its front face (R3 = −234.162 mm) nearly follows L1's rear face (R2 = −240.523 mm), so the air space between them is a thin meniscus. The patent states that keeping this space, d2, small reduces the front group's Petzval sum and corrects spherical aberration at every wavelength.

### L3a and L3b — Cemented Positive Meniscus

- **L3a, Biconvex Positive:** nd = 1.61800, νd = 63.4. Glass: PCD4 (HOYA) equivalent, a dense phosphate crown. f = +134.5 mm.
- **L3b, Biconcave Negative:** nd = 1.73980, νd = 31.7. Glass: BPH50 (OHARA), the nearest catalog row (1.73999 / 31.71); the patent calls it an abnormal-dispersion glass of KzSF type. f = −191.7 mm.

The pair is convex to the object (R5 = 95.45 mm, R7 = 184.817 mm) and forms a weak positive meniscus (f = +399.7 mm) that closes the front group 0.1 mm behind L2. The patent places the KzSF-type glass in the negative element on the image side of this component, where it serves to minimize chromatic aberration. Short flints of this kind have a lower blue partial dispersion than ordinary flints of the same Abbe number, which reduces the secondary spectrum of a long lens; that reading of the patent's choice is an interpretation, since the patent prints no partial dispersion.

### L4a and L4b — Cemented Negative Meniscus

- **L4a, Biconcave Negative:** nd = 1.71300, νd = 54.0. Glass: LAC8 (HOYA) equivalent, a lanthanum crown. f = −43.9 mm.
- **L4b, Biconvex Positive:** nd = 1.59270, νd = 35.6. Glass: FF5 (HOYA) equivalent, a flint (catalog νd 35.45). f = +67.2 mm.

The rear group is a single cemented meniscus concave to the object (R8 = −41.868 mm, R10 = −57.411 mm), f = −142.8 mm, standing 100 mm behind the front group. L4a is the strongest element in the lens. As in any negative achromat, the stronger negative element is the crown and the weaker positive element the flint, so the group corrects its own colour while it supplies the telephoto power.

## Glass Identification

The patent gives d-line indices and Abbe numbers. Five elements have catalog rows that match both values to the printed precision; the supplier of the production glass is not established.

| Element | Patent nd / νd | Catalog equivalent | Catalog nd / νd | Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | 1.48749 / 70.1 | S-FSL5 (OHARA) | 1.48749 / 70.24 | Fluor crown |
| L2 | 1.69350 / 53.3 | LAC13 (HOYA) | 1.69350 / 53.34 | Lanthanum crown |
| L3a | 1.61800 / 63.4 | PCD4 (HOYA) | 1.61800 / 63.40 | Dense phosphate crown |
| L3b | 1.73980 / 31.7 | BPH50 (OHARA), nearest row | 1.73999 / 31.71 | KzSF-type anomalous-dispersion flint, named in the patent |
| L4a | 1.71300 / 54.0 | LAC8 (HOYA) | 1.71300 / 53.94 | Lanthanum crown |
| L4b | 1.59270 / 35.6 | FF5 (HOYA) | 1.59270 / 35.45 | Flint |

No catalog row reproduces L3b's pair exactly. The trace uses the nearest, OHARA BPH50, 0.0002 higher in nd; its catalog partial dispersion is close to the normal line, so the anomalous partial dispersion the patent names is not represented. Fig. 2A gives a check on the colour. On axis, the patent plots the C-line focus about 0.24 mm behind the d-line focus, the F-line about 0.15 mm ahead and the g-line about 0.08 mm behind. With the catalog glasses the model gives +0.27, −0.14 and +0.13 mm. Traced on Abbe numbers alone, the g-line would fall 0.34 mm behind. A partial-dispersion deviation of about −0.005 in L3b, within the range of short flints, would close the remaining g-line difference; the patent prints no value, so the model leaves it out.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent names no focusing method and prints only the infinity state, so the model stays at infinity and no gap varies. The lens focuses to 3.5 m with a straight helicoid, which fits a lens that extends as a unit but does not show which elements move. Extending this example as a unit to 3.5 m from object to image would take 33.6 mm of travel and give 0.112×, the magnification of the published 21 × 32 cm minimum field; that state is not modeled.

## Aperture Stop and Clear Apertures

The patent neither prints nor draws a stop; Fig. 1 shows the lenses alone, with d7 marked across the empty space. The model follows the PhotonsToPhotos Optical Bench transcription, which puts the stop at the midpoint of d7, 50 mm behind S7, and lists a clear diameter for every surface. The stop radius, 18.70 mm, follows from the f/4.5 entrance pupil of 66.63 mm; PhotonsToPhotos lists a stop diameter of 38.38 mm, 2.6 % larger.

Fig. 1 is drawn close to scale. Its vertex spacings give one scale through the front group and across d7, within 2 %, though the thin rear doublet is drawn somewhat thin, and arc fits to the drawn surfaces give a similar scale across the axis. Read at that scale, the figure agrees with the PhotonsToPhotos diameters within about 1 % for L1 and the front cemented pair, and the model uses half of each. The figure draws L2 with bevelled corners and its front face ending 1.4 mm inside L1's rim, so S3 is 34.6 mm; L2's rear face, 32.96 mm, matches the figure. The rear doublet is drawn about 7 % smaller than the listed diameters. It keeps the listed values, which set the light at the edge of the field and match the proportions of the mir.com.my drawing. With these rims the 4° field edge receives 85 % of the axial light.

The mir.com.my drawing does not settle where the diaphragm sits. Its barrel narrows behind the front group, and a pair of thin plates about 70 mm behind S7 may be the diaphragm housing, but the drawing is too small to read reliably. A stop there instead of at the midpoint would leave the focal length, the f-number and the axial beam unchanged; only the off-axis vignetting and the pupil positions would differ.

## Model Scope and Limitations

| Quantity | Patent | Model |
|---|---|---|
| Focal length | 300 | 299.845 mm |
| F-number | 4.5 | 4.5 |
| Half-field | 4° (Figs. 2C, 2D) | 4.0° |
| Back focus | not printed | 84.787 mm, paraxial |
| S1 to image | not printed | 224.887 mm |
| Telephoto ratio | within 0.8 (claimed aim) | 0.750 |

- The prescription is the patent's, not a confirmed factory one.
- The stop position and every semi-diameter but S3's come from the PhotonsToPhotos transcription, and S3 from Fig. 1; the patent prints neither a stop nor diameters.
- The image plane is the computed paraxial back focus.
- The model is static at infinity and says nothing about performance at 3.5 m.
- Colour away from the d-line rests on catalog equivalents for five elements and on the nearest catalog row for L3b, whose anomalous partial dispersion is not represented.
- At the 4° half-field the image height is 21.16 mm, 0.5 mm short of the 21.65 mm format corner.
