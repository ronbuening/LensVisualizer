# OLYMPUS G.ZUIKO AUTO-W 25mm f/2.8

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP S39-18282 Y1, Japanese utility-model publication, “Wide-angle lens.”

**Inventors:** Saburo Yanagawa; Jihei Nakagawa, in source order (柳川三郎; 中川治平). The Latin forms are supported by bilingual technical-paper bylines.

**Applicant:** Olympus Optical Co., Ltd. (オリンパス光学工業株式会社), the historical applicant.

**Published:** 29 June 1964.

**Application Number:** 実願昭38-64986. The cover gives 21 September 1959 with a prior-patent-application-date-adopted notation; the complete parent-application history is unresolved.

**Example details:** Figure 1, uniformly scaled ×25 from f = 1.

The probable production association is the Pen F/FT/FV G.Zuiko Auto-W 25mm f/2.8 shown in Ponder & Best's May 1968 literature, page B-6. The seven-element/five-group count, f/2.8 aperture, unusual component sequence and long front-to-rear separation support that association. The patent describes about 60° full field; the product sheet states 62°. The same sheet distinguishes the 25mm f/4 as five elements/five groups and the 20mm f/3.5 as seven/six. Its schematic section supports the architecture but supplies no exact radii or clear apertures. The identification remains probable and does not establish every production version or measured factory performance.

## Optical Architecture

The seven all-spherical elements form five air-separated components, 2+1+2+1+1. The patent numbers them first to fifth member (第1–第5部材); the diagram brackets them C1–C5 with their power signs, and marks the two cemented pairs D1 and D2. A strongly negative cemented front component precedes a 32.075 mm airspace. The rear section contains a positive plano-convex singlet, a cemented positive/negative pair, the iris cavity, a negative meniscus and a positive rear meniscus. Cemented interfaces R2 and R7 occur once in the prescription. R5 is a true plane. Both R11 and R12 are negative.

Uniform multiplication of the source lengths by 25 gives EFL 24.999684 mm, infinity BFD 25.840429 mm and first-to-last-vertex length 54.675 mm. The image plane is 80.515429 mm from R1. The source does not print a terminal image distance; the modeled distance is the calculated Gaussian infinity plane. The nominal-EFL residual is −0.001264%; the overall scale is not adjusted to eliminate it.

Component powers are −0.016165, +0.031715, −0.012414, −0.017195 and +0.036422 mm⁻¹. The pre-stop cemented pair has weak net negative power despite its positive front element. BFD exceeds EFL by 0.840745 mm, consistent with the retrofocus architecture. Principal planes are H1 = +36.653495 mm from R1 and H2 = +0.840745 mm from R12, with positive distances directed toward the image.

## Source Readings and Numerical Consistency

The normalized prescription uses R1 = 3.147, R3 = 0.7743, R4 = 0.9043, N2 = 1.5163 and N6 = 1.7283. The scan's typeface makes 3 and 8 difficult to distinguish. Repeated tables and prose support these readings, including the explicit R4 = 0.9043 in the prose. The R3/R4 values are source-reading corrections, not patent errata or adjustments to force the nominal focal length.

The normalized spherical Seidel coefficients A3–A6 are −1.360885, 4.183134, 0.447209 and 2.880777, compared with printed −1.361, 4.184, 0.4472 and 2.881. The calculated sum A is 1.200100 versus printed 1.201. Petzval terms P3 = −0.439752 and P4 = 0.461781 agree closely with printed −0.4398 and 0.4618; the sum is 0.041627796 versus printed 0.04163. Small last-digit residuals remain with the rounded prescription. Numerical reproduction of the other Seidel columns and d/g aberration plots is unresolved.

## Glass Identification

The source table gives N and V without an explicit spectral reference. Figure 2 labels d/g curves, but that alone does not establish the table's reference line. The model uses a nominal d-line convention; the listed values are not authenticated d-line measurements.

The catalog coordinate equivalents listed above supply dispersion curves for all seven elements, including secondary-spectrum and chromatic calculations. The model retains the patent's printed N/V anchors under the nominal d convention rather than replacing them with catalog coordinates. None of the equivalents establishes a historical melt or production supplier. Reference-wavelength calculations at 587.5618 nm retain the spectral-reference uncertainty, and photopic calculations remain qualified by the catalog proxies rather than establishing production chromatic performance.

## Stop, Clear Apertures and Geometry

Figure 1 places the iris in the R8–R9 airspace without a numerical split or opening. The source total of 5.95 mm is represented as 2.975 + 2.975 mm, placing the inferred stop 47.65 mm from R1. Exact axial marginal-ray calibration gives a 4.701091 mm stop radius at f/2.8 and a 4.464229 mm entrance-beam radius. The paraxial pupil radius is 4.486227 mm, corresponding to f/2.786271; the finite-ray calibration sets the modeled f/2.8 aperture.

The paraxial entrance pupil is +30.569851 mm from R1. The exit pupil is −7.199477 mm from R12; its radius referred to the nominal entrance beam is 5.899983 mm. These quantities depend on the inferred iris position.

Surface semi-diameters, excluding the iris, are 18.1, 18.1, 14, 6.79, 6.79, 6.32, 6.32, 5.1, 6, 6, 6 and 6 mm. They are estimated from Figure 1 and floor-checked by real-ray trace, not factory dimensions. The front component's reduced rear rim, like the chamfer Figure 1 draws on its concave face, is represented by a straight rim join; R1/R2 sit 0.05 mm above the 15 mm half-frame corner chief ray. The second cemented component steps down to a 5.1 mm R8 rim, following the chamfered rear corner in the figure. L3 and the R6/R7 junction stay above the drawn heights because the f/2.8 axial ray needs them; the last two singlets keep square-cut rims. The corrected f/2.8 axial marginal ray reaches 6.786984 mm at R4 and 6.313401 mm at R6, clearing the existing rims by 0.003016 and 0.006599 mm. The brochure's 43 mm accessory thread is a mechanical dimension.

Minimum shared-band edge thickness is 0.880371 mm (L1), maximum spherical rim angle is 46.3221° (R3), and maximum shared-air-gap sag intrusion is 59.7847% (R8–R9 across the iris). The default geometry allowance remains 90%. Ordinary peripheral vignetting remains part of the model.

## Focus Mechanism

The whole optical assembly moves toward the object on a helicoid, with all internal spacings fixed. The modeled close endpoint is 0.25 m from the image plane, approximating the brochure's 10 inches (0.254 m). The patent supplies the infinity prescription; close-focus spacing is calculated from its paraxial matrix.

For the air-to-air system matrix [A B; C D], first-to-last-vertex length T = 54.675 mm and rear gap b, the object distance from R1 is s = −(B + D b)/(A + C b). Solving s + T + b = 250 mm gives b = 29.359093 mm. The infinity gap is 25.840429 mm, so the whole-lens travel is 3.518665 mm. The close object is 165.965907 mm ahead of R1 and paraxial magnification is approximately −0.1407. Intermediate spacing is interpolated; the source does not tabulate finite-focus performance.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The patent's 30° half-field and the product's 31° half-field are distinct. The modeled half-frame format uses a 30 mm diagonal as a computational reference. The runtime analysis half-field is 27.845612°; with the 18.1 mm R1/R2 rims, the stop-centered ray reaches the 15.00 mm format corner near 31.4° without clipping. Chief-ray admission alone does not describe photometric coverage; wide-open geometric transmission at that corner is about 41.7%.

The modeled aperture settings are f/2.8, 4, 5.6, 8, 11 and 16. The source explicitly gives f/2.8 and plots f/4; the higher values do not establish the factory aperture range. The current on-axis aperture assessment clears the stated f/2.8 beam. Off-axis clipping remains possible and does not imply malformed glass geometry.

Earlier diffraction calculations considered image heights 0, 7.5 and 15 mm, frequencies 0, 5, 10, 20 and 40 lp/mm, and separate Gaussian and best-axial image planes. Their convergence and approximately −0.101146 mm best-axial offset applied to the preceding radius transcription; those results do not characterize this corrected prescription. Current MTF, finite-focus aberrations and full-field transmission remain unresolved. Numerical convergence cannot establish unknown manufacturing, coatings, full dispersion or sensor-stack behavior, and does not constitute measured production-lens performance.
