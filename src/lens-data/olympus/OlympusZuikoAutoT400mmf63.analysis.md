# OLYMPUS ZUIKO AUTO-T 400mm f/6.3

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 3,864,027  
**Title:** Telephoto Lens System Having Small Overall Length  
**Inventor:** Kenichi Harada  
**Assignee:** Olympus Optical Co., Ltd.  
**Priority:** 1972-05-10, Japanese application 47-46086  
**Filed:** 1973-05-08  
**Granted:** 1975-02-04  
**Example details:** Table 1, repeated in claim 2; section in Fig. 1; infinity curves in Figs. 4a–4d; 1/10× curves in Figs. 5a–5c. All lengths are the patent's f = 1 values multiplied by 400.

The patent describes a normalized family, not a named product. Each of its three examples prints f = 1, F/6.3, 2ω = 6°10′ and a telephoto ratio t = B/f of 0.75, where B runs from the front vertex to the image surface. None prints a back focus or any length in millimetres. The abstract gives the field as 6°11′; Table 1's 6°10′ is kept here. Example 1 is modeled because it is the only example with five elements in five groups: Example 2 cements L2 to L3 and Example 3 cements L4 to L5, each giving five elements in four groups.

The link to the production OM lens is an inference, not a factory identification. Three independent comparisons support the 400 mm scale:

1. **Image height.** Fig. 5 labels the edge of the field y′ = 0.0054f and the 0.7 zone y′ = 0.0378f. The first is a slip for 0.054f: 0.0378f is 0.7 × 0.054f, and the trace gives 0.0541f at 3°05′. At 400 mm, 0.054f is 21.6 mm, the half-diagonal of the 24 × 36 mm frame. At 300 mm it would be 16.2 mm.
2. **Close focus.** The patent's close state is 1/10×. With the whole lens extended, 1/10× at the 400 mm scale falls at 4.999 m object to image, the catalogue's 5 m minimum focus. At 300 mm it would be 3.75 m.
3. **Length.** The printed t × 400 gives 300 mm, and the catalogue's 255 mm barrel plus the 46 mm OM flange distance gives 301 mm.

The drawn section agrees. A 1973 Zuiko catalogue page draws five separate elements: biconvex, meniscus and plano-type at the front, then two at the rear. With the drawing scaled at 1.98 px/mm from L1's front, the model's L3 rear face, S7, S9 and S10 land within 1–2 px of it, or about 1 mm, and L1's outline measures about 34.6 mm semi-diameter against the model's 34.29 mm.

The same page's text does not agree. It prints "6 elements in 5 groups", with 1300 g, 255 mm length, 80 mm diameter, 72 mm filter, 5 m minimum focus and f/6.3–32. That count is not explained by the page's own drawing. An earlier catalogue scan lists 5 elements in 5 groups and 1400 g, the OM-10 FC manual lists the Zuiko MC 400mm F6.3 at 5 elements in 5 groups and 1300 g, and retail listings give 5 elements in 5 groups. The model has five elements in five groups, from the patent and the drawn section. No redesign is claimed, and the model does not distinguish single-coated from MC production.

## Optical Architecture

The design is an all-spherical telephoto of five air-spaced singlets, with the power sequence positive-negative-negative-negative-positive. A front group F (L1–L3, f = +251.2 mm) and a rear group R (L4–L5, f = −257.3 mm) sit either side of the 142.4 mm diaphragm space, which is 70 % of the 203.7 mm track. The patent also names three components: I (L1), II (L2 and L3) and III (L4 and L5). These are functional divisions, not cemented groups. L2 and L3 act together as a negative lens (f = −200.0 mm), and the patent says the same of L4 and L5.

The patent's aim is a telephoto ratio of 0.75 from common glass. It notes that CaF2, LaSF01 or LaSF02 had reached 0.65 to 0.76, and that ordinary FK glass had kept the ratio above 0.8 because of the Petzval sum and chromatic aberration (column 1). Example 1's Petzval sum prints as −0.157, and the prescription gives the same value.

**Image plane.** The last gap is the computed paraxial back focus, 96.63 mm, because the patent prints none. The image plane is therefore 300.36 mm behind the front vertex, and B/f = 0.7509 rounds to the printed 0.75. This plane is the origin of the patent's own curves. Fig. 4a's d curve starts at zero on axis, and the values traced from this plane agree with the plots at 3°05′: Δm −0.00028f against about −0.00026f in Fig. 4c, ΔS +0.00008f against about +0.00008f, and distortion +0.35 % against about +0.38 % in Fig. 4d.

**Stop.** The stop position is inferred; the patent draws the diaphragm D but prints no position. The mark in Fig. 1 sits at 0.683 of d6, and the model places the stop at 0.68, splitting the space into 96.83 and 45.57 mm. Fig. 4c confirms this separately: the traced field curves fit the plot only for a stop between 0.67 and 0.69 of d6. The stop radius, 13.84 mm, follows from a paraxial entrance pupil of F/6.3. The real marginal ray at that radius gives a working f-number of 6.35.

**Rims.** The semi-diameters are inferred from the Fig. 1 outline and are not factory clear apertures: L1 34.29 mm, which is 8 % above the 31.75 mm entrance-pupil radius; L2 and L3 30.00 mm; L4 and L5 18.21 mm. Both faces of L5 share one height because the figure draws its edge square. The full axial beam clears every rim, with the smallest margin 1.78 mm at S3. Off axis, the rims of L1 and L2 set the vignetting together, leaving 90.7 % of the light at 1.5° and 74.8 % at the 3.1° corner.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.1. Glass: S-FSL5 (OHARA, catalog equivalent; the patent names FK5). f = +120.1 mm.

The biconvex first component carries most of the front group's positive power, with its stronger curvature facing the object. The air lens between r2 and L2's r3 is the subject of condition (1), which the patent ties to chromatic aberration and meridional field curvature. The width of that air space, d2, is condition (3), which governs spherical aberration.

### L2 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.7495, νd = 35.2. Glass: J-LAF7 (HIKARI, catalog equivalent; the patent names LaF7). f = −243.2 mm.

This high-index negative meniscus is concave to the object. Strengthening L1 leaves spherical aberration undercorrected, and the patent answers that through condition (5): keeping |f3| greater than |f2| raises L2's diverging power, which corrects it.

### L3 — Plano-concave Negative

nd = 1.5927, νd = 35.6. Glass: FF5 (HOYA, catalog equivalent; the patent names F16). f = −1163.5 mm.

L3 is weak, and its rear surface is flat (r6 = ∞). The patent stresses that L2 and L3 are not one negative lens divided in two: L3's power is particularly sensitive to the meridional field curvature Δm at short object distances, and condition (4) uses that sensitivity. Its flat rear face is also the rF of condition (2), which is why that condition's value is exactly 0.

### L4 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.6510, νd = 56.2. Glass: S-LAL54Q (OHARA, catalog equivalent; the patent names LAK04). f = −77.6 mm.

L4 is the first element behind the stop and the strongest in the lens, with a steeply concave front surface and a nearly flat rear. It supplies the rear group's negative power, which produces the telephoto effect. Its front surface is the rB of condition (2), which the patent uses to correct the Petzval sum and sagittal field curvature.

### L5 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.60342, νd = 38.0. Glass: F5 (SCHOTT, catalog equivalent; the patent names F5). f = +134.8 mm.

L5 has a nearly flat front and a strongly convex rear surface, and the patent places it close behind L4 so that the pair works as a negative lens. The rear group reverses the usual crown and flint pairing, with the crown-type glass negative and the flint positive. Since the group is net negative, that arrangement is what achromatizes it; this reading is an inference, as the patent does not state it.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent prints n and ν without naming a spectral line. They are read as d-line values, an inference supported by Fig. 4a: its d curve starts from zero on axis, and the traced d-line spherical aberration follows it, at −0.00054f at F/9 against about −0.0005f on the plot.

Column 3 says the five lenses may be made of FK5, LaF7, F16, LAK04 and F5, and describes them as inexpensive and easy to work. The design relies on no anomalous-dispersion glass. The labels below are current catalog equivalents of the printed values; the actual supplier is not established.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | FK5 | 1.48749 / 70.1 | S-FSL5 (OHARA) | 70.24 | 0.5301 |
| L2 | LaF7 | 1.7495 / 35.2 | J-LAF7 (HIKARI) | 35.25 | 0.5881 |
| L3 | F16 | 1.5927 / 35.6 | FF5 (HOYA) | 35.45 | 0.5927 |

Every equivalent matches the printed index. L3 has the largest νd difference, 0.15. Hikari's discontinued J-F16 carries the patent's name but lists νd = 35.27, which is farther from the printed 35.6, so FF5 is used.

## Focus Mechanism

The model uses unit focus: the whole lens moves forward and only the back focus changes, with the stop fixed in the lens. The patent prints no focus travel and does not describe the focusing method. It gives aberration curves at 1/10× in Figs. 5a–5c, and extending Example 1 as a unit reproduces them, so unit focus is inferred from that agreement.

## Model Scope and Limitations

- The prescription is a patent design associated with the product, not a confirmed factory prescription. The 400 mm scale, the unit focus, the stop position and the rims are inferred, and the image plane is computed. The traced focal length is 399.98 mm.
- The field stops at the printed half-field of 3°05′, which reaches 21.62 mm at the image plane. That is 0.03 mm, or 0.13 %, short of the 21.65 mm corner the viewer uses for this format.
- The catalogue's "6 elements in 5 groups" remains unexplained, and the production versions are not separated.
