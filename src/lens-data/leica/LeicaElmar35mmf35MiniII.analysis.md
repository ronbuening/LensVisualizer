## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 5,321,555 A  
**Application Number:** 07/960,108  
**Priority:** 12 October 1991, DE 41 33 844  
**Filed:** 13 October 1992  
**Granted:** 14 June 1994  
**Inventors:** Lothar Koelsch; Klaus-Dieter Schaefer  
**Assignee:** Leica Camera GmbH  
**Title:** Compact photographic objective of the triplet type having improved chromatic and monochromatic characteristics  
**Embodiment analyzed:** The sole unnumbered prescription, Figure 1 and claim 2; the patent does not label it “Example 1.”

The model is a qualified correlation to the LEICA ELMAR 35mm f/3.5 in the Leica Mini II. The following evidence converges:

1. The patent describes a compact-camera 35 mm f/3.5 objective; the manufacturer brochure specifies the same nominal lens specifications and a 24 × 36 mm camera.
2. The prescription has four elements in three groups. The front pair is cemented, the middle lens is biconcave, and the rear lens is biconvex.
3. The brochure's section drawing (p. 3) shows the layout of Figure 1 and of the model: a thick cemented front pair whose junction is weakly curved and convex to the object, a thin biconcave middle lens about two-thirds the diameter of the front pair, and a biconvex rear lens. The drawing does not mark the diaphragm; the rear-stop position comes from the patent.
4. Leica Camera GmbH is the patent assignee. The priority and filing dates fit the product period, although the brochure edition alone does not establish the release year.

Neither the patent nor the brochure states that this prescription is the production Mini II design, so the association remains a correlation.

### Source Erratum: Third Radius

The table as printed does not reproduce the patent's own paraxial data. With the printed third radius, 19.1519 mm, it traces to a focal length of 34.5026 mm against the stated 33.6323 mm. A third radius of **19.635 mm** reproduces three stated values at once: the focal length (33.6326 mm), the printed stop-to-image distance (paraxial focus 24.5161 mm behind the stop, against 24.5165 mm) and the pupil magnification (0.7289 against 0.7290). No other single radius, thickness or index does so.

The patent's plots agree. Figure 2A shows +2.03 % distortion at an image height of 18 mm; the corrected table gives +2.00 % and the printed one +1.77 %. The sagittal curve of Figure 2D reads −0.32 mm at 12 mm, against −0.33 mm corrected and −0.28 mm printed. The printed decimals repeat those of the row above (39.1519), which points to a copying slip.

The model therefore uses R3 = 19.635 mm. This is an inferred correction, not a published value: its fourth decimal follows from the stated focal length (within 0.0005 mm), and the US and European prints both carry 19.1519. Every other radius, thickness and index is as printed, and no scale factor is applied.

The source summary also gives a back focus of 26.7165 mm from the last lens surface, while the table's last two spacings sum to 25.7165 mm. The difference is exactly 1.0000 mm and the corrected table focuses at the tabulated distance, so the summary figure is read as a digit slip. The model keeps the tabulated spacings.

## Optical Architecture

The design is a rear-stop, modified triplet with positive–negative–positive group power. The front positive group uses a cemented positive and negative meniscus pair; two air-separated singlets complete the lens. All seven powered surfaces are spherical.

The effective focal length is 33.6326 mm. The first-to-last-glass length is 10.8000 mm, and the image plane is 36.5165 mm behind the first vertex. The total-track/EFL ratio is 1.0857 and BFD/EFL is 0.7646, so the lens is neither telephoto nor retrofocus.

At the e line, the three groups have calculated standalone focal lengths of +25.0327 mm, −12.6757 mm and +16.0468 mm.

The stop is behind every powered element, so the exit pupil is the physical stop. The calculated entrance pupil lies 12.3505 mm behind the first vertex, and the paraxial exit-to-entrance pupil diameter ratio is 0.7289, against the patent's 0.7290.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Positive Meniscus, Convex to the Object

nₑ = 1.808109, νₑ = 46.34. Glass: S-LAH65V (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). Standalone-in-air f = +18.2118 mm.

The first surface supplies the largest positive surface power in the lens. Because L1 is cemented to L2, the focal length above describes the isolated element only.

The patent requires a large Abbe-number separation within this doublet. OHARA S-LAH65V has the printed coordinates (catalog nₑ 1.808107, νₑ 46.338) and supplies the dispersion curve of the model. The source names no manufacturer or glass type.

### L2 — Negative Meniscus in the Front Cemented Group

nₑ = 1.812640, νₑ = 25.23. Glass: S-TIH6 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). Standalone-in-air f = −50.8310 mm. Its rear surface is the corrected third radius.

The index difference at the cemented junction is small, while the Abbe-number difference between L1 and L2 is 21.11. The junction therefore has almost no power but a strong chromatic effect. The patent links the cemented surface and the final surface to lateral-color control (its fourth condition); this is a design claim, not a measurement of the production camera.

OHARA S-TIH6 has the printed coordinates (catalog nₑ 1.812640, νₑ 25.220) and supplies the dispersion curve of the model; no supplier is identified. The complete front doublet has a calculated focal length of +25.0327 mm.

### L3 — Biconcave Negative Singlet

nₑ = 1.652230, νₑ = 33.57. Glass: E-FD2 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). Standalone-in-air f = −12.6757 mm.

This strong negative middle group lies between the two positive groups. Its contribution to the Petzval sum (−0.0474 mm⁻¹) partly offsets the front (+0.0177 mm⁻¹) and rear (+0.0375 mm⁻¹) contributions. The total Petzval sum is +0.007885 mm⁻¹, a Petzval radius of 126.8 mm.

Several SF2/PBM22/FD2-family catalog entries lie near the source coordinates. HOYA E-FD2 (catalog nₑ 1.65222, νₑ 33.58, against the printed 1.65223 and 33.57) is used as the catalog equivalent and supplies the dispersion curve of the model.

### L4 — Biconvex Positive Rear Singlet

nₑ = 1.716700, νₑ = 42.89. Glass: LAFL4 (HOYA spectral proxy; original type and production supplier unconfirmed). Standalone-in-air f = +16.0468 mm.

The final positive singlet completes the triplet and stands 1.2 mm before the rear stop. Figure 1 labels it L4; the description's single reference to "L5" is inconsistent with that figure and does not imply a fifth element.

The page image prints 1.716700; text-layer copies reading 1.76700 have dropped a digit. HOYA LAFL4 is the nearest catalog row, at nₑ 1.716614 and νₑ 43.056 from its published polynomial. The differences, −0.0000865 in index and +0.166 in Abbe number, are within the catalog resolver's compatibility window. LAFL4 is the only compatible row among the 670 reviewed repository entries and the 196 rows of HOYA's current catalog including obsolete glasses. It is used as a qualified spectral proxy, rather than an exact match at printed precision or an identification of the production glass.

## Glass Identification and Spectral Limits

The source table explicitly gives n(546), and Figure 2C labels the e, C′, f′ and g wavelengths. The related EP 0 537 469 B1 table explicitly labels both nₑ and νₑ. The data file's nd/vd fields therefore hold the e-line values, with indexReference set to e; no d-line conversion is made.

Catalog data from OHARA, HOYA, Schott, Hikari, CDGM and Sumita were compared. Three of the four printed pairs coincide with a catalog glass. OHARA S-LAH65V (nₑ 1.808107, νₑ 46.338) for L1 and OHARA S-TIH6 (1.812640, 25.220) for L2 agree with the print within 0.000002 in nₑ and 0.01 in νₑ. HOYA E-FD2 (1.652218, 33.584) for L3 differs by one unit in the last printed decimal of each value (1.65222 against 1.65223, 33.58 against 33.57). The data file names these three as catalog equivalents. They are equivalents at the printed precision, not identified suppliers: the patent names no glass, and two printed coordinates cannot single out a maker or a melt.

L4 now traces on the [vendor-published LAFL4 polynomial](https://www.hoya-opticalworld.com/common/agf/HOYA20260707_include_obsolete.agf). Its Abbe-number residual is about 0.39%, so the curve remains an approximation to the source material. The original type, melt and supplier are still unconfirmed. The earlier unmatched disposition required equality at printed precision; it was unnecessarily strict for a clearly qualified spectral proxy. No patent partial dispersion or line index is invented.

The catalog also preserves OHARA’s discontinued PBM22 curve, at nₑ / νₑ = 1.652226 / 33.554, as a historical alternative for L3. Its availability does not establish the production glass. The selected E-FD2 label and curve remain unchanged.

The patent supplies no C′, F′ or g indices and no partial dispersions. All four elements now take their spectral indices from the named catalog curves, anchored at the printed nₑ (the printed nₑ plus the catalog's index difference from the e line). For L4 that anchor adds 0.0000865 to the vendor indices; it preserves the patent's e-line power without rescaling the dispersion curve to force its Abbe number. With LAFL4, the paraxial F′–C′ focus difference is about 0.161 mm, compared with 0.165 mm under the previous Abbe fallback. The blue focus is nearer the lens, and the f/3.5 entrance-rim rays for C′, e and F′ land about 21, 28 and 26 µm from the axis in the e-line paraxial plane.

Figure 2C plots the same on-axis curves. Scaled from the ±20 µm ticks of the European print, its C′ curve ends about 20 µm from the axis at the edge of the aperture, and its e and F′ curves, drawn together there, end about 28 µm from it. The earlier Abbe-fallback comparison followed the three curves within about 1 µm from 0.4 of the aperture to its edge; LAFL4 changes the computed C′ and F′ fan positions by less than 0.8 µm over that range and leaves e unchanged. The redrawn Figure 2C of the US print sets its ±20 µm ticks about 1.4 times too far apart, while its curves are the same size as the European ones, so the US ticks are not used. The g-line trace now uses the LAFL4 proxy; neither the patent's plot nor its missing partial-dispersion data establishes that curve as the original material.

## Focus Mechanism

The focus slider now illustrates unit focusing from infinity to the brochure’s 0.65 m limit, interpreted as object-to-image distance. All four elements and the rear iris move together toward the object; their relative gaps and the iris radius stay fixed. This is a model assumption: neither patent nor brochure establishes the camera’s moving assembly or prints its travel. A service drawing would be needed to confirm that mechanism.

A paraxial conjugate solve of the corrected prescription gives a 1.957535 mm extension at 0.65 m. The stop-to-image gap grows from 24.5165 to 26.474035 mm, and the object is then 611.526 mm ahead of the first vertex. The calculated magnification is −0.058214 (about 1:17.18); effective focal length remains 33.6326 mm. No radius, internal spacing, glass or infinity image plane is altered.

Nine calculated samples, equally spaced in inverse object-to-image distance, keep the intermediate motion close to that solve. They are reconstructed control points, not source-published states. Finite-focus MTF remains unavailable because the camera’s optical movement is not independently established; the focus movement, ray diagrams and other qualified analyses use the current estimated position.

## Aperture, Clearances and Image Plane

The source does not publish clear semi-diameters. Figure 1, scaled from its vertex spacing, draws approximately 6.6 mm for the cemented front group, 4.0 mm for the middle singlet and 3.85 mm for the rear singlet, subject to schematic-drawing uncertainty. It draws the rear face of the front group polished only to about 4.0 mm, level with the rim of L3, and chamfered from there back to the full rim. The model uses 6.6 mm for the front and cemented surfaces, 4.05 mm for the rear face of the front group and for L3, and 3.9 mm for L4. The rendered section shows the chamfer as a straight edge from the cemented rim, without the short cylindrical land that Figure 1 gives L2.

The f/3.5 axial ray enters at 4.8047 mm and reaches 3.953 mm on the rear face of L2, 3.929 mm on the front of L3 and 3.811 mm on the front of L4, inside those radii. At the 4.05 mm shared radius the first air gap has 0.6237 mm of sag intrusion into its 0.7000 mm axial spacing, leaving 0.0763 mm of air at the rim.

The stop semi-diameter, 3.5447 mm, is the height at the stop of the real axial ray that enters at EFL/(2 × 3.5). It sets the model aperture and is not a published diaphragm size. The paraxial value is 3.5023 mm, a stop 7.00 mm in diameter; the difference is pupil aberration.

The corner of the 24 × 36 mm frame, at an image radius of 21.63 mm, corresponds to a half-field of 32.2°. Distortion is pincushion, +2.1 % at the corner. Off-axis bundles are clipped by the lens rims as well as by the stop, as the patent's relative-illumination plot (Figure 2B) also shows.

The f/3.5 axial marginal ray crosses the axis 25.5267 mm behind the last glass, 0.189 mm short of the paraxial focus at 25.7161 mm, and meets the image plane 0.028 mm from the axis. The image plane is the patent's tabulated one, 25.7165 mm behind the last glass. It has not been moved to a best-focus position.

## Patent Conditions and Model Agreement

With the 10.8000 mm lens length and the 25.7165 mm back focus, the model gives BL/S′ = 0.420 against the claimed limit of 0.6, and S′ = 0.765 f against the limit of 0.8 f. The glass conditions n₂ > 1.7 and ν₁ − ν₂ > 15 are met directly (1.8126 and 21.11).

The fourth condition compares the lateral-color Seidel coefficients F₂ of surfaces 2 and 7, and the patent prints it without absolute-value bars or a normalization. A primary lateral-color calculation gives contributions of nearly equal size and opposite sign (ratio −0.99). That matches the patent's description of the cemented surface compensating the last surface, and in magnitude the ratio lies inside the claimed 0.7 to 1.3 range.

With the third radius corrected, the model reproduces the patent's focal length, image distance and pupil magnification, its distortion plot (within 0.04 percentage points out to 20 mm) and its sagittal field curve (within 0.012 mm out to 12 mm). The tangential curve of Figure 2D is matched less closely, within 0.03 mm out to 15 mm. Beyond about 12 mm the plotted field curves depart from a calculation along the ray through the stop centre, because the bundles there are heavily vignetted. Along the central ray of the vignetted f/3.5 bundle the model follows both plotted curves over the whole field, the sagittal within 0.04 mm and the tangential within 0.03 mm. The patent does not state the reference ray its plots use.
