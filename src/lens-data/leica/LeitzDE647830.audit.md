## Transcription Check

The page images agree with all 37 printed numerical entries: 12 signed radii, 11 thicknesses and air separations, seven n′ values and seven Abbe numbers. No numerical mismatch was found. Every radius sign is explicit, including positive R2 and negative R10. R4 and R7 are cemented interfaces, giving seven spherical elements in five groups: 1 | 2+3 | 4+5 | 6 | 7.

The central air space A2 is 21.75 mm, divided 12.0 + 9.75 mm around the inferred stop. The three short outer air spaces are each 0.378 mm. The claim's conditions hold: R7 = 43.50 mm is less than half the nominal focal length, and |R7|/|R6| = 1.31 lies between 1.0 and 1.5. The rear positive component consists of two positive members, L6 and L7.

## First-Order Check

At the chosen scale, the calculated EFL is 99.994 mm, 0.006 mm below the printed F = 100. The paraxial f-number is 1.50 with a stop radius of 19.687 mm, agreeing with the printed f/1.5. Exact axial acceptance at this iris admits an entrance radius of about 34.44 mm, corresponding to about f/1.45.

The image plane lies at the calculated paraxial infinity back focus, 53.653 mm behind R12; back focus is not printed. The first-to-last vertex track is 101.36 mm and the front-vertex-to-image distance is 155.01 mm. The surfaces stay clear of each other, and the iris sets the axial aperture without a rim limit.

Individual in-air element focal lengths are +139.46, +66.51, −42.01, −31.48, +43.86, +185.84 and +131.64 mm. The front and rear cemented assemblies are weakly negative at −240.69 and −268.00 mm.

## Product Identification

Leitz ownership, the September 1934 effective date, f/1.5 aperture and seven-element split-rear Gauss layout make this plausibly the German design behind the Xenon 5 cm f/1.5. The auction record identifies “D.R.P.a” beside the Taylor-Hobson name and US 2,019,985 number on a 1936 lens. That German application marking does not identify DE 647,830 by number, and no published Xenon prescription confirms the numerical example.

Lee's US 2,019,985 A shares the broad split-rear architecture but has different numerical values. The Leitz claim specifically depends on its strongly curved rear cemented interface. The title therefore retains the patent's 100 mm design scale. No marketed focal length or aperture, mount, production format, blade count or shape, or marketed minimum-focus distance is assigned without an exact product identification.
