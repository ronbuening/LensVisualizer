# LEICA SUPER-ANGULON 21mm f/4 — Audit Log

## Source

[DE 1,107,416 B](https://patents.google.com/patent/DE1107416B/), sole numerical example, repeated in claim 2; Fig. 1 shows the section and Fig. 2 the aberration curves. Filed 14 February 1957 and published 25 May 1961, it is an addition to application Sch 16212 IX/42h. The applicant is Jos. Schneider & Co., Optische Werke. The front page prints “Günter Klemt und Karl Macher”; the recorded inventor names remain Günter Klemt and Karl Heinrich Macher.

## Transcription Check

All thirteen radii, twelve thicknesses and air spaces, the back focus and nine nd/νd pairs agree with the patent's two numerical tables. Dimensions are uniformly scaled by 0.21 from f′ = 100 to 21 mm; d-line indices and Abbe numbers are unscaled. There are no aspherical terms or variable gaps. Fig. 1 agrees with placing the stop near the midpoint of the air space between S6 and S7. The patent names its four cemented components I–IV, with a 2+2+3+2 element arrangement.

## First-Order Check

The scaled patent states f = 21.00 mm, f/4 and a 7.728 mm back focus. The model gives EFL 20.997 mm, f/4.00 and paraxial BFD 7.737 mm. The EFL differs by about −0.02%; the retained 7.728 mm final air gap lies 0.009 mm before paraxial best focus. Total first-vertex-to-image length is 52.26 mm and the derived stop radius is 3.411 mm.

At the patent's f′ = 100 scale, calculated longitudinal spherical aberration is approximately −0.25 mm at f/4 and −0.90 mm at the f/5.57 zone; Fig. 2 reads approximately −0.28 and −0.94 mm. At 45° the calculated distortion is −9.9‰ versus approximately −7.6‰ in Fig. 2, with the same sign and curve shape. Fig. 2 extends to 45° half-field; the approximately 100° coverage in the opening text describes the earlier six-element f/8 system.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The patent gives no clear semi-diameters. S3, S4, S10, S12 and S13 follow the optical rims in Fig. 1; the other clear radii are estimates checked against the figure (see the rim review below). The stop lies halfway along the 1.659 mm S6–S7 air space, split into two 0.8295 mm distances. Its radius is derived from f/4. Total length and individual element powers are calculated. No finite-focus spacings, focus travel or partial-dispersion values are printed.

Seven glasses have catalog dispersion equivalents; two retain only their printed index and Abbe number. No original glass supplier or melt is established. No separate spectral indices or anomalous-dispersion designations have been added.

## Product Identification

The patent likely describes the Schneider-made Leica Super-Angulon 21mm f/4, introduced at Photokina 1958. Its date, aperture, nine elements in four cemented components and the [published product section](http://allphotolenses.com/public/files/optical/86649b22361edca67e5766be172191bd.jpg) support the identification. Factory confirmation of the exact numerical prescription has not been found.

The [Leitz brochure, PDF p. 2](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/01191/01191.pdf), specifies 21 mm, nine elements, 92°, f/4–22, M version 11102 and screw-mount version 11002. The [product review](https://www.kenrockwell.com/leica/21mm-f4.htm) confirms four groups, 0.4 m minimum focus, full-stop clicks and nine straight diaphragm blades. These sources support Leica M and Leica thread mounts and the 24×36 mm format. [Summichronica's bayonet-mount history](https://www.summichronica.com/bayonet-mount-lenses-21mm) identifies SUMOM/11102; its [screw-mount history](https://www.summichronica.com/screw-mount-lenses-21mm) identifies SUOON and the 1958 introduction. Both marketing and scaled design values remain 21 mm and f/4.

## Corrections, 2026-11-20

- Leica thread mount was added alongside Leica M, and the marketed minimum focus was added as 0.4 m. The aperture range was extended from f/16 to f/22, following Leitz literature. Nine straight diaphragm blades were added from the cited product specifications.
- The 1 m model focus limit was removed because the patent supplies only infinity spacings. The production lens's minimum focus is 0.4 m.
- S12 clear radius changed from 6.8 to 8.8 mm and S13 from 10 to 11.3 mm, following Fig. 1. The full field changed from 70° to 92° and the half-field from 35° to 46°, following the product angle.
- Generic element types were replaced by optical shapes: negative menisci L1/L3/L5/L7/L8/L9, positive meniscus L2 and biconvex positive L4/L6. Calculated thick-element focal lengths in air were added: L1 −19.77, L2 +698.84, L3 −22.17, L4 +9.50, L5 −11.70, L6 +5.45, L7 −15.69, L8 −140.80 and L9 −23.21 mm.
- The patent's four cemented components I–IV span S1–S3, S4–S6, S7–S10 and S11–S13, respectively.
- Five glasses were assigned catalog dispersion equivalents; four remain unidentified. The catalog differences are listed below; the printed d-line indices and Abbe numbers are retained.

The resulting EFL is 20.997 mm, aperture f/4.00, paraxial BFD 7.737 mm, final air gap 7.728 mm, stop radius 3.411 mm and total length 52.26 mm. The surfaces stay clear of each other; the iris sets the full aperture.

Before the rim corrections, the corner chief ray reached S12 at 8.09 mm and was blocked by its 6.8 mm rim; field coverage was 67%. The 45° chief ray now meets S12/S13 at 7.96/10.32 mm, and the 24×36 mm corner at 8.10/10.61 mm. Both are within the adopted rims. At 46.0°, image height is 21.52 mm, 99% of the 21.65 mm format radius; the 46.2° corner chief ray is clear.

A diameter estimate based on exit-pupil geometry still suggests larger clear radii at S11–S13: 9.10/12.61/13.68 mm versus 6.8/8.8/11.3 mm. This approximation is unreliable for this wide-angle design. S11's radius of curvature has magnitude 7.638 mm, so a 9.10 mm clear radius would exceed the sphere itself. Direct corner-chief tracing confirms access to the format corner; it does not establish full-pupil illumination.

## Remaining Questions

- S1, S2, S5, S6 and S11 are estimates within about 6% of Fig. 1; L5 and L6 deliberately stay larger than drawn.
- L1 and L8 lack named glass equivalents. Production suppliers, melts and the missing partial-dispersion values remain unknown.
- No finite-focus prescription or factory statement confirming the exact numerical product design has been found. No manufacturer image-circle diameter is documented in the cited sources, so that value is omitted.
- Numerical convergence on the steep S11/S12 surfaces can fail beyond about 41.5°. Full-pupil illumination remains uncertain.
