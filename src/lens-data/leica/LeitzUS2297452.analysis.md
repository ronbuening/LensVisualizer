# LEITZ 100mm f/1.3 (US 2,297,452 Design)

## Patent and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,297,452 A — Photographic Objective
**Inventors:** Max Berek, Otto Zimmermann, Gustav Kleineberg
**Assignee:** No company named; the patent states “vested in the Alien Property Custodian.”
**Filed:** 22 April 1941; German priority 6 May 1940
**Published:** 29 September 1942
**Example details:** Sole numerical example; Figure 1 shows the section and Figure 2 the correction curves.

The patent describes a large-aperture triplet in which the positive front member is split into two cemented doublets. Its inventors were Leitz designers, but the publication names no company as assignee. No production lens is documented for this prescription. The Summitar and Summarit use Gauss constructions and do not establish a product match for this layout.

The example states f = 100 and aperture 1:1.3 without naming a physical unit. The model expresses those lengths in millimetres. Its computed EFL is 100.0056 mm; the catalogue design value is rounded to 100.01 mm. The printed radii, thicknesses, air spaces and glass coordinates are retained.

## Optical Architecture

Six elements form four air-spaced groups: two positive cemented doublets, a negative singlet and a positive rear singlet. The cemented interfaces are r2 and r5. The patent's four “lens elements” mean four optical members, comprising six pieces of glass. Surface r7 is the plane entrance face of Element 5.

The first and second doublets have computed focal lengths of +159.86 and +165.59 mm. Their negative, more dispersive partners provide colour correction within the collecting front assembly. The negative third member uses glass of higher Abbe number than those partners, followed by a positive final member. This dispersion arrangement is central to the patent's claim for correction at a large aperture.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are those of each element considered separately in air; cementing changes their contributions to the complete lens.

### Elements 1 and 2 — Front Cemented Doublet C1

Element 1 is biconvex positive: nD = 1.5338, ν = 55.4, glass unmatched, f = +103.10 mm. Element 2 is a negative meniscus: nD = 1.7283, ν = 28.3, glass unmatched, f = −268.67 mm.

The biconvex element supplies most of this member's positive power. Its cemented negative partner has greater dispersion. Their shared r2 surface is −167.68 mm, and the first doublet is separated from the second by the printed 2.96 mm air space.

### Elements 3 and 4 — Second Cemented Doublet C2

Element 3 is biconvex positive: nD = 1.6230, ν = 58.1, glass unmatched, f = +78.09 mm. Element 4 is biconcave negative: nD = 1.7283, ν = 28.3, glass unmatched, f = −122.94 mm.

The second member repeats the positive/negative pairing, with the more strongly powered negative element on its rear side. The cemented r5 surface is −986.34 mm. The following air space is 12.82 mm; an OCR reading of 2.82 omits the leading digit visible on the page.

### Element 5 — Plano-Concave Negative Singlet

nD = 1.6073, ν = 49.2, glass unmatched, f = −47.09 mm.

The plane front face and +28.60 mm rear radius form the negative third member. Its ν of 49.2 exceeds the doublets' negative-glass value of 28.3 by more than 30% and is about 13.2% below the rear element's 56.7, satisfying the patent's stated dispersion relationship.

### Element 6 — Biconvex Positive Rear Singlet

nD = 1.6074, ν = 56.7, glass unmatched, f = +56.07 mm.

The final positive element follows a 12.82 mm air space and brings the beam to the image. The small index difference between Elements 5 and 6 is printed and retained. The spacing also satisfies the claim that the final air separation exceed half the preceding one.

## Glass Identification / Selection

All six printed index/Abbe pairs remain unmatched to named catalogue glasses. The prescription heading gives “refractive powers and ν-values” without a spectral subscript; the adjacent Fraunhofer D/C/F correction table supports taking D as the reference line (taken as sodium, 589.3 nm). The patent gives no glass suppliers or individual C-, F- or g-line indices.

Elements 2 and 4 share the same index and Abbe number. The two cemented members combine less dispersive positive glass with more dispersive negative glass. The printed C/F focal-distance observations are system results, not individual glass indices. Spectral estimates based only on the Abbe numbers do not establish the historical glasses' partial dispersion or measured broadband performance.

## Focus Mechanism and Aperture

Only infinity focus is described. No close-focus spacing, focusing travel or commercial minimum focus distance is established.

Figure 1 draws no iris. The inferred stop lies 11.75 mm behind r8 and 1.07 mm before r9, preserving the printed 12.82 mm air space; its axial station is 77.83 mm from the first vertex. The paraxial f/1.3 setting gives a stop radius of 16.0916 mm. Exact rays through that inferred iris give an entrance-pupil equivalent of approximately f/1.365. The iris position and opening are model choices rather than dimensions supplied by the patent.

## First-Order and Spherical Correction

The computed paraxial back focus is 34.8788 mm, 0.0038 mm beyond the printed D-line axial back distance of 34.875. The stored image gap is rounded to 34.8788 mm. The first-to-last-surface track is 92.22 mm, placing the image 127.0988 mm behind the first vertex.

The printed Figure 2 values and exact D-line rays agree to about 0.012 mm. The patent measures these zonal distances from the ray's emergence point on r10, projected onto the axis; they are distinct from distances measured from r10's vertex.

## Field and Clear-Aperture Limits

The patent states only “moderate angles of view.” The displayed ±5° field is illustrative, with no assigned image format or image circle. All clear semi-diameters are inferred. Axial rays and the chief ray across this illustrative field clear the rims, although off-axis bundles are vignetted. A 10° marginal bundle is clipped.

Figure 1 draws r4–r6 at approximately equal heights, whereas their model semi-diameters are 37/33/33 mm. The concave r8 face is also cut lower in the model than in the drawing so that its rim stays ahead of the inferred stop. These differences remain unresolved; the drawing does not supply dimensioned clear diameters.
