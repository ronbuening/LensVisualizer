# LEITZ 100mm f/1.3 (US 2,297,452 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,297,452 A — Photographic Objective\
**Inventors:** Max Berek, Otto Zimmermann, Gustav Kleineberg\
**Assignee:** No company named; the patent states “vested in the Alien Property Custodian.”\
**Filed:** 22 April 1941; German priority 6 May 1940\
**Published:** 29 September 1942\
**Example details:** Sole numerical example; Figure 1 shows the section and Figure 2 the correction curves.

The patent splits the triplet's positive front member into two cemented doublets. The inventors' Leitz association is documented in other patents listed below; this publication names no company as assignee. No production lens is documented for this prescription. The Gauss-type Summitar and Summarit have different constructions.

The example states f = 100 and aperture 1:1.3 without naming a physical unit. The model expresses those lengths in millimetres, with a computed EFL of 100.01 mm. The printed radii, thicknesses, air spaces and glass coordinates are retained.

## Optical Architecture

Six elements form four air-spaced groups: two positive cemented doublets, a negative singlet and a positive rear singlet. The cemented interfaces are r2 and r5. The patent's four “lens elements” mean four optical members, comprising six pieces of glass. Surface r7 is the plane entrance face of Element 5.

The doublets' computed focal lengths are +159.86 and +165.59 mm. Their more dispersive negative partners correct colour in the collecting front assembly. The negative third member has a higher Abbe number than those partners and precedes the positive final member. This dispersion arrangement underlies the patent's large-aperture correction claim.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Element focal lengths are calculated in air; cementing changes their contributions to the complete lens.

### Elements 1 and 2 — Front Cemented Doublet C1

Element 1 is biconvex positive: nD = 1.5338, ν = 55.4. Glass: Unmatched. f = +103.10 mm.

Element 2 is a negative meniscus: nD = 1.7283, ν = 28.3. Glass: H-ZF4A (CDGM), spectral proxy. f = −268.67 mm.

The biconvex element supplies most of this member's positive power. Its cemented negative partner has greater dispersion. Their shared r2 surface is −167.68 mm, and the first doublet is separated from the second by the printed 2.96 mm air space.

### Elements 3 and 4 — Second Cemented Doublet C2

Element 3 is biconvex positive: nD = 1.6230, ν = 58.1. Glass: BACD15 (Hoya), matching the printed coordinates. f = +78.09 mm.

Element 4 is biconcave negative: nD = 1.7283, ν = 28.3. Glass: H-ZF4A (CDGM), spectral proxy. f = −122.94 mm.

The second member repeats the positive/negative pairing, with the more strongly powered negative element on its rear side. The cemented r5 surface is −986.34 mm. The following air space is 12.82 mm; an OCR reading of 2.82 omits the leading digit visible on the page.

### Element 5 — Plano-Concave Negative Singlet

nD = 1.6073, ν = 49.2. Glass: BAF5 (Hoya), spectral proxy. f = −47.09 mm.

The plane front face and +28.60 mm rear radius form the negative third member. Its ν of 49.2 exceeds the doublets' negative-glass value of 28.3 by more than 30% and is about 13.2% below the rear element's 56.7, satisfying the patent's stated dispersion relationship.

### Element 6 — Biconvex Positive Rear Singlet

nD = 1.6074, ν = 56.7. Glass: BACD2 (Hoya), spectral proxy. f = +56.07 mm.

The final positive element follows a 12.82 mm air space and focuses the beam. The small index difference between Elements 5 and 6 is printed and retained. The spacing also satisfies the claim that the final air separation exceed half the preceding one.

## Glass Identification / Selection

The prescription heading gives “refractive powers and ν-values” without a spectral subscript; the adjacent Fraunhofer D/C/F correction table supports taking sodium D, 589.3 nm, as the reference line. Five of the six elements use catalog curves while retaining their printed index and Abbe number. Differences below are catalog minus printed at sodium D.

| Elements | Catalog row | Use | ΔnD | Δν |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 2, 4 | H-ZF4A (CDGM) | Spectral proxy | −0.00027 | +0.01 |
| 3 | BACD15 (Hoya) | Exact match | −0.00010 | +0.01 |
| 5 | BAF5 (Hoya) | Spectral proxy | −0.00012 | +0.13 |
| 6 | BACD2 (Hoya) | Spectral proxy | −0.00011 | +0.01 |

Element 3 matches BACD15 within the printed precision. Elements 2, 4, 5 and 6 use spectral proxies within index 0.002 and Abbe number 0.25 at sodium D. These rows supply only the dispersion-curve shape, so colour estimates follow published material curves rather than Abbe numbers alone. Element 1 remains unmatched: the only catalog curve within those limits is an optical polymer, not a glass, so its dispersion follows an estimate from the printed index and Abbe number.

The row names establish neither the historical suppliers, melts nor material compositions. The patent gives no individual C-, F- or g-line indices or measured partial dispersions; its C/F focal distances describe the complete lens. The two cemented members combine less dispersive positive glass with more dispersive negative glass, but catalog curves do not establish measured broadband performance.

## Focus Mechanism and Aperture

The model is focused at infinity. No close-focus spacing, focusing travel or production minimum focus distance is documented.

Figure 1 draws no iris. The inferred stop lies 11.75 mm behind r8 and 1.07 mm before r9, preserving the printed 12.82 mm air space; its axial station is 77.83 mm from the first vertex. Paraxial f/1.3 gives a stop radius of 16.09 mm. Exact rays through that iris give an entrance-pupil equivalent of approximately f/1.36. The iris position and opening are inferred.

## First-Order and Spherical Correction

The computed paraxial back focus is 34.879 mm, 0.004 mm beyond the printed D-line axial back distance of 34.875 mm. The image plane lies there, 127.10 mm behind the first vertex; the first-to-last-surface track is 92.22 mm.

The printed Figure 2 values and exact D-line rays agree to about 0.012 mm. The patent's text defines these zonal distances from the ray's emergence point projected onto the axis, but the printed values agree with intercepts measured from r10's vertex, which the computed values use.

## Field and Clear-Aperture Limits

The patent states only “moderate angles of view.” The displayed ±5.0° field is illustrative, with no assigned image format or image circle. All clear semi-diameters are inferred. Axial rays and the chief ray across this field clear the rims, although off-axis bundles are vignetted. A 10.0° marginal bundle is clipped.

The inferred clear radii are 40 mm at r1–r3, 35.3 mm at r4/r5, 33 mm at r6, 30 mm at r7, 22 mm at r8 and 26 mm at r9/r10. Opaque lands at r6 and r8 extend their drawn edges to 35.3 and 30 mm without increasing the clear apertures. Figure 1 supports the level r4/r5 edges, a short bevel at r6 and a flat outer edge on Element 5. The lands approximate those bevels with vertical edges. The drawn r8 curve reaches about 26 mm, while the smaller modeled optical rim stays ahead of the inferred iris. The drawing's short axial spacings are approximate, and it supplies no dimensioned clear diameters. Wide-open geometric transmission is about 89% at the illustrative 5.0° field edge.
