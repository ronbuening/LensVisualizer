# LEICA SUPER-ANGULON-M 21mm f/3.4 — Audit Log

## Source

[DE 1,273,853 B](https://patents.google.com/patent/DE1273853B/), sole example, filed 8 March 1961 and published 25 July 1968. The front page names Karl Macher and Jos. Schneider & Co., Optische Werke. The full name Karl Heinrich Macher is retained from the [US family publication, US 3,209,649 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3209649A/en). The source year, example and assignee remain unchanged.

The front page, prescription table and Fig. 1 have been examined. All dimensions are uniformly multiplied by 0.21 from the printed focal length of 100 to 21 mm. The table and drawing name four members I–IV: a front negative singlet, two positive cemented triplets and a rear negative singlet.

## Transcription Check

All twelve radii, eleven thicknesses and air gaps, the printed back focus, eight index/Abbe pairs and the diaphragm space agree with the German table after scaling. There are no transcription mismatches. The two equal distances around the iris total the printed diaphragm space of 8.27 before scaling; its midpoint is consistent with Fig. 1, which draws the two iris bars at about 45% and 58% of that space.

The patent specifies the yellow d line. The catalog matches support the helium d wavelength of 587.6 nm, and the d-line reference is retained for every element. L5 uses νd = 42.82 from the German table. The US description also gives 42.82, but its claim table gives 42.32; the claim value is not substituted.

## First-Order Check

The nominal 21 mm design computes to EFL 21.021 mm, about 0.10% longer, at f/3.40. The first-to-last surface length is 44.894 mm. The printed back focus gives an 8.190 mm final image gap and a total track of 53.084 mm; the computed paraxial back focus is 8.193 mm, a 0.003 mm difference. The image plane retains the printed distance.

The iris is at z = 24.027 mm with a derived radius of 3.828 mm. The half-field remains 45.0°. At that angle the chief ray reaches an image height of 20.72 mm. Distortion is −1.32% relative to nominal 21 mm, consistent with Fig. 2, and −1.42% relative to computed EFL. The printed radius, index and Abbe-number conditions are satisfied, including r2/r1 = 0.179.

The surfaces stay clear of each other; the iris sets the full aperture.

The 45° field reaches 96% of the 24×36 mm corner height: 20.72 of 21.65 mm. The 46.3° corner is geometrically clear, although full-pupil transmission is not implied. A paraxial estimate suggests rear clear semi-diameters of 10.03 mm at S11 and 13.21 mm at S12, exceeding the 7.00 and 11.00 mm rims. That estimate is unreliable for this wide-angle design. Exact spherical tracing places the 45.0° chief ray at 6.46 and 10.05 mm on those surfaces, within both rims.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

- The iris position is inferred at the midpoint of the diaphragm space, and its radius is calculated from f/3.4. Neither is a printed physical iris measurement.
- The patent gives no clear diameters. Every semi-diameter is read from Fig. 1, which is drawn to scale; S6 also carries a drawn land out to 6.0 mm. The rim review below gives the readings.
- Individual element focal lengths in air and the two triplet focal lengths are calculated. The latter are 19.08 and 23.14 mm. Element powers do not isolate aberration contributions inside the cemented triplets.
- Catalog curves supply spectral behavior for seven glasses. Their agreement with printed index and Abbe values does not establish the historical supplier or melt. L2 has no identified catalog equivalent. No extra spectral-line indices or anomalous-dispersion designations have been added.
- The 0.4 m product focus limit, four diaphragm blades and f/22 minimum aperture come from product references; the patent supplies no finite-focus prescription or movement law.

## Product Identification

The design likely belongs to the Super-Angulon-M introduced in 1963: Schneider, the 1961 filing, f/3.4, a 90° field and eight elements in four groups agree with the product's construction and timing. No manufacturer statement identifies this exact patent table as the factory prescription. The Leica maker and product name are retained.

Leitz's [General Catalogue, p. 106](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/03321/03321.pdf) identifies code 11103, eight elements, f/22 and manual focus to 0.4 m. Its rangefinder-coupling limit is separately listed as 0.7 m for the M2/M4/M5 bodies shown and 1 m for the M3. The nominal focal length and maximum aperture remain 21 mm and f/3.4. Leica M mount, 24×36 mm format and four optical groups are consistent with [AllPhotoLenses](https://allphotolenses.com/lenses/item/c_2888.html); its conflicting f/32 and 1 m entries are not used.

[CineLensWiki](https://cinelenswiki.com/lens:leicamsuper-angulon21mmf_3.4) also lists f/22 and eight elements in four groups. [35mmc's account](https://www.35mmc.com/24/06/2019/leica-21mm-f-3-4-super-angulon-review/) supports Schneider design/manufacture, the 1963 introduction, 0.4 m focus and M5/CL meter-arm interference on early barrels; later barrels have a clearance cutout. The 8.19 mm back focus places the rear vertex about 19.6 mm behind the Leica M flange, explaining the deep rear assembly.

[Paul Kay's technical information and first-hand account](https://marinewildlife.co.uk/info/leica-21mm-f3-4-super-angulon-m/) identifies four diaphragm blades. Its different designer and patent attribution is not used to identify this prescription. Leitz's advertised 92° field and the patent's 90° field remain distinct; the optical field remains the patent's value.

## Corrections, 2026-11-20

- The lens uses the Leica product name. Its eight-element/four-group construction, Leica M mount, 35 mm format, nominal and design 21 mm focal length, and nominal and design f/3.4 were retained.
- The subtitle reads “DE 1,273,853 B, sole example — Schneider / Macher”. The specifications are “8 ELEMENTS / 4 GROUPS”, “DESIGN F/3.4”, “ALL SPHERICAL” and “90° FIELD”.
- Product close focus was added as 0.4 m. The previous 1 m optical focus endpoint was replaced by an effectively infinite distance because the patent gives no finite-focus state. The focus description states that limitation. The stop series and smallest aperture were extended from f/16 to f/22, supported by the product sources above.
- Four diaphragm blades were added from Kay's account. Brackets I–IV were added at S1–S2, S3–S6, S7–S10 and S11–S12 to match the patent's named members. The existing complete cemented-triplet brackets remain C1 and C2.
- S1 clear semi-diameter was increased from 12.5 to 14.2 mm and S10 from 4.8 to 6.5 mm following Fig. 1. Both admit more 45.0° meridional rays while preserving edge thickness and air clearance. The meridional ratio, EFL divided by admitted incident-height width, improves from 1:12.86 to 1:5.87, compared with about 1:6.0 in Fig. 2. S1 alone gives 1:11.16; S10 alone gives 1:6.31. Increasing all four proposed rims gives the same 45.0° width as S1 and S10 alone. These widths are not radiometric illumination measurements.
- S3 was increased from 6.0 to 7.0 mm following Fig. 1. At 30.0° and 40.0°, the meridional ratios improve from 1:3.41 to 1:3.17 and from 1:3.94 to 1:3.76; the 45.0° ratio remains 1:5.87. S4 stays 6.0 mm because Fig. 1 shows L2's beveled rim with unequal face extents. S12 stays 11.0 mm: increasing it to 11.5 mm adds light only at the 46.3° corner, outside the 45° field, with no gain at 30.0°, 40.0° or 45.0°.
- Seven formerly unidentified glass labels were replaced by the catalog curves below. The vendor names identify the catalogs supplying those curves. Even where another vendor was previously suggested, K5 matches Sumita and BAF5/FEL3 match Hoya. L2 retains only its printed optical coordinates. Signed differences are catalog minus patent.

### 2026-11-20 rim review

- Fig. 1 on printed sheet 1 is drawn to scale. At 600 dpi the first-to-last vertex span is 2491 px for the printed 213.78, or 55.5 px per mm at 21 mm. The 36.50 thickness of L3 and the 44.59 span from r8 to r10 agree within 0.6%. Only the image distance s′ is drawn long. Heights are read as half the distance between the top and bottom outlines, which cancels the slight tilt of the scanned axis.
- Readings at 21 mm compared with the file: S1 14.3 (14.2); S2 7.5 to 8.1 (7.5); S3 7.1 (7.0); S4 and S5 5.7 (6.0); S6 4.1 to 4.2 for the curved face, then a bevel to L4's 5.7 mm edge (6.0); S7 3.8 for the curved face, then a small chamfer to L5's 4.7 mm edge (4.8); S8 and S9 4.7 (4.8); S10 6.7 (6.5); S11 7.1 to 7.4 (7.0); S12 10.8 to 11.8 (11.0). The two S2 and S12 values come from the drawn heights and from the drawn arcs at the printed radii; the stored values fall inside both ranges.
- S6 changed from 6.0 to 4.2 mm, the end of the curved face before Fig. 1's bevel. A flat land at the face's rim carries the drawn edge out to 6.0 mm, so L4 keeps the square top it shares with L3. The 4.2 mm rim clears the f/3.4 axial beam, about 4.0 mm there. Wide-open geometric transmission falls by at most 2.4 points, at 33°: 98.7% to 96.3%. The 45° corner stays at 52.0%, and the field and f-number are unchanged.
- S7 stays 4.8 mm. The start of Fig. 1's chamfer, about 3.8 mm, lies inside the f/3.4 axial beam, so it cannot be a clear-aperture edge. Moving the rim to the smallest value within the light limit, 4.1 mm, would not change the drawing, because S7 is nearly flat.
- Every other rim stays within about 8% of the drawing. S2 at 7.9 mm, the steepest rim its curvature allows, would raise the 45° transmission from 52% to 69%. Fig. 1's drawn arc supports 7.5 mm, so no light-driven raise was made. Fig. 1's flat tops on L1, L7 and L8 are drawn as straight edges between the two face rims.
- The meridional bundle ratios are now 1:3.23 at 30°, 1:3.76 at 40° and 1:5.88 at 45°. Edge thickness, rim slope, air-gap clearance, the stop clearance and the full f/3.4 axial beam all pass.

### 2026-11-20 final check against the drawing

- The live page, each element's detail panel and the chromatic analysis were compared with Fig. 1, Fig. 2 and the page 2 table. All radii, spacings, indices and Abbe numbers match the table after the 0.21 scaling.
- Fig. 1 was measured again at 600 dpi. The S1 to S12 vertex span is 2490 px for the printed 213.78, or 55.46 px per mm at 21 mm; L3's thickness and the S8 to S10 span agree within 0.7%. Circle fits to the drawn S2, S4, S5 and S11 arcs give radii 5–11% larger than printed, so a rim read from its drawn height can sit up to about 8% above the same rim read from its axial position on the printed curve.
- Readings at 21 mm, drawn height first, with the file's value in brackets: S1 14.3 (14.2); S2 8.1, or 7.5 on the printed curve (7.5); S3 7.1 (7.0); the shared L3 and L4 edge at S4 and S5 5.7 (6.0); the end of S6's curved face 4.2 above the axis and 4.6 below (4.2, with the land to 6.0); the end of S7's face 3.8 and L5's edge 4.7 (4.8); S8 and S9 4.7 (4.8); S10 6.7 (6.5); S11 6.9, or 7.1 on the printed curve (7.0); S12 11.8, or 11.6 on the printed curve (11.0). Every rim lies within about 8% of a reading and the height order between neighbouring elements matches the drawing, so no rim changed.
- S6 is the only face below its value before the rim review. Wide open, the whole lens loses at most 2.4 points of light against those earlier rims, at 33° (98.7% to 96.3%), and the land costs nothing. Both stay. Raising S12 to the drawn 11.6–11.8 mm would change no meridional bundle inside the 45° field.
- Names L1–L8, members I–IV, the cemented triplets C1 and C2 and their brackets follow the patent. The detail panels read TRIPLET C1 and C2, every shape agrees with the signs of its radii, and the stored focal lengths match the computed ones. The patent names no special glass, so no element carries a special-glass tag. The stop sits between L4 and L5.
- The lens is infinity-only: the focus control shows the infinity state with the plain description, and there is no zoom control.
- The seven catalog curves match the printed values within 5×10⁻⁶ in nd and 0.09 in νd. No vendor row matches L2; the nearest, at nd 1.72342, differ by 3×10⁻⁴. The patent prints only nd and νd and has one example, so L2 cannot be identified further. L2 takes its dispersion from its Abbe number, so no element is shown with constant dispersion.
- Fig. 2's distortion at 45° reads −1.36%, against −1.32% relative to 21 mm in the model. Fig. 2 labels its coma curves 1:3.98, 1:4.55, 1:5.40 and 1:6.00 at 20°, 30°, 40° and 45°. The model reproduces only the 45° label, and only as EFL over the incident-height width; the beam width across the ray and the image-side cone do no better. The analysis sentence that quoted only the 45° label now quotes all four. The data file is unchanged.

## Remaining Questions

- Fig. 2 does not define the measure behind its oblique aperture labels, and the model does not reproduce them at 20°, 30° or 40°.
- Sagittal transmission is not established by the meridional widths. The straight connecting edges on L1, L7 and L8 do not show Fig. 1's short flat tops beside the bevels.
- L2's material identity and all historical glass suppliers remain unknown. The catalog curves do not prove supplier or melt identity.
- No finite-focus prescription or manufacturer confirmation of the exact factory prescription is available. Product references disagree about designer and patent attribution.
- No measured image-circle diameter or numerical blade-roundedness value was found, so neither is added.
