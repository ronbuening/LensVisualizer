## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** EP 2 321 688 B1  
**Application Number:** 09785005.1  
**Filed:** 25 August 2009  
**Granted:** 22 July 2020  
**Inventor:** Iain A. Neil (printed as Ian A. Neil on the patent)  
**Assignee:** Ernst Leitz Wetzlar GmbH  
**Title:** Objective lens system  
**Embodiment analyzed:** First embodiment, Table 1 / Figure 7

This prescription is the patent's first embodiment, a wide-angle cine prime of about 18 mm, and is a research
correlation to the Leica / Leitz Summilux-C 18mm T1.4; the production prescription is unconfirmed. The proprietor above
is the one on the patent front page, which prints the inventor as Ian A. Neil; the designer's own article is signed
Iain Neil. The 2020 grant date is not a lens-release date. [1, 2]

The production correlation rests on three separate observations:

1. Neil's signed design article presents an 18 mm optical section described as similar to the Summilux-C lenses. Its
   arrangement supports the selected patent embodiment but supplies no numerical production prescription. [2, pp. 30–31]
2. Leica's announcement attributes the Summilux-C optical design to Iain Neil. It supports the designer connection, not
   numerical equality. [3]
3. The manufacturer's lens shares the nominal 18 mm focal length and is likewise a 35 mm-format cine prime, while the
   aperture, coverage, size and close-focus figures are defined differently or disagree. [1, ¶0036, ¶0040; 4, 5]

The patent states approximately f/1.4; the product is marketed at T1.4. A T-number includes transmission losses, so it
is not used as the model's f-number. The manufacturer's 33 mm image circle is larger than the 28 mm image diagonal the
patent evaluates, and the model covers only the latter. The 142 mm product length is a mechanical dimension, not an
optical vertex-to-image distance. The manufacturer's web page gives 0.45 m close focus, its data sheet 0.5 m and a
rental listing 0.35 m; none replaces the patent's tabulated conjugates. [4, 5, 12]

Only Table 1 of EP 2 321 688 B1 is transcribed, including both of its asphere coefficient sets; nothing is taken from
another publication or embodiment. [1, Table 1]

## Optical Architecture

The model contains 18 elements in 15 air-separated groups, with three cemented doublets and two aspheric surfaces.
Element names L2–L19 are the patent's own numbering: L1 is its optional flat front filter, which is omitted here. The
functional division is a fixed negative G1a, a moving positive G1b, and a moving positive G2 containing the iris. These
three functional groups are distinct from the count of 15 air-separated groups. [1, Table 1; ¶0041–¶0045]

At infinity focus (F1) and the d line, the calculated EFL is 18.258034 mm and the paraxial back focal distance from S36
is 44.844480 mm. Because the back focal distance exceeds the EFL, the design is a retrofocus type. The printed
S36-to-image gap of 44.800 mm is kept as the image position. The track from S3 to the image is 188.273 mm.

| Functional group | Elements             | Calculated d-line EFL at F1 (mm) |
| ---------------- | -------------------- | -------------------------------: |
| G1a              | L2–L3                |                         -180.355 |
| G1b              | L4–L11               |                          +96.842 |
| G2               | iris and L12–L19     |                          +41.565 |
| G1               | combined G1a and G1b |                         +111.962 |

The negative front sub-group and the long back focal distance define the architecture; the two moving positive groups
focus the lens together while the image plane stays fixed. The model reproduces the patent's group-power signs, and G1
stays positive at all three printed focus states. [1, ¶0041, ¶0048–¶0049]

The patent prints group focal lengths of -179.99, +95.67 and +41.48 mm. At 546.074 nm, the wavelength the patent uses
for its tabulated apertures, current OHARA catalog indices give -179.998, +95.669 and +41.477 mm; the d-line values in
the table above differ only through that choice of wavelength. The 0.008 mm difference on G1a is at the level of
catalog and rounding precision. [1, ¶0039, ¶0041]

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The nd, νd and partial-dispersion values below are current OHARA catalog data for the glasses Table 1 names. Each focal
length is that of the thick element alone in air at the d line. Shape names refer to the vertex radii; an aspheric
surface can depart from that shape toward its rim. The patent does not describe an aberration role for each element.
[1, Table 1; ¶0038; 6]

### L2 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.74100, νd = 52.64. Glass: S-LAL61 (OHARA). f = +376.01 mm.

L2 is the weak positive singlet at the front of fixed G1a. It precedes the stronger negative aspheric L3, so the fixed
pair is net negative despite beginning with a positive component. Table 1 and ¶0043 agree on its biconvex shape.

### L3 — Negative Meniscus (1× Asph)

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.55. Glass: S-FPL51 (OHARA). f = -119.74 mm.

L3 supplies the negative power of fixed G1a and carries the first asphere on its rear surface 6A. The patent names its
S-FPL51 glass as an abnormal-dispersion type that adds freedom for chromatic correction. The aspheric departure at the
printed clear aperture is given below. [1, ¶0043, ¶0046]

### L4 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.74400, νd = 44.79. Glass: S-LAM2 (OHARA). f = -27.77 mm.

L4 opens the moving group G1b as a strongly negative biconcave element. Its deeply concave rear surface 8 nearly meets
L5 at the rim: with the printed radii and clear apertures, the two rims are 0.27 mm apart across a 9.22 mm axial gap.
[1, Table 1; ¶0044]

### L5 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.43. Glass: S-TIH6 (OHARA). f = +110.74 mm.

The positive meniscus L5 follows L4 across that air gap. Its S-TIH6 dense flint has much higher dispersion than the
fluorophosphate crowns elsewhere in the design. [1, Table 1; ¶0044]

### L6 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.43875, νd = 94.95. Glass: S-FPL53 (OHARA). f = -48.78 mm.

L6 is a negative biconcave S-FPL53 singlet within G1b, so the patent's low-dispersion glass is not confined to positive
elements. Paragraph 0046 lists L6 among the abnormal-dispersion elements.

### L7 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.74950, νd = 35.33. Glass: S-NBH51 (OHARA). f = +51.88 mm.

The biconvex S-NBH51 L7 restores positive power after L6 and precedes another positive singlet, L8; both belong to the
moving group G1b. L7 is the strongest positive singlet of that group. [1, ¶0044]

### L8 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.88300, νd = 40.77. Glass: S-LAH58 (OHARA). f = +109.89 mm.

L8 is the higher-index biconvex partner after L7, in S-LAH58 lanthanum glass. The 15.761 mm air space behind it is the
widest in G1b. [1, Table 1; ¶0044]

### L9 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.43875, νd = 94.95. Glass: S-FPL53 (OHARA). f = +88.25 mm.

L9 is a positive S-FPL53 meniscus immediately before the L10–L11 cemented pair. Paragraph 0046 lists it among the
abnormal-dispersion elements.

### L10 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.61800, νd = 63.33. Glass: S-PHM52 (OHARA). f = +66.23 mm.

L10 is the positive S-PHM52 member of the first cemented doublet. Its rear surface 20 is also the front surface of L11,
a single glass-to-glass interface. The doublet's combined focal length is listed after L19. [1, Table 1; ¶0044]

### L11 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.80100, νd = 34.97. Glass: S-LAM66 (OHARA). f = -49.04 mm.

The negative S-LAM66 member closes G1b immediately before the variable gap to the iris. The L10–L11 doublet has
negative net power at the d line, although G1b as a whole is positive. [1, ¶0044]

### L12 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.74950, νd = 35.33. Glass: S-NBH51 (OHARA). f = -41.39 mm.

The biconcave L12 is the first element after the iris in G2. It belongs to the rear focusing group, and the iris moves
with that group. Its glass is the high-dispersion S-NBH51. [1, ¶0045]

### L13 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.43875, νd = 94.95. Glass: S-FPL53 (OHARA). f = +96.80 mm.

L13 is a positive S-FPL53 biconvex singlet between L12 and the second cemented pair. The patent lists it among the
abnormal-dispersion elements. [1, ¶0045–¶0046]

### L14 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.43875, νd = 94.95. Glass: S-FPL53 (OHARA). f = +42.27 mm.

L14 is the positive low-dispersion member of the L14–L15 cemented pair. Paragraph 0045 calls it a positive meniscus, but
Table 1 gives vertex radii of opposite sign and Figure 7 draws it biconvex. The model uses the Table 1 radii. [1, Table
1; Figure 7; ¶0045–¶0046]

### L15 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.81600, νd = 46.62. Glass: S-LAH59 (OHARA). f = -169.52 mm.

The negative S-LAH59 meniscus is cemented to L14 at surface 28. The pair has positive net power at the d line, and the
interface joins a low-index fluorophosphate crown to a high-index lanthanum glass. [1, Table 1; ¶0045]

### L16 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.43875, νd = 94.95. Glass: S-FPL53 (OHARA). f = +79.84 mm.

L16 is the positive S-FPL53 member of the final cemented pair, listed by the patent among the abnormal-dispersion
elements. Its rear surface 31 is the cemented interface with L17. Some off-axis rays are clipped at the printed 17.78 mm
half-diameter of that interface. [1, Table 1; ¶0045–¶0046]

### L17 — Negative Meniscus (1× Asph)

nd = 1.74950, νd = 35.33. Glass: S-NBH51 (OHARA). f = -34.42 mm.

L17 is the negative S-NBH51 member with the second asphere on surface 32A. Both vertex radii are negative, so the vertex
shape is a negative meniscus, whereas paragraph 0045 calls the element biconcave. The polynomial reverses the slope of
the rear surface about 15.2 mm from the axis, so its outer zone is concave toward the image. The model uses the Table 1
radii and coefficients. [1, Table 1; ¶0045]

### L18 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.80809, νd = 22.76. Glass: S-NPH1 (OHARA). f = +88.05 mm.

The positive S-NPH1 singlet follows the final cemented pair. Paragraph 0046 gives this very dense flint the job of
compensating secondary dispersion generated elsewhere in the lens; its catalog ΔPgF of +0.0252 is consistent with that
role.

### L19 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.61800, νd = 63.33. Glass: S-PHM52 (OHARA). f = +54.29 mm.

The final S-PHM52 singlet is positive and biconvex according to its radii of opposite sign, Figure 7 and claim 1, which
describes L18 and L19 as a pair of positive singlets. Paragraph 0045 and claim 12 call it biconcave; the model uses the
Table 1 radii. Its rear surface S36 is the vertex from which the back focal distance is measured. [1, Table 1; Figure 7;
claims 1, 12]

The d-line focal lengths of the three cemented doublets are:

| L10–L11       |     -153.900 |
| L14–L15       |      +57.930 |

## Glass Identification and Selection

Table 1 gives OHARA glass names as well as rounded six-digit codes, and its note 2 states that the listed glasses are
available from OHARA. The model uses eleven distinct S-family glasses; the twelfth, S-BSL7, belongs to the omitted front
filter. Each name was resolved in the current OHARA catalog, and the nd/νd pairs were also compared with the HOYA,
SCHOTT, HIKARI, CDGM and SUMITA catalogs; the OHARA names are kept because the patent prints them. [1, ¶0038; 6–11]

The table gives the d-line nd and νd used in the model and the catalog-derived ΔPgF. Current catalog data stand in for
the melts of the original design, which the patent does not publish. All eleven printed codes are the code numbers the
current OHARA catalog still lists for these glasses. Four of them end in a digit that today's catalog νd no longer
rounds to: 741527 for S-LAL61 (now 52.637), 497816 for S-FPL51 (81.546), 439950 for S-FPL53 (94.946) and 618634 for
S-PHM52 (63.334). Each is less than 0.07 from the code's νd, and a code carries the index to three decimals only, so
the catalog values are used. With them the group focal lengths match the printed ones; the three-decimal code indices
would put G1b at +95.49 mm against the printed +95.67 mm.

| S-FPL51     | 1.49700 | 81.55 |                  +0.030820 | L3                    |
| S-LAM2      | 1.74400 | 44.79 |                  -0.002896 | L4                    |

Here PgF = (ng − nF)/(nF − nC), and ΔPgF is measured from the normal line 0.6438 − 0.001682νd, not from OHARA's own
catalog line. Each element names its OHARA glass and traces on that glass's catalog dispersion curve at every
wavelength; the seven elements the patent calls abnormal-dispersion are marked as such in the element inspector.

Paragraph 0046 connects S-FPL51 and S-FPL53 with extra freedom for chromatic correction and S-NPH1 with compensation of
secondary dispersion. That is the patent's stated strategy; the five-wavelength ray fans and polychromatic MTF
recomputed under Source Performance and Discrepancies are consistent with it. [1, ¶0046; 6]

## Focus Mechanism

The patent describes a floating internal focus: G1a is fixed, G1b moves toward the image and G2, which carries the
iris, moves toward the object as the object approaches. The model uses the three spacing states of Table 1 and adds
none; the cam law of the production lens is not published. [1, ¶0041, ¶0048–¶0049; 4]

| State | Gap after 6A (mm) | Gap after 21 (mm) | Gap after 36 (mm) | G1b shift (mm) | G2 shift (mm) |
| ----- | ----------------: | ----------------: | ----------------: | -------------: | ------------: |
| F1    |             7.955 |             4.734 |            44.800 |         +0.000 |        +0.000 |
| F2    |             8.654 |             3.136 |            45.699 |         +0.699 |        -0.899 |
| F3    |             9.612 |             1.300 |            46.577 |         +1.657 |        -1.777 |

The image plane stays 188.273 mm behind S3. Paragraph 0042 makes the flat front filter optional, allowing an embodiment
with “no such filter”, and the model omits it. Object distances therefore replace its 3 mm of S-BSL7 and the following
2 mm air gap (5 mm in all) by the d-line air-equivalent 2 + 3/n_d = 3.978461251 mm. This preserves paraxial imaging but
not the filter's small oblique and chromatic effects. [1, Table 1; ¶0042]

| State | Object to image with the filter (mm) | Object to image in the model (mm) |
| ----- | -----------------------------------: | --------------------------------: |
| F2    |                              533.273 |                     532.251461251 |
| F3    |                              342.573 |                     341.551461251 |

Paragraph 0048 quotes F2 and F3 from the first surface S1 instead, as about 34 cm and 15 cm (340.000 mm and 149.300 mm
in Table 1); the distances above and on the focus control are measured to the image plane. [1, Table 1; ¶0048]

The model therefore uses a close-focus distance of 0.341551461251 m and places F2 at 0.641710706530 of the focus
control, with F1 at 0 and F3 at 1. These values come from the patent's conjugates, not from the marketed minimum focus
distance. F1's object distance of 1000010 mm is treated as infinity, and F2 and F3 keep their printed spacings. Slider
positions between the printed states interpolate the spacings linearly; the distances shown for them are estimates and
do not represent the production cam.

| State | Model d-line EFL (mm) | Printed minus paraxial image gap (mm) |
| ----- | --------------------: | ------------------------------------: |
| F1    |             18.258034 |                             -0.044480 |
| F2    |             18.082248 |                             -0.047657 |
| F3    |             17.869685 |                             -0.050393 |

The printed image plane therefore lies 0.044–0.050 mm inside the paraxial focus at all three states. That nearly
constant offset is consistent with an image plane placed at the best focus of the full-aperture beam, not at the
paraxial focus; the prescription is not refocused.

## Aspherical Surfaces

The rear surfaces of L3 and L17 are the patent's S6 and S32, labeled 6A and 32A in the model. Table 1 uses the
conventional conic-plus-even-polynomial sag:

z(h) = (h²/R)/(1 + √(1 − (1 + K)h²/R²)) + A4h⁴ + A6h⁶ + A8h⁸ + A10h¹⁰ + A12h¹² + A14h¹⁴.

The patent's K is this standard conic constant, so no conversion is applied. Lengths are in mm and Ap has units
mm^(1−p). The prescription is unscaled, so the patent's coefficients A–F are used directly as A4–A14. [1, Table 1 notes,
p. 8]

| Coefficient |         6A |         32A |
| ----------- | ---------: | ----------: |
| K           |     0.2145 |      0.0000 |
| A4          | -9.422e-06 |   6.314e-06 |
| A6          | -1.178e-08 |   1.691e-09 |
| A8          |  6.305e-12 |  -2.004e-12 |
| A10         | -6.072e-15 | 2.24381e-15 |
| A12         |  1.659e-17 |  -1.305e-17 |
| A14         | -1.548e-20 |   1.259e-20 |

| Surface | Published semi-diameter (mm) | Total sag (mm) | Departure from same-vertex-radius sphere (mm) | Actual rim angle (°) |
| ------- | ---------------------------: | -------------: | --------------------------------------------: | -------------------: |
| 6A      |                        26.45 |      +5.931690 |                                     -6.570669 |             8.195581 |
| 32A     |                        20.05 |      -0.173049 |                                     +1.067741 |             4.859276 |

At 6A the conic and polynomial terms together flatten the rim strongly relative to a sphere of the vertex radius. At
32A the polynomial makes the rim sag much less negative than that sphere. The positive K on 6A stays within the valid
range of the conic term at the printed aperture. Both aspheric elements are glass; the patent does not state how the
surfaces are made.

## Aperture and Model Limitations

The patent states the aperture only as approximately f/1.4, and Table 1 prints a clear half-diameter of 15.80 mm for the
stop S22. An axial ray through the edge of that stop enters the lens 6.910911547 mm from the axis at the d line, and the
EFL divided by twice that height is 1.3209570317518873. The model uses this value as its full aperture, f/1.321, so
that the drawn iris matches the printed one. That ray exceeds the printed half-diameters of S17–S21 by 0.07–0.09 mm;
the largest axial beam that clears every printed aperture is f/1.328. Behind the stop the ray clears S23–S25 by only
0.01–0.05 mm, and the margin grows to 0.53 mm at the last surface. The paraxial ratio of EFL to entrance-pupil diameter
is a different quantity, 1.288581805 at the d line and 1.285818 at 546.074 nm, and the marketed T1.4 is a transmission
stop. [1, Table 1; ¶0045, ¶0057]

The printed spherical surfaces S8 and S9 come within 0.272295555 mm of each other at their rims, using 0.970466859550 of
the 9.22 mm axial gap. The model therefore lets the rims of this lens use up to 0.9705 of an air gap, instead of the
usual 0.90, without changing a radius, spacing or clear aperture.

All printed clear apertures act as hard limits. They vignette off-axis bundles, as in the patent: ¶0051 puts the
full-field beam area at about 42% of the axial beam. The stop is therefore not filled at every field and focus state,
and at wide field some rays are clipped at the cemented interface S31. The relative illumination that results is
compared with Table 2 below. [1, Table 1; ¶0051]

## Source Performance and Discrepancies

The patent's field angles are chief-ray half-angles, not full field widths. Figures 11a–c print 38.04°, 37.68° and
37.13° at full field for F1, F2 and F3; paragraph 0057 quotes 37.13° for Figure 11a, which is the Figure 11c value. The
figure labels are used here. [1, Figures 11a–c; ¶0055–¶0057]

Applying the patent's breathing equation to those printed angles gives 2.392219%. Tracing the chief ray back from the
14 mm image height at 546.074 nm, with the front filter included, gives full-field angles of 38.037682°, 37.675378° and
37.124847° and a breathing of 2.399818%, in agreement with the published +2.4%. The manufacturer describes the product
as having no breathing. [1, ¶0055; 4]

The same trace of the printed prescription, using current OHARA catalog indices, reproduces the Table 2 distortion at
all twelve nonzero field and focus combinations within 0.006189 percentage points. The patent also gives 769 mm Hg as
its standard atmospheric pressure, where 760 mm Hg is the usual value; no correction is applied for it. [1, Table 2;
¶0035]

Integrated over the pupil at 546.074 nm, with the front filter in place and every printed clear aperture acting as a
hard limit, the same prescription reproduces Table 2's relative illumination within 0.2 percentage points at ten of
the twelve points: 83.7, 70.6, 56.6 and 44.8% at F1 against the printed 83.8, 70.6, 56.6 and 45.0%, and 47.3% and 51.4%
at full field for F2 and F3 against 47.3% and 51.5%. The other two entries are misprints. Table 2 prints 753 for F3 at
normalized height 0.65, where the trace gives 75.1, and 79.1 for F2 at the same height, where it gives 71.9. Just behind
the last surface the full-field beam is 41.8% of the axial beam, the “around 42%” of ¶0051. These values belong to the
printed 15.80 mm stop: a 14.86 mm stop, f/1.4, would give 49.8, 53.0 and 56.9% at full field. With the filter omitted,
as in the model, the F1 values are 84.0, 70.8, 57.0 and 45.2% at the d line. [1, Table 2; ¶0051]

Recomputed for the five wavelengths of Figures 10a and 10b, the model's ray fans stay within 0.01 mm over the inner 85%
of the pupil, on axis and at full field, and reach 0.014–0.015 mm at the rim for 455 nm; ¶0053 puts the patent's at
around 0.01 mm except for the extreme on-axis rays. [1, Figures 10a–10b; ¶0053]

The polychromatic diffraction MTF was recomputed at the printed image plane with the wavelengths and weights printed
in Figures 11a–c. At infinity and 60 cycles/mm it gives 0.70 for the radial curve at 0.4 field, 0.66 on axis and
0.48–0.55 for the other seven curves; Figure 11a shows 0.70, 0.68 and 0.48–0.54. At 40 cycles/mm the model gives 0.84,
0.80 and 0.70–0.75 against 0.84, 0.81 and 0.70–0.75. One micrometre of focus accounts for the on-axis difference. The
curves of Figures 11b and 11c are matched within about 0.03. The diffraction-limit curve drawn in the three figures
corresponds to f/1.31 ± 0.03. The fields that Figure 11a labels 0.7 and 0.8 are the normalized image heights 0.65 and
0.85 of Table 2 (26.84° and 33.63°). [1, Figures 11a–c; ¶0057]

The d-line Petzval sum is 0.002667623619 mm⁻¹, calculated surface by surface as φ/(n·n′); it is a first-order quantity,
not a traced field-curvature result.

None of these discrepancies changes a number in the model: the Table 1 values are used throughout.
