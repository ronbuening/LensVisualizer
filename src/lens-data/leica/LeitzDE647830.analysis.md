# LEITZ 100mm f/1.5 (DE 647,830 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** DE 647,830 C, _Photographisches Objektiv_\
**Inventors:** Not named in the patent\
**Assignee:** Ernst Leitz GmbH\
**Patent effective from:** 27 September 1934\
**Published:** 14 July 1937\
**Example details:** Sole numerical example, called “Ausführungsbeispiel”; table on PDF page 2 and section drawing on page 3.

The [patent](https://depatisnet.dpma.de/DepatisNet/depatisnet?window=1&space=menu&content=treffer&action=pdf&docid=DE000000647830A) describes a seven-element, five-group Gauss objective at F = 100 and f/1.5. The table supplies every radius sign, thickness, air separation, refractive index n′ and Abbe number. No length unit is printed; the model uses a 100 mm focal-length scale. No individual inventor is named. The grant announcement is dated 24 June 1937; the cover's issue date is 14 July 1937.

The design is plausibly the German design behind the Leitz Xenon 5 cm f/1.5. Its Leitz ownership, September 1934 effective date, f/1.5 aperture and seven-element split-rear Gauss arrangement fit that association. A [Leitz Auction listing for a 1936 Xenon](https://www.leitz-auction.com/en/Xenon-1.5-5cm-Taylor-Hobson/AI-11-16440) records the front-ring engraving “D.R.P.a” beside the Taylor-Hobson name and US 2,019,985 number. The German application marking supports the timing but does not identify DE 647,830 by number. No published Xenon prescription establishes an exact match, so the model retains the patent-design name.

## Optical Architecture

Seven spherical elements form five groups in the order 1 | 2+3 | 4+5 | 6 | 7. The front meniscus is followed by two cemented negative members with their concave air surfaces facing the central stop. The rear positive component is split into two positive members, L6 and L7. The group-power sequence is positive–negative–negative–positive–positive.

The cemented interfaces are R4 between L2 and L3, and R7 between L4 and L5. The central air space A2 is 21.75 mm; the first-to-last optical vertex distance is 101.36 mm. The three short outer air spaces are each 0.378 mm.

The patent emphasizes the strongly curved, converging R7 cemented interface in the rear negative member. It attributes reduced zonal spherical aberration and improved chromatic correction to this choice (PDF page 2, description and claim). R7 = 43.50 mm is less than half the nominal focal length, and |R7|/|R6| = 1.31 satisfies the stated range of 1.0–1.5.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The indices below are the printed n′ values. The model traces them at helium d; the source does not name a line. Each focal length is calculated for the individual element in air, rather than for its cemented assembly.

### L1 — Positive Meniscus

n′ = 1.6202, ν = 60.4; Glass: BACD16 (Hoya), spectral proxy; f = +139.46 mm.

The front collector has a weakly curved rear face. Both radii are explicitly positive in the table, including R2 = +1068.5 mm.

### L2–L3 — Front Cemented Member

L2: n′ = 1.6202, ν = 60.4; Glass: BACD16 (Hoya), spectral proxy; f = +66.51 mm.\
L3: n′ = 1.5814, ν = 40.8; LF5 (Schott) catalogue match; f = −42.01 mm.

The biconvex crown L2 is cemented to the biconcave flint L3 at R4. The assembly is weakly negative, with calculated focal length −240.69 mm. L3's strongly curved rear face points toward the stop. Its inferred clear radius tapers from 31 mm at R4 to 21.4 mm at R5.

### L4–L5 — Rear Cemented Member

L4: n′ = 1.5822, ν = 42.0; LF3 (Sumita) catalogue match; f = −31.48 mm.\
L5: n′ = 1.6202, ν = 60.4; Glass: BACD16 (Hoya), spectral proxy; f = +43.86 mm.

The biconcave flint L4 faces the stop and is cemented to the positive crown L5 at R7. The assembly remains weakly negative, with calculated focal length −268.00 mm. The index increase across the positive-radius cemented surface makes that interface converging, as the patent describes.

### L6–L7 — Split Rear Collector

L6: n′ = 1.6202, ν = 60.4; Glass: BACD16 (Hoya), spectral proxy; f = +185.84 mm.\
L7: n′ = 1.6202, ν = 60.4; Glass: BACD16 (Hoya), spectral proxy; f = +131.64 mm.

L6 is a positive meniscus; its R10 = −71.20 mm is explicitly negative. L7 is biconvex. Together these two air-spaced positive elements form the split rear collector.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The table prints n′ and ν without identifying a spectral line. Helium d, 587.6 nm, is inferred from the two flints: LF5 has catalogue coordinates 1.58144/40.85, and LF3 has 1.58215/42.0. These reproduce the printed indices at four decimal places. Their calculated indices at sodium D, 589.3 nm, are about 1.58132 and 1.58203, which round differently from the printed values.

L3 matches Schott LF5 at the adopted d reference, with catalogue-minus-printed Δn = +0.00004 and Δν = +0.05. L4 matches Sumita LF3, with tabulated Δn = −0.00005 and Δν = 0.00; its dispersion curve gives Δν ≈ +0.03. These matches lie within the printed precision and do not establish the historical supplier or melt.

L1, L2, L5, L6 and L7 retain the printed 1.6202/60.4 pair and use Hoya BACD16 as a spectral proxy. At the adopted d reference, its catalogue-minus-printed differences are Δn = +0.00021 and Δν = −0.06, within the proxy limits of 0.002 in index and 0.25 in Abbe number. The present-day row supplies only the dispersion-curve shape, allowing colour analysis to follow a real glass rather than an Abbe-number estimate. It does not identify Leitz's historical glass, supplier or melt. The patent's spectral line remains uncertain.

No individual C-, F- or g-line indices or partial-dispersion values are printed. All seven elements use catalogue dispersion curves, with the five crowns using spectral proxies. These calculations do not establish the production lens's measured chromatic performance.

## Focus Mechanism

The model is focused at infinity. The patent gives no close-focus distance, movement law or additional focus-state prescription. All element positions remain fixed, and no marketed minimum-focus distance is assigned.

## Stop, Clear Apertures and Image Plane

The patent depicts no iris and prints no stop diameter or axial station. The inferred stop divides A2 into 12.0 mm after R5 and 9.75 mm before R6, placing it 47.36 mm behind the front vertex. A midpoint stop would intersect the R5 rim sag; the adopted position leaves about 0.92 mm and 1.93 mm clearance to the adjacent glass boundaries.

All clear semi-diameters are inferred. In surface order, excluding the stop, they are 36, 36, 32, 31, 21.4, 21.4, 27, 27, 28, 28, 29.7 and 29.7 mm. The square-cut rear elements follow the section drawing's approximate 28 mm and 29.7 mm radii. The front collector and cemented members retain the drawing's relative heights and tapered ends; its short air gaps and chamfers are schematic. The minimum glass edge thickness at the common clear radius is about 3.16 mm, and the central air space remains open. All rims clear the f/1.5 axial beam and the illustrative 10° chief ray.

The calculated EFL is 99.994 mm, 0.006 mm below the printed F = 100 at the chosen scale. The unprinted image plane is the paraxial infinity back focus, 53.65 mm behind R12, giving a total front-vertex-to-image distance of 155.01 mm.

The paraxial iris radius is 19.687 mm at nominal f/1.5; exact axial acceptance at that iris corresponds to about f/1.45.

## Field and Performance Limits

The patent states no field angle or image format. The model's ±10° field is illustrative, and no image-circle diameter or camera format is assigned. At 10° the chief ray reaches the image plane at about 17.64 mm height and clears every rim. About 2–6% of the meridional beam width is clipped at R7/R8 and up to 11% at R10. Across the full pupil, wide-open geometric transmission is about 95.8% at 5°, 87.9% at 8.3° and 81.5% at 10°.

The patent publishes no measured MTF or aberration plots. Calculated image quality at the paraxial plane describes this inferred stop and rim geometry. It does not establish the physical Xenon aperture, full-field illumination or production performance.

## Design Heritage and Sources

The Xenon engraving also refers to [Horace William Lee's US 2,019,985 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2019985A/en), a separate split-rear Gauss design. Similar topology does not make its prescription identical to the Leitz example. The Leitz claim specifically depends on the strongly curved rear cemented interface.
