# LEICA SUPER-ANGULON-M 21mm f/3.4

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [DE 1,273,853 B](https://patents.google.com/patent/DE1273853B/)
**Filed:** 8 March 1961
**Published:** 25 July 1968
**Inventors:** Karl Heinrich Macher
**Assignee:** Jos. Schneider & Co., Optische Werke
**Example details:** Sole example, printed page 2, Fig. 1; dimensions multiplied by 0.21 from the patent's f = 100 to 21 mm.

The design likely belongs to the Super-Angulon-M: Schneider's sole example combines f/3.4, a 90° field and eight elements in four groups, and its 1961 filing precedes the product's 1963 introduction. The German and US family publications identify Macher. Product references confirm Schneider's design and manufacture for Leica, the 8/4 construction and launch date. Leitz lists the product as code 11103; no manufacturer statement identifies this exact table as the factory prescription. [1–5, 7]

## Optical architecture

The approximately symmetrical wide-angle design has negative outer menisci surrounding two positive cemented triplets. The patent names these four members I–IV: front meniscus I, triplets II and III, and rear meniscus IV. Each triplet contains a positive biconvex element between two negative menisci. The iris lies between the triplets. Their combined focal lengths in air are 19.08 and 23.14 mm; the outer negative elements expand the field. [1, 5]

The computed EFL is 21.02 mm, 0.10% above the nominal 21 mm, at f/3.4. The first-to-last surface length is 44.894 mm. The printed 8.190 mm back focus places the image at z = 53.084 mm; the paraxial back focus is 8.193 mm. At the patent's 45° half-field the chief-ray image height is 20.72 mm. Distortion is about −1.32% relative to the nominal 21 mm, consistent with Fig. 2, or −1.42% relative to the computed EFL. [1]

## Glass identification

The glass values use the patent's yellow d line; exact catalog matches confirm the helium d line (587.6 nm). The patent prints only “(gelbe) d-Linie”. Seven catalog curves differ from the printed values by at most 5×10⁻⁶ in nd and 0.0869 in νd. These matches supply dispersion curves without proving the historical supplier or melt. L2 remains unmatched. The German table prints νd = 42.82 for L5; the US family claim prints 42.32. The German value is retained. [1, 5]

## Focus mechanism and conjugates

The prescription describes infinity focus only; the patent supplies no near-focus state or movement law. The product focuses to 0.4 m and stops down to f/22. Leitz distinguishes this manual close-focus limit from rangefinder coupling, which ends at 0.7 m on the listed M2/M4/M5 bodies and 1 m on the M3. Its diaphragm has four blades. [2, 3, 6, 7]

## Aspherical surfaces

All twelve refracting surfaces are spherical. No aspherical terms are printed or inferred. [1]

## Apertures, image plane and model limits

The iris is inferred at the midpoint of the printed diaphragm space, z = 24.027 mm; Fig. 1 draws its two iris bars at about 45% and 58% of that space. Its 3.828 mm radius follows the f/3.4 aperture. The patent gives no clear diameters, but Fig. 1 is drawn to scale, and every rim follows it. The semi-diameters are S1 14.2, S2 7.5, S3 7.0, S4 and S5 6.0, S7 to S9 4.8, S10 6.5, S11 7.0 and S12 11.0 mm, each within about 8% of the drawing. L4's rear face S6 ends at 4.2 mm, where Fig. 1 bevels the element; a flat land carries the drawn edge out to 6.0 mm. L2 has a beveled rim with unequal face extents in Fig. 1, consistent with S3 at 7.0 mm and S4 at 6.0 mm. Fig. 1 also starts a small chamfer on L5's front face at about 3.8 mm, inside the f/3.4 axial beam, so S7 follows L5's drawn edge instead. The ratios of EFL to admitted meridional bundle width are 1:3.23 at 30°, 1:3.76 at 40° and about 1:5.9 at 45°, compared with about 1:6.0 at 45° in Fig. 2. S12's 11.0 mm semi-diameter admits the same meridional width as 11.5 mm at these angles; the larger rim admits more light only at the 46.3° corner, outside the 45° field. These bundle-width comparisons are not measurements of radiometric illumination. Wide open, the modeled geometric transmission is 98% at 30°, 85% at 39° and 52% at 45°. [1]

The 45° field reaches 96% of the 24×36 mm corner height. A geometrically clear corner does not imply full-pupil transmission. The short 8.19 mm back focus places the rear vertex about 19.6 mm behind the Leica M flange, explaining the deep rear assembly that fouls the M5/CL meter arm on early barrels; later barrels have a clearance cutout.
