# LEITZ 100mm f/1.3 (US 2,297,452 Design) — Audit

## Source

[US 2,297,452 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2297452A/en), Photographic Objective, was published on 29 September 1942. It names Max Berek, Otto Zimmermann and Gustav Kleineberg, with a US filing date of 22 April 1941 and a German filing date of 6 May 1940. The specification says “vested in the Alien Property Custodian” and names no company as assignee. The inventors were Leitz designers; the maker attribution and the empty company-assignee list express different facts.

## Transcription

Two independent readings of the page images agree on all ten signed radii, six thicknesses, three air spaces, six index/Abbe pairs and the sole numerical example. Figure 1 shows six glass elements in four members, with cemented interfaces r2 and r5 and a plane r7. Figure 2 shows the correction curves; its numerical D/C/F table is on the specification page.

The second air space is 12.82, not the OCR reading 2.82. The last two indices are separately printed as 1.6073 and 1.6074. The prescription heading has no spectral subscript; the adjoining Fraunhofer D/C/F table supports taking D as the reference line (taken as sodium, 589.3 nm). No printed prescription value needs correction.

## First-order

The patent states f = 100 and aperture 1:1.3 without naming a physical unit. The model expresses its lengths in millimetres. Before and after cleanup, EFL is 100.0056176325 mm, paraxial f-number is 1.300 and computed BFD is 34.8788227557 mm. The printed D-line axial back distance is 34.875 mm, a difference of 0.003823 mm.

The first-to-last-surface track is 92.22 mm. Rounding the stored image gap from 34.87882275568537 to 34.8788 mm changes the first-vertex-to-image distance from 127.0988227557 to 127.0988 mm, a reduction of 0.000022756 mm. The image remains at the computed paraxial plane to the stored precision.

At entrance heights 19.7/29.6/38/38.5 mm, the independent D-line ray calculation gives vertex-to-axis-crossing distances of 34.659/34.587/35.008/35.067 mm against the printed 34.652/34.580/34.998/35.055 mm. Agreement is within about 0.012 mm. The Abbe-based paraxial C−F separation is about 0.263 mm against 0.269 mm printed; individual partial dispersions are unknown.

## Not printed

Figure 1 gives no iris position or bore. The model divides the final 12.82 mm air space into 11.75 and 1.07 mm around the inferred stop at z = 77.83 mm. Its paraxial radius is 16.0915945523 mm. Exact entrance-pupil and image-sine equivalents are approximately f/1.36452 and f/1.36471. These describe the inferred iris, rather than a printed aperture dimension.

All clear semi-diameters, the f-stop list and the illustrative ±5° field are inferred. The patent says only moderate angles of view and provides no image format, image circle, close-focus prescription or glass trade names. Off-axis bundles are vignetted even though axial and chief rays clear the illustrative field. A 10° marginal bundle is clipped.

## Product

No production lens is documented for this prescription. The Summitar and Summarit are Gauss types and do not establish a match for the two-doublet, negative-singlet, positive-singlet construction. The related Leitz US 1,899,934 split-front triplet has a different prescription. Meopta US 2,543,856 cites this patent but has a different five-element construction.

## Corrections

The numerical prescription agrees with the page images. The only change to an axial distance is rounding the computed image gap. All ten radii, glass thicknesses and indices, Abbe numbers, internal air separations, stop position and clear rims retain their delivered values.

The publication suffix, catalogue identity, element descriptions, cemented-group identifiers and accompanying text have been brought into the catalogue's form. The analysis now distinguishes the printed values from the inferred stop, rims, field and image plane.

## Remaining questions

Open: Figure 1 draws r4–r6 level at about 35.5 mm, compared with the retained 37/33/33 mm semi-diameters. The r8 drawing reaches about 25–26 mm, while the retained model uses 22 mm and a longer tapered edge to keep the rim ahead of the inferred stop. A different stop position and rim treatment could change off-axis transmission, but the figure provides no dimensioned clear apertures. Glass suppliers, partial dispersion, a numerical patent field and a production lens identity remain unknown.
