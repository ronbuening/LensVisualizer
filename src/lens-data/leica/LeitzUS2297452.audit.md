# LEITZ 100mm f/1.3 (US 2,297,452 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,297,452 A, _Photographic Objective_](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2297452A/en), by Max Berek, Otto Zimmermann and Gustav Kleineberg, was filed on 22 April 1941 with German priority of 6 May 1940 and published on 29 September 1942. The sole numerical example and D/C/F correction table are on specification page 1, the second page of the facsimile. The first page carries Figure 1, the lens section, and Figure 2, the correction curves.

The publication says “vested in the Alien Property Custodian” and names no company as assignee. Berek's [US 1,899,934 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US1899934A/en) and Zimmermann and Kleineberg's [US 3,012,476 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3012476A/en) document their Leitz association. This supports the Leitz attribution without establishing a production lens or a company assignment for US 2,297,452 A.

## Transcription Check

The page images agree with all ten signed radii, six glass thicknesses, three air spaces and six index/Abbe pairs. Figure 1 confirms six glass elements in four air-spaced members, cemented interfaces r2 and r5, and the plane r7. No numerical transcription mismatch was found.

The second air space is printed as 12.82; the OCR reading 2.82 loses its leading digit. The last two indices are separately printed as 1.6073 and 1.6074. The prescription heading has no spectral subscript; the adjacent Fraunhofer D/C/F correction table supports taking sodium D, 589.3 nm, as the index reference. The printed f = 100 scale has no physical unit; the model expresses its lengths in millimetres.

## First-Order Check

The model's EFL is 100.01 mm against the printed 100, and the paraxial aperture is f/1.30 against 1:1.3. Computed D-line back focus is 34.879 mm, 0.004 mm beyond the printed axial value of 34.875 mm. The image plane is at that paraxial focus to within 0.0001 mm. The first-to-last-surface track is 92.22 mm, placing the image 127.10 mm behind the first vertex.

At entrance heights of 19.7, 29.6, 38 and 38.5 mm, exact D-line rays cross the axis 34.659, 34.587, 35.008 and 35.067 mm behind r10's vertex. The printed distances are 34.652, 34.580, 34.998 and 35.055 mm, agreeing within about 0.012 mm. Although the patent defines the zonal distances from the ray's emergence point projected onto the axis, its numbers agree with distances from r10's vertex. The Abbe-based paraxial C−F separation is about 0.263 mm against the printed 0.269 mm; the glasses' partial dispersions are unknown.

The surfaces stay clear of each other and the iris sets the axial aperture. The axial bundle and chief rays across the illustrative ±5.0° field clear the rims. Off-axis bundles are vignetted; a 10.0° marginal bundle is clipped.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

Figure 1 gives no iris position or diameter. The inferred stop is 77.83 mm behind the first vertex, dividing the final 12.82 mm air space into 11.75 mm after r8 and 1.07 mm before r9. Its paraxial radius is 16.09 mm. Exact entrance-pupil and image-sine equivalents are both approximately f/1.36, about 5.0% slower than f/1.3. These describe the inferred iris, whose position and opening are unprinted.

All clear semi-diameters are inferred. Figure 1 shows r4–r6 at approximately equal heights, about 35.5 mm, while the model uses 37/33/33 mm. The drawn r8 rim reaches about 25–26 mm and lies behind r9's vertex; the model uses 22 mm with a longer tapered edge to keep the rim ahead of the inferred stop. The drawing gives no dimensioned clear apertures, leaving these differences unresolved.

The ±5.0° half-field is illustrative: the patent states only moderate angles of view. No numerical field, image format or image circle is supplied, so no image-circle diameter is assigned. The selectable f-stops extend from f/1.3 to f/22, but no minimum iris opening is published. `focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity; no close-focus spacings or focusing travel are given.

All six glasses retain the printed index/Abbe pairs and remain unmatched to named catalog curves at sodium D. Dispersion estimates use the Abbe numbers. No glass supplier, melt identity, individual C-, F- or g-line index, measured partial dispersion or anomalous-dispersion designation is established. The printed C/F distances describe the complete lens, not the individual glasses.

## Product Identification

No production lens is documented for this prescription. The Summitar and Summarit have Gauss constructions, distinct from the two-doublet, negative-singlet, positive-singlet layout here. Berek's Leitz US 1,899,934 A uses a different split-front triplet prescription. Meopta US 2,543,856 A cites this patent but has five glass elements. None establishes a product match.

No production mount, image format, diaphragm blade count or shape, marketed focal length, marketed f-number or minimum focus distance is assigned. The title preserves the patent's f = 100 design scale expressed in millimetres.

## Corrections, 2026-11-21

- The author-based name and identifier became “LEITZ 100mm f/1.3 (US 2,297,452 Design)” and `leitz-100mm-f13-us2297452`. The maker changed from unspecified to Leica, reflecting the inventors' Leitz association; `visible` became true. No production identity is implied.
- The patent number gained the A publication suffix. The three inventors, 1942 publication year, sole example and absence of a company assignee agree with the publication.
- The subtitle identifies Berek, Zimmermann and Kleineberg. The specifications state six elements in four groups, all spherical surfaces, design f = 100 mm, design f/1.3 and the split-front triplet construction. The focus description states infinity only.
- The catalog design focal length changed from 100 to 100.01 mm to record the calculated EFL; the patent's printed f = 100 scale and f/1.3 aperture are retained.
- Element labels became Element 1 through Element 6. Generic shape descriptions became biconvex positive, negative meniscus, biconvex positive, biconcave negative, plano-concave negative and biconvex positive, consistent with the signed radii. Their optical roles and in-air focal lengths were added: +103.10, −268.67, +78.09, −122.94, −47.09 and +56.07 mm. The two cemented members have focal lengths of +159.86 and +165.59 mm.
- Cemented-pair identifiers changed from D1/D2 to C1/C2, with brackets spanning r1–r3 and r4–r6. The four group captions identify the front doublet, second doublet, negative singlet and rear positive singlet.
- Generic unidentified-glass descriptions became explicit `Unmatched` labels carrying each printed index/Abbe pair: 1.5338/55.4, 1.7283/28.3, 1.6230/58.1, 1.7283/28.3, 1.6073/49.2 and 1.6074/56.7. Each label change has Δn = 0 and Δν = 0. The sodium-D reference is retained; no named glass or anomalous-dispersion identity is assigned.
- The final image gap was rounded from the full-precision paraxial back focus, shortening the first-vertex-to-image distance by about 0.000023 mm. The image plane remains within 0.0001 mm of paraxial focus.

The resulting EFL is 100.01 mm, paraxial aperture f/1.30, BFD 34.879 mm, stop radius 16.09 mm and illustrative half-field 5.0°. The signed radii, glass thicknesses, internal air separations, index/Abbe pairs, stop position and clear rims are unchanged.

## Remaining Questions

- Figure 1 and the model differ at the r4–r6 and r8 rims. Dimensioned clear apertures and an iris location are unavailable; a different iris position and rim outline could alter off-axis transmission.
- Historical glass suppliers, melt identities and partial dispersions remain unknown. No named catalog curves reproduce the printed sodium-D pairs within their printed precision.
- The zonal-distance wording conflicts with the vertex-based numerical table.
- No source establishes a numerical field, production lens identity, mount, image format, image circle, diaphragm blade count or shape, minimum iris opening, marketed specifications or close-focus mechanism.
