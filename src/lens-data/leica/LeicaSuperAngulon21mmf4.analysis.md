# LEICA SUPER-ANGULON 21mm f/4

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [DE 1,107,416 B, “Optisches System großer Bildwinkelleistung”](https://patents.google.com/patent/DE1107416B/)
**Inventors:** Günter Klemt; Karl Heinrich Macher
**Assignee:** Jos. Schneider & Co., Optische Werke
**Filed:** 14 February 1957
**Published:** 25 May 1961
**Example details:** Sole numerical example, repeated in claim 2; Fig. 1 section and Fig. 2 aberrations.

The patent is a Zusatz, or addition, to application Sch 16212 IX/42h. Its f′ = 100 prescription is uniformly scaled by 0.21 to 21 mm; all dimensional values follow that scale, while the printed d-line indices and Abbe numbers are unchanged. The front page names “Günter Klemt und Karl Macher”; the inventor names above follow the catalog's established spelling.

The link to the Leica Super-Angulon 21mm f/4 is strong: Schneider's 1957 filing precedes the Photokina 1958 introduction, and the sole example matches the product's f/4 aperture and nine elements in four groups. The [published product section](http://allphotolenses.com/public/files/optical/86649b22361edca67e5766be172191bd.jpg) also has the same 2+2+3+2 arrangement and element shapes. This establishes a close design correlation; a factory statement identifying the exact numerical prescription has not been found.

The [Leitz lens brochure](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/01191/01191.pdf), PDF p. 2, specifies nine elements, a 92° angle, f/4–22 and versions 11102 for Leica M2/M3 and 11002 in screw mount. The corresponding codes are SUMOM and SUOON. The [product review and specifications](https://www.kenrockwell.com/leica/21mm-f4.htm) document Schneider manufacture, the 1958 introduction, four groups and 0.4 m minimum focus; the [screw-mount history](https://www.summichronica.com/screw-mount-lenses-21mm) also records the Photokina introduction and both mounts. These sources support the Leica M / LTM and 24×36 mm catalog metadata. Fig. 2 extends to 45° half-field, or 90° full field, distinct from the marketed 92° angle.

## Optical Architecture

The design has four cemented components separated by air: a negative front doublet, a positive doublet before the stop, a positive triplet after the stop and a negative rear doublet. All thirteen refracting surfaces are spherical. The stop lies between the two positive components. The broadly symmetric arrangement gives a short back focus rather than the mirror clearance of a retrofocus lens.

The patent adds cemented interfaces to the outer negative menisci and uses three elements in the positive component behind the stop. Its discussion associates these choices with increased aperture and correction of spherical aberration, coma, astigmatism and field curvature. The approximately 100° coverage mentioned in the opening text belongs to the earlier six-element f/8 system; it is separate from the f/4 example's plotted field.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are computed for each thick element in air. Cemented component powers include the shared interfaces and therefore differ from those individual values.

### C1 — Front Negative Doublet, L1–L2

L1: nd = 1.65830, νd = 57.49. Glass: Unmatched. f = −19.77 mm. The negative meniscus forms the front of the outer component.

L2: nd = 1.46450, νd = 65.79. Glass: FK3 (Schott catalog equivalent). f = +698.84 mm. This weak positive meniscus is cemented to L1 at S2. Together S1–S3 form a negative component with f = −18.48 mm.

### C2 — Front Positive Doublet, L3–L4

L3: nd = 1.58904, νd = 53.16. Glass: Unmatched. f = −22.17 mm. The negative meniscus faces the outer front component and joins L4 at S5.

L4: nd = 1.60729, νd = 49.25. Glass: BAF5 (Hoya catalog equivalent). f = +9.50 mm. The thick biconvex element supplies the positive power before the stop. S4–S6 together have f = +16.77 mm.

### C3 — Rear Positive Triplet, L5–L7

L5: nd = 1.52944, νd = 51.80. Glass: Unmatched. f = −11.70 mm. This negative meniscus begins the triplet immediately behind the stop.

L6: nd = 1.61772, νd = 49.78. Glass: N-SSK8 (Schott catalog equivalent). f = +5.45 mm. The strongly positive biconvex center is cemented between two negative menisci.

L7: nd = 1.78470, νd = 26.10. Glass: SF56A (Schott catalog equivalent). f = −15.69 mm. The high-index, low-Abbe rear element completes the triplet. The S7–S10 component has f = +24.50 mm. The patent identifies the positive action of the S8 cemented interface and the shallow outer surfaces of this component as correction choices.

### C4 — Rear Negative Doublet, L8–L9

L8: nd = 1.53375, νd = 55.37. Glass: Unmatched. f = −140.80 mm. This weak negative meniscus forms the front of the rear outer component.

L9: nd = 1.65844, νd = 50.84. Glass: N-SSK5 (Schott catalog equivalent). f = −23.21 mm. It joins L8 at S12 and provides most of the component's negative power. S11–S13 together have f = −17.61 mm. Both individual elements are negative at their printed thicknesses.

The five named rows agree within 0.0001 in nd and 0.15 in νd. Their catalog dispersion curves are spectral equivalents, not evidence of the original suppliers or melts. Four glasses retain the patent coordinates without a named catalog curve. In particular, Ohara S-LAL11 is close to L1 in index but differs by −0.1574 in νd; Hoya SBF2 differs from L5 by −0.1569. Both fall outside that Abbe limit. The patent supplies no partial-dispersion values, so the remaining glasses use Abbe-based dispersion and secondary-spectrum behavior remains uncertain.

## Focus Mechanism

The numerical prescription describes infinity focus only and gives no close-focus spacings or moving-group law. The production lens focuses to 0.4 m, which is recorded as catalog information. The model keeps the printed infinity spacings and supplies no finite focus state.

## Image Plane, Apertures and Field

The calculated EFL is 20.9965 mm, against the scaled 21.00 mm source value, and the derived stop gives f/4.000. The printed back focus sets the final air gap to 7.7280 mm. The paraxial BFD is 7.7374 mm, leaving a −0.0094 mm difference; the image plane retains the printed value. Total first-vertex-to-image length is 52.2585 mm, computed from the prescription rather than printed separately.

The stop position is inferred at the midpoint of the 1.659 mm air gap between S6 and S7, consistent with Fig. 1. Its 3.41147 mm radius is derived from f/4, not a printed iris dimension. The production aperture range through f/22 follows Leitz literature.

The patent prints no clear semi-diameters. S12 = 8.8 mm and S13 = 11.3 mm follow the Fig. 1 optical rims; the other rims remain estimates. At 45° the chief ray meets S12/S13 at 7.96/10.32 mm. At the 24×36 mm corner, about 46.2°, those heights are 8.10/10.61 mm, within the adopted rims. At the declared 46° half-field the chief-ray image height is 21.52 mm, about 99% of the 21.65 mm format radius, and the actual format-corner chief ray is clear. This establishes chief-ray access, not full-pupil illumination or a measured production light-transmission curve. The viewer's routine ray display uses a smaller 36.20° half-field.

## Aberrations and Model Scope

At the patent's f′ = 100 scale, the calculated longitudinal spherical aberration is approximately −0.25 mm at f/4 and −0.90 mm at the intermediate zone corresponding to f/5.57. Fig. 2 reads approximately −0.28 and −0.94 mm. At 45° the calculated distortion is −9.9‰, compared with approximately −7.6‰ from Fig. 2; the sign and curve shape agree, while the magnitude differs. These comparisons describe the printed prescription and the figure's reading precision, rather than measured product performance.

Unknown clear apertures, inferred stop dimensions, four unresolved glasses and absent finite-focus spacings limit predictions of vignetting, chromatic behavior and close-focus performance. No aspherical terms or variable internal gaps are introduced.
