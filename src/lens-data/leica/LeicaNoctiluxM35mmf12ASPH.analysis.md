**Title:** Wide-angle photographic objective  
**Embodiment analyzed:** Second embodiment example, Figure 2; printed prescription page 4  
**Status:** Literal transcription of the rounded patent table, scaled 35×; production correlation, not a factory prescription

The association with the Leica Noctilux-M 35 f/1.2 ASPH. is strong but remains a
production-to-patent correlation. The patent does not name that marketed lens.
The following independently published details converge:

1. The embodiment and Leica technical sheet both show ten elements in five
   air-separated assemblies: three doublets, one triplet and one singlet.
2. Three aspherical surfaces occur on the front of L3, rear of L7 and rear of
   L10. Their placement matches the manufacturer's optical section.
3. The flat object-side face of L5 agrees with Example 2 specifically; Examples
   1 and 3 use concave and convex faces there respectively.
4. The patent explicitly scales its normalized 1 mm design by 35 for a
   24 × 36 mm format. Example 2 specifies f/1.24 and a 32° half angle; the
   manufacturer's values are marketed f/1.2 and a 63.3° diagonal full angle.
5. The rear doublet and singlet float together, as shown in both sources.

The accompanying data retains every printed radius, axial thickness, index,
Abbe number and aspheric coefficient. It does not silently force the tabulated
prescription to reproduce the separately rounded first-order summary.

## Optical Architecture

The layout is a compact quasi-symmetrical high-speed wide-angle design with
three functional assemblies and five air-separated groups. The functional
front assembly VG contains G1 and G2, middle assembly MG is cemented triplet
G3, and rear assembly HG comprises G4 and G5 (¶0039–0044).

VG has the element-power sequence negative–positive–positive–negative. MG has
positive–negative–positive powers. HG combines a positive–negative doublet
with a weak negative rear singlet. The stop is source surface 7, between VG
and MG. There is no inferred stop-plane relocation.

First-order calculation of the literal rounded data gives VG,
MG and HG focal lengths of 1177.9426, 58.8824 and 64.2120 mm at 35× scale.
The corresponding source-summary values are 1216.25, 59.15 and 65.10 mm.
The very weak net power of VG makes its quoted focal length particularly
sensitive to cancellation between the constituent powers.

The first and last surface vertices enclose 55.3 mm in the literal table.
Including the printed 15.4 mm image gap gives 70.7 mm, whereas the summary's
normalized 2.01 scales to 70.35 mm. These are optical distances and should not
be equated with the manufacturer's approximately 50.2 mm mechanical length.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Element focal lengths below are calculated for each isolated element in air
at the native e line. They describe first-order power rather than the power
assigned to that element inside a cemented assembly. Glass suppliers and
commercial glass identities are not disclosed.

### L1 — Biconcave Negative

ne = 1.608, νe = 38. Glass: unmatched patent glass. f = −30.838 mm.

L1 forms the front negative member of G1. Its concave object-side surface and
stronger cemented curvature distribute the incoming bundle before the much
higher-index L2. The doublet structure is published in ¶0040; this power
interpretation follows the transcribed prescription.

### L2 — Biconvex Positive

ne = 2.007, νe = 28. Glass: unmatched very-high-index patent glass. f = +23.979 mm.

L2 provides strong positive power against L1. Its index exceeds the patent's
front-positive-element condition of 1.95. The almost flat rear radius is
negative, −1577.38 mm after scaling, and is kept as printed although the
drawing shows the face as nearly planar.

### L3 — Biconvex Positive with Front Asphere

ne = 1.743, νe = 48. Glass: unmatched patent glass. f = +30.597 mm.

L3 starts G2 and carries source surface 4. The patent assigns this aspheric
region to monochromatic correction, particularly spherical aberration and
coma (¶0015, ¶0049). The relatively high Abbe number provides a dispersive
contrast with its negative partner L4.

### L4 — Biconcave Negative

ne = 1.760, νe = 28. Glass: unmatched patent glass. f = −21.036 mm.

L4 completes the front power sequence and faces the aperture. Its published
ΔPgF of +0.0003 lies within the patent's selected interval for negative glass.
No catalog identity or full spectral curve follows uniquely from those
rounded coordinates.

### L5 — Plano-convex Positive

ne = 1.890, νe = 40. Glass: unmatched high-index patent glass. f = +26.545 mm.

L5 opens cemented triplet G3 directly behind the diaphragm. Surface 8 is
explicitly planar in Example 2, a distinguishing feature of this embodiment.
The patent allows the corresponding face to be concave, flat or convex in
its broader design family (¶0029, ¶0054).

### L6 — Biconcave Negative

ne = 1.732, νe = 29. Glass: unmatched patent glass. f = −15.769 mm.

The strong negative center of the triplet is cemented to both positive outer
members. Its ΔPgF is +0.0003. The combination is a chromatic and monochromatic
correction strategy; it is not evidence by itself of a verified apochromatic
production model.

### L7 — Biconvex Positive with Rear Asphere

ne = 1.743, νe = 48. Glass: unmatched patent glass. f = +24.532 mm.

L7 uses the same printed ne/νe/ΔPgF coordinates as L3. Its rear face is source
surface 11, immediately before the floating separation. The patent associates
this asphere with spherical-aberration and coma correction (¶0015, ¶0049).

### L8 — Biconvex Positive

ne = 1.945, νe = 33. Glass: unmatched high-index patent glass. f = +17.132 mm.

L8 supplies strong positive power in the floating rear assembly. Its index
satisfies the stated rear-positive-glass threshold of at least 1.9, a design
choice the patent relates to Petzval-sum control.

### L9 — Biconcave Negative

ne = 1.644, νe = 34. Glass: unmatched patent glass. f = −20.397 mm.

L9 is cemented to L8 and partly balances that element's refractive power.
Its published partial-dispersion deviation of +0.009 is the largest positive
value in the design.

### L10 — Weak Negative Meniscus with Rear Asphere

ne = 1.918, νe = 27. Glass: unmatched high-index patent glass. f = −656.593 mm.

The final singlet has weak negative paraxial power but a substantial high-order
aspheric correction on surface 16. The patent links the rear asphere to
monochromatic aberrations that increase across the image field (¶0015).
L10 travels with G4 as the floating rear assembly.

## Glass Identification and Selection

All ten glasses remain explicitly unmatched. The patent uses ne and νe at
546.07 nm (¶0047), not nd and νd. The authored indexReference is therefore e
for every element, and no d-line catalog glass is substituted.

| Elements | ne | νe | Published ΔPgF |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| L1 | 1.608 | 38 | +0.002 |
| L2 | 2.007 | 28 | +0.005 |
| L3, L7 | 1.743 | 48 | −0.005 |
| L4 | 1.760 | 28 | +0.0003 |
| L5 | 1.890 | 40 | −0.009 |
| L6 | 1.732 | 29 | +0.0003 |
| L8 | 1.945 | 33 | +0.001 |
| L9 | 1.644 | 34 | +0.009 |
| L10 | 1.918 | 27 | +0.007 |

The partial-dispersion values are retained as published facts. They do not
supply complete dispersion curves for these e-line glasses, so photopic and
C/d/F MTF are unavailable; only e-line monochromatic results are quantitative.
The model traces the e line at 546.074 nm; the source rounds it to 546.07 nm.

## Focus Mechanism

Source ¶0044 moves VG, the stop and MG together toward the object, while HG
moves in the same direction by a smaller amount. Only infinity spacings are
numerically tabulated. Leica specifies 0.5 m minimum focus and a largest
reproduction ratio of 1:11.6, but neither source provides the factory cam law.

The model is therefore infinity-only. The 0.5 m figure is carried as the
marketed minimum focus distance, but no close-focus spacings are modeled,
and finite-focus performance cannot be read from this prescription.

## Aspherical Surfaces

The patent uses the standard conic sag plus a2 h⁴ + a3 h⁶ + … + a6 h¹².
Its k is the standard conic constant K, and all three values are zero.
The coefficients a2 through a6 multiply h⁴ through h¹², not h² through h⁶.

| Source surface | K | a2 / h⁴ | a3 / h⁶ | a4 / h⁸ | a5 / h¹⁰ | a6 / h¹² |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 0 | −4.673e−1 | −9.672e−1 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 11 | 0 | −1.578e−1 | −6.938e−1 | +1.3729e0 | 0 | 0 |
| 16 | 0 | +1.0152e0 | −1.0694e0 | +7.02429e1 | −4.521379e2 | +1.5399660e3 |

These are the normalized source coefficients. Every dimensional quantity is
scaled by s = 35. Each coefficient of power p is divided by s^(p−1), and K
is unchanged. The negative fourth- and sixth-order departures on surface 4,
the mixed terms on surface 11 and the higher-order rear terms serve different
zones of the ray bundle. No manufacturing process is inferred from the
coefficient table alone.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The printed table gives an EFL of 34.746 mm at 35× scale rather than the
stated 35 mm. Its Gaussian back focus is 15.096 mm, so paraxial focus lies
0.30 mm ahead of the printed 15.4 mm final gap. The printed total length
(2.01, or 70.35 mm) matches the Gaussian focus rather than the printed gap.
The discrepancy follows from two-decimal rounding of the normalized spacings:
varying them within ±0.005 and ne within ±0.0005 moves the Gaussian image
plane to either side of the printed gap. On-axis spherical aberration is
small (−0.10 mm at full aperture), so no sign misprint is indicated. The
printed gap is kept as published. A rounding adjustment consistent with the
patent's first-order summary is possible but is not used here.

Semi-diameters are estimates measured from Figure 2, which is drawn to the
prescription's proportions, and checked against a real-ray trace; no clear
apertures are printed. The figure draws L2 square-cut inside a flat flange on
L1 and ends the rear face of L4 below a stepped outer rim, and the model
follows both. With these estimates the full axial
f/1.24 pupil passes, and the chief ray reaches the full-frame corner at an
image height of 21.65 mm.

At the printed image plane the axial image is defocused; best axial focus lies
about 0.34 mm ahead of it. At that plane, the e-line geometric 40 lp/mm MTF is
approximately 0.552 on axis but only 0.068 sagittal and 0.043 tangential at
half-diagonal height. Leica's white-light infinity charts (f/1.2, f/2.8 and
f/5.6; 5 to 40 lp/mm) are materially stronger over much of the field. The
difference reflects the lost precision of the rounded table together with
unknown glass dispersion, so the model shows the design's layout and first-order
behavior rather than production image quality. No value was adjusted against
the published curves.

Leica gives the entrance pupil as 24.5 mm in front of the bayonet. The model's
paraxial entrance pupil lies 52.66 mm ahead of the printed image plane, or
24.86 mm in front of the Leica M bayonet (27.80 mm flange distance).

## Rear-Asphere Clear-Aperture Uncertainty

The printed surface-16 polynomial has an annular sag minimum at radius
9.329902890 mm. Its slope is negative inside and positive outside this point.
The assumed 11.35 mm clear rim extends beyond that extremum, with a modest
5.88° rim angle. A sag extremum alone is not a conic-domain failure or a
self-intersecting surface, but the patent supplies no clear-aperture value
that independently certifies this annulus. Ordinary clearance and rim-angle
checks therefore do not settle the valid optical domain. The corner chief ray
meets surface 16 at about 10.3 mm, outside the extremum, so a rim inside it
would block the corner chief ray.
