## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** DE L 12741 (Leitz application L 12741 IXa/42h, an addition to application L 753)
**Filed:** 9 April 1941 per the cover and DEPATISnet; the 27 June 1952 filing letter claims 2 April 1941, the date the subject was disclosed in parent application L 753
**Published:** 6 August 1953, announced and laid open for opposition under § 30 of the patent law (DEPATISnet DE000L0012741MAZ); no later grant was found
**Inventor:** Max Berek
**Applicant:** Ernst Leitz GmbH
**Title:** Photographisches Objektiv
**Example details:** Sole table in the claim (p. 5 of the file copy), normalized to f = 1 at 1:1.5; all lengths scaled ×85

The application is a recomputation ("besondere Durchrechnung") of the lens claimed in parent application L 753, which Leitz filed on 2 September 1936 as Reich application L 91 070 ("Lichtstarkes Objektiv") and which was laid open on 20 November 1952 (DEPATISnet DE000L0000753MAZ). The parent prints a different f = 1 example and the drawing that locates the diaphragm. The 1953 inventor statement names Berek as deceased.

No document names the Summarex or a focal length, so the attribution rests on converging evidence:

1. **Construction.** Leitz describes the Summarex 85 mm f/1.5 as a seven-lens variant of the Gauss type; the table has seven elements in five groups at 1:1.5.
2. **Section drawing.** The 1953 brochure's Summarex drawing shows the same sequence: a front meniscus with a nearly flat rear face, a cemented meniscus on each side of the stop, a flat-backed rear pair, a flat-fronted positive element and a biconvex last element (here r8 = +3.0855 and r9 = ∞ at f = 1).
3. **Authorship.** The application is Leitz's own and names Max Berek, to whom the Summarex design is credited.
4. **Dating.** The parent's 1936 filing matches the 1936 dating of Berek's Summarex design in collector literature, and the 1941 disclosure date of this recomputation precedes the lens's first catalogue listing in 1943.

The same seven-element arrangement also appears in H. W. Lee's Taylor-Hobson f/1.4 patent (US 2,019,985), which collector sources cite as the basis of the Leitz Xenon 5 cm f/1.5, so the layout alone does not single out the Summarex. The correlation is inferred, not manufacturer-confirmed. The ×85 scale follows the marketed focal length and is not a fit (traced EFL 84.998 mm).

The May 1956 Leitz directory gives a 28° field, f/1.5–f/16, a 5 ft (1.524 m) minimum focus and screw mounting only; the 1953 brochure gives 28.5°. Neither application gives a field angle. The directory's 59.8 mm entry is the filter (front-barrel) size, not a clear aperture.

## Optical Architecture

The lens has seven spherical elements in five groups: a positive front meniscus (L1), a cemented meniscus concave to the stop (L2–L3), the stop, a cemented meniscus concave to the stop (L4–L5), and two separate positive collectors (L6, L7). It is a Gauss type with the rear positive member split in two. All surfaces are spherical or plane.

Parent application L 753 states the design rule. In the negative member behind the stop, a strongly curved cemented surface, concave to the stop, has the more highly refracting glass in front of it, so the surface has positive power, and the marginal ray from the field centre meets it at more than 45°. Its share of the third-order spherical aberration stays small, but it adds negative higher-order spherical aberration that offsets the positive zonal residual limiting the aperture of the Gauss type. Here that surface is r7 (n 1.5891 to 1.5317); the f/1.5 marginal ray meets it at about 57° in the model.

The table splits the space between r5 and r6 into A2 = 0.125 and A3 = 0.109 with no surface between them. The parent's drawing shows the diaphragm between A2 and A3, so the stop is placed at their junction, 32.8355 mm behind the first vertex. Its diameter follows from the f/1.5 rating; the source does not print it.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Focal lengths are for each element alone in air.

### L1: Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.5523, νd = 63.3. Glass: Unmatched (Hoya PCD3 coordinates if read at d). f = +102.3 mm.

The front collector. Its rear face (r2 = +322.5 mm) is nearly flat.

### L2–L3: Front Cemented Meniscus (D1)

- **L2, Positive Meniscus:** nd = 1.6202, νd = 60.4. Glass: Unmatched (no exact catalog row). f = +92.6 mm.
- **L3, Negative Meniscus:** nd = 1.6727, νd = 32.2. Glass: Unmatched (SF5 coordinates if read at d). f = −56.0 mm.

Together a weak negative meniscus (f = −234.3 mm) whose deep rear surface (r5 = +21.92 mm) faces the stop. The cemented surface r4 is converging; the parent application asks that this front cemented surface contribute almost nothing to the spherical correction.

### L4–L5: Rear Cemented Meniscus (D2)

- **L4, Positive Meniscus:** nd = 1.5891, νd = 61.4. Glass: Unmatched (SK5 coordinates if read at d). f = +86.9 mm.
- **L5, Biconcave Negative:** nd = 1.5317, νd = 48.9. Glass: Unmatched (LLF6 coordinates if read at d). f = −32.2 mm.

Together a negative meniscus (f = −45.2 mm) concave to the stop. Its cemented surface r7 (R = −18.40 mm) is the strongly curved converging surface of the parent claim. The nearly flat rear face (r8 = +262.3 mm) sits 0.6715 mm ahead of the plane front of L6.

### L6: Plano-convex Positive

nd = 1.5523, νd = 63.3. Glass: Unmatched (Hoya PCD3 coordinates if read at d). f = +67.2 mm.

The first of the two rear collectors, flat on its front face.

### L7: Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.6202, νd = 60.4. Glass: Unmatched (no exact catalog row). f = +81.5 mm.

The last collector; with L6 it shares the rear positive power that a plain Gauss type gives to one element.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The table gives n/Nü without naming a spectral line, and the model traces the values as d-line indices. Three of the five glasses reproduce catalog rows at the helium d line: 1.6727/32.2 (SF5, 1.67270/32.21), 1.5891/61.4 (SK5, 1.58913/61.27) and 1.5317/48.9 (LLF6, 1.53172/48.87). At the sodium D line the same glasses read 1.6725, 1.5890 and 1.5316, so the printed indices fit d. 1.5523/63.3 (L1, L6) matches Hoya's discontinued PSK3-type crown PCD3 (1.55232/63.42), which the site's glass catalog does not carry; Schott N-PSK3 differs by 0.16 in νd. 1.6202/60.4 (L2, L7) matches no catalog row: the SK16 family sits at 1.62041, 0.0002 higher. Parent application L 753 prints the same glass types rounded to three decimals (1.620/60 three times, 1.673/32, 1.533/49), which cannot settle the fourth digit. Five of the seven printed pairs match at d, short of the 80 % the reference-line test requires, so every label stays Unmatched and the dispersion is traced from nd and νd alone, without partial-dispersion data.

The palette is conventional for its period: crowns of νd 60–63 in every positive element, a dense flint (L3) in the front cemented pair and a light flint (L5) in the rear one.

## Focus Mechanism

The application gives the infinity state only and no focusing data, so the model is static at infinity. The production lens focuses to 5 ft (1.5 m) in a rotating focusing mount. Back focus is the derived paraxial value, 46.546 mm (0.5476 f), which puts the image plane 110.899 mm behind the first vertex.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The source prints no clear diameters and has no drawing, so all semi-diameters are inferred. Their proportions follow the Summarex section in the 1953 Leitz brochure, checked against the to-scale drawing of parent application L 753, which shows a different example. Surfaces 7 and 8 are sized to pass the f/1.5 axial beam (16.874 and 18.410 mm needed; rims 16.9 and 18.42 mm). That gives the steep cemented surface 7 a 66.7° rim angle and leaves the 0.6715 mm space between surfaces 8 and 9 only 0.024 mm wide at the rim, so the file raises the rim-angle limit to 67° and the gap-sag limit to 0.965; no other geometry check is relaxed. Both drawings give L5 a flat top as tall as its rear face, but r7 (R = −18.40 mm) cannot reach the 18.41 mm that r8 needs for the f/1.5 beam, so L5 is drawn with a sloping top. L1's 30 mm semi-diameter keeps the brochure's proportion to the front cemented pair, which the f/1.5 beam holds at 24.4 mm; a 60 mm front element is slightly larger than the 59.8 mm front barrel would hold, so the corner illumination of the model is an upper bound.

Neither application gives a field angle. On the 24 × 36 mm format the corner lies at 14.28°, matching the maker's 28° and 28.5° full-field figures.

Spherical aberration is slightly undercorrected: relative to the paraxial image plane the f/1.5 marginal ray falls 0.12 mm short and the 0.7 zone 0.35 mm short. This agrees with the brochure's note of slightly reduced sharpness at full aperture that clears on stopping down. Monochromatic behaviour assumes the d-line reading of the indices.
