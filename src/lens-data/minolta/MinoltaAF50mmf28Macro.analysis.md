## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP S59-228220 A
**Filed:** 1983-06-08
**Published:** 1984-12-21
**Inventor:** Hisashi Tokumaru
**Assignee:** Minolta Camera Co., Ltd.
**Title:** Large-aperture macro lens (大口径マクロレンズ)
**Example details:** 実施例3 (Embodiment 3), table on p. 5, section Fig. 3, aberration plots Fig. 6; normalized to f = 100 and scaled by 0.5 to 50 mm

The patent is Minolta's design family for the original 1985 AF 50mm f/2.8 Macro. The evidence converges:

1. Seven elements in six groups, matching Minolta's published construction.
2. Three groups that all move toward the object, with both intergroup gaps growing — the "double floating" focus Minolta describes.
3. f = 100 at FNO 2.8 with a near state at β = −0.997, matching a 50 mm f/2.8 lens focusing to 1:1.
4. Image height Y′ = 42 at f = 100 (21 mm at 50 mm), close to the 47° full-frame angle of view Minolta quotes.
5. Filing in June 1983, before the lens appeared with the Maxxum system in 1985.

Embodiments 1–3 share the same layout, so the choice of Embodiment 3 rests on finer evidence. In a Minolta-style lens section hosted by a collector site (original source not identified), the vertex spacings fit Embodiments 1 and 3 equally well (rms 0.42 at f = 100) and Embodiment 2 less well (0.60). Embodiment 3's near state gives 199.9 mm object to image, the closest match to the marketed 0.2 m (Embodiment 1: 194 mm; Embodiment 2: 188 mm). Against that, the section's rear-element curvature looks closer to Embodiment 1. The 60.0–106.0 mm barrel length listed by a third-party database implies about 46 mm of front-group travel, closer to Embodiment 2 (45.6 mm) than to Embodiment 3 (55.4 mm). No factory prescription is known, and Embodiment 3 is a patent design, not a confirmed production revision.

The inventor's name, 得丸祥, is romanized as Hisashi Tokumaru, the form used across the corpus and on Minolta's US filings. The patent prints no half-field angle; the 23.5° trace limit follows Minolta's 47° figure.

## Optical Architecture

Seven spherical elements in six air-spaced groups form three functional groups, positive–positive–negative, holding 3 + 3 + 1 elements. Group I (f = +135.3 mm) is a positive lens, a positive meniscus convex to the object and a negative lens. Group II (f = +50.4 mm) is a negative–positive cemented pair followed by a positive singlet, and Group III (f = −647 mm) is a weak negative meniscus convex to the object, as claim 4 describes. The iris sits between Groups I and II. Both intergroup gaps increase on close focus, and all three groups move toward the object (claim 1, Fig. 3).

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.72000, νd = 50.31. Glass: LAC10 (HOYA) catalog equivalent. f = +81.9 mm.

Weak biconvex front collector of Group I, nearly flat on its front face.

### L2 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.77. Glass: TAF1 (HOYA) catalog equivalent. f = +41.8 mm.

Positive meniscus convex to the object and the strongest positive element of Group I.

### L3 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.60342, νd = 38.01. Glass: E-F5 (HOYA) catalog equivalent. f = −28.0 mm.

Thick flint element with a nearly flat front and a deep concave rear facing the iris. It closes Group I and supplies its negative correction.

### L4 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.67339, νd = 29.25. Glass: Unmatched; no catalog glass sits at these coordinates. f = −35.7 mm.

Dense-flint front member of the Group II cemented pair, with its strong concave face toward the iris. The pair is nearly afocal (f ≈ +644 mm), so it works mainly as a chromatic and aberration corrector.

### L5 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.71300, νd = 53.93. Glass: LAC8 (HOYA) catalog equivalent. f = +38.6 mm.

Lanthanum-crown rear member of the cemented pair, strongly curved toward the image.

### L6 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.77. Glass: TAF1 (HOYA) catalog equivalent. f = +62.2 mm.

Biconvex singlet closing Group II, with its stronger face toward the image.

### L7 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.49140, νd = 57.82. Glass: Unmatched; PMMA-type acrylic values. f = −647 mm.

Weak negative meniscus forming Group III. The patent notes that its low power keeps temperature-driven back-focus shift small, so it may be plastic (claim 5; the description points to Embodiment 3); Embodiments 1 and 2 use a 1.51680 crown here. There is no evidence the production element is plastic.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The table names Nd and νd. Five elements resolve to HOYA catalog equivalents: LAC10 (L1), TAF1 (L2, L6; catalog νd 49.62 against the printed 49.77), E-F5 (L3) and LAC8 (L5). These are equivalents for dispersion modeling, not production identifications. L4 (1.67339 / 29.25) has no catalog row, and L7 carries PMMA-type acrylic values; both keep the printed nd/νd. The palette pairs lanthanum crowns with flints: the L4/L5 cemented pair joins the strongest flint to a lanthanum crown.

## Focus Mechanism

Double floating focus: all three groups move toward the object at different rates. The patent explains that widening the I–II gap mainly corrects spherical aberration and coma, while widening the II–III gap balances distortion and astigmatism. Placing the weak negative group last also shortens the travel of Groups I and II.

| Spacing (mm, at 50 mm) | Infinity | β = −0.997 |
| ---------------------- | -------: | ---------: |
| D6 (I–II, iris at mid) |    5.760 |     14.006 |
| D11 (II–III)           |    0.486 |     41.952 |
| Back focus             |   39.031 |     44.704 |

The patent prints the near-state gaps (d6′ = 28.012, d11′ = 83.903 at f = 100) and β = −0.997, but no object distance. L.B. = 78.058 and L.B.′ = 89.363 are the printed back focus. The model's paraxial infinity back focus matches L.B./2 within 0.002 mm. Its near image gap, solved from β, sits 0.022 mm beyond L.B.′/2, and L.B.′ − L.B. reproduces the printed group-travel ratios. The solved near state puts the object 78.38 mm from the front vertex and 199.9 mm from the image, close to Minolta's 0.2 m minimum focus. With the image plane fixed, Groups I, II and III travel 55.39, 47.14 and 5.67 mm. Settings between the two endpoints are interpolated, not printed.

The patent does not say where the iris is mounted. The model keeps it midway between Groups I and II at both endpoints, at its infinity size. Fig. 6 labels the β = −1 plot "effective F 5.93"; the model's near working f-number is about 5.2 with the midway iris (5.0 to 5.4 if the iris rides with Group I or Group II), so that figure is not reproduced.

## Model Scope and Limitations

All lengths are the patent values scaled by 0.5. The model traces to EFL = 50.0006 mm and back focus 39.031 mm. The table gives FNO 2.8, while Fig. 6 is drawn at F2.88; the model uses f/2.8.

The patent prints no clear apertures or iris position. The front rim is the f/2.8 entrance pupil plus 10% (9.8 mm). The other rims follow the Fig. 3 proportions, and S3 to S9 are opened just enough for the full f/2.8 axial beam (8.6, 8.2 and 7.8 mm), within the glass diameters the lens section shows. Each element keeps one height on both faces. The iris sits at the midpoint of d6, inside the gap where the section draws the blades. Corner illumination at f/2.8 is about 39% of the axis at the 21.6 mm format corner.

Against Fig. 6, the model's real-ray spherical aberration at infinity is −0.08 mm at full aperture (about −0.06 mm read from the plot at F2.88). At the near state it is about −0.75 mm, matching the plotted undercorrection. At the 21 mm image height, the sagittal curve dips to about −0.17 mm mid-field and returns near zero, and the tangential curve reaches about −0.20 mm. Distortion is +0.2%. All of these follow the plotted shapes.
