# MINOLTA MD 28mm f/2.8 (7 elements / 7 groups)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 3,973,832\
**Application Number:** 436,763\
**Filed:** January 25, 1974\
**Priority:** January 30, 1973 (Japan 48-11571)\
**Granted:** August 10, 1976\
**Inventors:** Toshinobu Ogura; Akiyoshi Nakamura\
**Assignee:** Minolta Camera Co., Ltd.\
**Title:** *Retro-Focus Type Wide Angle Lens System*\
**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 2 / Embodiment 2

US 3,973,832 describes a retrofocus wide-angle objective for a single-lens reflex camera. Its general construction divides the lens into three components: a negative front component A, a positive second component B, and a four-lens rear component C. Example 2 is the seven-element, seven-group embodiment with normalized focal length `f = 1`, relative aperture F/2.8, full field angle `2ω = 76°`, and published back focus `s′ = 1.31`. The numerical prescription is the source of the modeled radii, spacings, and refractive-index/Abbe coordinates; the model interprets those coordinates as d-line `nd/νd`. The architectural description and the four governing conditions appear on PDF pages 7–8 of the supplied patent; the Example 2 table is on PDF page 8, printed page 4 (Source 1).

The production identification is a strong correlation rather than a manufacturer-confirmed patent attribution. Minolta-authored system literature documents 28 mm f/2.8 lenses with seven elements in seven groups, approximately 75–76° angle of view, and 0.3 m minimum focus. Those specifications converge with Example 2 in focal-length class, aperture, element/group count, and field coverage. The patent itself never names the commercial lens, and the broad “MD 28mm f/2.8” designation also encompasses a later five-element/five-group redesign. This model therefore represents only the inferred seven-element/seven-group MD-family variant (Sources 2–4; the later 5/5 variant is retained only as a correlation limit in the dossier evidence).

The patent prescription is normalized to `f = 1`; the implemented model applies a uniform scale of 28.0 mm per patent unit. The rounded prescription then computes an effective focal length of 27.99547564 mm rather than exactly 28 mm. Marketing and design values are kept separate: 28 mm is the product-class focal length, while 27.99547564 mm is the verified paraxial EFL of the implemented prescription.

The patent figure shows the diaphragm in the `dC0` air space between B2 and C1 but does not dimension its axial station or diameter. The implemented `STO` is consequently a modeling inference: it is placed 41% of the B2-to-C1 gap from B2, and its 7.17457899 mm physical semi-diameter is calibrated so the paraxial entrance pupil reproduces F/2.8. The patent likewise publishes no clear apertures, so all refracting-surface semi-diameters are modeled quantities rather than source measurements.

## Optical Architecture

The verified system is a seven-element, seven-group all-spherical retrofocus prime. Its computed rear-vertex back focal distance is 36.72681608 mm against an EFL of 27.99547564 mm, giving `BFD/EFL = 1.311884`. The back focus therefore exceeds the focal length, matching both the patent’s retrofocus description and the project’s numerical retrofocus criterion.

The three patent components have the following standalone paraxial powers when each component is evaluated by itself in air:

| Patent component | Physical contents | Standalone focal length | Sign |
|---|---|---:|---|
| A | IA + IIA | −26.3894 mm | Negative |
| B | IB | +25.5991 mm | Positive |
| C | IC + IIC + IIIC + IVC | +75.1078 mm | Positive |

These standalone focal lengths describe isolated component power, not additive contributions to the complete lens. The full verifier separately tracks in-situ reduced-angle changes through the assembled system so that component power is not conflated with complete-system behavior.

Component A is the strongly negative front section required to create the long SLR back focus. In the patent’s general description, the first component has negative refractive power and contains at least one negative meniscus. Example 2 uses two negative menisci, IA and IIA. The patent explicitly presents the two-negative-lens version as a way of reducing aberration generated by the first component while retaining its diverging function (Source 1, PDF p. 7).

Component B is a single biconvex positive lens, IB. The patent assigns the second component positive refractive power and describes it as compensating aberration of opposite sign to that created by the negative first component before the residual is handled by the rear component.

Component C is the four-lens rear section IC–IVC. The patent gives this section particular importance: IC and IIC form a diverging pair as a whole, with the air space between them intentionally used as an “air lens.” The patent states that this construction addresses coma that would otherwise arise if the negative IIC lens acted alone. IIIC and IVC complete the positive rear section. The four patent inequalities constrain this rear assembly’s spacing, curvature, and dispersion relationships.

The design is entirely spherical. There are no aspheric surfaces, diffractive phase terms, cemented interfaces, cover-glass plates, dummy refracting planes, or folded optical paths in the selected embodiment.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### IA — Negative Meniscus

`nd = 1.6214, νd = 61.3. Glass: N-SK16 class (coordinate-compatible spectral proxy; native d 1.6214/61.3; supplier/melt unproven). f = −62.5507 mm.`

IA is the first lens of patent component A and the first of two negative menisci in Example 2. Its role is established at the architectural level by the patent: component A supplies the negative front power needed by the retrofocus layout. The patent does not identify a glass supplier for this coordinate, and the model uses an N-SK16 spectral proxy while retaining the distinct source coordinate.

### IIA — Negative Meniscus

`nd = 1.6204, νd = 60.3. Glass: N-SK16 class (coordinate-compatible spectral proxy; coordinate code 620603; supplier/melt unproven). f = −49.2034 mm.`

IIA is the second lens of component A. Together, IA and IIA form the negative front component with a verified standalone focal length of −26.3894 mm. The patent specifically identifies the two-negative-lens form used by the second embodiment as a means of easing the aberration burden of the front diverging component. The stored glass label remains a six-digit class description because the patent provides only refractive-index/Abbe coordinates, not a vendor or melt designation; the model treats that pair as `nd/νd`.

### IB — Biconvex Positive

`nd = 1.7170, νd = 47.9. Glass: S-LAM3 class (coordinate-compatible spectral proxy; coordinate code 717479/480; supplier/melt unproven). f = +25.5991 mm.`

IB is the sole lens in component B and is therefore identical to that component’s standalone power. Its positive power follows the patent’s prescribed second-component architecture. The patent describes B as an intermediate compensating component between the strong negative front section and the more complex four-lens rear section; the analysis does not assign a more specific aberration contribution than the patent supports.

### IC — Positive Meniscus

`nd = 1.7440, νd = 44.9. Glass: H-LaF3B class (coordinate-compatible spectral proxy; coordinate code 744449; supplier/melt unproven). f = +56.8849 mm.`

IC is the first lens of component C and the positive member of the patent’s deliberately constructed IC–IIC diverging pair. The patent states that IC is spaced from IIC so that the intervening air space acts as an air lens used in coma correction (Source 1, PDF pp. 7–8). This is a source-described design relationship, not an inference from the sign of IC’s standalone power.

IC also participates directly in the patent’s third condition, `VC1 > VC2`, where the Abbe number of IC must exceed that of IIC. In Example 2 the values are 44.9 and 27.6 respectively. The patent associates this ordering with simultaneous correction of longitudinal and lateral chromatic aberration.

### IIC — Biconcave Negative

`nd = 1.7552, νd = 27.6. Glass: SF4 class (coordinate-compatible spectral proxy; coordinate code 755276; supplier/melt unproven). f = −16.3902 mm.`

IIC is the strongest standalone negative element in the prescription. It is the second lens of component C and forms the rear component’s diverging sub-group together with IC. The patent warns that using a single negative IIC lens for this diverging function would produce excessive coma; the positive IC lens and their intervening air space are therefore part of the patented correction strategy.

Its much lower Abbe number than IC is also an explicit part of the patent’s chromatic condition. The data preserves the dense-flint coordinate at the class level because both SCHOTT- and HIKARI-family equivalents are compatible with the model’s d-line interpretation of the source coordinate, leaving the actual supplier unresolved.

### IIIC — Positive Meniscus

`nd = 1.7200, νd = 50.3. Glass: LAC10 class (coordinate-compatible spectral proxy; coordinate code 720503; supplier/melt unproven). f = +30.2629 mm.`

IIIC is the third lens of component C and returns the rear group to positive standalone power after IIC. Its placement is involved in the patent’s fourth spacing condition through `dC4`, the air gap between IIC and IIIC. The patent links that spacing condition to avoiding an increase in negative distortion and a coma balance that would be difficult to maintain over the full field.

The coordinate is retained as a lanthanum-class identification. A modern OHARA S-LAL10 row is a close coordinate analogue, but the patent does not identify the supplier, so the data does not promote that analogue to historical glass identity.

### IVC — Biconvex Positive

`nd = 1.6214, νd = 61.3. Glass: N-SK16 class (coordinate-compatible spectral proxy; native d 1.6214/61.3; supplier/melt unproven). f = +68.7082 mm.`

IVC is the fourth and final lens of component C and the rear positive element of the prescription. It shares the same published refractive-index/Abbe coordinate as IA. Under the model’s d-line interpretation, that coordinate is deliberately kept distinct from IIA’s nearby `1.6204/60.3` value, even though compatible modern catalog curves can serve as spectral proxies for both. The authored source coordinates are not replaced by catalog coordinates.

The patent does not assign IVC an isolated aberration-correction function in the same explicit manner as the IC–IIC pair. Its role is therefore described conservatively as the rear positive element completing component C rather than by inferring a specific correction from its power sign or crown-like dispersion alone.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent table publishes refractive-index and Abbe-number coordinates but does not label the reference wavelength. The model treats the pairs as d-line `nd/νd`, consistent with the six-digit glass-code and current catalog comparisons used in the dossier. The patent gives no supplier names, melt designations, C/F/g-line indices, anomalous partial-dispersion values, or Sellmeier coefficients. The data file therefore treats glass identity as a catalog-correlation problem rather than a source fact.

| Element(s) | Patent coordinate | Final glass annotation | Identification status |
|---|---:|---|---|
| IA, IVC | 1.6214 / 61.3 | N-SK16 spectral proxy | Source coordinate preserved; supplier/melt unproven |
| IIA | 1.6204 / 60.3 | 620603 crown class | Coordinate-equivalent modern catalog candidates exist; supplier unresolved |
| IB | 1.7170 / 47.9 | 717479/480 lanthanum class | Multiple compatible vendor families; supplier unresolved |
| IC | 1.7440 / 44.9 | 744449 lanthanum class | Multiple compatible vendor families; supplier unresolved |
| IIC | 1.7552 / 27.6 | 755276 dense-flint class | Multiple compatible vendor families; supplier unresolved |
| IIIC | 1.7200 / 50.3 | 720503 lanthanum class | Coordinate-equivalent candidate exists; supplier unresolved |

Current SCHOTT, HIKARI, and OHARA catalogs provide useful coordinate analogues for several elements, but coordinate agreement does not establish the glass actually used by Minolta in the 1970s (Sources 5–7). The analysis therefore follows the data file’s class/code annotations rather than substituting modern vendor names as historical facts.

No element carries `nC`, `nF`, `ng`, or `dPgF` in the final data. Consequently, the model has no basis for an apochromatic or anomalous-partial-dispersion claim. The patent’s chromatic discussion is limited here to the explicit `νd` relationship between IC and IIC and to the source’s stated intent for that condition.

## Focus Mechanism

The optical prescription contains only the published design/infinity state. The focus status is `NO_INTERNAL_RECONSTRUCTION`.

Minolta product literature gives a minimum focus distance of 0.3 m for the correlated seven-element/seven-group 28 mm f/2.8 family, and the data retains that value as `closeFocusM` (Sources 2–4). Neither the patent nor the manufacturer sources used for this dossier provide a mechanism-constrained finite-focus spacing law for Example 2. The model therefore has an empty `var` structure and does not invent internal lens motion from minimum-focus distance alone.

This distinction is important: 0.3 m is a production specification, not a solved optical focus state in the current model. No focus breathing, finite-conjugate magnification, group travel, or close-focus aberration behavior is claimed from the infinity prescription.

## Conditional Expressions

US 3,973,832 gives four conditions for the retrofocus construction (Source 1, PDF pp. 7–8). The final scaled data preserves the Example 2 values and satisfies all four conditions:

| Patent condition | Implemented values | Result | Patent-stated purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dC0 > dC2` | 3.9228 mm > 2.7552 mm | Satisfied | Constrains the position of IC relative to B and the rear group; the patent discusses coma/astigmatism balance and back-focus retention |
| `|rC2| ≥ |rC3|` | 33.0148 mm ≥ 17.1136 mm | Satisfied | Preserves the effective air-lens action between IC and IIC; the patent discusses coma and spherical-aberration balance |
| `VC1 > VC2` | 44.9 > 27.6 | Satisfied | Establishes the dispersion ordering used for longitudinal and lateral chromatic correction |
| `dC0 > dC4` | 3.9228 mm > 0.8512 mm | Satisfied | The patent associates the spacing with control of negative distortion and coma over the field |

The condition values above are evaluated from the final scaled prescription. The descriptive purposes are paraphrases of the patent’s discussion on PDF page 8, printed page 3; they are not reverse-engineered explanations from the finished lens model.

## Verification Summary

The implemented prescription was recomputed from the parsed `.data.ts`, not from a separate copy of the intended design. Independent reduced-angle and ordinary-angle ABCD formulations agree to floating-point precision, and a separately stepped sequential paraxial ray reproduces the same first-order system.

Key verified infinite-conjugate results are:

- EFL: 27.99547564 mm.
- Rear-vertex BFD: 36.72681608 mm.
- First-vertex-to-image track: 84.04681608 mm.
- `BFD/EFL`: 1.311884, satisfying the retrofocus criterion.
- Entrance-pupil semi-diameter: 4.99919208 mm.
- Modeled wide-open f-number: 2.8000000016. This is a stop-size calibration to the published F/2.8, not independent evidence for the physical diaphragm diameter.
- Normalized-equivalent Petzval sum: 0.11506110, compared with the patent Table 2 total of 0.1150.

The patent’s printed normalized back focus `s′ = 1.31` would scale to 36.68 mm. The rounded prescription instead computes 36.72681608 mm, a +0.04681608 mm difference. The implemented final-surface spacing follows the recomputed paraxial image plane while preserving the printed patent value as a source comparison rather than silently forcing the two to agree.

Because the patent publishes no semi-diameters, the implemented clear apertures are modeled. Portable geometry checks find a maximum spherical rim angle of 46.1019°, a minimum checked element edge thickness of 1.1316 mm, and a minimum shared-gap margin of 0.00712 mm under the current `0.90 × gap` intrusion rule. Exact meridional sampling traced 73 rays: 69 passed, four extreme field/pupil combinations were geometrically unavailable even before modeled-aperture clipping, and no solvable ray was clipped by the modeled surface semi-diameters. Both ±38° chief rays reached the image plane.

These are dossier-level checks, not substitutes for LensVisualizer repository validation. Real `LensDataInput` type checking, `buildLens()/validateLensData()`, project exact tracing, runtime glass resolution, Prettier, and production render-trim diagnostics remain integration-stage checks.
