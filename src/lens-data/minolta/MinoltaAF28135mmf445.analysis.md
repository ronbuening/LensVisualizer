## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [US 4,844,600 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4844600A/en)
**Filed:** January 7, 1987, as a division of application Ser. No. 467,433 (February 17, 1983; US 4,636,040).
**Priority:** JP 57-026637 (February 19, 1982) and JP 57-085279 (May 19, 1982).
**Granted:** July 4, 1989.
**Inventor:** Hisashi Tokumaru.
**Assignee:** Minolta Camera Co., Ltd. (Minolta Camera Kabushiki Kaisha on the patent).
**Title:** Zoom Lens System.
**Example details:** Table 9 (cols. 23–24) prescription; Table 10 group data; Table 11 focusing shifts; Fig. 12 section; Figs. 13–15 aberrations. Patent scale, f = 28.8–60.0–131.5 mm, F3.6–4.2–4.63.

The patent describes zoom lenses that focus with a group behind the front group, so the front group stays fixed during focusing. Embodiment 5 is the patent's realisation of that idea at 28–135 mm, and it fits the Minolta AF Zoom 28-135mm f/4-4.5 introduced with the Maxxum/α-7000 system in 1985 (order codes 2553-100 and 2553-600):

1. **Construction.** Table 9 has 16 elements in 13 groups, with three cemented doublets, which is Minolta's published 16/13 count. Embodiment 6, the only other example of this type in the patent, has 17 elements.
2. **Focal range.** 28.8–131.5 mm, matching the marketed 28–135 mm.
3. **Focusing.** The rear assembly F moves as a unit while the front assembly stays fixed. The production lens is an internal-focus design.
4. **Close focus.** Table 11 ends at 1.5 m, which is Minolta's normal minimum focus distance. The production lens adds a macro setting (0.25×) that the patent does not describe.
5. **Timing.** The priority applications date from 1982 and the parent US application from 1983, two years before the lens appeared.

The aperture differs. The patent gives F3.6 at 28.8 mm, F4.2 at 60 mm and F4.63 at 131.5 mm, while the production lens is rated f/4–4.5. The model keeps the patent's values. The association is strong, but no exact production prescription is claimed.

## Optical Architecture

The lens is a negative-lead zoom of five components: A I (positive), A II (negative), F I (positive), F II (negative) and F III (positive). A I and A II form the negative front assembly A, with fA = −23.26, −33.16 and −52.01 mm at the three printed focal lengths (Table 10). F I to F III form the positive rear assembly F (fF = 35.47, 31.93 and 27.23 mm), which also focuses the lens. Its lateral magnification βF runs from −1.24 to −1.81 to −2.53.

From wide to tele, the four variable gaps change as follows:

- d5 (A I to A II) opens from 0.600 to 39.068 mm.
- d13 (A to F) closes from 20.202 to 2.000 mm.
- d20 (F I to F II) opens from 1.200 to 14.934 mm.
- d23 (F II to F III) closes from 15.234 to 1.500 mm.

The patent states that F II stays stationary during zooming in this embodiment. With the derived back focus, F II sits 80.55 mm in front of the image at all three printed states. The front element moves 34.0 mm forward between 28.8 and 131.5 mm.

The design target is a small change in focusing travel across the zoom range. For the same object distance, the tele shift is about twice the wide shift. The patent achieves this by splitting F into three sub-groups whose spacings change during zooming: fF falls as the focal length rises, and βFW is held within −2 < βFW ≤ −1.05.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Focal lengths are thick-lens values for each element alone in air. Glass names are catalogue equivalents of the printed nd/νd; the patent names no supplier.

### L1 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.88. Glass: PBH53 (Ohara), dense flint. f = −171.3 mm.

Front element, convex to the object, cemented to L2. Its high dispersion pairs with the crown of L2 to correct colour in the large front sub-group.

### L2 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.69100, νd = 54.75. Glass: S-LAL9 (Ohara), lanthanum crown. f = +92.3 mm.

Rear member of the front doublet C1, nearly flat on its rear face (R 1404.138 mm).

### L3 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.67000, νd = 57.07. Glass: Unmatched (patent nd 1.67000, νd 57.07). f = +190.0 mm.

Weak meniscus closing A I. The gap d5 behind it carries most of the front assembly's zoom motion.

### L4 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.77. Glass: TAF1 (Hoya), lanthanum flint. f = −31.1 mm.

Front element of A II. The strongly curved rear face (R 15.985 mm) supplies much of the front assembly's negative power.

### L5 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.77. Glass: TAF1 (Hoya). f = −38.2 mm.

Nearly plano-concave (front R −332.034 mm), sharing the negative power of A II with L4 and L7.

### L6 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.43. Glass: PBH6 (Ohara), dense flint. f = +28.0 mm.

The only positive element of A II. Its dense flint offsets the colour of the three negative lanthanum-flint elements around it.

### L7 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.77. Glass: TAF1 (Hoya). f = −31.3 mm.

Last element of the front assembly, ahead of the variable gap d13 in which the iris is modelled.

### L8 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.69100, νd = 54.75. Glass: S-LAL9 (Ohara). f = +96.8 mm.

First element of the focusing assembly F and of sub-group F I.

### L9 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.88. Glass: PBH53 (Ohara). f = −38.3 mm.

Dense-flint front member of doublet C2.

### L10 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.58913, νd = 61.11. Glass: S-BAL35 (Ohara), barium crown. f = +25.5 mm.

Rear member of C2 and the strongest positive element of F I. The doublet corrects the colour of the positive sub-group.

### L11 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.58913, νd = 61.11. Glass: S-BAL35 (Ohara). f = +47.3 mm.

Closes F I. The gap d20 behind it opens by 13.7 mm toward tele.

### L12 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.43. Glass: PBH6 (Ohara). f = +29.1 mm.

Dense-flint front member of doublet C3, concave to the object.

### L13 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.77. Glass: TAF1 (Hoya). f = −17.1 mm.

Rear member of C3. The doublet forms the negative sub-group F II, which keeps a fixed distance from the image during zooming.

### L14 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.51680, νd = 64.12. Glass: BSC7 (Hoya), borosilicate crown. f = +91.5 mm.

Weak meniscus at the front of F III, concave to the object.

### L15 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.69100, νd = 54.75. Glass: S-LAL9 (Ohara). f = +31.6 mm.

Thick (6.5 mm) main positive element of F III, with its strong face (R −24.092 mm) toward the image.

### L16 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.43. Glass: PBH6 (Ohara). f = −38.5 mm.

Last element, 1.424 mm behind L15. Its concave front face (R −21.225 mm) closely follows the strong rear face of L15.

## Focus Mechanism

Rear (internal) focusing: the whole rear assembly F (L8–L16) moves toward the image as a unit, with its two internal variable spacings held fixed (col. 22, feature (e)). The front assembly A and the image plane do not move. As F moves by Δx, carrying the iris with it, d13 grows by Δx and the back focus shrinks by Δx. Table 11 prints Δx for 13 photographing distances, which the patent measures to the image plane:

## Model Scope and Limitations

- **Back focus.** The patent does not print it. Each station's infinity value is the paraxial back focus: 48.270, 54.715 and 62.005 mm at the printed states. The printed Σd (99.240, 109.797 and 119.506 mm) and the Table 10 group values reproduce.
- **Intermediate zoom stations.** The model has 22 stations between the three printed ones. At each, d5, d13 and d20 are interpolated between the printed values. d23 and the back focus are then solved so that F II stays at its printed distance from the image (80.549 mm) and the paraxial image stays on the image plane. Defocus between adjacent stations stays below 0.015 mm. At these stations, the focus shift is the paraxial shift for the same distance plus the interpolated difference between printed and paraxial shifts (at most 0.0008 mm). This is not Minolta's zoom cam.
- **Stop.** The patent does not locate the iris. It is placed 1.0 mm in front of L8, at the front of the focusing assembly F, and it moves with F during zooming and focusing. This position fits the wide-end curves of Fig. 13. At 28.8 mm the model's distortion at full field is −3.5 %, as printed. Its meridional and sagittal fields at 0.75 of the image height are −0.46 and −0.25 mm, against about −0.58 and −0.23 mm in Fig. 13b. At the tele end the concave rear face of L7 leaves 0.08 mm of air in front of the fully open iris. The iris radius gives the printed F-numbers at the printed stations, interpolated in between.
- **Semi-diameters.** These are estimated from Fig. 12 and agree with it to within about 5 %. As drawn, the concave rear face of L5 ends at 11.9 mm, inside the 12.5 mm front edge. L11 (10.5 mm), the F II doublet (8.6 mm) and L14 (14.1 mm) sit 2–4 % above the drawing. The full axial cone and the chief ray to the image corner clear every rim at the printed stations and distances. The smallest margins are 0.36 mm at L10's rear face (axial, tele end) and 0.56 mm at L4's rear face (chief ray, wide end at 1.5 m).
- **Field.** The image height is Y′ = 21.63 mm (Figs. 13–15), a 43.26 mm image circle. This is reached at half-fields of 37.9°, 19.5° and 9.2°. The real-ray distortion at full field is about −3.5 %, +1.9 % and +2.1 % at the three printed focal lengths, against about −3.5 %, +2.1 % and +2.5 % in Figs. 13c–15c. Wide open at infinity, vignetting leaves about 50 %, 60 % and 51 % of the full beam at the image corner at these focal lengths.
- **Aperture.** The patent's F3.6–4.2–4.63 are kept rather than the production f/4–4.5. The macro setting of the production lens is not modelled.
