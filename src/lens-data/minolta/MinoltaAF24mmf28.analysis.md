## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 4,759,619
**Filed:** 1985-12-10
**Published:** 1988-07-26
**Inventors:** Akiyoshi Nakamura; Tetsuya Arimoto
**Assignee:** Minolta Camera Co., Ltd.
**Title:** Inverted telephoto type wide angle lens system with a rear focusing unit
**Example details:** Table 2 (columns 6–7), Fig. 4, aberration plots Fig. 5 (infinity) and Fig. 6 (β = −0.1); normalized to f = 100 and scaled 0.24× to 24 mm.

Embodiment 2 is the closest patent match to the original 1985 MINOLTA AF 24mm f/2.8; no maker statement names the patent:

1. Eight singlets in eight groups, as marketed. Embodiment 1 cements L5 and L6 (seven groups); Embodiments 4 and 5 are 94° ten-element designs.
2. f/2.8, an 84° field and rear-group focusing, as marketed.
3. Japanese priority of 13 December 1984, before the 1985 release.
4. A Minolta-style barrel section on a collector site shows the same element shapes, and its spacings fit Embodiment 2 (d4/d6 about 1.22 against 1.19) far better than Embodiment 3 (1.92).

The drawing confirms the layout, not factory dimensions. The printed spacings sum to 200.869 against the printed Σd = 200.862; no single misprinted spacing explains the difference, so all are kept as printed.

## Optical Architecture

The design is an inverted telephoto of eight singlets: a fixed negative front group F (L1–L2, f = −101.0 mm) and a positive rear group R (L3–L8, f = +25.16 mm) that moves for focusing, with |fF|/fR = 4.01 inside the patent's condition (1), 2.65–7.5. The stop lies between L4 and L5 and moves with R. All sixteen surfaces are spherical.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.71060, νd = 43.25. Glass: Unmatched (no catalog row at these coordinates). f = +238.58 mm in air.

Weak positive meniscus convex to the object. Condition (2) (r1/r2 = 0.28) bounds its power, which offsets the negative distortion of L2 and L3.

### L2 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.51112, νd = 60.50. Glass: NSL7 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = −69.75 mm in air.

Negative meniscus convex to the object, supplying most of the fixed front group's negative power. Condition (3) (r2/r3 = 16.6) shapes the air space after L1, which the patent links to distortion and corner illumination.

### L3 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.51680, νd = 64.20. Glass: BSC7 (HOYA catalog equivalent). f = −32.20 mm in air.

Negative meniscus convex to the object at the front of the moving rear group. Condition (4) (d4/d6 = 1.19) places it between the focusing gap d4 and the air space d6.

### L4 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.77551, νd = 37.90. Glass: Unmatched (no catalog row at these coordinates). f = +37.62 mm in air.

Thick biconvex positive element directly ahead of the stop, nearly flat at the rear; condition (6) (|r7/r8| = 0.22) balances coma against astigmatism.

### L5 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.74100, νd = 52.67. Glass: LAK011 (Hikari catalog equivalent). f = +36.17 mm in air.

Biconvex positive element directly behind the stop, separated from L6 by an air space; Embodiment 1 cements these two.

### L6 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.40. Glass: S-TIH6 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = −22.21 mm in air.

Dense-flint biconcave negative element, with the lowest νd and the strongest negative power in the lens.

### L7 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.62135, νd = 61.28. Glass: Unmatched (no catalog row at these coordinates). f = +60.20 mm in air.

Positive meniscus convex to the image. Its concave front face nests against the concave rear face of L6.

### L8 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.65830, νd = 58.52. Glass: Unmatched (no catalog row at these coordinates). f = +35.46 mm in air.

Final biconvex positive element with a nearly flat front face, 0.117 mm behind L7 on the axis.

## Glass Identification and Selection

Table 2 heads its columns Nd and νd. Four pairs match catalog glasses, named as catalog equivalents rather than production identifications: L2 OHARA NSL7, L3 HOYA BSC7, L5 Hikari LAK011 and L6 OHARA S-TIH6. L1, L4, L7 and L8 have no catalog row and stay Unmatched, so their dispersion beyond νd is approximate.

## Focus Mechanism

Table 2 prints the near state: d4 shrinks from 38.618 to 27.728 (f = 100 units) at β = −0.1, so the rear group and iris move 2.614 mm toward the object and the back focus grows from 36.103 to 38.716 mm. The patent prints no object distance; with the image plane fixed, the model derives 238.42 mm from the front vertex (0.323 m object to image) and β = −0.10006.

The marketed minimum focus is 0.25 m (0.16×), so the modeled near end is the patent's state, not the retail limit. Intermediate positions are linear interpolations, not a published cam.

## Geometry, Aperture and Image Plane

The model's focal length is 24.0007 mm. The image plane sits at the computed paraxial back focus, 36.103 mm behind the last surface, with no best-focus shift.

The stop position, 52% of the way through d8, is inferred from Fig. 4, and the iris diameter follows from f/2.8. All semi-diameters are estimated from Fig. 4. As the figure draws it, L7 (7.6 mm) stands above L6 (7.0 mm) and its concave front face reaches over the rim of L6; the facing surfaces of the two elements are 0.37 mm apart at the edge of L6.

The 42° half-field reaches 21.15 mm, inside the 21.63 mm frame corner, because of barrel distortion: −2.1% at 42° and about −2.8% at 35–38°, as in Fig. 5c. Spherical aberration (about −0.15 mm at 0.7 of the pupil) and astigmatism also follow Fig. 5. At f/2.8 the 42° bundle keeps about 36% of the axial light.

## Model Scope and Limitations

Back focus, stop position, iris diameter, semi-diameters and the near object distance are derived or inferred rather than printed. The patent field is not enlarged to reach the frame corner.
