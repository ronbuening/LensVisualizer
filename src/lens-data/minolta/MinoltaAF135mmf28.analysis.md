## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 4,610,514
**Filed:** 1983-10-06
**Published:** 1986-09-09
**Inventor:** Akiyoshi Nakamura
**Assignee:** Minolta Camera Co., Ltd. (printed Minolta Camera Kabushiki Kaisha)
**Title:** Telephoto Lens System
**Priority:** JP 57-176702 and 57-176703, 1982-10-07
**Example details:** Table 3 and Fig. 7 (f = 100, F/2.8, 2ω = 18°); every length scaled ×1.35 to 135 mm

The identification with the original MINOLTA AF 135mm f/2.8 (1985) rests on construction and timing, not on a statement by Minolta. The 9000AF brochure (page 39) lists the lens with seven elements in five groups, 18° angle of view, f/2.8–32, 1 m minimum focus and a 55 mm filter, and the lens keeps its length while focusing. Embodiment 3 has the same seven elements in five groups, the same aperture and field, and focuses by moving only its middle unit. A collector-hosted mechanical section shows the same layout: two front singlets, two cemented pairs, a small internal focusing carrier and a rear singlet. Its original publication is not identified.

Embodiments 1, 2 and 5 share this layout, aperture, field and moving group, and fit the production lens as well as Embodiment 3; no public source distinguishes them. Embodiment 5 lies outside the patent's condition (1). The design is unrelated to the MD 135mm f/2.8 lenses and to the apodized STF 135mm. The Certificate of Correction (1987-11-17) amends prose, condition labels and the Table 5 layout, not Table 3.

## Optical Architecture

A positive–negative–positive telephoto of seven spherical elements in five groups. Unit I holds two positive singlets (E1, E2) and a weakly negative cemented doublet (E3–E4). Unit II is a negative cemented doublet (E5–E6), the only group that moves for focusing. Unit III is a single positive element (E7). The patent labels its five components L1–L5; the data file numbers the elements E1–E7. The front face of E5 is printed as R8 = +9885.29 at patent scale, nearly flat but not a plane. The patent's conditions hold for this example: f(I+II) = 1.79f (1.5f to 2.1f), l1/l2 = 0.582 (0.4 to 1.5), D/Σd = 0.275 (0.15 to 0.4), νa − νb = 24.2 (15 to 38).

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### E1 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.61270, νd = 58.5. Glass: BACD4 (HOYA), catalog equivalent. f = +171.45 mm.

Front collector (patent L1) with a nearly flat rear face (R2 = −1606.64 at patent scale).

### E2 — Positive Meniscus, convex to object

nd = 1.61270, νd = 58.5. Glass: BACD4 (HOYA), catalog equivalent. f = +143.26 mm.

Second positive member of Unit I (patent L2), sharing the front convergence with E1.

### E3 — Positive Meniscus, convex to object

nd = 1.67100, νd = 51.7. Glass: Unmatched (no catalog row at these coordinates). f = +66.49 mm.

Positive member of the front cemented doublet (patent L3). Its strongly curved front face and the cemented face S6 meet at a thin edge, as Fig. 7 draws them.

### E4 — Negative Meniscus, convex to object

nd = 1.75520, νd = 27.5. Glass: S-TIH4 (OHARA), catalog equivalent. f = −45.63 mm.

Dense-flint partner of E3. The doublet's net focal length is −237 mm. Condition (5), ra − da < rb < f, shapes the cemented face S6 so that it keeps positive power at wavelengths other than the d-line and balances the colour correction of condition (4); here 15.55 < 47.84 < 100 at patent scale.

### E5 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.80520, νd = 25.4. Glass: S-TIH6 (OHARA), catalog equivalent. f = +96.78 mm.

Positive front element of the focusing doublet (patent L4), with a nearly flat front face.

### E6 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.65840, νd = 50.9. Glass: S-BSM25 (OHARA), catalog equivalent. f = −40.02 mm.

Negative element that gives the Unit II doublet its net focal length of −68.9 mm.

### E7 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.51680, νd = 64.1. Glass: N-BK7 (Schott), catalog equivalent. f = +104.36 mm.

Fixed rear positive element (patent L5) behind the long d10 air space.

## Glass Identification and Selection

Table 3 prints Nd and νd only; the patent names the d-line as its design wavelength. Six of the seven pairs match catalog glasses, which the data file uses as catalog equivalents, not as production identifications:

| Glass (vendor) | nd / νd | Elements | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BACD4 (HOYA), SK4 type | 1.6127 / 58.5 | E1, E2 | Front positive crowns |
| Unmatched | 1.6710 / 51.7 | E3 | Positive member of the front doublet |
| S-TIH4 (OHARA), SF4 type | 1.7552 / 27.5 | E4 | Dense flint of the front doublet |
| S-TIH6 (OHARA), SF6 type | 1.8052 / 25.4 | E5 | Dense flint of the focusing doublet |
| S-BSM25 (OHARA), SSK5 type | 1.6584 / 50.9 | E6 | Negative crown of the focusing doublet |
| N-BK7 (Schott) | 1.5168 / 64.1 | E7 | Rear crown |

The front doublet's Abbe difference (νa − νb = 24.2) is the patent's condition (4) for axial and lateral colour. The moving unit is a doublet to limit the change of colour during focusing; its positive member is the flint and its negative member the crown, the usual pairing for a negative achromat. The patent prints no partial-dispersion data.

## Focus Mechanism

Internal focus: Unit II (E5–E6) moves toward the image while Units I and III stay fixed, so the lens does not change length. Table 3 prints the infinity spacings and a close state at β = 1/10; at patent scale d7 goes from 14.36 to 19.79 and d10 from 24.66 to 19.23, a travel of 5.43 (7.33 mm at 135 mm). Table 3 omits the asterisk on d10, but its footnote gives the close value.

| State | d7 (stop to S8 part) | d10 | Object to image | Magnification |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Infinity | 19.39 mm (1.74 mm) | 33.29 mm | ∞ | 0 |
| Printed β = 1/10 | 26.72 mm (9.08 mm) | 25.96 mm | 1.525 m | −0.1006 |

Both states are published keyframes. The object distance of the close state is computed: it is the distance at which the printed spacings focus on the fixed image plane. The patent does not say whether the stop moves; the model keeps it fixed with Unit I, so only the stop-to-S8 part of d7 changes. The production lens focuses to 1.0 m (0.16×), closer than any state the patent prints.

## Geometry, Aperture and Image Plane

The patent prints no semi-diameters; all are read from Fig. 7 and are not manufacturer dimensions. The front radius, 26.5 mm, is about 10% above the f/2.8 entrance-pupil radius of 24.1 mm. E3 and E4 meet at a thin edge at 21.1 mm, and E4's rear face is bevelled to 17.3 mm, as the figure draws them. The collector-hosted section shows a somewhat larger E4 rear face.

Fig. 7 draws the iris near the rear of d7. The stop sits at 0.91 of the 19.39 mm infinity gap, 1.74 mm in front of S8, with a radius of 12.59 mm at f/2.8.

The patent prints no back focus. The image plane is the paraxial focus, 42.81 mm behind S12 (31.71 mm at patent scale), 128.09 mm from S1. The computed focal length is 135.05 mm (100.04 at patent scale); the example is not refitted to exactly 135 mm.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The model reproduces the patent's aberration plots for this example (Fig. 8) in sign and size. Spherical aberration is undercorrected by about 0.05 at patent scale near 0.7 of the pupil and returns to zero at f/2.8. Distortion is about +0.6% pincushion at 9°. At f/2.8 the rear element vignettes the field edge, leaving about 67% relative illumination at the 9° corner. Corner illumination does not depend on the inferred E3 rim. A stop placed closer to S8 would bring the tangential field at 9° nearer the plot's value. The semi-diameters, the stop position and the close-focus object distance are inferred, and Embodiments 1, 2 and 5 are equally plausible prescriptions for the production lens.
