# Audit Log — MILES 100mm f/6.3 (US 2,527,370 Design)

## Source

US 2,527,370 A, John R. Miles, assigned to La Croix Optical Company; filed 11 April 1949 and published 24 October 1950. Figure 1 is the sole numerical example. Figure 2 is prior art. The table in column 2 and its repetition in claim 1 agree. [Patent text and drawings](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2527370A/en).

## Transcription

The printed radii are +35.01, −50.70 and +187.36; centre thicknesses are 7.87 and 5.25. The two glass rows are nD/νD 1.516/59.1 and 1.617/36.6. Stop distance S = 24.36, stop diameter 10.12, approximately f/6.3, nominal focal length 100 and approximately 19° total field are all printed. The patent uses arbitrary length units, interpreted as millimetres in this model. No prescription mismatch was found.

## First-Order

The initial model gave EFL 100.042892149 mm and glass-referenced back focus 88.198578061 mm, placing the image 63.838578061 mm behind the stop and 101.318578061 mm behind the first vertex. Its f/6.308159887 setting reproduced the printed 10.12 mm stop diameter. At that opening, a real-ray entrance-pupil calculation gave f/6.34033; the image-sine definition gave f/6.24045. These are different aperture definitions, not measured transmission.

## Not Printed

Back focus, individual glass clear diameters, close-focus data, a film format and specific glass suppliers are absent. The original 13 mm rims were estimates. The final image gap was computed from paraxial infinity focus. The D-line Abbe numbers do not supply individual spectral-line indices or partial dispersion.

## Product

No commercial product is documented for this prescription. The patent describes inexpensive subminiature colour-camera use.

## Corrections

The prescription radii, thicknesses, refractive indices, Abbe numbers and stop station agree with the source and remain unchanged. Figure 1 supports larger glass rims than the original estimates; the aperture designation follows the patent's approximate f/6.3 rather than the previous value derived from the printed bore.

## Remaining Questions

Glass supplier and melt identities, secondary spectrum, a production-lens connection and measured image quality remain unknown. The figure supplies an approximate rim measurement rather than a manufacturing drawing.
