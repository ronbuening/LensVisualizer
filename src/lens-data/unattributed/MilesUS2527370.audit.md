# MILES 100mm f/6.3 (US 2,527,370 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,527,370 A, _Optical System for Photographic Objectives_](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2527370A/en), by John R. Miles, assigned to La Croix Optical Company. Filed and claiming priority from 11 April 1949; published 24 October 1950. Figure 1 on PDF page 1 illustrates the sole numerical example. The prescription appears on PDF page 2, column 2, and is repeated in claim 1 on PDF page 3, column 3. Figure 2 shows prior art and has no separate numerical prescription.

## Transcription Check

All three radii and their signs, both centre thicknesses, both D-line index/Abbe pairs and the rear stop dimensions were re-read against the page images. The table and claim 1 agree: radii +35.01, −50.70 and +187.36; thicknesses 7.87 and 5.25; nD/νD pairs 1.516/59.1 and 1.617/36.6; stop distance 24.36 and stop diameter 10.12. No transcription mismatch was found. The signs and drawing agree with a biconvex crown followed by a biconcave flint, cemented at surface 2: two elements in one group.

The patent prints a focal length of 100 arbitrary units, approximately f/6.3 and approximately 19.0° total field. One source unit is taken as one millimetre throughout; the physical size is a choice, rather than a documented production specification.

## First-Order Check

The D-line paraxial EFL is 100.04 mm, less than 0.1% above the printed 100 at the chosen scale. The model uses f/6.30, with a stop radius of 5.067 mm and diameter of 10.133 mm. This is 0.1% larger than the printed 10.12 mm bore, consistent with the approximate aperture designation.

At the printed bore, the paraxial aperture is f/6.31, the real-ray entrance-pupil definition gives f/6.34 and the image-sine definition gives f/6.24. These are distinct aperture definitions, rather than transmission measurements.

The image plane is at D-line paraxial infinity focus: 88.20 mm behind the last glass vertex, 63.84 mm behind the stop and 101.32 mm behind the first vertex. There is no shift to minimise aberrations. The patent prints no back focal distance.

The surfaces stay clear of each other, the iris sets the full aperture and no glass rim crosses the stop plane. The 9.5° chief ray passes the front surface at 8.68 mm height, clears all three glass rims and reaches an image height of 16.92 mm. Chief-ray coverage extends across the stated field, although the corner bundle is slightly vignetted.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The stop station and bore are printed; the 15.3 mm glass semi-diameters are measured from Figure 1. The drawn stop distance and opening independently give consistent scales, within about 0.3%. The drawing is illustrative, so these rims are approximate rather than manufacturing dimensions. The front element has about 2.0 mm edge thickness.

The 9.5° half-field follows the printed 19.0° full field. Twice the real chief-ray image height gives the inferred 33.84 mm image-circle diameter, rounded upward. This is not a printed film format or a claim of a completely unvignetted field.

The image distance is calculated paraxially. `focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. No close-focus distance or focusing mechanism is given. The selectable f-stops extend from f/6.3 to f/32, but the patent supplies no minimum iris opening, diaphragm blade count or blade shape.

The patent describes ordinary window glass and ordinary flint. Its indices and Abbe numbers use the standard Fraunhofer D, F and C lines; the uppercase D reference is retained as sodium D, approximately 589.3 nm. Neither glass has an identified catalog equivalent. Individual C-, F- and g-line indices, partial dispersion, supplier and melt identities are absent. Abbe-based spectral estimates do not establish the historical glasses' secondary spectrum or measured broadband performance.

## Product Identification

No commercial product is documented for this prescription. The patent proposes inexpensive achromatic objectives for low-priced subminiature cameras using colour film. The manufacturer remains unconfirmed. No mount, film format, marketed focal length or aperture, marketed minimum focus distance, or diaphragm blade data is assigned. The patent's resolution claims express design intent without measured image-quality results.

## Corrections, 2026-11-21

- The key and title identify Miles's 100 mm f/6.3 patent design, and the lens is visible. The manufacturer remains unconfirmed because the patent's assignee alone does not establish production use. The 100-unit scale is retained as 100 mm without changing the printed prescription.
- The patent number includes the A publication code, and the assignee is given in full as La Croix Optical Company. The example is identified as the sole numerical example, rather than a numbered Figure 1 example, because Figure 2 contains only prior art. John R. Miles and the 1950 publication year are retained.
- The nominal aperture and first selectable stop changed from f/6.31 to the patent's approximate f/6.3. The resulting stop radius changed from 5.06 to 5.067 mm. Its station remains 24.36 mm behind surface 3; f/32 remains the smallest selectable aperture. The small bore difference is stated above.
- All three glass semi-diameters increased from 13 to 15.3 mm to follow Figure 1. At the printed bore and 9.5° half-field, passage of the stop-limited bundle increased from about 83.2% to 99.3%. Front-element edge thickness decreased from 3.67 mm to about 2.0 mm, with clear surfaces and no rim crossing the stop plane.
- The catalog design focal length changed from the nominal 100 to the calculated 100.04 mm. The inferred 33.84 mm image circle was added from the printed field and the real chief-ray height. The two-element, one-group counts and 19.0° full / 9.5° half-field are retained. The image plane remains at D-line paraxial infinity focus.
- The elements are labelled Element 1 and Element 2 and described as Biconvex Positive and Biconcave Negative. Their calculated in-air focal lengths, +41.43 and −64.13 mm, and the patent's image-forming, chromatic-balancing and field-flattening roles were added. Both cemented-element identifiers changed from D1 to C1, with a bracket across surfaces 1–3 and a cemented-achromatic-doublet group label.
- The generic unidentified glass descriptions were replaced by `Unmatched (ordinary window glass; nD 1.516 / ν 59.1)` and `Unmatched (ordinary flint; nD 1.617 / ν 36.6)`, using the patent's material names. Both remain unidentified: ΔnD = 0 and ΔνD = 0 for each description change. The sodium-D reference is retained, with no supplier or anomalous-dispersion claim.
- Floating-point noise in the stop-to-image gap was rounded, from 63.838578061040295 to 63.838578 mm; the image plane moves by less than 0.000001 mm.
- The subtitle names La Croix Optical and Miles beside the patent and sole example. The specifications state two elements in one group, all spherical surfaces, the chosen 100 mm design scale and design f/6.3.

After these corrections: EFL 100.04 mm; f/6.30; stop radius 5.067 mm; back focus 88.20 mm from the glass and 63.84 mm from the stop; first-vertex-to-image distance 101.32 mm; half-field 9.5°. The iris limits the axial beam, and the glass rims cover the inferred image circle.

## Remaining Questions

- Exact glass identities, suppliers, melts and secondary spectrum remain unknown.
- No production lens, manufacturer, mount, film format, marketed specifications, close-focus mechanism, diaphragm blade data or minimum iris opening is documented.
- The 15.3 mm rims follow the drawing to its measurement precision. About 0.7% corner-bundle clipping remains at the printed bore, and measured illumination and image quality are unknown.
