# HASSELKUS & RICHMOND 100mm f/4 (GB 295,519 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[GB 295,519 A, _Improvements in and relating to Objectives suitable for Photographic Purposes_](https://patents.google.com/patent/GB295519A/en), by John William Hasselkus and George Arthur Richmond. Filing and priority: 7 September 1927, application 23,531/27; complete specification left: 30 May 1928; publication and acceptance: 16 August 1928. The sole numerical example is on printed p. 2, with its section on the drawing sheet, PDF p. 4. Both applicants give 3 North Side, Clapham Common, London SW4. No company is named as applicant or assignee.

Product and historical sources are the [Science Museum Group's Ross Xpres Wide Angle Lens record](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co8204618/ross-xpres-wide-angle-lens), object 1990-5036/1287; Early Photography's [Xpres history](http://www.earlyphotography.co.uk/site/entry_L58.html); [Grace's Guide to Ross](https://gracesguide.co.uk/Ross), for the works address; and the Orwell Astronomical Society's [E. H. Collinson account](https://oasi.org.uk/OPO/EHC/EHC.php), for aerial-survey use.

## Transcription Check

All ten explicitly signed radii, six glass thicknesses, two outer air spaces, both central half-gaps, six helium indices and the three mercury-index pairs agree with the page images. No numerical mismatch was found. The patent expressly identifies Nd and Ng with helium and mercury. R2 and R9 are cemented interfaces, and all element shapes agree with the section. There are no aspherical surfaces or variable spacings.

The printed focal length is 100 units, without a physical unit. Each unit is represented as 1 mm. The printed b1 = b2 = 2.32 places the stop 14.63 mm behind R1, halfway through the 4.64 mm central air space.

## First-Order Check

The calculated helium d-line EFL is 100.08 mm, 0.1% above the stated 100-unit focal length. The paraxial f-number is 4.00, matching the printed f/4; the derived stop radius is 10.29 mm. Calculated back focus is 85.73 mm, which is not printed. The image plane lies at that distance behind R10, or 116.65 mm from R1; the first-to-last vertex span is 30.92 mm. The 35° half-field follows the printed 70° full field.

The front and rear doublets have focal lengths of −794.94 and −4859.31 mm; the menisci have +120.31 and +135.19 mm. The complete halves are approximately 167 and 161 mm. These values agree with the patent's stronger front negative doublet, stronger front positive meniscus and approximately equal half-lens focal lengths.

The surfaces stay clear of each other. The stated f/4 axial marginal clears every rim, with about 0.01 mm to spare at R3. The effective aperture of the real beam is f/4.02, 0.6% above nominal, because R3 clips the outer edge of the slightly larger beam admitted by the derived iris. No rim reduces the aperture beyond that small difference. The 35° chief ray clears every surface and reaches a 70.098 mm image height, about 0.002 mm short of the rounded 70.1 mm image-circle radius. Off-axis bundles are vignetted; a complete f/4 pupil is not transmitted at the field edge.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

Stop diameter, back focus, clear radii and physical image diameter are not tabulated. The paraxial entrance-pupil radius is 12.51 mm at f/4, giving the 10.29 mm stop radius through pupil magnification. The real f/4 marginal meets the stop plane at 10.22 mm. The stop opening lies in air, with about 0.89 mm clearance to the front glass and 1.21 mm to the rear meniscus's straight chamfer at its edge.

The clear radii R1–R10 are 13.1, 13.1, 10.64, 11.5, 10.3, 10.2, 12, 10.4, 13.1 and 13.1 mm. R1/R2 and R9/R10 follow the drawing's 13.1 mm doublet height, inside the positive elements' face crossings at about 13.57 and 14.86 mm. R4 follows the drawn meniscus height; R7 lies within about 4% of it. R3/R5/R6 retain the f/4 axial beam above the drawn chamfer ends, while R8 preserves off-axis light. All are inferred clear apertures. The R5 rim ends about 0.89 mm short of the stop plane.

The R3/R4 air gap closes near a radius of 10.90 mm, while the f/4 marginal needs heights of 10.628 and 10.631 mm and has about 0.093 mm air clearance. A 90% sag allowance would restrict the common radius to 10.47 mm and clip that beam. The retained 94% allowance accommodates 93.8% sag intrusion at the shared rim, leaving about 0.089 mm of air. The central gap retains about 2.03 mm at the shared 10.2 mm radius, and R7/R8 retain about 0.56 mm at 10.4 mm. These clearances do not establish manufacturing tolerances.

The 140.2 mm image circle is twice the real chief-ray height at the printed 35° half-field, rounded to 0.1 mm. Its field basis is printed, but its diameter is calculated at the chosen millimetre scale. It does not establish a production film format or unvignetted illumination.

`focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. No finite object distance, close-focus spacing or movement law is printed. All optical spacings remain fixed. The modeled iris spans f/4 to f/22; the patent supplies no minimum opening.

The helium d indices are stored for all six elements. The printed mercury g values are recorded in the glass labels. BALK3 supplies a dispersion curve for the two menisci, anchored to their printed d index. The four doublet elements have only their d index available to the model, so lens-wide results are monochromatic at helium d, approximately 587.6 nm.

## Product Identification

The applicants' address agrees with Ross Ltd's optical works address. Historical sources associate BP 295519 with the late-1920s Ross Wide Angle Xpres, and an f/4 example is documented in aerial-survey use. The Science Museum Group records a surviving 5-inch f/4–11 lens, serial 132787, with Air Ministry reference 14A/843. It supplies no construction drawing or numerical prescription establishing that the manufactured lens used this exact example. The connection is likely but unconfirmed.

The design retains the inventors' names and the patent-design title, with no confirmed maker. The same inventors' US 2,116,264 describes a different five-meniscus design. No production mount, film format, diaphragm blade count or shape, marketed focal length, marketed aperture or marketed minimum focus distance is assigned to this example.

## Remaining Questions

- The clear radii remain inferred. Lands approximate the doublets' short chamfers and broad shoulders, while straight joins approximate the meniscus chamfers; no numerical edge dimensions or manufacturing tolerances are printed.
- The derived iris admits a slightly larger real beam than the stated f/4 marginal; R3 clips its outermost rays. Increasing R3 to about 10.70 mm would exceed the retained 94% sag allowance. The stated f/4 marginal remains clear.
- Historical glass suppliers and melts remain unidentified. The meniscus proxy estimates dispersion away from the helium d reference. The doublet curves and the patent's chromatic-correction claim remain unresolved.
- No production prescription confirms the exact Ross Wide Angle Xpres connection. A mount, physical image format, diaphragm blade count or shape, minimum iris opening, barrel and baffle dimensions, marketed specifications and close-focus mechanism remain undocumented for this example.
