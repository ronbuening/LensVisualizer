# HASSELKUS & RICHMOND 100mm f/4 (GB 295,519 Design)

## Source

GB 295,519 A contains one numerical example on printed p. 2 and one section on the drawing sheet, PDF p. 4. The applicants are John William Hasselkus and George Arthur Richmond, of 3 North Side, Clapham Common, London SW4; no company is printed. Application: 7 September 1927; complete specification left: 30 May 1928; accepted: 16 August 1928.

## Transcription

Two independent readings of the page images agree on all ten explicitly signed radii, six glass thicknesses, two outer air spaces, both central half-gaps, six helium reference indices and the three mercury-index pairs. No numerical mismatch was found. R2 and R9 are cemented interfaces. The printed b1 = b2 = 2.32 puts the stop 14.63 mm behind R1. The patent expressly identifies Nd and Ng with the helium and mercury lines.

## First-Order Properties

The stated focal length is 100 units and aperture f/4. With dimensions represented in millimetres, the table computes to EFL 100.08235423 mm, paraxial f/4.000 and BFD 85.73492068 mm. The first-to-last vertex span is 30.92 mm; total distance to the paraxial image plane is 116.65492068 mm. The derived stop radius is 10.28905836 mm. The nominal half-field is 35°.

The front/rear doublets compute to −794.94/−4859.31 mm and the two menisci to +120.31/+135.19 mm. The complete halves are approximately 167/161 mm. These agree with the stated stronger front negative doublet, stronger front positive meniscus and approximately equal half-lens focal lengths.

## Values Not Printed

Clear radii, stop diameter, back focus and image diameter are not tabulated. The image spacing is the computed paraxial back focus; the stop radius follows f/4. The doublet rims are read from the section and the other rims remain estimated. The 94% sag allowance is a model setting: R3/R4 close near 10.9 mm, while the stated axial beam requires about 10.63 mm. No Abbe numbers, finite-focus data, mount, physical film format or production prescription are supplied.

## Product Connection

The applicants' address is Ross Ltd's works address. The Ross Wide Angle Xpres f/4, dating from around 1928 and associated with aerial survey, is a likely but unconfirmed connection. The [Science Museum Group record](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co8204618/ross-xpres-wide-angle-lens) documents a 5-inch f/4–11 example, but supplies no construction or prescription. Historical references connect the name to BP 295519 without establishing which numerical design was manufactured. The same inventors' US 2,116,264 is a different five-meniscus design.

## Corrections

The helium reference line was misidentified as D in the previous data. Its numerical indices were already correct. The former R1 and R10 rims extended beyond the positive elements' face crossings at approximately 13.57 and 14.86 mm. Reducing R1/R2 and R9/R10 to the drawn doublet height of 13.1 mm removes the crossing edges while passing the stated f/4 marginal and the 35° chief ray.

## Remaining Questions

The R5 rim ends about 0.05 mm short of the stop plane. Those outlines need further comparison with the figure; they have not been narrowed here.

Open: the derived iris admits a slightly larger real beam than the stated f/4 marginal. R3 limits its outermost rays; the effective aperture calculation gives f/4.024. Raising R3/R4 to 10.70/10.71 mm would conflict with the retained 94% gap allowance. The stated f/4 marginal clears both by about 0.01 mm.

Open: the historical melts and a production prescription connecting this table to the Ross Wide Angle Xpres remain undocumented. The two printed indices per glass do not supply a full dispersion curve.
