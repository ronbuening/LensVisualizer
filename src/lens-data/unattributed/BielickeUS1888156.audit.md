# BIELICKE 100mm f/2.5 (US 1,888,156 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 1,888,156 A, _Objective Lens System_](https://patents.google.com/patent/US1888156A/en), by William F. Bielicke (printed as “Willy F. Bielicke”), names no assignee. The US application was filed March 3, 1932, Serial No. 596,650; the German application dates from January 22, 1931. The patent was published November 15, 1932. Its sole numerical example appears on the drawing sheet and specification page 1, PDF pages 1–2.

The same invention appears in [GB 375,723 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/GB375723A/en) and [DE 634,843 C](https://patents.google.com/patent/DE634843C/en). It expressly improves Bielicke's earlier four-singlet [US 1,540,752 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US1540752A/en) design.

[Espen Susort's Astro-Berlin timeline](https://espensusort.no/posts/timeline-of-astro-berlin/), including its 1922 and 1931–1932 sections and lens list, supplies company history and an Astrar attribution. The [Pentacon Six Astro lens table](https://www.pentaconsix.com/astro.htm) lists later products and element counts, without a construction linking them to this prescription.

## Transcription Check

All nine radii, including plane R2, five glass thicknesses, three air spaces, five D-line indices, five G-line indices and five Abbe numbers agree with both printed tables. The page images show explicit radius signs and confirm five elements in four groups. Only R6 is cemented, joining elements 3 and 4. No numerical discrepancy was found; the dimensions retain the printed 100 mm scale and millimetre units. All curved surfaces are spherical, and no variable spacings are printed.

The inserted stop preserves the printed 9.2 mm air space as 5.4 + 3.8 mm. The printed G-line indices for elements 1–5 are 1.6741, 1.7015, 1.5998, 1.6741 and 1.6732. The analysis retains them with that designation; the source specifies no wavelength for G, so they are not treated as indices at 435.8 nm. The D-line indices remain referenced to sodium D.

## First-Order Check

The patent states 100 mm and f/2.5. The D-line prescription gives EFL 99.90 mm, 0.1% below the printed focal length, and paraxial BFD 78.28 mm. The image plane lies at this calculated infinity focus after R9; no back focus is printed. The first-to-last vertex distance is 48.1 mm and the first-vertex-to-image distance is 126.38 mm.

The paraxial aperture is f/2.50 at an inferred stop radius of 15.269 mm. Finite entrance-equivalent and image-sine f-numbers are both approximately 2.58 because of pupil aberration. A finite entrance-equivalent f/2.50 would require a stop radius of 15.820 mm; neither radius is a printed iris dimension. The iris sets the axial aperture without rim obstruction. The cemented group's combined focal length is +82.82 mm.

The 10.0° chief ray reaches image height 17.584 mm without blockage. Marginal off-axis rays remain vignetted by the front element; this selected field establishes no patent coverage claim.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The stop position, bore, clear semi-diameters, back focus, selected ±10.0° field and f-stop list are inferred or chosen. The stop lies 26.4 mm after the first vertex, within the R4–R5 air space, about 1 mm beyond the R4 rim sag; its placement follows the earlier four-singlet design. The drawing shows no iris. The stop radius follows paraxial f/2.5, and the image plane follows calculated paraxial infinity focus.

The surfaces stay clear of each other. Minimum glass edge thickness is about 0.381 mm; element 4 has about 0.711 mm thickness at the common rim. The largest air-gap sag consumption is about 57.5% of the axial gap, and no glass crosses the stop plane.

`focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. No close-focus distance, focusing mechanism, field angle, image format or image circle is printed. The selectable apertures span f/2.5 to f/22 without a published minimum iris opening. No image circle is assigned because the field is illustrative and no cited source supplies a field angle or format for this design.

The glass names and suppliers are unknown. All four distinct printed glass pairs remain Unmatched. A modern LF3 (Sumita) curve gives sodium-D index 1.58203 and Abbe number 42.02, differences of ΔnD ≈ −0.00007 and Δν ≈ +0.02 from element 3. This proximity does not establish the identity of the 1932 glass. The other pairs have no comparably close catalog curve. Approximate chromatic behavior follows the printed indices and Abbe numbers; complete dispersion curves, secondary spectrum, transmission and anomalous partial dispersion are not established.

## Product Identification

Bielicke co-founded Astro-Gesellschaft in Berlin, but this patent names no assignee. Susort's timeline lists the US, British and German patents under the Astrar, an f/2.7 line described there as a Cooke triplet. That description conflicts with this five-element/four-group f/2.5 design. No cited Astrar construction or prescription confirms the association, so the maker remains unidentified.

No confirmed product supplies a mount, image format, marketed focal length or aperture, minimum focus distance, diaphragm blade count or blade shape. Those properties are left unspecified.

## Corrections, 2026-11-21

- The key and title changed to the inventor-and-patent form, identifying the 100 mm f/2.5 design, and visibility changed from false to true. The maker remains unidentified and no assignee is assigned.
- The patent number gained the publication suffix A. The inventor's name changed from Willy F. Bielicke to William F. Bielicke to use the same form as his earlier US patent; the printed spelling remains recorded above. The sole example and 1932 publication year were retained.
- The subtitle is “US 1,888,156 A, sole example — Bielicke.” The specification chips are “5 ELEMENTS / 4 GROUPS”, “ALL SPHERICAL”, “DESIGN f = 100 mm” and “DESIGN F/2.5”, distinguishing the printed design scale from a marketed product.
- The design focal-length metadata changed from 100 to 99.90 mm to match the calculated EFL. The printed design aperture and the five-element/four-group counts were retained. No product mount, format, image circle or marketing values were added.
- Element labels changed from L1–L5 to Element 1–Element 5. Shape descriptions became Plano-Convex Positive, Biconcave Negative, Biconcave Negative, Biconvex Positive and Biconvex Positive. Optical roles and in-air focal lengths were added: +64.13, −38.09, −53.60, +34.19 and +194.57 mm.
- The cemented pair's identifier changed from D1 to C1, with its bracket spanning surfaces 5–7. The four group brackets identify the front collective lens, dispersive lens, cemented collective lens and rear collective lens, following the patent's description.
- Long glass descriptions became concise Unmatched labels for each printed pair: elements 1/4, 1.6580 / 51.4; element 2, 1.6741 / 32.0; element 3, 1.5821 / 42.0; element 5, 1.6513 / 38.3. Every element retains ΔnD = 0 and Δν = 0, its sodium-D reference and no anomalous-dispersion claim.

After these corrections, EFL is 99.90 mm, paraxial aperture f/2.50, BFD 78.28 mm, stop radius 15.269 mm and selected half-field 10.0°. The prescription scale, radii, thicknesses, air spaces, glass coordinates, index reference, stop position, clear rims, field and image plane are unchanged.

## Remaining Questions

- The drawing suggests smaller clear radii at R1/R2, about 21.1 mm versus 23 mm; R3, about 18.8 mm versus 22 mm; and R8/R9, about 21.3 mm versus 22 mm. At R1/R2 the retained edge is about 0.38 mm thick, against about 1.5 mm in the drawing. The smaller drawn rims admit the axial beam but increase vignetting at 10.0° from about 4.0% to 15.0%. The intended physical rim dimensions remain uncertain; the retained rims have not changed.
- Historical glass identities, suppliers, full dispersion curves and the wavelength meant by G are unknown.
- Physical iris position and diameter, blade count and shape, aperture range, field, image format, close-focus distance and focusing mechanism are undocumented.
- The Astrar attribution lacks a matching construction or prescription. A confirmed production association, maker, mount and marketed specifications remain unknown.
