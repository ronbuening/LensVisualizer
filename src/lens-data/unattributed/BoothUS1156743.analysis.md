# BOOTH 300mm f/8 (US 1,156,743 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [US 1,156,743 A, _Objective for Photographic and Like Purposes_](https://patents.google.com/patent/US1156743A/en)

**Inventors:** Lionel Barton Booth

**Assignee:** None printed

**Filed:** January 25, 1915

**Published:** October 12, 1915

**Example details:** Example 2, corresponding to Figure 2; the second of two numerical examples.

The patent describes a telephoto objective with a positive front assembly and a negative rear assembly. No production lens is documented as using this prescription.

The patent calls the focal length “approximately twelve units.” All printed radii, thicknesses and air spaces are multiplied by 25 mm per source unit, giving a nominal 300 mm lens. The source does not identify a physical unit. The computed D-line EFL is 302.10 mm, 0.7% above nominal. No evident transcription error explains the difference; the printed prescription is retained.

## Optical Architecture

The four spherical elements form three optical groups: a cemented front pair, followed by two air-spaced singlets. The front pair has positive combined power and the rear pair negative combined power, as the patent states (specification p. 1, Figure 2). The negative rear assembly produces a long effective focal length within a shorter lens-to-image distance.

The first-to-last surface distance is 59.49 mm. The calculated back focal distance is 152.88 mm, giving a total first-surface-to-image distance of 212.37 mm. The patent prints no back focus; the image plane is the computed paraxial infinity focus.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### Element 1 / L1 — Biconvex Positive

Printed nD = 1.5899; glass identity and Abbe number are unknown. Its standalone focal length in air is +46.74 mm. This is the strong positive front element, cemented to L2 at R2. The complete front pair has a focal length of +107.52 mm.

### Element 2 / L2 — Biconcave Negative

Printed nD = 1.6259; glass identity and Abbe number are unknown. Its standalone focal length in air is −76.07 mm. Its negative power balances L1. The patent describes the cemented R2 surface as dispersive (specification p. 1).

### Element 3 / L3 — Biconvex Positive

Printed nD = 1.6129; glass identity and Abbe number are unknown. Its standalone focal length in air is +65.00 mm. The weakly curved front face and strongly curved rear face lead the rear assembly. The air gap between L3 and L4 is 1 mm in the model.

### Element 4 / L4 — Biconcave Negative

Printed nD = 1.6119; glass identity and Abbe number are unknown. Its standalone focal length in air is −35.49 mm. Its stronger negative power makes the rear assembly negative overall. The patent describes the facing R5 and R6 surfaces as dispersive.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent prints three refractive indices per element and identifies the D and F lines of the solar spectrum and the Hγ line of hydrogen (specification p. 2, “Glasses used”). The D line is taken as sodium, 589.3 nm. The hydrogen G′ / Hγ line is about 434.05 nm and differs from the mercury g line at 435.8 nm.

| Element | nD     | nF      | nG′ / Hγ |
| ------- | ------ | ------- | -------- |
| L1      | 1.5899 | 1.5967  | 1.6022   |
| L2      | 1.6259 | 1.6381  | 1.6485   |
| L3      | 1.6129 | 1.6247  | 1.6348   |
| L4      | 1.6119 | 1.61965 | 1.62593  |

No glass identity, C-line index or Abbe number is printed. The glass labels retain all three printed indices, but tracing uses only nD and remains monochromatic. No dispersion curve is inferred, and the hydrogen index is not stored as a mercury g-line value. The patent's discussion of colour correction therefore cannot be assessed with this model.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent supplies no close-focus data or optical movement law. The model is focused at infinity.

## Stop and Clear Apertures

Neither a diaphragm nor numerical clear apertures appear in the patent. The inferred stop is 15 mm behind R3 and 20.175 mm ahead of R4, within the 35.175 mm air space. It lies 29.275 mm behind the first surface. At nominal f/8, its calculated paraxial radius is 14.444 mm; real-ray tracing gives an entrance-equivalent aperture of approximately f/8.16.

The estimated clear semi-diameters are 20.9 mm for surfaces R1–R3 and 13.5 mm for R4–R7. The front cemented pair and both rear singlets have square-cut rims, following Figure 2. The drawing suggests approximately 18.1 mm at the front and 12.4 mm at the rear at the chosen scale; the modeled rims retain about 97% geometric transmission at the edge of the illustrative ±7.0° field. They pass the axial f/8 bundle without rim clipping. The minimum shared-radius clearance between R5 and R6 is about 0.62 mm. No numerical clear diameters are printed.

## Field and Performance Limits

The patent states no field angle, image format or image circle. The displayed ±7.0° field is illustrative. With the estimated apertures, the 7.0° chief ray reaches an image height of about 38.00 mm. Manufactured coverage is undocumented.

The calculated focal length and back focus describe the printed prescription at the D line. Off-axis performance depends on the inferred stop and rims. Monochromatic geometric results do not establish broadband performance or the performance of a manufactured lens.
