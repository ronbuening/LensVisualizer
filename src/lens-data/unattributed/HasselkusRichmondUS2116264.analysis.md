# HASSELKUS & RICHMOND 195mm f/5.6 (US 2,116,264 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,116,264 A, *Objective Suitable for Photographic Purposes*  
**Application Number:** Serial No. 147,102  
**Filed:** June 8, 1937 (British priority June 9, 1936)  
**Granted:** May 3, 1938  
**Inventors:** John William Hasselkus; George Arthur Richmond  
**Example details:** the single numerical table on the first text page (right column) and the single drawing

The patent names no assignee; it issued to the two inventors, both of Clapham, London. It is three pages long: one drawing sheet and two pages of text. It contains exactly one numerical example, and the data file transcribes it at the patent's own scale in millimetres. The patent states a focal length of 194.5 mm, a relative aperture of "about F/5.6" and a field of view of "approximately 100°".

No production lens is identified with this design. The reasons are set out so that a reader can judge them:

1. The patent is unassigned. Both inventors give Clapham, London as their address, and a collector reference lists the same two men as the designers of Ross Xpres lenses, so a connection with Ross of London is likely. Neither that reference nor the patent states their employer.
2. The best-known Ross wide-angle lens of the period, the Wide Angle Xpres f/4, is described in the same collector reference as a Plasmat-type lens, which has cemented components. That is a different construction from the five separate menisci of this patent, so it is not this design.
3. No maker's catalogue, museum record or published section drawing was found that shows a five-meniscus Ross lens of 194.5 mm (about 7⅝ inches) at f/5.6.

The model therefore represents the patent example alone, at the patent's own 194.5 mm scale, and is filed under the inventors' names with no maker.

## Optical Architecture

The objective is a wide-angle design of the kind in which thin, deeply curved menisci surround a central diaphragm and every lens turns its concave side toward that diaphragm. The patent describes it as an improvement on British specification 423,156, which covers the four-meniscus form (a positive and a negative meniscus on each side of the stop). The improvement is a fifth lens: a second deep positive meniscus added behind the rear pair. The patent says this extra power allows the aperture to be raised to F/5.6 while keeping spherical aberration, coma, distortion and astigmatism corrected.

In order from the front the lens has:

- lens I, a thick positive meniscus;
- lens II, a thin negative meniscus, with its convex face 0.06 mm from the concave face of lens I;
- the diaphragm, in a 40.56 mm air space;
- lens III, a thin negative meniscus that mirrors lens II;
- lenses IV and V, two positive menisci, each separated from its neighbour by 0.06 mm.

That is five elements in five groups, with no cemented surface and no aspheric surface. From the first to the last surface the lens is 75.19 mm long, about 0.39 of its focal length.

Two features of the printed table stand out. The two negative shells are identical and are placed symmetrically, 20.28 mm from the middle of the central space if the diaphragm sits at that middle. And the two outermost surfaces are almost exactly concentric with the centre of the diaphragm: the centre of curvature of the first surface lies 0.13 mm in front of the mid-point of the central space and that of the last surface 0.04 mm in front of it. This matches the patent's claim that the exterior positive menisci are "substantially portions of a bounding spherical surface". A chief ray therefore meets the outer surfaces close to the normal at any field angle, which is what lets the design cover so wide a field.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are computed for each element alone in air from the printed radii, thicknesses and Nd.

### L1 / Lens I — Positive Meniscus

R1 = +36.15 mm, R2 = +54.15 mm, thickness 14.33 mm, Nd 1.6202, ν 60.4. Focal length +134.39 mm. This is the strongest positive element. The patent requires the single front positive lens to be stronger than either of the two rear positive lenses, and it is.

### L2 / Lens II — Negative Meniscus

R3 = +29.45 mm, R4 = +23.75 mm, thickness 1.61 mm, Nd 1.7174, ν 29.5. Focal length −193.93 mm. A thin dense-flint shell that supplies negative power close to the diaphragm for colour correction and field flattening.

### L3 / Lens III — Negative Meniscus

R5 = −23.75 mm, R6 = −29.45 mm, thickness 1.61 mm, Nd 1.7174, ν 29.5. Focal length −193.93 mm. The mirror image of lens II. The patent asks only that the two negative lenses have approximately the same radii; the example makes them identical.

### L4 / Lens IV — Positive Meniscus

R7 = −55.65 mm, R8 = −44.05 mm, thickness 6.20 mm, Nd 1.6202, ν 60.4. Focal length +282.87 mm.

### L5 / Lens V — Positive Meniscus

R9 = −47.90 mm, R10 = −38.95 mm, thickness 10.70 mm, Nd 1.6202, ν 60.4. Focal length +230.59 mm. This is the added lens that distinguishes the patent from the earlier four-meniscus form.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent prints three figures for each of two glasses and names neither:

| Lenses | Nd | Ng | ν |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I, IV, V | 1.6202 | 1.6329 | 60.4 |
| II, III | 1.7174 | 1.7492 | 29.5 |

The model traces the printed Nd and ν as helium d-line values. The first pair is a dense barium crown and the second a dense flint; the patent names neither and the supplier of the 1936 melts is unknown. The flint agrees with the Schott SF1 catalogue row (1.71736, 29.51) at the printed precision, and the data file labels it SF1 on that basis; the trace keeps the printed Nd. The crown is 0.0002 from the nearest catalogue row, SK16 (1.62041, 60.32), which is outside the tolerance for a label, so it stays unmatched. The printed Ng values are recorded in the data file as notes.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent gives one infinity prescription and says nothing about focusing. No focus movement is modeled. The image plane is placed at the computed paraxial back focus, 152.98 mm behind the last surface, because the patent prints no back focus.

## Patent Conditions and Model Scope

What is printed and what is inferred:

- Printed and copied without change: ten radii, five thicknesses, four air spaces, and Nd and ν for both glasses. Every value was checked against the page image and none differs.
- Inferred from the drawing and text: the signs of the radii (the table prints magnitudes only).
- Inferred: the diaphragm position, taken as the middle of the 40.56 mm central air space, which is where the drawing shows it.
- Inferred: every clear semi-diameter. The patent prints no diameters; the values are read from the drawing, which is to scale.
- Computed: the back focus.

First-order results of the model:

| Quantity | Patent | Model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Focal length | 194.5 mm | 194.93 mm (+0.22 %) |
| Relative aperture | about F/5.6 | F/5.6 (stop semi-diameter 13.14 mm) |
| Back focus | not printed | 152.98 mm |
| First to last surface | not printed | 75.19 mm |
| Field of view | approximately 100° | 100° (chief ray to 50.0°; see below) |

The patent's conditions hold in the model: every radius is shorter than the focal length, the negative lenses share their radii, and lens I (+134.39 mm) is stronger than lens IV (+282.87 mm) or lens V (+230.59 mm).

At F/5.6 the marginal axial ray of the printed prescription focuses 1.91 mm beyond the paraxial focus, the 85 % zone 0.42 mm beyond it, and the 70 % and 50 % zones 0.19 mm and 0.32 mm short of it. That is the usual zonal pattern of this lens type at an aperture the patent itself calls approximate.

The field needs a note. The drawing is to scale, and it draws each lens with its convex face running out to the element's edge and its concave face, the one toward the diaphragm, ending a little lower with a short flat step square to the axis. The model follows it. The rims are the heights a chief ray reaches at the stated 50° half-field, all within the drawn outlines: the convex faces end at 33.4 mm (I), 23.7 mm (II, III), 33.4 mm (IV) and 35.8 mm (V), and the concave faces end lower with drawn flat steps out to the element's edge. The outer surfaces of lenses I and V turn steeply near their rims, as drawn: at its 33.4 mm rim the front surface is tilted 67.5° from the plane square to the axis, beyond the 64.2° the model allows by default, so this lens carries a 68° limit. The chief ray then passes every rim to a half-field of 50.0°, an image height of about 231.5 mm, which is the patent's 100° field. Off-axis bundles are progressively vignetted well before that edge, as the drawing's small inner shells also imply.
