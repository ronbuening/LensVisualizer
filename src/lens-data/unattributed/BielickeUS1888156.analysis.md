# BIELICKE 100mm f/2.5 (US 1,888,156 Design)

## Patent and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 1,888,156 A, _Objective Lens System_

**Inventors:** William F. Bielicke

**Assignee:** None stated

**Published:** November 15, 1932

**Filed:** March 3, 1932; German application January 22, 1931

**Example details:** Sole numerical example, repeated on the drawing sheet and specification page 1.

The [patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US1888156A/en) gives a 100 mm f/2.5 objective containing five elements in four groups. It improves Bielicke's earlier US 1,540,752 four-singlet design by making a rear collective group a cemented flint-and-crown pair to improve correction of oblique beams (specification p. 1, lines 13–32).

The same invention was filed in Britain and Germany as [GB 375,723 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/GB375723A/en) and [DE 634,843 C](https://patents.google.com/patent/DE634843C/en). Bielicke co-founded Astro-Gesellschaft in Berlin; the patent names no assignee. [Espen Susort's collector timeline](https://espensusort.no/posts/timeline-of-astro-berlin/) lists all three patents under the Astrar, an f/2.7 line it describes as a Cooke triplet. This conflicts with the five-element/four-group f/2.5 design; no Astrar construction or prescription confirms the link.

## Optical Architecture

From object to image, the groups are a positive plano-convex singlet, a negative biconcave singlet, a positive cemented pair, and a weak positive biconvex singlet. Element 1 has a plane rear face at R2. Only elements 3 and 4 are cemented, sharing R6; all curved surfaces are spherical.

The central positive pair places a dispersive flint before a crown of higher index and lower dispersion, as the patent specifies. Its combined focal length is +82.82 mm; the individual element focal lengths below are calculated in air and describe their separate powers.

The first-to-last surface distance is 48.1 mm. At the D line the printed prescription gives EFL 99.90 mm, 0.1% below the stated 100 mm. The image plane is the calculated paraxial back focus, 78.28 mm after R9, giving 126.38 mm from the first vertex to the image. The patent does not print a back focus.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The diagram uses the patent's element designations I–V, in order from object to image. All refractive indices below refer to the printed D line, taken as sodium at 589.3 nm.

### Element 1 — Plano-Convex Positive

nD = 1.6580, ν = 51.4. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.6580 / ν 51.4). In-air f = +64.13 mm.

The front crown collects the incident beam. Its convex front face supplies the refractive power; the rear face is plane. It shares its printed glass values with element 4.

### Element 2 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.6741, ν = 32.0. Glass: S-TIM25 (Ohara), spectral proxy. In-air f = −38.09 mm.

This is the dispersive singlet between the front collective element and the rear collective groups. It has the lowest Abbe number in the prescription and works with the positive elements in chromatic correction.

### Element 3 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.5821, ν = 42.0. Glass: LF3 (Sumita), matches the printed pair. In-air f = −53.60 mm.

The negative front member of cemented group C1 adds dispersive power behind the stop. Its shared rear surface R6 joins element 4 without an air space.

### Element 4 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.6580, ν = 51.4. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.6580 / ν 51.4). In-air f = +34.19 mm.

This thick positive crown is the rear member of C1. Its greater positive power makes the pair collective despite element 3's negative power. The higher index and lower dispersion relative to element 3 follow the patent's stated cemented-pair construction.

### Element 5 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.6513, ν = 38.3. Glass: BASF4 (Sumita), spectral proxy. In-air f = +194.57 mm.

The rear singlet supplies weak positive power through equal and opposite radii of 252.5 mm. A 1.1 mm axial air space separates it from the cemented group.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent prints D-line and G-line indices and Abbe numbers for every element. Its discussion identifies the 1.6580 / 51.4 pair shared by elements I and IV as high-index baryta crown. The stocks and suppliers are unspecified.

| Element |     nD | nG, as printed |    ν |
| ------- | -----: | -------------: | ---: |
| 1 / I   | 1.6580 |         1.6741 | 51.4 |
| 2 / II  | 1.6741 |         1.7015 | 32.0 |
| 3 / III | 1.5821 |         1.5998 | 42.0 |
| 4 / IV  | 1.6580 |         1.6741 | 51.4 |
| 5 / V   | 1.6513 |         1.6732 | 38.3 |

Element 3 matches LF3 (Sumita) within printed precision: ΔnD = −0.00007 and Δν = +0.02. Elements 2 and 5 use spectral proxies: S-TIM25 (Ohara), ΔnD = −0.00158 and Δν = +0.09; and BASF4 (Sumita), ΔnD = −0.00017 and Δν = +0.02. These differences are catalog minus printed values at sodium D.

The model uses the printed D-line indices and Abbe numbers. Catalog curves supply the dispersion shape for elements 2, 3 and 5. The spectral proxies are within the site's spectral-proxy tolerance of 0.002 in index and 0.25 in Abbe number at sodium D. Elements 1 and 4 use Abbe-based dispersion because no catalog row falls within that tolerance.

The G-line values retain their printed designation. The patent gives no wavelength for G, so the model does not treat them as indices at 435.8 nm. Neither the printed values nor the catalog curves establish the complete dispersion curves or secondary spectrum of the historical glasses.

The patent describes the use of high-index baryta crown to improve field flatness. Adding the cemented flint member permits chromatic correction without relying on a very heavy flint whose yellow tint would absorb blue light (specification p. 1, lines 33–58).

## Focus Mechanism and Aperture

The model is focused at infinity; the patent gives no close-focus data or focusing mechanism. The stop is inferred within the printed 9.2 mm air space between R4 and R5, divided into 5.4 mm before and 3.8 mm after the stop. Its axial station is 26.4 mm after the first vertex; the figure draws no iris.

Nominal f/2.5 gives an inferred stop radius of 15.269 mm. The admitted axial beam has entrance-equivalent and image-sine f-numbers of about f/2.58. These finite-ray values differ from the paraxial f-number because of pupil aberration. No clear rim limits the axial aperture. A finite entrance-equivalent f/2.5 would require a larger stop radius of 15.820 mm, which is not a printed iris dimension.

## Model Scope and Limitations

All clear semi-diameters are inferred from the drawing's relative heights and ray clearance. The front and rear singlets have 22 mm rims. On either side of the inferred stop, the drawing tapers the negative singlet from its outer shoulder to R4 and the front of the cemented pair from its outer shoulder to R5. The modeled semi-diameters are 19/17 mm at R3/R4 and 16.6/19/19 mm at R5/R6/R7. Straight joins between the modeled rim heights approximate the drawn shoulders and bevels. The drawing's local thicknesses differ from the table, so the semi-diameters are approximate. The front element's edge is 1.01 mm thick. No rim obstructs the axial beam or the chief ray at the displayed 10.0° half-field. Some off-axis marginal rays are vignetted by the front element. Geometric pupil transmission is 98.9% at 8.3° and 96.7% at 10.0°. The ±10.0° field and f-stop list are chosen for the model. The source gives no image format or image circle.

The model preserves the printed prescription and uses its paraxial infinity image plane. Geometric image-quality calculations describe this modeled aperture and field; measured production performance and full spectral behavior are not established.
