# MILES 100mm f/6.3 (US 2,527,370 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,527,370 A, _Optical System for Photographic Objectives_

**Inventors:** John R. Miles

**Assignee:** La Croix Optical Company

**Filed:** 11 April 1949

**Published:** 24 October 1950

**Example details:** Sole numerical example, illustrated in Figure 1; the table in column 2 is repeated in claim 1.

The patent proposes an inexpensive achromatic objective for low-priced subminiature cameras using colour film. No commercial product is documented for this prescription. Figure 2 shows prior art, with shorter radii and no separate numerical table. [1]

The patent specifies a focal length of 100 arbitrary units, taken as millimetres in this model. Its approximately f/6.3 aperture and approximately 19° total field describe the design at that chosen size, rather than a documented production lens.

## Optical Architecture

Two spherical elements form one cemented group: a biconvex positive crown followed by a biconcave negative flint. Surface 2 is their shared interface. The front element supplies the positive power; the rear element provides opposing chromatic power. The patent also assigns both elements a field-flattening role. [1, column 2]

The aperture stop lies 24.36 mm behind the last glass vertex. Its position and 10.12 mm diameter are printed dimensions. The moderate field and relatively long radii are intended to limit spherical aberration while allowing inexpensive glass and construction. [1, columns 1–3]

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### Element 1 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.516, νD = 59.1. Glass: ordinary window glass, unmatched to a specific catalogue glass. Focal length in air: +41.43 mm.

The 7.87 mm front element provides the image-forming power. Its shared rear surface couples it to the more dispersive negative element, which balances the crown's chromatic power. The patent explicitly describes ordinary window glass as the low-cost crown material. [1, columns 1–2]

### Element 2 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.617, νD = 36.6. Glass: F4 (Sumita), spectral proxy for ordinary flint. Focal length in air: −64.13 mm.

The 5.25 mm rear flint supplies negative power and stronger dispersion. Together the two elements form the achromat described in the patent; their combined D-line focal length is 100.04 mm. [1, column 2]

## Glass Identification and Spectral Scope

The printed indices refer to the D line (taken as sodium, 589.3 nm). The patent defines the Abbe number as (nD − 1)/(nF − nC), using the standard Fraunhofer lines. These uppercase-D values are retained without conversion to the helium d line. [1, column 2 and claim 1]

Element 2 uses F4 (Sumita) as a spectral proxy, with ΔnD = −0.00056 and ΔνD = +0.01 (catalogue minus printed at sodium D). These differences fall within the proxy limits of 0.002 in index and 0.25 in Abbe number. The printed values are kept; the catalogue curve supplies only the dispersion shape, so colour analysis follows a real glass rather than an Abbe-number estimate. The row does not identify the historical supplier or melt. [2]

Element 1 remains unmatched because no catalogue row falls within those limits, and its dispersion is estimated from the Abbe number. Individual C-, F- and g-line indices and partial dispersions are not printed for either glass; the model does not establish their historical secondary spectra or measured broadband performance.

## Focus Mechanism

The model is focused at infinity; the patent gives no close-focus data or focusing mechanism. Its image plane is computed from D-line paraxial focus: 88.20 mm behind the last glass vertex, or 63.84 mm behind the stop. The patent prints no back focal distance.

## Aperture, Field and Clear Apertures

The printed 10.12 mm stop diameter at the stated position gives a paraxial aperture of f/6.31, consistent with the patent's approximately f/6.3. The model uses f/6.3, giving a 10.133 mm iris, 0.1% larger than the printed diameter. This small difference follows from using the rounded aperture designation.

The 19.0° total field gives a 9.5° half-field. At that angle the real chief ray clears all three glass rims and reaches an image height of 16.92 mm. The model's 33.84 mm image circle is twice that height, rounded up; it follows from the patent's stated field, and the patent names no film format.

Glass semi-diameters are not tabulated. All three rims are estimated as 15.3 mm from Figure 1, preserving the square-cut outline of the cemented pair. The drawn stop spacing and opening both give about 15.5 pixels per source unit; the glass rims read about 15.2–15.3 mm within the ink-line precision. The axial beam and 9.5° chief ray clear all three surfaces. At model f/6.3, geometric transmission is about 99.2% at 9.5° and 100% through 7.9°. The front element retains about 2.0 mm edge thickness. Some corner vignetting remains; the field designation is not a claim of completely uniform illumination.

## First-Order Properties and Limits

The printed prescription gives EFL 100.04 mm, less than 0.1% above the nominal 100 at the chosen size. The first-vertex-to-image distance is 101.32 mm. The image plane remains the paraxial plane, without a shift to minimise aberrations.

The prescription, stop position and D-line glass values are source dimensions; the glass rims are measured from the drawing, and back focus and image-circle diameter are computed. The patent's claims of improved resolution describe its design intent and are not measured performance data.
