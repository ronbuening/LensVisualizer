# BOOTH 300mm f/8 (US 1,156,743 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 1,156,743 A, _Objective for Photographic and Like Purposes_](https://patents.google.com/patent/US1156743A/en), Lionel Barton Booth, of Cambridge, England. The application was filed January 25, 1915, also the listed priority date, and the patent was published October 12, 1915. No assignee is printed. Example 2 is on specification p. 2 (PDF p. 3), corresponding to Figure 2 on the drawing sheet. There are two numerical examples.

## Transcription Check

All seven signed radii, four glass thicknesses, two air spaces and four D-line indices agree with the patent page images. Every printed dimension is multiplied by 25 mm per source unit; the source does not identify a physical unit. The 35.175 mm first air space is divided at the inferred stop into 15 mm and 20.175 mm. The 1 mm rear gap retains the printed proportion. The cemented R2 interface and the shapes and connectivity of all four elements agree with Figure 2. No transcription mismatch was found.

The printed nD / nF / nG′ values are L1: 1.5899 / 1.5967 / 1.6022; L2: 1.6259 / 1.6381 / 1.6485; L3: 1.6129 / 1.6247 / 1.6348; L4: 1.6119 / 1.61965 / 1.62593. The patent identifies its third index as hydrogen Hγ, about 434.05 nm; this differs from the mercury g line at 435.8 nm. No C-line index, Abbe number or glass identity is printed. Tracing uses only the printed D-line indices, taken as sodium at 589.3 nm, and remains monochromatic.

## First-Order Check

The patent describes a focal length of approximately twelve units and prints f/8. At 25 mm per source unit, the nominal focal length is 300 mm and the computed EFL is 302.10 mm, a +0.7% difference. No evident transcription error explains the difference. The approximate wording supports retaining the printed prescription; the historical cause of the residual is unconfirmed.

The calculated paraxial BFD is 152.88 mm, equal to the last gap and placing the image plane at infinity focus. No back focus is printed. The first-to-last surface distance is 59.49 mm and the first-surface-to-image distance is 212.37 mm. The paraxial f-number is 8.00 with a stop radius of 14.444 mm; the real-ray entrance-equivalent aperture is approximately f/8.16. The front cemented pair has focal length +107.52 mm; the individual element focal lengths in air are +46.74, −76.07, +65.00 and −35.49 mm.

The surfaces stay clear of each other, with about 0.168 mm clearance between R5 and R6 at the shared rim. The iris sets the axial full aperture without rim clipping. The 7.0° chief ray clears every rim and reaches an image height of about 38.00 mm.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

No diaphragm is shown, and the patent gives no stop station or diameter, clear semi-diameters, back focus, field angle, image format or close-focus data. The nominal 300 mm scale, stop, rims and paraxial image plane are inferred. The stop lies 15 mm behind R3 and 20.175 mm ahead of R4, or 29.275 mm behind the first surface. Its radius follows paraxial f/8. The estimated front semi-diameters are 22.5 mm and the rear semi-diameters 19 mm.

The ±7.0° field is illustrative, with no declared image circle or format. `focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. No finite-distance focus state is specified. The selectable apertures run from f/8 to f/32; the source prints only f/8 and supplies no stop range. The printed F and Hγ indices are retained in the glass labels, without additional spectral indices or an inferred dispersion curve. The patent's colour-correction claims cannot be assessed by the monochromatic model.

Figure 2 suggests front semi-heights of about 18.1 mm and rear semi-heights of about 12.4 mm. Clear radii of 19 mm at the front and 12.5 mm at the rear would admit the axial f/8 bundle but give approximately 15.0% corner vignetting at the illustrative 7.0° field. The retained radii have no axial clipping defect; neither numerical diameters nor a field angle are printed to settle the difference.

## Product Identification

No documented production lens is tied to this prescription. Its four elements form three air-separated groups: a positive cemented front pair and a negative rear assembly of air-spaced positive and negative singlets. Booth's later [US 1,421,156 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US1421156A/en) describes a different five-element, three-group design.

No maker, mount, production image format, diaphragm blade count or shape, marketed focal length, marketed f-number or marketed minimum focus distance is assigned.
