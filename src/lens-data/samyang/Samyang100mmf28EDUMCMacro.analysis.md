# Samyang 100mm F2.8 ED UMC Macro — Optical Analysis

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** KR 10-2017-0073883 A  
**Application Number:** 10-2015-0182611  
**Filed:** 2015-12-21  
**Published:** 2017-06-29  
**Inventors:** Jong Young Park (박종영), Jung Du Lee (이정두)  
**Applicant:** Samyang Optics Co., Ltd.  
**Title:** 렌즈계 및 이를 포함하는 광학 기기 / Lens system and optical device comprising the same  
**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 1, Table 1, Figure 1

The patent's only numerical example is associated with the manual-focus Samyang 100mm F2.8 ED UMC Macro through its optical construction. Samyang has not confirmed that the example gives the production radii, dimensions, glasses or coatings.

1. Fifteen spherical elements form twelve groups. Two separated object-convex front menisci precede a long gap; three central cemented pairs are followed by five rear singlets.
2. Samyang's published optical section shows 15 elements in 12 groups with the HR mark on element 3 and the ED mark on element 4, agreeing with Figure 1 and with the EFDS1_HOYA and FCD1_HOYA glasses the patent names at those positions.
3. The computed EFL is 98.043026 mm, consistent with the printed 98.04 and close to the marketed 100 mm. The printed f/2.9 and marketed f/2.8 remain distinct. The patent's aberration figures are labelled "EFL=100 MACRO 1:1".
4. The patent and Samyang's section agree on the rear sequence, including the final two image-convex negative menisci. Claim 1's contrary wording for the shape of L33 is noted as a discrepancy.

The lens was announced on 2015-04-02, before the 2015-12-21 filing; that order is noted and is not itself evidence for the identification. The optical formula is represented for the documented Canon EF, Nikon F, Pentax K, Sony A, Canon EF-M, Fujifilm X, Samsung NX, Sony E, Four Thirds and Micro Four Thirds variants; Four Thirds is in the 2015 launch list of ten mounts and absent from the current product page. Full-frame is the design's image-format classification; the smaller-format mounts use a crop.

The model covers the infinity state at the d line only. The stop position, the in-air image plane and the six clear apertures that differ from the printed C column are modelling choices, explained below; none is presented as a proven correction of the patent.

## Optical Architecture

The source divides the lens into a fixed front G1, a central G2 and a rear G3. The numerical table gives the following standalone group values in air:

| Group | Elements | Source surfaces | Computed EFL (mm) |
|---|---|---|---:|
| G1 | L10–L11 | 1–4 | −752.448607 |
| G2 | L20–L27 | 5–17 | +51.213202 |
| G3 | L30–L34 | 19–28 | −88.286184 |

The weak negative first group, strong positive center and negative rear group agree in sign with the abstract and ¶0054. They do not agree with the summary's definitions of f1=+53.1259 and f2=−88.477. The table and source-defined group boundaries are retained rather than altered to reproduce those summaries.

The first-to-last vertex distance is 123.498002 mm. The adopted all-air infinity plane is another 40.641335 mm behind S28, giving a total first-vertex-to-image distance of 164.139337 mm. Total track/EFL is 1.674156, so the construction is not described as a telephoto-ratio-below-one system. BFD is below EFL, so no retrofocus classification is claimed either. Manufacturer “macro telephoto” wording describes the product category, not these first-order ratios.

The central cemented components have net EFLs of −63.902670 mm for L21/L22, +337.721559 mm for L23/L24 and +159.718877 mm for L25/L26. The opposing standalone element powers do not sum directly to these thick, cemented component powers.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Elements carry the patent's reference signs, which are also the labels on the diagram: L10 and L11 in G1, L20–L27 in G2 and L30–L34 in G3. The number in each heading is the patent's ordinal (first lens to fifteenth lens), which is the count Samyang's section uses.

Every focal length below is calculated for that individual glass element surrounded by air. It is not an in-situ power assigned to a shared cemented interface. Modern catalog names identify source-named glass-family approximations, not production melt identity.

### L10 (element 1) — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.658436, νd = 50.85. Glass: BACED5 (Hoya; source-named catalog equivalent). f = -132.443127 mm.

L10 is the object-convex negative front meniscus described in ¶0056. Its large clear aperture accommodates off-axis incident bundles ahead of the long central separation.

### L11 (element 2) — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.785897, νd = 43.93. Glass: NBFD11 (Hoya; source-named catalog equivalent). f = +151.440632 mm.

L11 is an object-convex positive meniscus. Together with L10 and their separation, it forms the weak net-negative G1; the sign of a compound group cannot be inferred from its last element alone.

### L20 (element 3) — Plano-Convex

nd = 1.922860, νd = 20.88. Glass: E-FDS1 (Hoya; source-named catalog equivalent). f = +54.006025 mm.

L20 is front-convex and plane-backed. Its high index matches the HR mark on the third element of Samyang's section. It begins the source G2, behind the 41.915080 mm separation after S4.

### L21 (element 4) — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.496997, νd = 81.61. Glass: FCD1 (Hoya; source-named catalog equivalent). f = +59.333674 mm.

L21 is the low-dispersion positive member of the first cemented doublet. FCD1 is an extra-low-dispersion crown (ΔPgF ≈ +0.032 from its catalog lines), and the element sits where Samyang's section marks ED; the data file tags it as inferred anomalous-dispersion glass on that basis, since the patent itself makes no such statement. The modeled S7 clear radius is the source C value, 15.57 mm.

### L22 (element 5) — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.846663, νd = 23.78. Glass: FDS90 (Hoya; source-named catalog equivalent). f = -28.444777 mm.

L22 is the high-dispersion negative partner of L21. Their shared boundary is S8, carrying L22 as the downstream material. The complete L21/L22 pair is net negative, as ¶0058 states. S8 and S9 are modeled at 14.58 and 13.06 mm, 0.01 and 0.02 mm above the printed C, which is where the traced f/2.904 axial ray meets them.

### L23 (element 6) — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.487489, νd = 70.44. Glass: FC5 (Hoya; source-named catalog equivalent). f = +39.664113 mm.

L23 is the positive crown member of the second doublet. Its front face S10 is modeled at 13.05 mm, 0.02 mm above the printed C, where the traced f/2.904 axial ray meets it; at that height the L22 rear and L23 front faces use 91.4% of their 1.676 mm air gap.

### L24 (element 7) — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.846663, νd = 23.78. Glass: FDS90 (Hoya; source-named catalog equivalent). f = -43.479933 mm.

L24 is the image-convex negative flint member of the second doublet, joined to L23 at S11. The whole pair remains weakly positive despite L24’s negative standalone power. S11 is modeled at 12.66 mm, 0.02 mm above the printed C, for the same axial ray.

### L25 (element 8) — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.688930, νd = 31.16. Glass: E-FD8 (Hoya; source-named catalog equivalent). f = -50.822874 mm.

L25 is the negative first member of the third cemented pair. Its object-side surface is concave; it joins L26 at the weakly curved S14 interface.

### L26 (element 9) — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.805181, νd = 25.46. Glass: FD60 (Hoya; source-named catalog equivalent). f = +41.656524 mm.

L26 is the positive member of the third pair. The combined L25/L26 component is positive, in agreement with ¶0058. No measured aberration contribution is assigned to either member separately.

### L27 (element 10) — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.719998, νd = 50.34. Glass: LAC10 (Hoya; source-named catalog equivalent). f = +96.575494 mm.

L27 is a separated biconvex singlet with equal-magnitude radii. It completes G2 immediately before the adopted plane stop. Paragraph 0064 places the stop near this lens.

### L30 (element 11) — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.701543, νd = 41.15. Glass: BAFD7 (Hoya; source-named catalog equivalent). f = -56.397481 mm.

L30 is an object-convex negative meniscus beginning G3. Its powered front face S19 is where Table 1 prints STO; the model places the stop on plane S18 instead, for the reasons given below. With the iris on S18 the traced axial ray meets S19 at 10.7007 mm, so S19 is modeled at 10.71 mm against the printed 10.7.

### L31 (element 12) — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.487489, νd = 70.44. Glass: FC5 (Hoya; source-named catalog equivalent). f = -78.538864 mm.

L31 is the biconcave negative singlet of the rear group. It uses the same FC5 source coordinate as L23 but has the opposite standalone sign because its curvatures differ.

### L32 (element 13) — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.834001, νd = 37.35. Glass: NBFD10 (Hoya; source-named catalog equivalent). f = +28.953429 mm.

L32 is the strong positive singlet between the rear negative elements. Its catalog-family nd/Vd differs from the rounded source coordinate only slightly; the original source numbers are not replaced.

### L33 (element 14) — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.516798, νd = 64.20. Glass: BSC7 (Hoya; source-named catalog equivalent). f = -109.114112 mm.

L33 is an image-convex negative meniscus according to its two negative radii, the abstract, ¶0059 and Figure 1. Claim 1 calls it object-convex. S26 carries the printed 13.23 mm. The front face of L34, S27, is modeled at 12.72 mm against the printed 13.23 mm, because with both faces at the printed value the L33 rear and L34 front faces would fill 97.6% of their 2.514 mm air gap; neither curvature changes.

### L34 (element 15) — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.728250, νd = 28.32. Glass: E-FD10 (Hoya; source-named catalog equivalent). f = -93.311527 mm.

L34 is the final image-convex negative meniscus. It ends the fifteen-element lens prescription. The optional filter that Figure 1 draws farther behind it has no numerical prescription and is not modeled.

The modern catalog coordinates differ by at most 4×10⁻⁶ in nd and 0.01 in νd from the printed source coordinates. All fifteen model elements carry explicit nC, nF and ng evaluated from the HOYA catalog dispersion formulas at 656.2725, 486.1327 and 435.8343 nm. These are catalog-derived values, not patent-published or measured production spectra; for FCD1 (L21) they give P_g,F = 0.5389. The reference index remains the source's d-line value, and no e-line conversion is performed.

The low-dispersion crown/high-dispersion flint combinations are consistent with a primary chromatic-balancing strategy. That interpretation does not establish apochromatic correction, secondary spectrum or measured performance. The computed d-line Petzval sum is +0.001053505836275079 mm⁻¹; it is not itself a measurement of image-surface curvature or a chromatic-performance result.

## Focus Mechanism and Conjugate Scope

The patent tabulates only the infinity state. Paragraph 0051 describes a fixed G1 and rear focusing by G2 and G3; ¶0065 says the third through fifteenth lenses (L20–L34) move toward the object at closer focus. No numerical travel, relative group motion, near-state gap table or continuous motion law is published.

The model therefore has no variable gaps and no finite conjugates. The source infinity geometry remains fixed for every focus-slider value. It does not implement a 1:1 macro configuration or the product's close-focus performance.

Samyang's product page states a minimum focus distance of 0.307 m in its text and 0.30 m in its specification table, with 1:1 maximum magnification. The data file records 0.307 m as product metadata only; the production figures do not supply an unprinted optical state. The lens is manually focused, and no drive-system motion is inferred from the patent's optional shift-lens wording.

## Stop, Image Plane and Clear Apertures

Table 1 marks row 19(STO), whose radius is 80.36 mm and whose medium is glass. Plane row 18 lies 3 mm before that vertex. Figure 1 and ¶0064 place a separate stop adjacent to L27 between G2 and G3, and row 18 carries the table's only unrounded aperture value. The model therefore places the stop on row 18. The table's label is kept on record as an unresolved source inconsistency rather than a proven misprint.

C is called diameter size in the prose. Treating its values as semi-diameters is supported by two internal observations: exact aperture near f/2.9, and claim 1's diameter-based ratio, which, using printed F=98.04, is 0.770094 when physical diameters are 2C rather than 0.385047 for literal C. Neither observation bears on the stop position.

At row 18, the unchanged C=11.92834 mm radius corresponds to paraxial f/2.802622 but exact iris-derived f/2.904148. The latter agrees with printed f/2.9; a stop on S19 at its printed C would give f/2.904327, so the F-number does not decide between the two rows. The full source-C system already slightly vignettes that iris bundle, giving an admitted axial aperture near f/2.907412: in the printed, rounded prescription the iris-filling axial ray stands 0.008, 0.014, 0.012, 0.015 and 0.0007 mm above C at S8, S9, S10, S11 and S19. The model raises those five surfaces to that ray, so the axial beam is limited by the iris at f/2.9041.

| Modeled boundary | Original C (mm) | Modeled sd (mm) | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| S8 | 14.57 | 14.58 | traced f/2.904 axial ray at 14.578 mm |
| S9 | 13.04 | 13.06 | traced f/2.904 axial ray at 13.054 mm |
| S10 | 13.03 | 13.05 | traced f/2.904 axial ray at 13.042 mm |
| S11 | 12.64 | 12.66 | traced f/2.904 axial ray at 12.655 mm |
| S19 | 10.7 | 10.71 | traced f/2.904 axial ray at 10.7007 mm |
| S27 | 13.23 | 12.72 | kept clear of S26; 97.6% of the air gap would be filled at the printed value. Figure 1 ends the S27 arc at 12.5–12.9 mm |

Every other C, S7 at 15.57 mm included, and the iris radius are as printed. With S10 at 13.05 mm the L22 rear and L23 front faces use 91.4% of their 1.676098 mm gap and keep 0.144 mm of rim air; the faces do not cross, and Figure 1 draws them meeting at the rim. This is the tightest pair in the model, and the data file sets its cross-gap allowance to 0.92 to admit it. S27 is the one surface held below the printed column: with both faces at 13.23 mm the L33 rear and L34 front faces would use 97.6% of their 2.514227 mm gap and keep 0.061 mm, while with S27 at 12.72 mm they use 89.9%. Figure 1 draws it that way round: the L33 rear face runs to the edge of its block and the concave S27 arc stops at 12.5–12.9 mm on a flat land. The smaller S27 rim costs nothing on axis and adds some clipping of the corner bundle. These are model clear apertures, not corrections to the source C values.

The table terminates with D28=1.002 and no image row. Its separate Bf=41.375 is defined from the twelfth lens (L31) in ¶0120 and from the fifteenth (L34) in the conditions. The model's last gap is the in-air paraxial back focus of rows 1–28, 40.641335 mm, not a printed image distance. The 0.734 mm difference from Bf corresponds to a plate about 2.2 mm thick at an index near 1.5, such as the filter Figure 1 draws ahead of the image plane; the table gives that plate no row, so none is modeled.

## Source Conditions and Unresolved Statements

The following table preserves all eight numbered conditions. Here C1 and C15 are physical diameters, F is the printed 98.04 mm, and the source's conflicting definitions remain explicit. Calculated group quantities are not substituted into the source summary without disclosure.

| Equation | Published bounds | Evaluation and status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.5 < (C1+C15)/F < 1.0 | 0.770094 with physical diameters 2C; literal table C gives 0.385047 and fails. |
| 2 | 170 < abs(f6/7−f8/9) < 220 | Computed 178.002682 mm; passes the formal bounds. |
| 3 | 5 < (Nd2+Vd2)/f2 < 10 | Underdetermined: aggregation of material nd/νd over a compound group is not defined. |
| 4 | −0.3 < (Nd1+Vd1)/f1 < 0 | Underdetermined for the same group-coordinate reason. |
| 5 | −3 < (Nd3+Vd3)/f3 < −2 | Underdetermined for the same group-coordinate reason. |
| 6 | 0 < (Bf+T1)/F < 1.5 | 1.346376 using printed Bf=41.375 and the stop at z=90.623672 mm; 1.338892 with the in-air back focus. Both pass. |
| 7 | 0 < (G4/5R1+G4/5R2)/(F+F2) < 1.0 | 0.454329 using the outer radii of the L21/L22 doublet, 37.81 and 30.00 mm, and computed G2 EFL=51.213202 mm; passes on this expressly calculated interpretation. |
| 8 | 2.0 < T1/T2 < 3.0 | Literal source definition gives 0.362756543 and fails; its reciprocal is only a diagnostic. |

Equation 6 appears in claim 2 with lower bound 0 and again in ¶0098; ¶0032 instead prints a lower bound of 1. Neither version fixes the position of the image plane. Equation 7's calculated passing value likewise does not repair the printed f2 summary. These comparisons do not settle the stop position or the image plane.

The computed difference between the L23/L24 and L25/L26 net focal lengths (the patent's f6/7 and f8/9) is 178.002682 mm. It meets the formal 170–220 range, but not the preferred 188–205 interval.

Equation 8 (claim 3) defines T1 as stop-to-last and T2 as first-to-stop; its literal ratio with the stop on row 18 is 0.362756543, outside 2–3. The reciprocal is 2.756669779, but the definition is not silently reversed. The printed T2=91.07 also differs from the table's first-to-row18 distance, 90.623672 mm.

The computed G1/G2/G3 values remain separate from the mismatching printed f1/f2 summaries. Group-level Nd/Vd in conditions 3–5 lack an explicit aggregation definition. A sum-over-members interpretation is possible, but it is not an established source convention. Wavelength prose prints “mm” beside visible-spectrum numbers and differs between ¶0129 and ¶0134; figures use NM. These inconsistencies are left as printed.

The data file lists the source inconsistencies as unresolved. None is treated as a corrected patent value.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The model reproduces the printed focal length (98.043 mm against 98.04) and, through the stop radius, the printed F-number (f/2.904 against 2.9). It represents the infinity state at the d line only. No near-focus state, 1:1 configuration or filter plate is modeled, and the C, F and g indices are catalog-derived rather than published.

For this all-spherical model the maximum rim angle is 37.4° (S3), the minimum glass thickness is 2.0 mm, the largest cross-gap fill is 91.4% (L22/L23) and the smallest air space is 0.1 mm, on axis between L26 and L27. On axis the beam is limited by the iris. Off axis the element rims vignette the bundle progressively and from both ends of the lens. At the 21.63 mm corner (12.38°) the chief ray clears every surface; the front group stops the rays on one side of the bundle, first at S4, and the rear group those on the other, first at S26. The rays that pass cross the iris between 3.15 mm on one side of its centre and 6.51 mm on the other, 40.5% of its 23.86 mm diameter in the meridional section, and the model's geometric transmission falls from 100% on axis to 72% at 8.3° and 42% at 12.4°.

That vignetting belongs to the printed column; the model adds only the S27 hold to it. With the unaltered C values, one ray traced at 12.38° passes S1–S4 at 23.882, 22.253, 22.247 and 21.499 mm against the printed 23.9, 22.3, 22.3 and 21.5, and another passes S22–S28 at 10.399, 12.986, 13.213, 13.121, 13.226, 13.228 and 13.845 mm against 10.4, 13, 13.23, 13.13, 13.23, 13.23 and 13.85. Those eleven apertures are therefore the heights of the two rays that bound the designer's corner bundle, which fills 43.9% of the iris diameter. Holding S27 at 12.72 mm lowers the model's transmission at 12.4° from 45.8% to 42.2% and leaves it unchanged at 10.3° and below.

The rendered section follows the element sequence and proportions of Figure 1 and of Samyang's published section. It differs where Figure 1 draws square mechanical blocks: each rendered element tapers between its two clear apertures, and the front face of L34 stops 0.5 mm short of the L33 rim because S27 is held at 12.72 mm.
