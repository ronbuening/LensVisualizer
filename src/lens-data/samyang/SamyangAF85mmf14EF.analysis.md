# Samyang AF 85mm f/1.4 EF

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** KR 10-2213072 B1
**Filed:** 2019-05-09
**Granted:** 2021-02-01
**Published:** 2021-02-08
**Inventors:** Jae Myung Ryu, Hae Jin Lee, Jeong Du Lee
**Assignee:** Samyang Optics Co., Ltd.
**Example details:** Tables 4–6, Figure 3

The prescription follows Example 2 at its original scale, including both printed focus states. Its earlier publication is KR20200129711A. The association with the Samyang AF 85mm f/1.4 EF rests on convergent evidence:

1. Samyang's EF specifications and its 2022 catalogue, page 19, describe nine glass elements in seven groups, rear cemented doublets and a hybrid asphere on L8. The construction diagram supports the element shapes and rear-group arrangement; it does not mark the iris.
2. The marketed 85 mm f/1.4, Canon EF, full-frame lens has a stated 0.9 m minimum focus distance and an f/1.4–16 aperture range. The patent model is close in focal length and aperture, but its printed near-focus distances differ from that marketing value, as explained below.
3. Example 2 fits the hybrid construction better than the ED-containing Example 3. This is evidence of a design-family association, not proof that the numerical prescription is the exact factory design. The EF product's reported 2018 release also precedes this patent's filing.

This is distinct from the AF 85mm f/1.4 FE already in the catalog. That model has eleven elements in eight groups and uses Example 3 of WO 2021/085655 A1, with an all-spherical construction. The EF source belongs to the KR family; the FE source belongs to a different WO family. The shared focal-length and aperture labels do not establish prescription identity.

## Optical Architecture

The design has nine glass elements in seven air-spaced groups, excluding the thin hybrid layer and camera-side filter. Four separate front elements precede the negative focusing element L5 and the stop. The rear section contains the L6–L7 and L8–L9 cemented doublets, with the hybrid layer on L8. These component and motion descriptions follow the transcribed surfaces and variable gaps; detailed aberration roles below are optical interpretations of that layout.

All twenty source optical, stop and filter rows are retained without rescaling or index fitting. The calculated EFL is 84.548650747 mm at f/1.479, rather than the marketed f/1.4. Table 6 gives 84.499585 mm, whereas Table 10 gives 84.548355 mm and the prose gives 84.54 mm. These source differences remain unresolved.

The camera-side filter is a physical 2.5 mm plate. The image plane follows D4 after the plate. The printed D5 row follows Image and is retained only as source metadata; it is not propagated as an incoming image spacing. Its purpose remains unresolved.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The following glass values are published nd/νd pairs. The Hoya catalog equivalents supply dispersion curves without identifying the production glass supplier. Individual-element focal lengths are not specified. Element shapes and qualitative power roles are inferred from the curvatures, rather than presented as separate patent claims.

### L1 — Positive meniscus, convex to object

nd = 1.7725, νd = 49.62. Glass: TAF1 (Hoya) catalog equivalent.

L1 opens the front collecting section. Its positive meniscus form contributes convergence before the remaining front elements.

### L2 — Positive meniscus, convex to object

nd = 1.7433, νd = 49.22. Glass: NBF1 (Hoya) catalog equivalent.

L2 supplies another positive contribution in the front section. It is separated from L1 and L3 by air gaps.

### L3 — Negative meniscus

nd = 1.72825, νd = 28.32. Glass: E-FD10 (Hoya) catalog equivalent.

L3 introduces negative power between the front positive elements. Its lower Abbe number provides dispersion contrast with the surrounding glasses, although the available data do not establish a specific partial-dispersion correction.

### L4 — Positive meniscus, convex to object

nd = 1.7725, νd = 49.62. Glass: TAF1 (Hoya) catalog equivalent.

L4 closes the front section before the variable gap D1. Its square 20.2 mm rim follows Figure 3 subject to the near-state axial ray clearance; it is not a published mechanical dimension.

### L5 — Negative meniscus focusing element

nd = 1.8061, νd = 33.27. Glass: NBFD15 (Hoya) catalog equivalent.

L5 lies between D1 and D2. Their opposing changes describe its movement toward the image side for close focus, with the stop behind it.

### L6 + L7 — Rear cemented doublet

L6: nd = 1.54814, νd = 45.82. Glass: E-FEL1 (Hoya) catalog equivalent.

L7: nd = 1.7859, νd = 43.93. Glass: NBFD11 (Hoya) catalog equivalent.

The biconcave L6 and biconvex L7 share the interface at surface 13. Their opposite power contributions and refractive-index contrast provide rear-group correction freedom; their similar Abbe numbers alone do not prove an achromatic pairing.

### Hybrid layer + L8 + L9 — Aspherical rear cemented group

Hybrid layer: nd = 1.517, νd = 52. Material: Unmatched resin; approximate dispersion.

L8: nd = 1.7725, νd = 49.62. Glass: TAF1 (Hoya) catalog equivalent.

L9: nd = 1.64769, νd = 33.84. Glass: E-FD2 (Hoya) catalog equivalent.

The 0.130 mm hybrid layer precedes the positive L8 substrate. Its exposed surface 15 is aspherical. L8 and the negative meniscus L9 share surface 17, forming the last glass doublet. The layer is modeled separately to preserve the source thickness and index; it does not increase the maker's nine-glass-element count. The prescription does not establish the layer's chemistry or supplier.

### Camera-side filter — Plane-parallel plate

nd = 1.5168, νd = 64.2. Glass: BSC7 (Hoya) catalog equivalent.

Surfaces 19–20 represent the published filter. Its thickness and the physical gaps on either side remain explicit; the plate is excluded from the production lens's element/group count.

## Focus Mechanism

The variable gaps describe single-element internal focusing by L5, which moves 4.928652 mm toward the image. The front section, stop and rear glass groups keep their internal spacings; the source also changes the short image gap after the filter. Both published endpoints are represented; the patent supplies no intermediate focus cam.

| Source gap | Infinity | Printed near state |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| D1, after L4 | 1.6125 mm | 6.541152 mm |
| D2, after L5 | 10.4271 mm | 5.498448 mm |
| D3, before filter | 37.3 mm | 37.3 mm |
| D4, after filter | 0.507836 mm | 0.687847 mm |

The near object distance D0 is 813.676935 mm before the first vertex. Adding the physical image track gives 926.326382 mm from object to image, despite the source's 0.9 m heading. The modeled close-focus endpoint records 0.926326 m; the maker's 0.9 m minimum is retained separately as a marketing specification. The calculated paraxial conjugate is approximately 0.92647534 m and is distinct from the printed source distance.

The source's near f/1.584 and 11.939° half-field remain source claims. The modeled physical iris retains its semi-diameter of 15.090708516 mm, derived from infinity f/1.479; the fixed-iris near working f-number is 1.483. No iris adjustment was used to force the printed near f-number.

The filter remains drawn because its trailing gap D4 changes with focus. A separate rear-plate representation allows only a fixed trailing gap; moving D4's 0.180011 mm change into D3 would relocate the near-state filter. Both physical gaps are preserved. The source's D5 values, −0.005027/−0.133249 mm after Image, remain non-propagated metadata.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The source's in-air back-focus and overall-length summaries remain inconsistent with the prescription. The source gives an air back focus of 39.403047 mm versus 39.457638 mm calculated, and an overall length of 112.418323 mm versus 112.469436 mm calculated. The calculated after-filter infinity back focus is 0.509431 mm versus the stored 0.507836 mm. Residual paraxial image-plane offsets are +0.001595473 mm at infinity and +0.001772322 mm near focus. Neither source plane was refocused.

The inferred rims follow Figure 3 at the source scale, subject to axial clearance at both printed focus states. The near-state beam requires slightly larger front-section optical caps than the drawing alone suggests. L4 has a square 20.2 mm rim; the rear doublets and L8's hybrid layer share 18 mm rims. Flat lands on the rear faces of L2, L3 and L5 extend their optical caps to drawn body radii of 26.3, 24.1 and 18 mm, respectively. The surface clear semi-diameters, in source-row order with the stop marked separately, are:

30, 30, 26.3, 25.6, 24.1, 21, 20.2, 20.2, 18, 16, STOP, 18, 18, 18, 18, 18, 18, 18, 24, 24 mm.

These are inferred clear apertures, not patent-published or factory-measured rims. The model reaches the full-frame corner, but some edge rays are blocked. Geometric clipping is substantial at the source's field edge: 57.6355% at infinity (14.239°) and 45.8128% at the printed near state (11.939°). Analytic sphere intersections confirm that blocked incoming lines miss the 30 mm front cap. These calculated fractions exclude coating losses and do not establish production illumination.

Axial rays pass at both states. The calculated axial RMS radius is 0.0029263 mm at infinity and 0.0899632 mm near focus at the unchanged source planes. The near RMS improves to 0.017048 mm at a plane 0.358718 mm forward; the much smaller paraxial offset indicates source spherical aberration rather than a mistaken last gap. That refocus is not applied. These calculations do not demonstrate agreement with factory MTF. A near-focus field of 14.239° extends beyond the source's 11.939° near edge.
