## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** CN 217606166 U  
**Application Number:** 202221381175.8  
**Filed:** 2022-06-06  
**Granted:** 2022-10-18 (utility model)  
**Inventor:** Yunhong Li (李云洪)  
**Assignee:** Shenzhen Huitian Film and Television Equipment Co., Ltd. (深圳市慧天影视器材有限公司)  
**Title:** 一种85mmF1.8全画幅镜头 (An 85mm F1.8 full-frame lens)  
**Example details:** One numerical example, tabulated in ¶0034 (PDF page 7) with f = 85 mm and F = 1.8 stated in ¶0035; section in Fig. 1 (PDF page 9). Printed at the production focal length; no scaling.

This is a patent design associated with the original Meike AF 85mm f/1.8 FF STM, not a confirmed factory prescription. The patentee is Meike's own company: Meike's corporate history states that its Shenzhen firm took the name 深圳市慧天影视器材有限公司 in July 2019 and trades under the 美科 (Meike) brand. The patent's nine spherical elements in six groups match the lens's published construction, its 85 mm focal length and its f/1.8 aperture, and the filing date of 6 June 2022 falls shortly before the Sony E version went on sale later that month. Meike's own construction illustration is a stylized transparent rendering labeled "6 groups, 9 elements"; it agrees with Fig. 1 in outline (large front elements, a narrow waist at the stop, a larger rear group) but is not a section and cannot confirm the radii or which surfaces are cemented. The later 85mm f/1.8 Pro (11 elements in 8 groups, with ED and aspherical elements) and the 2025 SE II (11 elements in 7 groups) are different designs. Meike's 2018 Canon EF 85mm f/1.8 autofocus lens is also listed with nine elements in six groups; whether it shares this formula is not known. The patent describes a linear-motor drive, while the retail lens uses a stepping motor.

Meike lists the lens, now sold as the AF 85mm F1.8 SE, at 85 mm, f/1.8 to f/22, nine diaphragm blades, a 67 mm filter and 0.85 m minimum focus; Lenstip gives a maximum magnification of 0.13×. It is made for Sony E, Nikon Z, Fujifilm X and L-mount.

## Optical Architecture

Nine spherical elements form six air-spaced groups: two positive singlets (L1, L2), the cemented doublet U1 (L3 + L4), the aperture stop, the cemented focusing doublet U2 (L5 + L6), the cemented doublet U3 (L7 + L8) and a positive rear singlet (L9). The patent describes the front pair with U1 and U3 with L9 as front and rear groups whose powers are arranged symmetrically about the stop and U2 (claim 3, ¶0030). The front pair (combined f = 78.4 mm) and L9 (f = 63.6 mm) carry most of the positive power; U1 (f = −434.4 mm) and U3 (f = +638.0 mm) are weak doublets of opposite sign; U2 (f = −59.3 mm) is the only strongly negative group. Every surface is spherical, and ¶0020 attributes the low cost and compact build to the nine spherical glass elements.

The table prints each cemented interface twice with 0.00 mm of air between (rows 6, 11 and 15). The model merges each into one cemented surface, as the text describes, which leaves 15 refracting surfaces and the stop. Surface labels keep the patent's row numbers, so rows 6, 11 and 15 have no surface of their own and row 9 is the stop. The lens is 71.39 mm from S1 to S19, and the image plane lies 113.92 mm behind S1.

The example meets the patent's power and index conditions; the travel condition cannot be checked from the printed data:

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.60. Glass: H-LaF50B (CDGM). f = +189.4 mm.

A weak front collector with radii 217.50 and −440.08 mm, more strongly curved at the front. It begins converging the 47.6 mm entrance beam and has the largest printed clear diameter in the lens, 48.20 mm.

### L2 — Positive Meniscus, convex to the object

nd = 1.83481, νd = 42.73. Glass: H-ZLaF55D (CDGM). f = +132.6 mm.

With L1 it forms the positive front pair whose combined power is the φ12 of claim 3. Splitting the front power over two high-index elements keeps each surface's bending moderate at f/1.8.

### L3 + L4 — Cemented Doublet U1

- L3, Positive Meniscus, convex to the object: nd = 1.65160, νd = 58.40. Glass: H-LaK50A (CDGM). f = +53.9 mm.
- L4, Negative Meniscus, convex to the object: nd = 1.72825, νd = 28.32. Glass: E-FD10 (HOYA). f = −42.1 mm.

Cemented at R 509.45 mm, the crown–flint pair is weakly negative (f = −434.4 mm). It is the achromatizing doublet ahead of the stop that ¶0020 credits, with U3, for correcting axial and lateral color. L4's deep rear concave (R 28.87 mm) faces the stop across a 9.25 mm air space.

### L5 + L6 — Cemented Focusing Doublet U2

- L5, Positive Meniscus, concave to the object: nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.78. Glass: H-ZF52 (CDGM). f = +158.3 mm.
- L6, Biconcave Negative: nd = 1.51680, νd = 64.20. Glass: H-K9L (CDGM). f = −43.5 mm.

Cemented at R −59.18 mm and placed 2.27 mm behind the stop, the pair is the strongest negative group in the lens (f = −59.3 mm). Its negative power sits in the low-dispersion crown and its weaker positive power in the dense flint, the usual arrangement for a color-corrected negative doublet, which limits color change as the group moves. Claim 2 and ¶0029 make U2 the autofocus group; ¶0020 notes that keeping it small lightens the load on the motor.

### L7 + L8 — Cemented Doublet U3

- L7, Biconcave Negative: nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.78. Glass: H-ZF52 (CDGM). f = −57.1 mm.
- L8, Biconvex Positive: nd = 1.83481, νd = 42.73. Glass: H-ZLaF55D (CDGM). f = +54.5 mm.

U3 follows an 18.66 mm air space and is nearly afocal (f = +638.0 mm). The two glasses differ in index by only 0.012 but in Abbe number by 19, so the R 268.12 mm junction has little monochromatic power and acts mainly on color: this is the achromatizing doublet near the image that ¶0020 describes.

### L9 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.83481, νd = 42.73. Glass: H-ZLaF55D (CDGM). f = +63.6 mm.

The rear positive element balances the front pair about the stop, with φ9/φ12 = 1.233 inside claim 3's range. The printed 42.53 mm air space to the image follows it.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent names each glass but prints no index or Abbe number. The nd and νd stored for each element are the CDGM and HOYA catalog values for the named types, and each element traces on that glass's catalog dispersion curve, so the lens's chromatic behavior here rests on the catalogs, not on measured melts.

| Glass | nd | νd | Elements | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H-LaF50B (CDGM) | 1.77250 | 49.60 | L1 | Lanthanum flint, front collector |
| H-ZLaF55D (CDGM) | 1.83481 | 42.73 | L2, L8, L9 | Dense lanthanum flint, high-index positive elements |
| H-LaK50A (CDGM) | 1.65160 | 58.40 | L3 | Lanthanum crown, positive half of U1 |
| E-FD10 (HOYA) | 1.72825 | 28.32 | L4 | Dense flint, negative half of U1 |
| H-ZF52 (CDGM) | 1.84666 | 23.78 | L5, L7 | Dense flint, flint halves of U2 and U3 |
| H-K9L (CDGM) | 1.51680 | 64.20 | L6 | Borosilicate crown, negative half of U2 |

All nine glasses, H-ZLaF55D and H-LaK50A included, are in the site's glass catalog. None of the nine glasses is an ED or anomalous-dispersion type, and Meike advertises no special elements; the three doublets are ordinary crown–flint achromats.

## Focus Mechanism

Claim 2 and ¶0029 make U2 (L5 + L6) the focusing group, moved by a linear motor from infinity to 1 m with a travel L that satisfies 0.12 < L/|fU2| < 0.14. With fU2 = −59.31 mm that allows 7.1 to 8.3 mm of travel. The patent prints neither the direction nor any close-range spacing. A paraxial check finds that moving U2 about 8.1 mm toward the image, holding the printed image plane, focuses an object 1 m from that plane (L/|fU2| = 0.137); holding the paraxial image plane instead needs 8.4 mm (0.141). The model does not apply this travel: it is static at infinity. The retail lens focuses to 0.85 m with a stepping motor.

## Aperture Stop and Clear Apertures

The table's last column is headed 孔径 (aperture) without saying whether it is a radius or a diameter. Read as a diameter, the stop's 29.75 mm gives a paraxial f/1.785, consistent with the stated F = 1.8; read as a radius it would give f/0.89. Every printed value is therefore taken as a clear diameter and halved. Where the two printed cells at a cemented join differ, the smaller is used: 36.97 mm rather than 37.30 mm at the L3–L4 join, and 28.42 mm rather than 29.00 mm at the L7–L8 join. Under the site's rule for primes, the stop radius follows the design F/1.8 (diameter 29.49 mm) rather than the printed 29.75 mm; the two agree to the patent's rounding of the f-number.

The printed clear apertures of S3 to S8 sit slightly inside the real f/1.8 axial bundle, by up to 0.35 mm in radius at S3, so the model works at about f/1.83 wide open, 1.6 % slower than stated. These rims are kept as printed rather than enlarged. The one printed value not used is S19's 43.00 mm: it reads as a transposed 34.00 mm, which halves to L9's printed front rim of 17.0 mm, and both the patent figure and Meike's own cutaway render draw L9 as a square-edged element at about that height, not flaring to 21.5 mm at its rear face. Off axis the rims vignette strongly; the patent puts the relative illumination at the image corner above 42 % (¶0039).

## Model Scope and Limitations

| Quantity | Patent | Model |
|---|---|---|
| Focal length | 85 mm (stated, ¶0035) | 85.645 mm (d line) |
| F-number | 1.8 (stated); printed stop gives f/1.785 | f/1.8 stop; about f/1.83 with the printed rims |
| S1 to image | 113.92 mm (sum of printed spacings) | 113.92 mm |
| Last surface to image | 42.53 mm (printed) | 42.53 mm stored; paraxial back focus 42.262 mm |

- The prescription is the patent's, not a confirmed factory one.
- The computed focal length is 0.76 % longer than the stated 85 mm. Across the C to g lines it runs from 85.55 to 85.74 mm, so no wavelength reaches 85.0 mm and the stated value is a nominal one.
- The stored image plane is the printed one, 0.268 mm beyond paraxial focus. The model's best axial focus lies about 0.3 mm in front of it, so sharpness at the stored plane falls short of the patent's MTF plots (Figs. 2 and 4).
- The model is static at infinity and says nothing about performance at 0.85 m.
- All semi-diameters are the printed clear apertures, halved, except S19 (read as a transposed 34.00 mm); the stop radius follows F/1.8.
- Dispersion rests on catalog data for the named glasses, not on production melts.
