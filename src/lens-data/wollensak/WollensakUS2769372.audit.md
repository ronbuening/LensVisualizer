# WOLLENSAK 100mm f/3.2 (US 2,769,372 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,769,372 A, _Three Component Objectives for Photographic and Projection Purposes_](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2769372A/en), by Alan A. Cook and David C. Gilkeson, assigned to Wollensak Optical Company. The filing and priority date is 25 July 1955; publication was 6 November 1956. Example I appears in Figure 2 on PDF page 1, in the specification on page 2 and in claim 1 on page 3. Figure 1 shows the common construction. Example II is a separate f/3.5 design with a 50° total field and is not used here.

The [Ohara optical-glass catalog](https://www.ohara-inc.co.jp/en/product/catalog/) supplies the TIM11 and S-LAM51 dispersion curves; the [Hoya optical-glass catalog](https://www.hoya-opticalworld.com/english/datadownload/index.html) supplies M-PCD55AR and F15. No production-lens source is documented for Example I.

## Transcription Check

All seven radii, four glass thicknesses, two air spaces, four D-line indices and four Abbe numbers agree with the page images. The Figure 2, Example I and claim 1 tables agree with one another. No prescription mismatch or source correction was found. Dimensions retain the printed 100 mm focal-length scale without rescaling. The patent explicitly defines the indices at the D line, taken as sodium at 589.3 nm.

R2 is a plane refracting surface. Elements 3 and 4 are cemented at R6. The drawing agrees with the four-element, three-component arrangement: plano-convex front singlet, biconcave middle singlet and a rear doublet comprising a negative meniscus and a biconvex positive element. The printed stop station and element diameters also agree.

## First-Order Check

The patent specifies EFL 100 mm, maximum aperture f/3.2 and back focus 82.9 mm. The model gives EFL 99.99 mm, f/3.20 and paraxial back focus 82.91 mm, agreeing at the printed precision. The first-to-last vertex distance is 35.8 mm. The image plane is at the paraxial infinity focus, 82.91 mm behind R7 and 118.71 mm behind R1; it is not a finite-aperture best-focus plane.

The derived stop radius is 12.611 mm. The iris sets the axial aperture, and the surfaces stay clear of one another. The axial marginal ray reaches R4 at approximately 12.455 mm, inside its 12.5 mm optical radius. An entrance height defined instead as f/(2 × 3.2), or 15.624 mm, gives a 12.691 mm height at R4 and is clipped. That different finite-ray aperture convention explains the earlier f/3.3 restriction; it does not describe the beam admitted by the present iris.

At the 27.5° half-field, half the printed 55° total field, the chief ray clears every optical rim and reaches an image height of 51.524 mm. Its largest front-element height is 13.108 mm against the 16 mm radius. The inferred image-circle diameter is 103.05 mm, with approximately −1.0% edge distortion. About 39% of the meridional fan passes at the edge; geometrical pupil transmission is approximately 30.1%, and relative illumination is 22.0% of the axis. These are distinct modeled quantities, exclude coating losses and do not establish measured illumination or MTF.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The iris diameter is not printed; its opening follows paraxial f/3.2. The stop station is printed as 4.4 mm before element 3, dividing the 8.7 mm R4–R5 space into 4.3 mm before the stop and 4.4 mm after it. The stop lies 22.0 mm behind R1; R4's optical rim lies at about 19.94 mm and remains clear of its plane.

Element diameters are printed as 32, 27, 28 and 28 mm, giving clear radii of 16 mm at R1–R2, 13.5 mm at R3 and 14 mm at R5–R7. The patent also specifies a 45° bevel at a 25 mm diameter on R4. Treating its 12.5 mm bevel-start radius as the clear optical boundary is an assumption; the bevel depth and full edge profile are not specified.

The exact paraxial image distance is calculated. The 103.05 mm image circle is derived from twice the real chief-ray height at the printed 55° total field, rounded upward to two decimals; it does not identify a camera format or imply an unvignetted edge bundle.

`focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. No close-focus prescription, movement law or minimum object distance is printed. The modeled aperture series extends from f/3.2 to f/32; the patent gives no minimum iris opening or blade details.

The patent supplies no glass trade names, suppliers, additional spectral indices or partial-dispersion data. Elements 1–3 use catalog dispersion curves as spectral proxies while retaining the printed D-line indices and Abbe numbers. Element 4 retains its Ohara catalog match at the printed precision. None of these rows establishes the historical supplier or melt. No anomalous dispersion, broadband performance or secondary-spectrum accuracy is established by these data.
