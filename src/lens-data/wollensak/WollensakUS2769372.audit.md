# WOLLENSAK 100mm f/3.2 (US 2,769,372 Design) — Optical Audit

## Source

US 2,769,372 A, Alan A. Cook and David C. Gilkeson, assigned to Wollensak Optical Company; filed 25 July 1955 and published 6 November 1956. Example I is tabulated in Figure 2 on PDF page 1, in the specification on page 2 and in claim 1 on page 3. Figure 1 shows the construction. Example II is a separate f/3.5 design.

## Transcription

Two independent readings of the page images agree on all seven radii, six thicknesses and separations, four D-line indices, four Abbe numbers, the rear cemented interface and the stop position. The three printings of Example I agree. No prescription mismatch or source correction was found. R2 is a plane refracting surface. The figure agrees with the four-element, three-component arrangement and the element shapes.

## First-Order

The patent specifies EFL 100 mm and back focus 82.9 mm. The model gives EFL 99.992697857 mm and paraxial back focus 82.910303500 mm, agreeing at the printed precision. The first-to-last vertex distance is 35.8 mm and the first-vertex-to-image distance is 118.7103035 mm. The image plane remains at the paraxial focus.

The corrected opening gives f/3.20. The axial marginal ray reaches R4 at approximately 12.455 mm, inside its 12.5 mm optical radius. A beam defined instead by an entrance height of f/(2 × 3.2) reaches R4 at 12.691 mm; this different finite-ray aperture definition explains the earlier aperture restriction.

## Not Printed

The iris opening is derived from f/3.2; its station is printed as 4.4 mm before element 3. The exact paraxial image distance is computed rather than printed. The optical radii follow the printed element diameters; treating the 25 mm bevel-start diameter as R4's clear boundary is an assumption. The bevel depth, historical glass suppliers, additional spectral indices, close-focus behavior, mount and camera format are not given.

## Product

No specific commercial lens is documented for this prescription. Its Tessar-type layout alone does not establish a Raptar or Optar identity.

## Corrections

The radii, element thicknesses, air separations, printed glass coordinates, rim radii, stop station and paraxial image plane are retained. The operating aperture and field now follow Example I. Public descriptions distinguish printed values from derived quantities and omit unrelated calculation records.

## Remaining Questions

Open: the R4 bevel depth and complete edge profile are unknown. Its clear radius remains the printed bevel-start radius; any later comparison with the drawing must retain the printed diameters and the light they admit. The historical glass identities and any production-lens association are unresolved.
