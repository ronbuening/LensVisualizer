# FUJIFILM FUJINON SW S 50mm f/5.6

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [JP S50-96221 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/JPS5096221A/)
**Filed:** 1973-12-24
**Published:** 1975-07-31
**Inventors:** Ryoichi Doi; Yutaka Sakai
**Assignee:** Fuji Photo Optical Co., Ltd.
**Example details:** Sole numerical example, Table 1; section in Fig. 1(a).

The patent's f = 1 table is scaled by 50 to millimetres. It specifies f/5.6, a 90° full field and eight elements in six groups. These values strongly connect the example to the Fujinon SW S 50mm f/5.6 for the Fujica G690/GL690 interchangeable-lens 6×9 rangefinder system. The December 1973 filing also coincides with the GL690's introduction. The [Fujica GL690/GM670 instruction manual](https://www.cameramanuals.org/fuji_pdf/fujica_gl690_gm670.pdf) lists the 50mm f/5.6 interchangeable lens and its external finder.

[Lallement's G690 lens reference](https://www.lallement.com/pictures/lenses.htm) gives the f/5.6–32 aperture range, 90° field, 1 m minimum focus distance, eight elements in six groups and 72 mm filter diameter. [Kamerastore's dealer listing](https://kamerastore.com/products/fujica-fujinon-50mm-f5-6-sw-s-fujica-g690) also reports eight elements in six groups, the G690 mount, 1 m close focus and the lens's own leaf shutter, with speeds of 1–1/500 s plus B. The five-blade diaphragm count comes from this dealer listing, the only source found for that count. No manufacturer optical diagram or production prescription has been found to establish an exact product identity.

## Optical architecture and modeled field

The all-spherical design has two negative front singlets, two positive cemented doublets around the stop, and two negative rear singlets. Its six air-separated groups have a negative–negative–positive–positive–negative–negative power sequence. Fig. 1(a) divides the lens into A1 (L1–L4) and A2 (L5–L8), subdivided into B1 (L1), B2 (L2–L4), B3 (L5–L7) and B4 (L8). The front doublet C1, L3/L4, has a standalone focal length of +44.24 mm; the rear doublet C2, L5/L6, is stronger at +23.68 mm.

The computed EFL is 49.998 mm and the paraxial back focal distance is 34.774 mm, close to the printed 50 mm and 34.775 mm. The image plane retains that printed back focus, giving a 109.52 mm total track. The stop lies 37.016 mm behind the first vertex, derived from the patent's 72.5 mm stop-to-film distance; its split of the central gap is inferred rather than tabulated. The model opens at f/5.6.

At the patent's 45° half-field the chief ray reaches 48.79 mm image height, approximately −2.4% distortion and 96.6% of the 50.5 mm 6×9 format half-diagonal. This agrees with the general shape of Fig. 2(c); the modeled field retains the patent's stated coverage.

## Glass identification

The source gives d-line refractive indices and Abbe numbers, with no commercial glass names or measured partial dispersions. The listed catalog equivalents supply estimated spectral curves. Catalog minus patent differences are zero in nd except LAL7 (−0.000003); νd differences range from −0.15 for LLF1 to +0.11 for SF10. Their vendor names do not identify Fujifilm's suppliers or production melts. No element is identified as anomalous-dispersion glass by the patent.

The positive members of C1/C2 use higher-index crowns paired with lower-index, more dispersive negative members. The rear SF10-class meniscus adds a distinctly more dispersive glass to the otherwise crown-rich palette. Unpublished spectral differences remain uncertain.

## Focus mechanism and conjugates

Only the infinity state is modeled. The patent supplies no near-focus spacings or moving-group table. The production lens focuses to 1 m; no finite-focus motion is inferred from that specification.

## Apertures and model scope

Clear apertures are not tabulated. The semi-diameters are read from Fig. 1(a), whose vertex spacings match the table to about 3%. The clear radii at S1 (20 mm), S3 (12.7 mm), S5 (12.5 mm) and S7/S8 (7.2 mm) follow the drawing, and the flat outer steps the figure draws on L1, L2, L7 and L8 are modeled as opaque lands: behind L1 to 20 mm, behind L2 to 12.7 mm, in front of L7 to 13 mm and in front of L8 to 17 mm. The central surfaces admit the f/5.6 axial beam and the 45° chief ray. The patent text gives the front element's effective diameter as about 44 mm, while the drawing shows about 40 mm; the model follows the drawing, and a 22 mm front radius would pass no additional light.

L2's rear face ends at 10.5 mm because it would cross L3's front face at about 11.2 mm; the opaque step behind it carries L2 up to its drawn square top, level with L3. The figure sets the small L4 and L5, about 7.2 mm in radius, into larger L3 and L6 faces with square tops at about 12.5 and 12 mm. With one radius per cemented junction, the model carries the junctions to those tops, 12.5 mm on L3 and 11.6 mm on L6, the steepest rim L6's strongly curved front face allows. L3 and L6 keep their square tops, while L4 and L5 taper toward the stop instead of showing the figure's square steps.

The 0.47 mm gap between S10/S11 prevents extending their spherical caps to the drawn outer radii of L6/L7: the two spheres would cross beyond about 10.4 mm. Both keep 10 mm clear radii, with opaque lands to 12 mm on L6 and to L7's drawn 13 mm top. The lands admit no additional rays. Geometric pupil transmission is 100% to 30°, 88.5% at 36° and about 45% at 45°; coating losses, absorption and measured production illumination are not established.
