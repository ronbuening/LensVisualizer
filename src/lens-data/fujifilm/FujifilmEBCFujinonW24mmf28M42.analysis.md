## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 3,958,865 A\
**Title:** Retrofocus Type Lens System\
**Filed:** January 27, 1975 (Japanese priority February 8, 1974)\
**Granted:** May 25, 1976\
**Inventor:** Yutaka Sakai\
**Assignee:** Fuji Photo Optical Co., Ltd.\
**Example details:** Sole numerical design, Table I on PDF page 4; Figure 1 on PDF page 2; all dimensions scaled by exactly 24.

The model represents the probable optical design of the **EBC FUJINON W 24mm f/2.8** for Fujica's M42 screw-mount SLRs. Fuji's own Fujica AZ-1 instruction manual lists that lens as 8 components / 9 elements with an 84° angle of view, f/16 minimum aperture, 0.3 m minimum focus and a 49 mm filter. The patent matches it in construction (nine elements in eight groups), field (84°), aperture (F/2.8) and maker, and it describes the same practical 24 mm application. These agreements make the correspondence probable; they do not establish the patent table as the factory prescription. The later **EBC X-FUJINON W 24mm f/2.8 DM** for the Fujica X bayonet follows US 4,158,482, a separate design.

The patent does not call this design “Example 1.” Its sole Table I is normalized to f = 1.0, with a printed back focal length of 1.560. Multiplication of every radius and axial interval by 24 gives a 38.40 mm first-to-last-vertex length and a **37.44 mm source back-focus gap**, close to the patent's “about 39 mm” overall length. The image plane remains 75.84 mm behind the first vertex. No curvature, index, spacing or scale was optimized to force agreement with a production lens.

## Optical Architecture

The all-spherical retrofocus layout has **nine elements in eight air-separated groups**. The patent divides it into a front negative component, L1 to L3, and a rear positive component, L4 to L9. A positive front meniscus L1 precedes two negative menisci L2 and L3. L4 and L5 restore positive convergence before the stop. The rear sequence comprises the cemented L6/L7 negative component, positive meniscus L8 and final positive lens L9. The long back focus, 1.56 times the focal length, leaves room for an SLR mirror.

The patent's design conditions explain the layout. The front component's focal length sits between 0.7 and 1.0 times the system focal length to keep the lens compact. L2 and L3 carry carefully balanced negative power and bending to control astigmatism and coma for steeply inclined rays. L4's concave front face moves the entrance pupil forward, which keeps the front element small. L4 and L5 together keep the sum of their Abbe numbers below 70 to limit lateral color at wide angles.

L6 and L7 share the physical surface **12**, with an index transition directly from 1.76182 to 1.77252. There is no air interval at this interface. The independent rear interval after surface 13 remains 1.44 mm at the representative scale.

Figure 1 places the stop in d10, between surfaces 10 and 11, nearer L5 than L6. The model uses an inferred **0.80/1.84 mm split** of the 2.64 mm gap, about 30% of the way from L5 to L6 (Figure 1 reads about 27%). The source does not dimension the split or the iris. The shared aperture rule derives the iris from the source F/2.8; the resulting 11.65 mm iris diameter agrees with the stop bars drawn in Figure 1 (about 11.5 mm at this scale).

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Focal lengths are computed isolated thick-element values in air at the representative scale; the L6/L7 values do not describe the power of their cemented combination. Glass names are catalog equivalents of the patent's coordinates (see Glass Identification), not the production melts.

### L1 — Front Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.60717, νd = 40.2. Glass: BAFD3 (HOYA) equivalent. f = +107.2093 mm.

Both faces are convex toward the object, with a stronger front curvature. Its positive power precedes the two diverging menisci and carries much of the front component's color correction. The inferred 15 mm clear radius follows the source's approximate 30 mm effective front-lens diameter.

### L2 — First Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.69350, νd = 53.4. Glass: LAC13 (HOYA) equivalent. f = −43.2314 mm.

The rear face is more strongly curved than the front face, giving negative power. Figure 1 draws a stepped blank, wider at the front face than at the rear.

### L3 — Second Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.69350, νd = 53.4. Glass: LAC13 (HOYA) equivalent. f = −27.3718 mm.

L3 repeats L2's glass but has different bending and stronger negative power. Together they form the diverging part of the retrofocus front component.

### L4 — Positive Meniscus, Convex to the Image

nd = 1.64769, νd = 33.9. Glass: E-FD2 (HOYA) equivalent. f = +174.9579 mm.

Its two negative radii form a weak positive meniscus facing the image. The patent assigns its concave front face the job of moving the entrance pupil forward.

### L5 — Biconvex Positive Lens Before the Stop

nd = 1.72825, νd = 28.3. Glass: E-FD10 (HOYA) equivalent. f = +31.6569 mm.

The comparatively thick biconvex element supplies strong convergence before the stop. Its dense-flint coordinates, with L4's, satisfy the patent's νL4 + νL5 < 70 condition (33.9 + 28.3 = 62.2).

### L6/L7 — Cemented Negative Component

L6: nd = 1.76182, νd = 26.5. Glass: FD140 (HOYA) equivalent. Isolated f = −15.5986 mm.

L7: nd = 1.77252, νd = 49.6. Glass: TAF1 (HOYA) equivalent. Isolated f = +33.7863 mm.

L6 is biconcave and L7 is a positive meniscus convex to the front, as the patent's second claim requires. Their shared interface has radius +16.07352 mm. The two indices are close, so the junction carries little monochromatic power, while the large Abbe contrast gives the pair its color-correcting role.

### L8 — Positive Meniscus, Convex to the Image

nd = 1.62041, νd = 60.3. Glass: BACD16 (HOYA) equivalent. f = +37.2241 mm.

The strong rear curvature gives positive power. L7's rear face and L8's front face would meet at about 7.74 mm from the axis, so that height is a hard ceiling for both rims.

### L9 — Final Positive Lens

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.0. Glass: S-BSL7 (OHARA) equivalent. f = +34.3856 mm.

Its weakly convex front and stronger convex rear face complete the converging sequence. The full 37.44 mm source back-focus interval remains after its rear vertex.

## Glass Identification and Spectral Reference

The patent text calls its indices “refractive indices for the sodium d-line”. The numbers say otherwise. Every one of the nine index/Abbe pairs reproduces a catalog glass at the **helium d line** within |Δn| ≤ 6×10⁻⁵ and |Δν| ≤ 0.15, and none reproduces a catalog coordinate at the sodium D line or the mercury e line in the same window. SF2-type 1.64769/33.9, SF10-type 1.72825/28.3, SF14-type 1.76182/26.5 and SK16-type 1.62041/60.3 are standard catalog nd values. Their sodium-D indices are measurably lower. The phrase “sodium d-line” is therefore read as a period misnomer for the d line, and the model stores the values as ordinary nd / νd, following the specification's numerical line test.

The patent names no supplier. One vendor, HOYA, has a catalog row for each of L1 to L8, so those elements carry HOYA equivalents; L9's 1.51633/64.0 is not in the HOYA rows here and carries OHARA S-BSL7. The engine resolves each name, checks it against the authored coordinates and anchors its dispersion curve to the authored index.

Several are modern lead-free successors (the E- prefix) of the glasses a 1974 design would have used, and none identifies the production supplier or melt.

## Focus Mechanism

Only the infinity state is modeled. The patent gives no finite-conjugate spacing, floating group or focus travel, so none is invented. The maker's 0.3 m minimum focus, from the Fujica AZ-1 manual, is recorded as catalog metadata and does not move the optics.

## Clear Apertures and Verification Limits

All clear radii are inferred; only the approximate front diameter has a source dimension. Figure 1 was measured against a millimetre grid on the original PDF page, averaging the upper and lower halves of the drawing:

| Element | Figure 1 reading (mm) | Model (mm) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | 15.2 | 15.0 / 15.0 |
| L2 | 10.4 front, 8.3 rear (stepped) | 9.5 / 7.8 |
| L3 | 8.7 front, 6.0 rear (stepped) | 7.5 / 7.5 |
| L4 | 5.4 front to a chamfer, 7.4 rear | 7.0 / 7.0 |
| L5 | 7.0–7.4 | 7.0 / 7.0 |
| L6–L8 | about 7.4, L6 front 5.4 to a chamfer | 6.5 (L6, L7), 6.6 (L8) |
| L9 | 9.0 | 8.5 / 8.5 |

Every stored rim is within the reading's noise band except where the rays decide. L8 was raised from 6.5 to 6.6 mm on both faces, the height of the stated f/2.8 on-axis ray; at 6.5 mm surface 15 clipped that beam. No other rim moved, so the modeled field vignetting is retained. The drawing's stepped and chamfered corners are blank or chamfer, not clear glass.

With these rims the stated f/2.8 axial beam passes every surface and is limited by the iris. The 84° source field is the declared field limit: the traced 42° chief ray lands 20.96 mm from the axis, just inside the 21.65 mm corner of the 35 mm frame. Inferred rims and the declared field qualify vignetting and off-axis performance.

The literal source indices give a computed paraxial EFL of **23.9998523881 mm** and BFL of **37.4335134734 mm**. The computed paraxial focus is **0.0064865266 mm before** the retained source plane. This residual remains visible; neither the last gap nor any curvature was retuned.
