# FUJIFILM FUJINON 95mm f/1.3 (US 2,718,174 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [US 2,718,174 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2718174A/en), “High Aperture Photographic Objective”

**Inventors:** Yoshikazu Doi

**Assignee:** Fuji Photo Film Co., Ltd.

**Filed:** 21 June 1954

**Published:** 20 September 1955

**Priority:** Japan, 25 June 1953

**Example details:** Figure 1; first numerical table in column 2, repeated in claim 1.

Figure 1 describes an eight-element f/1.3 objective. Figure 2 is a separate nine-element f/1.2 example. The model multiplies the table's dimensional values by 100, so the printed focal length f′T = .9500 becomes 95 mm. The printed radii, thicknesses, air spaces and eight refractive-index/Abbe pairs are retained.

The design likely belongs to the development of the Fujinon 5 cm f/1.2. [Oldlens](https://oldlens.com/fujinon%205cmf12.html) summarizes Doi's 1955 account in _Shashin Kogyo_: an f/1.5 design was extended toward f/1.3, followed by a nine-element f/1.2 prototype and an eight-element production lens. Figure 1's aperture and construction fit the f/1.3 stage; Figure 2 fits the nine-element stage. No production lens is documented for the Figure 1 prescription. [Fujifilm's historical account](https://www.fujifilm-x.com/en-us/stories/fujinon-speed-lenses-rebirth-of-the-legend-1/) confirms the production lens's introduction in 1954.

## Optical Architecture

Eight spherical elements form four air-separated groups, named B1–B4 in the patent: a positive front singlet, a negative cemented triplet, a positive cemented triplet and a positive rear singlet. Their computed focal lengths are +123.74, −147.62, +228.06 and +126.42 mm respectively. The construction combines a central stop gap with strongly refracting cemented surfaces.

The front triplet comprises L2–L4, joined at R4 and R5; the rear triplet comprises L5–L7, joined at R8 and R9. The air spaces between groups are 0.4, 18 and 0.4 mm. R2 is a shallow positive surface with radius +6083 mm, rather than a plane.

The drawing places the stop in the central gap but supplies no coordinate. The model divides that gap into 14 mm after R6 and 4 mm before R7, placing the stop 49.5 mm behind the first vertex. This agrees approximately with the drawn location. The stop diameter and all clear semi-diameters are inferred.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are those of the individual elements in air; the powers of the cemented assemblies differ.

### L1 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.6713, νd = 47.0. Glass: N-BAF10 (Schott), spectral proxy. f = +123.74 mm.

The positive front singlet collects the incoming beam. Its rear surface is almost planar but retains the printed positive radius.

### L2 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.6713, νd = 47.0. Glass: N-BAF10 (Schott), spectral proxy. f = +86.42 mm.

L2 is the positive front member of the first cemented triplet. Its R3 clear semi-diameter is 33 mm, below the element's calculated knife edge at about 33.39 mm.

### L3 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.5414, νd = 47.1. Glass: E-FEL2 (Hoya), spectral proxy. f = +156.98 mm.

L3 is the middle positive element of the front triplet. Both faces are cemented, joining L2 at R4 and L4 at R5.

### L4 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.7572, νd = 27.5. Glass: H-ZF6 (CDGM), spectral proxy. f = −32.81 mm.

The high-index, more dispersive rear member makes the front triplet negative overall. Its strongly curved rear surface faces the stop gap.

### L5 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.5414, νd = 47.1. Glass: E-FEL2 (Hoya), spectral proxy. f = −56.17 mm.

L5 begins the rear triplet immediately after the stop gap. Its rear face is cemented to the stronger positive L6.

### L6 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.6738, νd = 47.0. Glass: Unmatched. f = +27.63 mm.

L6 supplies the main positive power within the rear triplet, between the negative elements L5 and L7.

### L7 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.6480, νd = 47.8. Glass: Unmatched. f = −54.08 mm.

L7 forms the rear member of the positive rear triplet. Its concave cemented front face is more strongly curved than its rear face.

### L8 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.6216, νd = 60.2. Glass: J-SK16 (Hikari), spectral proxy. f = +126.42 mm.

The final positive singlet follows a 0.4 mm air space. It has the highest Abbe number in the example.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent explicitly defines its refractive indices for the d line, taken as helium at 587.6 nm. All eight index/Abbe pairs remain as printed, including the repeated pairs on L1/L2 and L3/L5. Repeated values do not combine distinct physical elements.

The text names BaF10 for the principal positive glass of the f/1.3 example, but the printed indices do not match an available catalogue row exactly. It also discusses SSK10, SK21, EK330 and EK210 in connection with faster designs; these names do not establish the identity of each Figure 1 element.

None of the six distinct printed pairs has an exact catalog match. Six elements use present-day catalog curves as spectral proxies, with differences measured as catalog minus printed at the d line:

| Elements | Spectral proxy | Δnd | Δνd |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L1/L2 | N-BAF10 (Schott) | −0.00127 | +0.11 |
| L3/L5 | E-FEL2 (Hoya) | −0.00068 | +0.10 |
| L4 | H-ZF6 (CDGM) | −0.00200 | +0.03 |
| L8 | J-SK16 (Hikari) | −0.00119 | +0.05 |

These rows meet the site's spectral-proxy tolerance of 0.002 in index and 0.25 in Abbe number at the printed line. Each proxy supplies the dispersion-curve shape while retaining the printed index and Abbe number. L6 (1.6738/47.0) and L7 (1.6480/47.8) use Abbe-based dispersion because no compatible catalog row meets both tolerances. The patent supplies no additional line indices or partial-dispersion values. Historical suppliers, melts, secondary spectrum and measured broadband performance remain unknown.

## Focus Mechanism

All four groups have fixed spacings at infinity. The patent supplies no focusing mechanism, close-focus distances or alternate focus spacings.

## First-Order Properties and Model Limits

The prescription computes to EFL 95.01 mm, about 0.01 mm above the table's nominal 95 mm. The first-to-last vertex distance is 83.82 mm. No back focus is printed; the image plane is placed at the calculated paraxial back focus of 44.02 mm, or 127.84 mm behind the first vertex.

The nominal f/1.3 aperture produces a paraxial stop radius of 20.09 mm. Exact axial tracing gives an entrance-equivalent aperture of about f/1.34 and an image-space sine aperture of about f/1.33. A real f/1.3 marginal ray would require a stop radius of 20.66 mm. These differences concern the inferred iris and the aperture convention; no historical diaphragm measurement is available.

The patent discusses picture angles up to about 45° for the lens class, without assigning a numerical field to Figure 1. The model retains an illustrative ±10.0° field. The chief ray clears the inferred rims there, but part of the off-axis pupil is vignetted. No image format or image-circle diameter is assigned from this illustrative field.

The clear radii are estimated from Figure 1's proportions and checked against the axial beam. They are 39 mm at R1/R2, 33/33/33/23 mm through the front triplet, 21/21/21/27 mm through the rear triplet, and 27 mm at R11/R12. The equal heights at R3–R5 follow the level front-triplet edge, and R10 reaches the height of the rear singlet. Flat opaque lands extend R6 and R7 to outer radii of 29 and 23.5 mm, retaining their 23 and 21 mm optical caps. Straight rim joins approximate the drawing's stop-side bevels and small shoulders at the cemented interfaces. Wide-open geometric transmission is about 84% at the illustrative 10° field. Calculated MTF depends on this inferred aperture geometry.
