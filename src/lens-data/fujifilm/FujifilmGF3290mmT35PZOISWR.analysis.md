# FUJIFILM FUJINON GF 32-90mm T3.5 PZ OIS WR — Optical Design Analysis

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP 2026-091132 A  
**Filed:** 22 November 2024  
**Published:** 3 June 2026  
**Inventors:** Hiroki Saito; Motoari Ota; Shunsuke Miyagishima  
**Applicant:** Fujifilm Corporation  
**Title:** Zoom Lens and Imaging Apparatus  
**Example details:** Example 1 (実施例1), Tables 1A, 1B, 2 and 3 on pages 32–34; Figures 1–3 on pages 79–81 of the 106-page publication. Dimensions in millimetres at the patent's own scale.

Example 1 is a patent design associated with the FUJINON GF32-90mmT3.5 PZ OIS WR. The match is strong by construction: 25 elements in 19 groups, three aspherical elements, three ED elements, and element-by-element agreement with Fujifilm's section, including aspherical L23, L33 and L41, ED L33, L51 and L54, and the flat L42/L43 joint. Fujifilm has not named the patent, so this is not a confirmed factory prescription. The patent's 32.94/59.13/87.33 mm, open F-number 3.19/3.25/3.30 and full fields 82.2°/46.4°/32.0° differ from the marketed 32–90 mm, T3.5 and 81.0°/33.8°. T3.5 is a transmission stop, not an F-number.

The Japanese front page names 斉藤 広樹, 大田 基在 and 宮城島 峻介 and prints no Latin names; its continuation on page 106 lists F-terms only. Family publication US 2026/0147192 A1 supplies Hiroki Saito, Motoari Ota and Shunsuke Miyagishima and claims the same Japanese application, 2024-204202, filed 22 November 2024. The optical tables are the Japanese publication's Example 1.

## Optical Architecture

The five groups have positive, negative, positive, negative and positive power (¶0159) and hold 3/5/9/3/5 elements. Six cemented doublets leave 19 air-separated groups; the camera-side plate PP is not counted. Fig. 2 divides G3 into GISf (L31–L33), the stabilizing group GIS (L34–L36) and GISr (L37–L39) (¶0069), and the stop St stands directly ahead of L31. S23 is a real flat glass face and S36 a real flat cemented joint; neither is a dummy surface.

## Glass Identification and Selection

Every element keeps the patent's nd, νd and θgF (¶0162–0163), as does the rear plate (nd = 1.51680, νd = 64.197, θgF = 0.534). The patent prints no C, F or g indices. The glass labels are catalog equivalents, and the production supplier is not established. The printed θgF pins most rows: 23 of 25 labelled glasses lie within 0.0015 of it, and L23 and L55 are the exceptions. L23 (L-BSL7) and L41 (L-LAL13) are moulding-glass proxies chosen by coordinates, and L33 (MC-FCD1-M20) is a moulding-glass row.

L33, L51 and L54 have the three highest Abbe numbers, νd = 81.4–81.6, and sit at the three positions Fujifilm's section marks ED. The patent does not call them ED, so their anomalous-dispersion tags are inferred; its ED column means maximum effective diameter. The printed thermal coefficients are not modeled.

## Focus Mechanism and Zoom States

From wide to tele G2 and G4 move toward the image, while G1, G3 and G5 stay fixed relative to the image plane (¶0160). Table 2 prints three stations:

| Gap after surface (mm) | Wide | Middle | Tele |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| DD6 | 2.1800 | 24.4145 | 36.4341 |
| DD16 | 36.2546 | 14.0201 | 2.0005 |
| DD32 | 5.0257 | 12.5441 | 19.2029 |
| DD37 | 26.2668 | 18.7484 | 12.0896 |

The model adds seven inferred stations, four from Wide to Middle and three from Middle to Tele, at geometric steps in f. They are solved so the paraxial image stays on the fixed plane, both groups move monotonically, and paraxial defocus between stations stays below 0.09 mm. The inferred stations are a model of the cam, not Fujifilm's; Patent positions mode selects the printed stations only.

G4 is the focus group and moves toward the image for close objects; GIS stabilizes (¶0160). The patent prints no close-focus spacings or decenter law, so the model stays at infinity focus with GIS centered. The file's 0.8 m close focus is Fujifilm's published figure.

The image plane stays at z = 248.034 mm. S45 keeps 31.4994 mm of air before the separate 3.2000 mm plate, followed by the printed 0.9910 mm gap, and no t/n air-equivalent fold replaces those distances. The plate's two tabulated effective diameters are not a common clear rim, so the plate does not limit the beam.

The printed prescription gives paraxial EFL 32.9422/59.1259/87.3314 mm, and each value rounds to the printed f. Paraxial back focus after the plate is 0.9884/1.0214/1.0529 mm against the printed 0.9910 mm gap. That offset is consistent with balanced spherical aberration: real axial rays cross within about 0.09 mm of the plane at all three printed stations, and no refocus is applied. The patent's conditional values for Example 1 (Table 34) agree: 21 values computed from this prescription fall within 1.5E−4 of the printed ones, including Bfw/fw = 1.0503 and DStw/TLw = 0.6691, with TLw/ft = 2.8277 against 2.8276. Conditional expression (10), |ΔsagM|/HaM, computes 0.01020 against the printed 0.01022. That difference, about 0.24 µm of sag on S11, is unexplained; the S11 coefficients agree with the US family text.

## Stop and Clear-Aperture Assumptions

The patent prints the stop as surface 17, at z = 81.7251 mm. Conditional expression (36) gives φSw/φSt = 1.0000: the axial beam at the stop has the same diameter at wide and tele, so the iris is a constant diameter. The printed F-number drift then follows from the design. Real rays at the printed stop radius of 12.3645 mm give 3.193/3.254/3.295, matching the printed 3.19/3.25/3.30.

The engine holds one radius at every station, the 12.2685 mm its paraxial stop rule gives at F/3.19. That rule reads F/3.233 at Middle and F/3.265 at Tele against the printed 3.25 and 3.30, because it scales paraxial ray heights while the printed values follow real marginal rays through the full aperture. The inferred stations carry the real-ray values at 12.3645 mm, rounded to two decimals. The iris plane stays at least 1.5 mm from its neighbors: that is the printed S17–S18 spacing, and DD16 never falls below 2.0005 mm. Blade and barrel mechanics are unknown. The f/22 slider limit is a modeled range; Fujifilm lists T32.

Lens face radii are half the printed maximum effective diameter. These ray envelopes are not proven clear rims, and nothing is added for throughput. The default 90% gap limit refuses those rims at S8→S9 and S14→S15 even though the surfaces clear by +0.919 mm and +0.148 mm, so the file sets gapSagFrac = 0.9134, the smallest value rounded to 1E−4 that admits them. It changes no optical value.

All ten stations trace within 3% of their stated F-number with no rim limit on the axial beam, and the 27.39 mm image height is covered at every station. Off-axis vignetting at the front-group rims stays in the model. This is modeled behavior, not measured illumination.
