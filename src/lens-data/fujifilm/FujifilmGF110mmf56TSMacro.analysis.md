**Example details:** Figure 39, Tables 45–47.

Example 18 is the closest match among the publication's eighteen examples: the 110 mm focal length, eleven elements in nine groups, ED positions at physical elements 4 and 8 (patent L14 and L18), additional rear element and rear asphere agree with the earlier recorded comparison with Fujifilm's construction drawing. Production correspondence is probable; the patent does not establish the exact manufactured prescription. Element names below follow Figure 39: L11–L18 in G1 and L21–L23 in G2; element IDs retain their front-to-rear sequence 1–11.

## Optical Architecture

Eleven powered elements form nine air-separated components: three front singlets, cemented L14/L15, the stop, cemented L16/L17, positive singlet L18, then three rear singlets. The positive first group G1 contains L11–L18 and the stop and moves objectward for close focus; the negative rear group G2 contains L21–L23 and stays fixed relative to the sensor during focusing (PDF page 74, ¶0268–0269). The low-index, high-Abbe L14 and L18 and the two-sided rear asphere match the earlier maker-drawing record. The sensor plate is represented by an air-equivalent spacing.

## Glass Identification and Selection

All powered-element nd and νd values are retained as printed. θgF=(ng−nF)/(nF−nC) is a partial-dispersion ratio, distinct from anomalous ΔPgF. Physical elements 1–9 carry catalog-equivalent labels whose nd/νd match the printed pairs: S-NBH5, S-TIM5 and S-LAH55V from OHARA, and LAC14, FCD1 and E-CF6 from HOYA. These elements trace on the catalog dispersion curves. Most catalog θgF values agree with the printed values within about 1e-4. The exceptions are FCD1 (0.5389 vs 0.53804) and E-CF6 (0.5589 vs 0.55800; catalog νd 52.15 vs printed 52.20). The labels name catalog equivalents only; the production supplier and melts are unspecified. L22 (physical element 10, 1.55298/55.07/0.54469) matches HIKARI J-KZFH4 (1.552981/55.07/0.5447) in all three printed quantities and traces on that curve. L23 (physical element 11, 1.58480/58.71) has no catalog row within tolerance (the nearest, SUMITA K-CSK120-M, is 0.4 Abbe units away) and keeps a three-term Cauchy curve that reproduces nd, the F−C difference and the printed g−F ratio. L14 and L18 have inferred ED designations from the earlier maker-drawing comparison and their compatible nd/νd values. The patent's ED column denotes effective diameter, not ED glass. Individual power signs do not uniquely establish aberration contributions.

Neither triple occurs in the other approximately 110 mm macro prescriptions: Example 11 Table 29 (PDF pages 70–71), Example 12 Table 31 (page 71), Example 13 Table 33 (page 72), Example 14 Table 35 (page 72), Example 15 Table 37 (page 73), Example 16 Table 39 (page 73), or Example 17 Table 42 (page 74). The last aspherical material in Examples 16 and 17 is instead 1.58313 / 59.38 / 0.54237. However, the exact L23 triple 1.58480 / 58.71 / 0.54116 recurs in the approximately 31 mm examples: Example 4 Table 10 (page 64, surfaces 1, 20 and 24), Example 5 Table 13 (page 65, surfaces 1, 20 and 24), Example 6 Table 16 (page 66, surface 1), Example 7 Table 19 (page 67, surfaces 1 and 20), and Example 8 Table 22 (page 68, surface 1). This is evidence of a reused material specification, not proof of commercial glass identity. The L22 triple 1.55298 / 55.07 / 0.54469 occurs only in Example 18 Table 45 (page 75); uniqueness is compatible with optimiser-floated glass but does not establish it. The patent names no glass brand, catalog code or supplier. It supplies nd, νd and θgF, but no independent absolute C/F/g line indices (definitions on page 60, ¶0205–0207); the stored line indices are calculated fits.

## Focus Mechanism

The first eight lenses and stop move together 23.9818 mm objectward relative to the rear group and sensor. DD15 increases from 2.9000 mm at infinity to 26.8818 mm at the printed −0.5000× near state. The 101 focus positions use calculated inverse object-to-image distance spacing; the intervening motion is not a measured cam. With the physical plate train, the near state implies an object distance of 257.939149977679 mm before the first vertex and 0.426259949977679 m to the sensor. These are derived distances; Fujifilm specifies 0.43 m and 0.5×. Intermediate finite-focus MTF is unavailable.

### Tilt and Shift Movements

Shift spans ±15 mm and tilt ±10°, following Fujifilm's specifications; model controls step by 0.1 mm and 0.1°. The camera frame and 44 × 33 mm sensor stay fixed while the optics move. The tilt pivot is 109.9746 mm objectward of the image plane, using Example 18's infinity EFL and the principal-point guidance in ¶0276, Figure 42. This is a design reference, not a measured hinge position.

The plate at surfaces 22–23 is replaced by an air-equivalent rear spacing: 78.8866 + 3.2/1.51680 + 0.9874 = 81.9837 mm. Reference-wavelength paraxial focus, EFL and focus travel are preserved; the plate's spherochromatism is omitted. Physical infinity vertex-to-image length is 144.339 mm; model track is 143.249 mm.

## Model Scope and Limitations

Combined extreme shift and tilt require about 27.5° traced half-field, beyond the patent's 43.4° full design field (21.7° half-field), and vignette. Shift alone places the sensor corner at √(37² + 16.5²) ≈ 40.5 mm, within the approximately 43.8 mm patent image height; this does not guarantee full-pupil illumination. Off-axis marginal rays vignette at surface 1.

The printed stop diameter is 17.440 mm; the model derives its radius from design f/5.76. Printed effective diameters and gaps are retained. At the surface 19–20 gap, a rim allowance of 0.911 accommodates positive physical separation; 0.90 and 0.91 do not. The 4.419 µm infinity defocus and calculated near magnification −0.499875264 reflect prescription rounding. MTF convergence varies with field, spectrum and spatial frequency across reference, photopic and C/d/F calculations. Numerical convergence describes sampling, not measured production performance.
