## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 10,274,698 B2
**Filed:** January 25, 2018
**Granted:** April 30, 2019
**Inventors:** Shinkichi Ikeda, Takuya Tanaka
**Assignee:** Fujifilm Corporation
**Title:** Zoom Lens and Imaging Apparatus
**Example details:** Tables 1–3, PDF page 27; Figures 1 and 11.

Related publication: US 2018/0217360 A1 (JP priority 2017-012817, January 27, 2017).

Fujifilm's specification for the FUJINON MKX50-135mm T2.9 — 22 elements in 17 groups with two Super ED and two ED elements, a front inner-focusing system, angle of view 31.7°–12.0° and a 1.2 m minimum focus distance — matches Example 1: 22 elements in 17 air-separated groups, five cemented doublets, low-dispersion glass at L14 and L22 (νd 81.54) and at L16 and L42 (νd 94.66), and 2ω = 32.2°–12.0°. The design F-number 2.75 equals the F2.75 that Fujifilm lists for the E-mount MK50-135mm T2.9; the X-mount MKX version followed in 2018 as part of the same MK line. The example is therefore read as the MK/MKX 50-135 design; the patent does not show that either production lens uses exactly these numbers. Example 3 has the same element and group counts with a different G2 order (singlet, cemented pair, singlet), and no public construction drawing was found that separates the two; Example 1 is the patent's lead example and the one shown on its front page. The printed radii, spacings and nd/νd are used without correction. T2.9 is a transmission rating; the model's iris is sized from the geometric design F-number.

## Optical Architecture

Example 1 is a positive–negative–positive–positive zoom of 22 elements in 17 air-separated groups, split 7 + 4 + 3 + 8 by zoom group. G1 (L11–L17) and G4 (L41–L48, behind the stop) stay fixed, so the length does not change. G2 (L21–L24, negative) moves toward the image throughout zooming; G3 (L31–L33, positive) moves toward the image and then back, keeping the image plane fixed (D25 reaches about 1.2 mm near 100 mm). G1 is split into G1a (L11–L13, negative), G1b (L14, positive, the focusing lens) and G1c (L15–L17, positive). Cemented interfaces are at surfaces 10, 19, 24, 30 and 35; the stop is surface 26, at the front of G4.

A camera-side plate PP (2.000 mm, nd 1.51633, νd 64.14) follows the last lens, with 5.000 mm of air before it and 30.658 mm after; the printed Bf 36.976 mm is the air-equivalent back focus. The plate is traced but not drawn and is not part of the 22/17 construction.

Printed zoom states (infinity focus):

- Wide: f = 51.501 mm, F/2.750, 2ω = 32.2°; D13 = 14.039, D20 = 22.245, D25 = 13.006 mm
- Tele: f = 134.933 mm, F/2.754, 2ω = 12.0°; D13 = 44.630, D20 = 1.243, D25 = 3.417 mm

The 33 zoom stations between the printed ends are an inferred paraxial cam, not the production cam: G2's travel is interpolated, the focal length follows the printed endpoints, and D20 and D25 are solved to hold the image plane. The printed wide and tele states themselves carry 0.8 and 2.5 µm of paraxial defocus from the rounding of the printed data; the inferred stations stay within 2.5 µm and the points between them within 4.5 µm, a blur under 2 µm at F/2.75.

Negative front meniscus of G1a, convex to the object. Its light, low-dispersion crown is the G1a negative lens that meets the patent's condition (1), which bounds specific gravity against Abbe number (0.035 for Example 1) to keep the large front group light.

## Glass Identification

The patent prints d-line indices and Abbe numbers only, with no glass names or partial dispersions. Every pair matches a current catalog row at the printed precision. The labels use OHARA rows, except TAFD37 and BAC4 (HOYA), which have no exact OHARA counterpart, TAFD25 (HOYA), the closer of two exact rows for 1.90366/31.31, and FDS90 (HOYA) for the four 1.84667/23.79 dense flints (L12, L15, L23, L44). OHARA S-TIH53 matches those four equally in nd and νd, but its higher g-line partial dispersion puts the model's paraxial g-line focus about 31 µm behind the d-line focus at the wide end and 41 µm in front of it at the tele end; with FDS90 these become about +43 and −24 µm, nearer the g-line curves of Fig. 11 (roughly +50 and −10 µm). Where OHARA and another maker otherwise tie exactly (S-LAH98 and HOYA TAFD45, S-PHM53 and Hikari J-PSK03), the OHARA row is used; the actual suppliers are not known. Fujifilm lists two Super ED and two ED elements. The νd 94.66 positions (L16, L42) fit S-FPL55-type glass and the νd 81.54 positions (L14, L22) S-FPL51-type ED glass; these four are tagged as inferred anomalous-dispersion elements. The model takes every element's C, F and g indices from the labelled catalog rows, not from measured melts.

## Focus Mechanism

Focusing moves only L14, the G1b subgroup between the fixed G1a and G1c (patent claim 1), which matches Fujifilm's description of a front inner-focusing system. Because G1 does not move during zooming, the focus setting holds while the focal length changes. The patent prints infinity states only and gives neither the direction nor the amount of travel. The model moves L14 toward the image, so D6 grows and D8 shrinks with D6 + D8 = 12.344 mm, and solves the travel from the printed prescription for Fujifilm's 1.2 m minimum focus distance (object to image):

- Wide: L14 shift +9.186 mm; D6 = 11.358, D8 = 0.986 mm
- Tele: L14 shift +9.188 mm; D6 = 11.360, D8 = 0.984 mm

These spacings are a reconstruction, not a published cam. The 0.85 m wide-end macro setting is not modeled.

## Aperture and Rim Inference

The patent places the stop on the axis and states that the drawn stop does not indicate its size or shape. The model sizes the iris from the design F-number 2.75 at every zoom position; the printed tele value, F/2.754, differs by 0.15 %. Clear semi-diameters are measured from the element outlines of Fig. 1 (wide end) and checked against the full-aperture axial beam and the corner chief ray at every zoom station and at close focus; they are not factory dimensions. Fig. 1 draws the facing surfaces of L21 and L22, and of L47 and L48, meeting at the rim; those four faces stop just short of where the printed curvatures would cross. The rear face of L21 and the front face of L22 (about 11.9 mm, just outside the tele axial beam) use 91.3 % of their 4.440 mm air gap (about 0.39 mm edge clearance remains), so the drawing allowance for rim sag in a gap is raised from the default 90 % to 91.5 %. These two faces set most of the light fall-off near the axis at the long end. Wide open at infinity, geometric transmission at the edge of the traced field is about 53 % at the wide end and 47 % at the tele end. Nine blades follow Fujifilm's specification.

## Model Scope and Limitations

No aspherical surfaces are used in this example. The model reproduces the printed focal lengths (51.501 mm; 134.929 mm against 134.933 mm printed) and the 36.976 mm air-equivalent back focus. Of the patent's conditional-expression values for Example 1, (3) f1a/f1b = −0.781 and (5) f1a1/f1a = 1.626 are reproduced exactly; (2) and (4) imply f1 ≈ 86.8 mm against 91.4 mm computed from the printed data, an offset the patent's Example 2 shows as well. Intermediate zoom states, the close-focus travel, the iris and the rims are inferred as described above; the model makes no claim about factory MTF or aberration performance.
