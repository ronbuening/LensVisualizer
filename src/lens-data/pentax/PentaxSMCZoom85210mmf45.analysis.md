# PENTAX SMC ZOOM 85-210mm f/4.5

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 3,506,337, *Variable Focal Length Lens System Suitable for 35 mm. Cameras*\
**Inventors:** Yasuo Takahashi\
**Assignee:** Asahi Kogaku Kogyo Co., Ltd.\
**Priority:** September 14, 1966, Japan\
**Filed:** September 6, 1967\
**Published:** April 14, 1970\
**Example details:** Sole example, Table 1 and Claim 1. Figures 1(a–c) show the lens sections.

Takahashi’s 85–205 mm design is a probable match for the K-mount SMC Pentax Zoom 85–210 mm f/4.5. The patent gives one numerical example and names no commercial lens. [US 3,506,337, Table 1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3506337A/en)

1. Asahi’s drawing on printed page 25 matches the patent’s telephoto section in the sequence of eleven elements in ten air-spaced groups, the cemented L5/L6 doublet and the two broad air spaces in the rear triplet. The drawing gives no factory dimensions, glass types or close-focus positions. [Asahi catalog, p. 25](https://hirokun.jp/pentax_lens_accessory_catalog_1976-77.pdf)
2. The patent’s f/4.5 plots span full fields of 29.6° and 11.8° at the two ends. Asahi lists 29°–11°, a 58 mm filter and a 3.5 m minimum for the production f/4.5 zoom. The K version, sold from 1975 to 1977, has SMC coating and a six-blade automatic diaphragm that closes to f/32. Asahi’s 85–210 mm f/3.5 has a different construction, with twelve elements in eleven groups. [Asahi catalog](https://hirokun.jp/pentax_lens_accessory_catalog_1976-77.pdf) · [Pentax Forums K-mount record](https://www.pentaxforums.com/lensreviews/SMC-Pentax-K-85-210mm-F4.5-Zoom-Lens.html)
3. The M42 S-M-C Takumar Zoom (1971–75) shares the optical design, 3.5 m minimum focus and 58 mm filter. Asahi’s ES manual illustrates both ends of its zoom and specifies f/22 as its smallest aperture. The f/32 scale here belongs to the K version. ## Optical Architecture

The patent divides the eleven spherical elements into four groups, G1–G4. These are the optical divisions marked in Figure 1. The catalog count of ten groups refers to the glass components separated by air. G2 moves as a unit. Within G3 only L7 moves.

| Patent group | Elements | Action during zooming |
| --- | --- | --- |
| G1 | L1–L3 | The stationary front triplet collects light. Two positive crowns follow a negative flint. |
| G2 | L4–L6 | A negative singlet and a negative cemented doublet move 62.78 mm toward the image from wide to tele. |
| G3 | L7–L8 | L7 moves toward the object and returns, keeping the image nearly stationary. L8 stays fixed. |
| G4 | L9–L11 | A fixed positive–negative–positive triplet forms the final image. The diaphragm sits between L9 and L10. |

The glass vertices span 143.10 mm throughout the zoom. At a 21.63 mm image height, the chief ray reaches the full-frame corner at half-angles of 14.80°, 9.09° and 5.94° at 85.00, 135.71 and 205.19 mm respectively. The patent plots extend to 14.8°, 9.1° and 5.9°.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below describe each element in air. Glass names give catalog equivalents of the patent’s d-line index and Abbe number.

### L1 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.66680, νd = 33.1. Ohara S-TIM39 equivalent. f = −133.33 mm.

The shallow front face has a radius of −3,000 mm. Most of L1’s diverging action lies at the rear face. Its flint glass balances the two lower-dispersion positive elements behind it.

### L2 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.61405, νd = 55.1. Ohara S-BSM9 equivalent. f = +168.50 mm.

The first positive crown bends the beam mainly at its front face. Its rear radius is about 4,545 mm.

### L3 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.62280, νd = 56.9. Schott N-SK10 equivalent. f = +144.41 mm.

L3 completes the converging front group. The space behind it grows from 2.00 to 64.78 mm as G2 moves toward the image.

### L4 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Ohara S-BSL7 equivalent. f = −131.11 mm.

L4 travels with the doublet in G2. The patent’s first condition relates the radius of its front face to the front group’s focal length to control coma across the zoom range.

### L5 and L6 — Cemented Doublet C1

- **L5, Biconcave Negative.** nd = 1.64000, νd = 60.2. Hikari J-LAK01 equivalent. f = −60.84 mm.
- **L6, Positive Meniscus.** nd = 1.74077, νd = 27.7. Ohara S-TIH13 equivalent. f = +99.86 mm.

The negative crown and positive flint meet at the 43.860 mm radius of surface 10. The doublet has a focal length of −151.23 mm in air. Its glass pair places the greater dispersion in the positive member of this diverging component.

### L7 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.66446, νd = 35.9. Hikari J-BASF2 equivalent. f = −248.29 mm.

L7 is the compensator. Moving it independently of G2 changes the vergence reaching L8 and the rear triplet. The patent relates its front radius and L8’s rear radius to the balance of spherical aberration and coma.

### L8 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Ohara S-BSL7 equivalent. f = +111.26 mm.

L8 is the stationary member of G3. Its front face bounds the reversing gap behind L7, while its rear face stays 0.10 mm ahead of L9.

### L9 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.62041, νd = 60.3. Schott N-SK16 equivalent. f = +63.98 mm.

L9 supplies positive power ahead of the diaphragm. Conditions (4) and (5) relate its power and its combined power with L10 to the Petzval sum.

### L10 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.64769, νd = 33.9. Schott SF2 equivalent. f = −37.52 mm.

L10 is the strongest diverging element in the lens. It stands behind the diaphragm, with an 18.50 mm air space to L11.

### L11 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.58913, νd = 61.2. Sumita SK5 equivalent. f = +75.15 mm.

The final crown converges the beam. Its 1,000 mm front radius is shallow, and its −46.238 mm rear radius supplies most of its power.

## Glass Identification

The eleven elements use ten distinct index and dispersion pairs. L4 and L8 share the same crown despite having opposite powers. Condition (6) gives the dispersion requirement for G1. The mean Abbe number of L2 and L3 exceeds that of L1 by 22.9, within the specified interval of 15 to 35. The patent relates this difference to chromatic correction in G1 and to the variation of image magnification with wavelength.

The ten catalog equivalents differ from the printed pairs by at most 0.00002 in nd and 0.11 in νd. The patent identifies no supplier, ED glass or anomalous-dispersion element. It describes every refracting face by a spherical radius.

## Zoom and Focus Mechanism

Table 1 gives the range of three air spaces. The first-to-last vertex distance stays constant, so d6 + d11 + d13 = 70.600 mm. At the long end, d6 is 64.78 mm and d11 is 5.72 mm. This leaves 0.10 mm for d13, the same as at the short end. The printed 7.41 mm is its greatest opening during the stroke.

| Position | d6, L3 to L4 (mm) | d11, L6 to L7 (mm) | d13, L7 to L8 (mm) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 85 mm, Table 1 limits | 2.00 | 68.50 | 0.10 |
| 138.52 mm, calculated | 40.0299 | 23.1601 | 7.41 |
| 205 mm, Table 1 limits | 64.78 | 5.72 | 0.10 |

The endpoint prescriptions give paraxial back focal distances of 111.739 and 111.741 mm. The image plane is fixed 111.740 mm behind L11. At 22 intermediate positions, the gaps are calculated for focal length and infinity focus with the same total spacing. Near 139 mm, exact focus would require d13 to open to about 7.436 mm. Holding it to 7.41 mm places the paraxial focus up to 0.026 mm behind the image. Elsewhere, while d13 is below the limit, the error stays below 0.020 mm between positions.

The patent places the stop between L9 and L10. Its Seidel coefficients locate it about 8 mm behind surface 17 in the 15 mm air space. The f/4.5 aperture gives a paraxial stop radius of approximately 13.10 mm. Clear glass radii come from the lower outlines in Figures 1(a–c). Neither the stop radius nor the glass diameters are tabulated.

The production lens has one ring that turns to focus and slides to zoom. Its minimum distance is 3.5 m, measured from the film plane under Asahi’s K-system convention. With the supplied close-up lens, a separate white scale reads down to 1.9 m. Table 1 gives only infinity spacings, so the model has no finite-focus position or magnification to compare with the production lens.
