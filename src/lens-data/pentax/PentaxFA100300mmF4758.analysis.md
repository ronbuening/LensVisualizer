# PENTAX SMC FA 100-300mm f/4.7-5.8: optical design

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2001/0006433 A1  
**Title:** Zoom Lens System  
**Inventor:** Hiroyasu Ozaki  
**Assignee:** Asahi Kogaku Kogyo Co., Ltd.  
**Priority:** December 27, 1999  
**Filed:** December 26, 2000  
**Published:** July 5, 2001  
**Example details:** Table 1 (printed p. 4, PDF p. 19); Fig. 1 section, Figs. 2–4 aberrations, Fig. 13 group motion.

The front page, field (73), prints the assignee as ASAHI KOGAKU KOGYO KABUSHIKI KAISHA; the catalog uses the corpus spelling of the same company.

The correlation with the production lens is **qualified**: the evidence below fits Embodiment 1, but nothing ties it to the factory prescription. No scaling or radius correction is applied.

1. Pentax's promotional brochure effective February 1, 2000 lists the SMC P-FA 100–300mm f/4.7–5.8 (product number 27610) with 11 elements in 9 groups, a 58 mm filter and 0.26× maximum magnification (PDF p. 9, printed p. 8). The published minimum focus distance is 1.5 m.
2. A Pentax dealer catalog lists 27610 separately from the earlier FA 100–300mm f/4.5–5.6 power zoom (27927) and marks 27610 as having no power zoom (PDF p. 12, printed p. 11); its accessory table also separates the two. This page describes the later lens.
3. Embodiment 1 has 11 elements in 9 air-separated groups (3 + 3 + 5 elements in three groups, no special-dispersion glass) and covers 103.00–290.04 mm at f/4.8–6.0. The December 1999 priority date falls shortly before the February 2000 brochure, and the marketed 100–300mm f/4.7–5.8 is an ordinary rounding of that range.
4. Embodiments 2 and 3 share the same layout and neither fits the product better. No Pentax construction diagram was found to compare with Fig. 1, so the element and group counts are the main structural link.

Table 1 gives fB as the back focal distance and has no object-distance row; the printed fB agrees with the paraxial infinity image distance, so the table is read as infinity focus. The ∞ on the diaphragm row is its plane radius. Semi-diameters and the intermediate zoom positions are inferred, as described below.

## Optical Architecture

The design is a positive–negative–positive telephoto zoom. G1 contains three singlets, G2 a cemented pair and a negative singlet, and G3 a positive singlet, a cemented pair, a positive singlet and a negative meniscus: eleven elements in nine air-separated groups.

| Optical group | Surfaces | Elements / air-separated groups | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1, positive | 1–6 | 3 / 3 | Front collector; whole-group focusing (¶0058) |
| G2, negative | 7–11 | 3 / 2 | Negative group, stationary relative to the image plane in Embodiment 1 |
| G3, positive | 12–20 | 5 / 4 | Moving rear group with a long 42.454 mm internal air space |

In Fig. 13 and ¶0067, G1 and G3 move toward the object from wide to tele while G2 stays fixed. The printed spacings place G2 at −143.149/−143.148/−143.153 mm from the image plane; the 0.005 mm spread is left as printed.

The diaphragm moves with G3, 0.750 mm ahead of S12; D11 is the S11-to-diaphragm spacing. There are no aspheres or cover-glass plates in this example.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Biconvex Positive
nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.2. Glass: S-FSL5 (Ohara catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = +205.32 mm.

The biconvex front element starts the converging first group. Condition (5) concerns the Abbe number of G1's positive elements: below its lower limit, axial color is under-corrected at the long end (¶0059–0060). Table 4 gives 70.2.

### L2 — Negative Meniscus
nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: S-TIH6 (Ohara catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = −232.87 mm.

This dense-flint negative meniscus is the achromatizing partner of the two crown positives. ¶0058 specifies G1 as positive, negative and positive singlets in that order.

### L3 — Positive Meniscus
nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.2. Glass: S-FSL5 (Ohara catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = +158.74 mm.

The second crown positive meniscus completes G1. The three singlets move together for focusing; ¶0058 prefers three elements to limit aberration change at close distances.

### L4 — Negative Meniscus
nd = 1.74400, νd = 44.8. Glass: S-LAM2 (Ohara catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = −25.11 mm.

The front member of G2's cemented doublet is the strongest negative element in the lens.

### L5 — Positive Meniscus
nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: S-TIH6 (Ohara catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = +30.75 mm.

The dense-flint positive partner, cemented to L4 at surface 8, corrects the color of G2's negative power.

### L6 — Biconcave Negative
nd = 1.80400, νd = 46.6. Glass: S-LAH65 (Ohara catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = −48.04 mm.

The rear element of G2 sits 2.684 mm behind the doublet. With L4 it sets condition (2), the mean index of G2's negative elements: (1.744 + 1.804) / 2 = 1.774, as Table 4 lists. The facing curves of S9 and S10 meet at 12.13 mm height, which limits this element's front clear radius (see Clear Apertures).

### L7 — Biconvex Positive
nd = 1.65160, νd = 58.5. Glass: S-LAL7 (Ohara catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = +58.65 mm.

This equiconvex element (R = ±75.674 mm) lies 0.750 mm behind the diaphragm and moves with it.

### L8 — Biconvex Positive
nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Glass: S-BSL7 (Ohara catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = +43.17 mm.

The positive member of the G3 cemented pair is a moderate-index crown.

### L9 — Biconcave Negative
nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.8. Glass: S-TIH53 (Ohara catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = −59.62 mm.

The dense low-Abbe negative partner follows the shared surface 15 and precedes the long 42.454 mm air space.

### L10 — Biconvex Positive
nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: S-TIH6 (Ohara catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = +69.58 mm.

The rear positive singlet contributes power ahead of the final negative meniscus.

### L11 — Negative Meniscus
nd = 1.80400, νd = 46.6. Glass: S-LAH65 (Ohara catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = −43.59 mm.

The final negative meniscus, concave to the object, makes G3 a telephoto group and shortens it (¶0061). Condition (6) relates G3's power to this element's power (¶0062–0064); Table 4 gives −0.803. The printed fB runs from its rear surface to the image plane.

## Glass Identification and Selection

Every nd/νd pair is copied from Table 1 and uses the d-line (¶0065). Element focal lengths are calculated thick-lens values in air, not published figures. The patent names no glasses; each pair matches an Ohara catalog glass exactly, and those are used as catalog equivalents for the dispersion model. The actual supplier is unconfirmed.

| nd | νd | Catalog equivalent | Elements |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.48749 | 70.2 | S-FSL5 | L1, L3 |
| 1.80518 | 25.4 | S-TIH6 | L2, L5, L10 |
| 1.74400 | 44.8 | S-LAM2 | L4 |
| 1.80400 | 46.6 | S-LAH65 | L6, L11 |

None of these is an ED or anomalous-dispersion glass. The patent's aberration plots show d, g and C curves but give no numerical dispersion data.

## Focus Mechanism

¶0058 describes focusing by moving the whole first group, but Table 1 gives no close-focus spacings, magnifications or object distances. **The model covers zooming at infinity only**: both focus positions repeat the infinity spacings, so the focus control does not change the optics.

The published minimum focus distance is 1.5 m. A paraxial calculation on the 290 mm state shows that moving G1 about 19.2 mm toward the object focuses at 1.5 m object-to-image distance with 0.259× magnification, matching the published 0.26× and consistent with front-group focusing. This travel is calculated, not printed, and is not modeled.

## Continuous Zoom Reconstruction

The three printed states keep their R, d, nd, νd, fB and f-numbers as printed. Between them the model uses 65 zoom positions, evenly spaced in zoom; only the first, middle and last are printed states. D11 and BF are interpolated from the printed values, and D6 is solved at each intermediate position so the paraxial image keeps the interpolated offset of the printed states. This places G1 while G2 stays fixed to print precision; it is not the production cam. Intermediate focal-length labels are calculated EFLs.

| Printed focal length | Calculated EFL | Printed image plane to paraxial focus |
|---|---|---|
| 103.00 mm | 103.0008 mm | +0.0004 mm |
| 160.00 mm | 160.0067 mm | +0.0028 mm |
| 290.04 mm | 290.0427 mm | −0.0069 mm |

These small offsets are left as printed. They are not the same as best axial focus.

## Clear Apertures

All clear radii are estimated from Fig. 1 proportions; the patent prints none. Scaled from Fig. 1, the rear G2 element reaches about 12.45 mm with a square edge, but at that height its front surface S10 would cross S9: the 2.684 mm air gap narrows to about 0.06 mm at 12 mm height and closes at 12.13 mm. Its rear surface S11 needs about 12.26 mm to pass the printed f/6.0 axial beam at 290 mm, so no single height suits both faces. The model sets S10 to 12.0 mm and S11 to 12.3 mm; the slightly tapered edge is an estimate, not the manufactured shape, and the two faces stay apart. Other radii follow the figure.

The stop is sized for the printed f/4.8, 5.3 and 6.0. At 103 and 160 mm the full axial beam passes; at 290 mm the S10 rim trims the edge of the beam slightly, passing about 97% of the nominal f/6.0 pupil area. The printed f-number is kept.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The patent gives half-angles of 11.8°, 7.5° and 4.2°, not image heights. Chief rays at those angles land at about 21.71, 21.59 and 21.88 mm, close to the 21.6 mm corner of the 24 × 36 mm frame. Pentax's 24.5°–8.2° is the full diagonal angle of view, a separate retail figure.

Wide open at those half-angles, the drawn rims pass about 56%, 52% and 47% of the nominal pupil area at 103, 160 and 290 mm.

Axial spherical aberration at 103 mm runs from about −0.30 mm at 0.7 of the pupil to +0.19 mm at the rim (−0.29 to +0.15 mm at 160 mm, under 0.03 mm at 290 mm), qualitatively matching the shape of Fig. 2A. Best axial focus at the short and middle settings therefore lies in front of the printed image plane, which lowers on-axis MTF there; the model keeps the printed image plane and applies no focus shift.
