## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP S50-138824 A  
**Application Number:** 49-46104  
**Filed:** 1974-04-24  
**Published:** 1975-11-06  
**Inventor:** Yasuo Takahashi  
**Applicant:** Asahi Kogaku Kogyo Co., Ltd.  
**Title:** 広角レンズ (Wide-angle lens)  
**Example details:** 実施例 on printed p. 116; Fig. 1 and Fig. 2 on p. 117. The patent's F = 100 is scaled ×0.35. US family member: US 3,963,325 A (filed 1975-04-21, granted 1976-06-15).

The prescription is the sole example of JP S50-138824 A, associated here with the **PENTAX SMC A 35mm f/2.8**. The patent names no product and the maker has not published a prescription, so this is a patent design linked to the lens by correlation, not a confirmed factory prescription. The evidence converges as follows:

1. **Construction.** The patent claims six separate elements in six groups: a negative meniscus convex to the object, two positive elements, a biconcave negative, a positive meniscus convex to the image and a positive element. The lens is listed as 6 elements in 6 groups.
2. **Speed and field.** Fig. 2 is plotted at 1:2.8 to a 32° half-field. The ×0.35 scale gives a paraxial focal length of 35.08 mm, and the maker lists a 63° angle of view.
3. **Maker's section.** The construction page of the SMC PENTAX-A lens manual (printed p. 36) draws the same six shapes in the same order, including the biconvex air space between L4 and L5 and the near contact of L5 and L6, with the diaphragm between L3 and L4.
4. **Date and lineage.** The application was filed in April 1974. Secondary references describe the A version as carrying over the 6-element, 6-group formula of the SMC Pentax-M 35mm f/2.8, introduced in 1977. The patent's timing fits the M version, so the M is the probable first production use of the design; this is an inference, not a maker statement.

The manual's lens list gives the A 35mm f/2.8 a smallest aperture of f/22, a 0.3 m minimum focus, a 49 mm filter, a 63 × 36.5 mm barrel and a weight of 170 g.

Two printing errors in the patent family are settled by computation. The description Table 1 of US 3,963,325 A prints r7 = 81.74 and r9 = 97.45 without minus signs, which would make L4 a meniscus and give an EFL of 27.0 at F = 100. The JP table, the US claim table, both texts and Fig. 1 all have the two radii negative, and the model uses them so. The relation printed in both documents as F1 = −100.46 = F/−0.922 has a misprinted digit: the prescription gives F1 = −108.43, and F/0.922 = 108.46.

## Optical Architecture

Six separate spherical singlets form a compact retrofocus wide-angle lens: a negative front meniscus, a long air space (d2 = 28.92 at F = 100), two positive elements, the diaphragm, a biconcave negative and two positive rear elements. There are no cemented interfaces or aspherical surfaces.

The patent's aim is a retrofocus lens made shorter in length rather than in diameter (p. 115). Its five conditions govern the front of the system, and the example meets all of them: |F1| = F/0.922 (limits F/1.2 to F/0.8), F1·2·3 = F/1.555 (F/1.8 to F/1.4), d2 = 0.289F (0.2F to 0.45F), d1 + … + d6 = 0.845F (0.7F to 0.9F) and (n2 + n3)/2 − n1 = 0.265 (0.2 to 0.32). A strongly negative L1 shortens the lens, and the high-index L2 and L3 restore positive power with moderate curvatures while keeping the Petzval sum from rising (p. 116).

At ×0.35 the computed EFL is 35.084 mm (100.24 at F = 100), the vertex track is 41.902 mm and the computed paraxial back focus is 37.471 mm, which places the image 79.373 mm behind the front vertex. The patent prints no back focus. R4 is printed as 14544.54 and is kept as a very weak curve rather than a plane.

The patent neither tabulates nor draws a stop. The maker's section places the diaphragm between L3 and L4, and the model puts it at the midpoint of d6 (8.74 at F = 100, 1.5295 + 1.5295 mm here); the exact station is inferred. A real-ray trace supports the region: with the stop at mid-gap the 32° sagittal and meridional field curves are +0.35 and −1.20 at F = 100, against Fig. 2 readings of about +0.3 and −1.05, and the meridional reading falls between the values for stops at 50 % and 75 % of the gap.

The patent prints no clear apertures. The semi-diameters are estimated from Fig. 1 at its own scale and checked against the real f/2.8 marginal ray, and they are inferred, not factory apertures. Fig. 1 draws half-heights of about 13.6, 9.6, 8.5, 7.2, 7.5 and 9.1 mm for L1–L6 at ×0.35; the model's rims sit up to 5 % inside them, with L4 at 7.1 mm and L5 at 7.3 mm set to pass the f/2.8 marginal ray. Fig. 1 is drawn to the JP spacings: every vertex lies within about 0.3 units (F = 100) of its tabulated position, including those set by the negative R7 and R9.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Glass: S-BSL7 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = −37.95 mm.

The front element is a crown meniscus convex to the object with a deep rear face. It carries the front group's negative power and creates the long back focus. The patent bounds its strength: shorter than F/1.2 the second surface becomes too steep and coma appears, and longer than F/0.8 the lens cannot be kept short within condition 3 (p. 116). The low index of L1 against the high-index L2 and L3 is the subject of condition 5.

### L2 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.80610, νd = 40.8. Glass: NBFD13 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +56.15 mm.

A high-index positive element with a convex front and a nearly flat rear face (R4 = 14544.54 at F = 100), so it is a meniscus only by the sign of R4. It sits behind the d2 air space that condition 3 controls.

### L3 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.75700, νd = 47.9. Glass: S-LAM54 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +26.52 mm.

The strongest positive element, just ahead of the diaphragm. With L1 and L2 it forms the front group whose focal length, F1·2·3 = 64.32 at F = 100, condition 2 bounds: shorter than F/1.8 the radii become short and coma rises, and longer than F/1.4 the d6 space must grow for color correction, against condition 4 (p. 116).

### L4 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: FD60 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = −18.07 mm.

The only dense flint, set just behind the diaphragm. It is the strongest element in the system and the chromatic partner of the positive lanthanum elements. Its rear face and L5's front face bound a biconvex air lens, 5.94 units thick on the axis at F = 100, that closes almost to contact at the rim, as both Fig. 1 and the maker's section draw it.

### L5 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.75700, νd = 47.9. Glass: S-LAM54 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +40.99 mm.

A positive meniscus concave to the object, in the same glass as L3. The patent describes it as a positive meniscus with its stronger face toward the image (p. 115), which holds only with the negative R9 of the JP table.

### L6 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.74320, νd = 49.4. Glass: S-LAM60 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +78.92 mm.

The weakest element, with a nearly flat front face and a convex rear face, 0.57 units behind L5 at F = 100. With L5 it supplies the positive power of the rear group.

## Glass Identification

The JP text defines n as the refractive index at the d-line (p. 116), and Fig. 2 plots the d and g lines. Every printed index equals a catalog helium-d (587.6 nm) value, while the sodium D line (589.3 nm) would read 0.00007 to 0.00027 lower, so the values are d-line data. The "sodium d-line" of the US text is a translation slip.

| Glass (catalog equivalent) | nd      | νd   | Elements | Role                    |
| -------------------------- | ------- | ---- | -------- | ----------------------- |
| S-BSL7 (OHARA)             | 1.51633 | 64.1 | L1       | Crown front meniscus    |
| NBFD13 (HOYA)              | 1.80610 | 40.8 | L2       | High-index positive     |
| S-LAM54 (OHARA)            | 1.75700 | 47.9 | L3, L5   | Lanthanum positives     |
| FD60 (HOYA)                | 1.80518 | 25.4 | L4       | Dense-flint negative    |
| S-LAM60 (OHARA)            | 1.74320 | 49.4 | L6       | Lanthanum rear positive |

The patent names no glass and the production supplier is not established; the labels are catalog equivalents at the printed coordinates. Several dense flints match L4. With FD60 the paraxial g-line focus falls 0.21 in front of the d-line focus at F = 100 (0.07 mm at this scale), where Fig. 2 reads about 0.20; SF6-type rows such as PBH6 (OHARA) give 0.24. Across the pupil the model's d-line curve follows Fig. 2 within about 0.04, but its g-line curve bends further toward overcorrection: it reaches +0.42 at 90 % of the pupil and +1.20 at full aperture, where the plot reads about +0.07 and +0.6. No catalog glass within the printed nd and νd moves the full-aperture value by more than 0.06, so the glass labels do not account for the difference.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent prints only the infinity state and describes no focusing movement. The model stays at infinity and no element moves. The 0.3 m minimum focus is the maker's figure and is not modeled.

A trace of the JP data at infinity reproduces Fig. 2 on the patent's F = 100 scale. Spherical aberration is +0.32 at full aperture and −0.39 near 70 % of the pupil, against plotted values of about +0.4 and −0.4, and distortion at 32° is −1.18 % against about −1.25 %.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The iris follows the paraxial f-number rule: at f/2.8 its radius is 6.98 mm for an entrance-pupil radius of 6.27 mm, and the real marginal ray through it corresponds to about f/2.88. No lens surface clips the axial beam. A real-ray f/2.8 cone, whose marginal ray reaches 7.20 mm at the stop plane, also passes every rim; the closest are surface 10 (7.23 mm against 7.3) and surface 7 (7.00 mm against 7.1).

The declared 63° field (31.5° half-field) lands at 21.24 mm. The 21.63 mm corner of the 24 × 36 mm frame is reached at 32.0°, the patent's plotted field edge. Geometric transmission at 31.5° is about 48 % of the axial value; it follows from the inferred rims and is not a factory figure.

Close focus, coatings and any production revision are not modeled.
