# PENTAX SMC FA J 75-300mm f/4.5-5.8 AL

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2003/0223122 A1  
**Title:** Zoom Lens System  
**Inventor:** Hiroyasu Ozaki  
**Applicant:** Pentax Corporation  
**Priority:** May 10, 2002, Japanese application 2002-135738  
**Filed:** May 8, 2003  
**Published:** December 4, 2003  
**Example details:** Table 3 (PDF pp. 15–16, printed pp. 5–6), Fig. 9 section, Figs. 10–12 aberrations and Fig. 17 group paths. The grant US 6,778,331 B2, issued August 17, 2004 on the same application (10/431,464), prints identical Tables 3 and 4 on PDF p. 15.

Embodiment 3 is identified with the PENTAX SMC FA J 75-300mm f/4.5-5.8 AL. The correlation is qualified: it rests on construction, specifications and timing, and does not show that the production lens uses every printed number. The model is infinity-only and applies no scaling or correction to the printed values.

1. Ricoh's FA J product page and film-camera lens page give 12 elements in 10 groups, with a **plastic aspherical tenth element**. They distinguish this FA J generation, which has no aperture ring and was developed alongside the film *ist, from the earlier F and FA 75–300mm designs. The black and silver versions differ only cosmetically.
2. The maker's film-lens table and the original MZ-S brochure give 75–300mm f/4.5–5.8, a 32–8.2° angle of view, 1.3 m minimum focus, 58 mm filter, a 69 × 116 mm barrel and 385 g. The later product page's 21.5–5.4° angle is not the film-format figure and is not used here.
3. Embodiments 3 and 4 both have 12 elements in 10 air-separated groups and a free-standing positive plastic L10 with aspherical rear surface S18; they differ only by an exchange of glasses between L7 and L8. Embodiment 1 has 11 elements with an aspherical glass element, and Embodiment 2 has 11 elements plus a resin aspherical layer bonded to glass (¶0120) rather than a free-standing plastic tenth lens.
4. The May 2002 priority date precedes the lens's February 2003 release with the film *ist.

These facts fit Embodiments 3 and 4 equally well. Embodiment 3 is used because Embodiment 4's printed middle and tele back focus (72.32 and 86.58 mm) repeats Table 2, while its own prescription focuses at 71.78 and 86.15 mm, a printing error that the grant repeats. Embodiment 3's printed back focus agrees with its prescription to within 0.018 mm at all three positions (+0.0051/−0.0175/+0.0038 mm); the model keeps the printed image planes.

## Optical Architecture

The design is a positive–negative–positive telephoto zoom. Twelve elements form ten air-separated groups; cemented interfaces S8 and S15 join L4/L5 and L8/L9. The third zoom group contains a positive forward subgroup and a negative rear subgroup separated by 20.50 mm. The maker's ten groups are these air-separated components, not the three zoom groups. The patent numbers the zoom groups 10, 20 and 30 and the subgroups 31 and 32, and writes the rear subgroup's focal length as f3b; this page calls them G1, G2, G3, G3a and G3b. The diagram brackets only G1–G3: Table 3 prints one d18 of 20.50 mm for all three positions, so in this embodiment the subgroups do not move apart (the patent allows either case, ¶0016 and ¶0056).

| Group | Surfaces | Elements/components | Role and calculated isolated-group focal length |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1, positive | 1–6 | 3/3 | Front collector; whole-group focus (¶0057); +122.723 mm |
| G2, negative | 7–11 | 3/2 | Zoom variator; −30.962 mm |
| G3, positive | 12–22 | 6/5 | Rear imaging group; +37.058 mm |
| G3a, positive | 12–18A | 4/3 | Aspheric forward subgroup; +43.048 mm |
| G3b, negative | 19–22 | 2/2 | Rear telephoto subgroup; −304.402 mm |

The front surface of each group sits at these positions relative to the fixed image plane:

| Zoom | G1 mm | G2 mm | G3 mm |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Wide | −159.41 | −142.20 | −102.80 |
| Middle | −194.10 | −137.95 | −117.02 |
| Tele | −205.99 | −143.64 | −131.39 |

G2 first moves imageward and then objectward (Fig. 17). Every group is closest to the object at the tele end, as claim 1 requires, but G2's motion is not monotonic.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Glass: S-BSL7 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = +161.594 mm (isolated thick element in air).

First positive collector in G1. A low-index crown with a shallow rear surface, it supplies much of the front group's positive power.

### L2 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: S-TIH6 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = −175.740 mm (isolated thick element in air).

Negative meniscus in G1. Its dense flint balances the color of the two positive crowns; the patent gives no per-element correction budget.

### L3 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Glass: S-BSL7 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = +129.314 mm (isolated thick element in air).

Final positive G1 element. All three front elements move together for focusing (¶0057); the model is infinity-only and does not include that travel.

### L4 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.73400, νd = 51.5. Glass: S-LAL59 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = −24.639 mm (isolated thick element in air).

Negative member of the G2 cemented pair. It shares S8 with L5; the listed isolated-in-air focal length is not the power of the cemented assembly.

### L5 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: S-TIH6 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = +33.800 mm (isolated thick element in air).

Positive dense-flint member of the G2 pair. Its high dispersion contrasts with L4 and the following negative singlet.

### L6 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.80610, νd = 40.9. Glass: S-LAH53 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = −52.140 mm (isolated thick element in air).

Negative G2 singlet. Its rim is square at 11.99 mm, close to the 11.96 mm that Fig. 9 draws and just enough to pass the printed f/6.2 axial beam at the tele end (see Model Scope and Limitations).

### L7 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Glass: S-BSL7 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = +56.901 mm (isolated thick element in air).

Positive forward element of G3a, just behind the stop. It is spherical; Embodiment 1 instead puts its asphere on the front of the corresponding glass element (S12), whereas here the asphere is on L10.

### L8 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.2. Glass: S-FSL5 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = +45.454 mm (isolated thick element in air).

Low-dispersion positive element of the G3a cemented pair. It shares S15 with L9 and forms part of the strong positive rear-group core.

### L9 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: S-TIH6 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = −48.015 mm (isolated thick element in air).

Negative dense-flint partner in G3a. The pair precedes the free-standing plastic aspherical element; no resin layer is bonded to it.

### L10 — Positive Meniscus (1× Asph)

nd = 1.52538, νd = 56.3. Glass: Unmatched (optical plastic; nd 1.52538, νd 56.3). f = +666.670 mm (isolated thick element in air).

Positive free-standing plastic element, with asphere S18A on its image side. ¶0123–0124 and the maker's description of the tenth element both identify it as a plastic asphere; the polymer is not named. Its weak power satisfies condition (4), fw/fp = 0.116 (Table 5); ¶0071 notes that a stronger plastic element would shift focus too much with temperature.

### L11 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.64769, νd = 33.8. Glass: S-TIM22 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = +35.651 mm (isolated thick element in air).

Positive singlet beginning G3b after the 20.50 mm air interval. Together with L12 it makes the weak negative rear subgroup.

### L12 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.80400, νd = 46.6. Glass: S-LAH65 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = −27.182 mm (isolated thick element in air).

Negative final element in G3b. The printed back focus is measured from S22; no cover glass or filter plate is listed.

## Glass Identification

The patent gives only d-line indices and Abbe numbers and names no supplier. Each glass coordinate matches an Ohara catalog glass, used here as a catalog equivalent; the actual supplier is unconfirmed. L10 is an optical plastic with no catalog match.

| nd | νd | Catalog equivalent | Elements | Role |
|---:|---:|---|---|---|
| 1.51633 | 64.1 | S-BSL7 | L1, L3, L7 | Positive crowns |
| 1.80518 | 25.4 | S-TIH6 | L2, L5, L9 | Dense-flint achromatizing partners |
| 1.73400 | 51.5 | S-LAL59 | L4 | Negative lanthanum crown of the G2 doublet |
| 1.80610 | 40.9 | S-LAH53 | L6 | High-index negative G2 singlet |
| 1.48749 | 70.2 | S-FSL5 | L8 | Low-dispersion crown of the G3a doublet |
| 1.52538 | 56.3 | Unmatched plastic | L10 | Plastic aspherical element |
| 1.64769 | 33.8 | S-TIM22 | L11 | Positive flint of G3b |
| 1.80400 | 46.6 | S-LAH65 | L12 | High-index rear negative |

The prescription has no extra-low-dispersion glass: S-FSL5's partial dispersion lies close to the normal line (ΔPgF ≈ +0.004), so the color correction is a conventional achromat's. C/d/F and photopic MTF use catalog dispersion for eleven elements and estimate L10's dispersion from nd and νd.

## Focus Mechanism

¶0057 focuses by moving the positive first group G1 toward the object. The patent prints no near-focus spacings, so both focus positions in this model repeat the infinity prescription, and the production lens's 1.3 m minimum focus is not modeled. A paraxial estimate puts the G1 travel to 1.3 m at about 15.0, 15.5 and 15.7 mm at the wide, middle and tele positions; it is not used in the model.

## Aspherical Surface

S18A is the image-side face of the plastic L10. The patent's equation (¶0073) is

`z = c h² / (1 + sqrt(1 − (1+K)c²h²)) + A4 h⁴ + A6 h⁶ + A8 h⁸ + A10 h¹⁰`, with `c = 1/R`.

| Surface | R mm | K | A4 mm^-3 | A6 mm^-5 | A8 mm^-7 | A10 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 18A | 62.570 | 0 | +7.0890e−6 | −1.8612e−9 | +1.4230e−11 | 0 |

K = 0 is a spherical base, not a paraboloid, and the coefficients are as printed. L10 is a solid plastic element, not a thin resin layer on glass. The aspherical terms change only marginal refraction, not the paraxial power; condition (3) ties the surface to spherical-aberration correction in G3 (¶0065–0067, ΔIasp = −36.923 in Table 5).

## Zoom Positions, Stop and Clear Apertures

The patent prints three zoom rows, all at infinity focus:

| Position | Printed f mm | Calculated EFL mm | Printed f-number | Printed W | Printed fB mm | Paraxial focus minus printed plane mm |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Wide | 77.25 | 77.244106 | 4.6 | 16.0° | 58.97 | +0.005059 |
| Middle | 200.01 | 199.952336 | 5.4 | 6.0° | 73.19 | −0.017472 |
| Tele | 289.96 | 289.934996 | 6.2 | 4.2° | 87.56 | +0.003777 |

The table states no object distance; the back-focus convention, the focusing description and the first-order agreement identify the rows as infinity focus. The printed image planes are kept.

The model has 65 zoom positions, of which positions 0, 32 and 64 are the printed rows. Between them D11 and the back focus are linear and D6 is calculated so that the image stays on the interpolated plane; interpolating all three gaps linearly would defocus intermediate positions by up to 7.70 mm. Intermediate focal lengths are calculated EFLs, not the production cam.

The diaphragm S in Fig. 9 is unnumbered and lies in D11 just before S12. The model places it 0.6 mm before S12; two independent readings of the figure give about 0.5 and 0.58 mm, and the drawn line's thickness allows roughly 0.2–1.0 mm. The stop is assumed to move with G3, since the patent gives no separate stop spacing, and the 0.6 mm is taken out of D11 so the printed track is unchanged. The iris radius follows the printed f-number.

The patent prints no clear apertures. The rims are inferred from Fig. 9 proportions and checked against the axial and field beams; the 58 mm filter and 69 mm barrel bound the front rims. L6 is square at 11.99 mm, against the 11.96 mm that Fig. 9 draws; that is the least rim that passes the printed f/6.2 axial beam at the tele end, where about 98.8% of the axial entrance area reaches the image. Wide and middle reach f/4.6 and f/5.4. At that height L6's front face comes close to L5's rear face across their 2.77 mm air gap without touching it, so the model allows 0.96 of the gap for rim sag instead of the default 0.90. No radius, thickness, index, f-number or image plane was changed.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The model reproduces the printed first-order data: EFL 77.244/199.952/289.935 mm against the printed 77.25/200.01/289.96 mm. At the patent's half-angles of 16.0°, 6.0° and 4.2° the chief ray reaches about 21.6–21.8 mm image height, the corner of the 135 format. Only the three printed rows are source data; the clear apertures, the stop position, the 62 intermediate zoom positions and the glass identities are inferred, and the model is infinity-only.

Beyond the tele aperture limit above, edge contrast at the calculated quarter-zoom position is low at full aperture. Stopping down to f/8 raises it mainly in the sagittal direction, and axial refocusing does not recover it. The paraxial focus at every zoom position stays within 0.018 mm of the image plane.
