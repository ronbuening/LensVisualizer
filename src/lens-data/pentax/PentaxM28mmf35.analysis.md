## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 3,955,883  
**Filed:** 1975-02-06  
**Granted:** 1976-05-11  
**Priority:** 1974-03-07 (JP 49-26594)  
**Inventor:** Takahiro Sugiyama  
**Assignee:** Asahi Kogaku Kogyo Co., Ltd.  
**Title:** Wide angle photographic lens  
**Example details:** Second example, Table II, repeated in claim 2; section in Fig. 3, aberration plots in Figs. 4a–4d. The patent is normalized to F = 100; all lengths are multiplied by exactly 0.28 for f = 28 mm.

This is a patent design associated with the SMC Pentax-M 28mm f/3.5, introduced in 1977, not a confirmed factory prescription. The association rests on five points:

1. The patent's six single lenses in six air-spaced components match the retail construction of six elements in six groups. The earlier Asahi 28mm f/3.5 lenses differ: the Super-Takumar (US 3,506,339) and SMC Takumar are seven elements in seven groups, and the K-mount SMC Pentax 28mm f/3.5 that the M lens replaced is listed with eight elements in seven groups and a 52 mm filter.
2. At the 0.28 scale the example is a 28 mm f/3.5. Its text gives a 75° field and its plots run to a 37.6° half-field, the angle of view of a 28 mm lens on 35 mm film.
3. The patent's stated aim is a compact retrofocus lens with a back focus 1.3 times its focal length, the brief of the compact M series.
4. The March 1974 priority date and the May 1976 grant fall shortly before the lens's introduction.
5. The Photons to Photos Optical Bench labels this example as the smc Pentax-M 28mm F3.5.

The retail 49 mm filter, f/3.5 to f/22 range, five-blade diaphragm and 0.3 m minimum focus fit a lens of this kind, but none of them fixes the radii, clear apertures or iris position. No Pentax construction drawing of the lens was found, so the element shapes and the stop position cannot be checked against the production lens; the match is probable rather than confirmed.

Example 2 is the one transcribed. Example 1 (Table I, Fig. 1) is the same six-element layout at f/2.8 and does not fit the f/3.5 lens. The catalog's SMC Pentax-M 28mm f/2.8 is a seven-element design from a different patent.

## Optical Architecture

Six spherical singlets form a retrofocus lens. L1 (weak positive meniscus) and L2 (strong negative meniscus), both convex to the object, make up a negative front pair. Behind a long air space (d4 = 12.81 mm) come the thick biconvex L3, the stop, the biconcave L4 and two positive menisci, L5 and L6, concave to the object. The power sequence is P-N, then P-N-P-P. There are no cemented surfaces, aspheric surfaces or cover plates.

The front pair has a composite focal length of −28.48 mm (−101.729 at F = 100). It pushes the back focus to 36.432 mm, 1.30 times the focal length, which leaves room for the reflex mirror. The lens is 42.143 mm from S1 to S12 and the image plane lies 78.574 mm behind S1.

The patent's three conditions set the layout, and Example 2 meets all of them:

| Condition | Range at F = 100 | Example 2 |
|---|---|---|
| (1) ν1, ν2 | above 50 | 57.0, 60.3 |
| (2) \|F1,2\|, F1,2 < 0 | 76.9 to 133.3 | 101.729 |
| (3) \|r5/r6\| | 0.1 to 1.5 | 0.832 |

The patent explains each. Condition (1) keeps the front pair's dispersion low, because lateral color grows with field angle and differs between the marginal and central light passing L1 and L2; at ν ≤ 50 the rear lenses would need higher indices and greater thicknesses or spacings to correct it. Condition (2) sets the back focus: a weaker front pair makes the lens too large for the back focus required, a stronger one overloads the negative lens and shortens the focal length of the converging system behind it. Condition (3) keeps the lens compact by correcting the sine condition (upper limit) and the negative distortion of the diverging front pair (lower limit). The patent singles out the correction of lateral chromatic aberration.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.62280, νd = 57.0. Glass: S-BSM10 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +130.6 mm.

The weakest element, convex to the object, with an almost flat rear face (R2 = 1111.7 mm). It is the largest element, at 17.42 mm semi-diameter, and one of the two front lenses whose Abbe number condition (1) holds above 50.

### L2 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.62041, νd = 60.3. Glass: S-BSM16 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = −22.30 mm.

The strongest negative element and the source of the front pair's negative power. Its deep rear surface (R4 = 10.97 mm) faces the long air space ahead of L3. Both claims call the second lens a positive meniscus, but the description of both examples and the prescription itself make it negative; the model follows the prescription.

### L3 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.72000, νd = 43.7. Glass: S-LAM52 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +19.55 mm.

The thickest element, 11.52 mm (41.13 at F = 100), and the strongest positive one, directly ahead of the stop. Its front face is the more strongly curved (R5 = 23.37 mm, R6 = −28.10 mm); condition (3) bounds that ratio to control the sine condition and the distortion of the front pair.

### L4 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.74077, νd = 27.8. Glass: S-TIH13 (OHARA catalog equivalent), a dense flint. f = −15.86 mm.

The only negative element behind the stop and the most dispersive glass in the lens. In the usual reading of this layout it balances the color and spherical aberration of the positive elements around it; the patent does not assign it a role.

### L5 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.62280, νd = 57.0. Glass: S-BSM10 (OHARA catalog equivalent), as in L1. f = +32.53 mm.

Concave to the object, with its stronger curve toward the image. It is the stronger of the two rear menisci.

### L6 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.62041, νd = 60.3. Glass: S-BSM16 (OHARA catalog equivalent), as in L2. f = +43.85 mm.

Concave to the object, with an almost flat front face (R11 = −591.5 mm). With L5 it carries the final convergence to the image, and its rims, with those of L4 and L5, trim the oblique beam toward the edge of the field.

## Glass Identification

The patent gives refractive indices and Abbe numbers without naming the line; they are read as d-line values, the reference line of its color plots. The table gives catalog equivalents by index and Abbe number; the supplier of the production glass is not established.

| Element | Patent nd / νd | Catalog equivalent | Catalog nd / νd | Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1, L5 | 1.62280 / 57.0 | S-BSM10 (OHARA) | 1.62280 / 57.05 | Dense barium crown |
| L2, L6 | 1.62041 / 60.3 | S-BSM16 (OHARA) | 1.62041 / 60.29 | Dense barium crown |
| L3 | 1.72000 / 43.7 | S-LAM52 (OHARA) | 1.72000 / 43.69 | Lanthanum flint |
| L4 | 1.74077 / 27.8 | S-TIH13 (OHARA) | 1.74077 / 27.79 | Dense flint |

All four match to the patent's printed precision, so every element traces on a catalog dispersion curve. The patent prints no partial dispersions. The palette is conventional: two dense barium crowns, each used twice, satisfy condition (1) in the front pair and supply the rear menisci; a lanthanum flint carries the main positive power, and the dense flint of L4 behind the stop is the only strongly dispersive glass. There is no special-dispersion glass.

## Focus Mechanism and Image Plane

The patent names no focusing group or movement and prints the infinity state only, so no gap varies and the focus control stays at infinity. The 0.3 m minimum focus is the retail specification and is not traced.

The final gap of 36.432 mm is the printed back focus of 130.113 at F = 100. The computed paraxial back focus is 36.429 mm (130.10 at F = 100); the 0.003 mm difference is rounding in the printed table, and the model keeps the printed plane.

## Aperture Stop and Clear Apertures

The patent neither prints nor draws a stop, and Fig. 3 shows none. The model places it at the rear vertex of L3, 32.833 mm behind S1 and 2.957 mm ahead of L4, as in the Photons to Photos transcription of this example; this is a modeling choice. At f/3.5 the entrance pupil radius is 4.00 mm and the stop radius 5.33 mm.

The patent prints no semi-diameters, so all are inferred. Fig. 3, read at the scale of its vertex spacing (29.5 px/mm on the native scan, at the model's 0.28 scale), gives:

| Element | Model sd (mm) | Fig. 3 reading (mm) | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | 17.42 / 15.70 | 16.9 / 15.6 | Photons to Photos diameter for S1; S2 at the drawn rear chamfer |
| L2 | 14.25 / 9.46 | 13.8 / 9.7 | Photons to Photos diameters, within about 3 % of the figure |
| L3 | 7.30 / 7.30 | 6.5 / 6.5 | Drawn square-cut; held above the figure by the oblique beam |
| L4 | 5.70 / 5.70 | 5.6 / 5.6 | Drawn square-cut |
| L5 | 6.20 / 6.20 | 6.0 / 6.0 | Drawn square-cut |
| L6 | 6.81 / 7.16 | 6.7 / 6.9 | Photons to Photos diameters, within about 4 % of the figure |

The figure is a patent schematic, so these readings are approximate; the drawn sags of L4's faces agree with the prescription at the same scale. L1's rear face, L4 and L5 follow the figure to within 0.1–0.2 mm. L3 is about 0.8 mm taller than drawn: at the drawn height it would remove up to 11 points of transmission between 20° and 37.6°, most at 34.7°, so the model keeps the taller rim. The axial f/3.5 beam needs no more than 5.8 mm at any of these surfaces. At the 37.6° edge about 60 % of the f/3.5 beam passes.

## Model Scope and Limitations

| Quantity | Patent, F = 100 | Patent × 0.28 | Model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Focal length | 100 | 28.0 | 27.995 |
| Back focus | 130.113 | 36.432 | 36.429 paraxial |
| S1 to S12 | 150.51 | 42.143 | 42.143 |
| F1,2 | −101.729 | −28.48 | −28.49 |

- The prescription is the patent's, not a confirmed factory one, and no Pentax drawing was found to compare it with.
- Stop position and all semi-diameters are inferred; L3 is drawn taller than Fig. 3 shows it.
- The image plane is the printed back focus.
- The model is static at infinity and says nothing about performance at 0.3 m.
- The 37.6° edge reaches 21.32 mm of image height, 0.33 mm short of the format corner; rays beyond 37.6° extend past the patent's stated field.
- The patent gives d-line indices and Abbe numbers only, so behavior away from the d-line rests on the catalog equivalents.
