# PENTAX SMC ZOOM 85-210mm f/4.5 — Audit Log

## Source

Yasuo Takahashi, *Variable Focal Length Lens System Suitable for 35 mm. Cameras*, US 3,506,337, assigned to Asahi Kogaku Kogyo Co., Ltd. Japanese priority September 14, 1966, US filing September 6, 1967, granted April 14, 1970. Table 1 in column 4 (PDF p. 5) gives the sole example, repeated in Claim 1 on the next page. Figures 1(a–c) show the sections, Figures 2(a–c) the aberrations, and Tables 2 and 3 the Seidel coefficients at the zoom endpoints. [US 3,506,337](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3506337A/en)

Asahi’s 1976–77 catalog illustrates the K-mount 85–210 mm f/4.5 on printed p. 25. The catalog and the SMC lens manual both list f/32 for that lens. The ES manual gives the earlier screw-mount Takumar’s f/22 limit and illustrates both ends of its zoom. [Asahi lens catalog](https://hirokun.jp/pentax_lens_accessory_catalog_1976-77.pdf) · [SMC Pentax manual, p. 38](https://cameramanuals.org/lenses/pentax_smc_lenses.pdf) · [Pentax ES manual, p. 37](https://www.cameramanuals.org/pentax_pdf/pentax_es.pdf)

## Transcription Check

The page images of Table 1 and Claim 1 agree with the model on all 21 signed spherical radii, 20 axial intervals and eleven d-line index and Abbe-number pairs. All lengths are in millimetres. The common surface of L5 and L6 is R10. There are no aspheres or plates.

Table 1 gives ranges for d6, d11 and d13. With the front and rear groups stationary, the three gaps sum to 70.600 mm. Their wide-end values are 2.00, 68.50 and 0.10 mm. At tele, the printed 64.78 and 5.72 mm leave 0.10 mm for d13. Its 7.41 mm maximum occurs between the endpoints. Apart from the four entries in Table 2 listed below, the calculated Seidel coefficients agree with the two printed tables to about 0.001.

The text places the diaphragm between L9 and L10. In that 15.00 mm air space, a stop 8.00 mm behind R17 and 7.00 mm ahead of R18 reproduces the coma coefficients to about 0.001. The calculated paraxial pupil at f/4.5 gives an iris radius of about 13.10 mm.

The two endpoint back focal distances bracket the fixed image distance of 111.740 mm. At the 22 intermediate positions, the three gaps satisfy the constant-sum condition and the specified focal length. Exact paraxial focus near 139 mm would require d13 to open to 7.436 mm. Where it reaches the printed 7.41 mm limit, d6 sets the focal length and a small focus error remains.

Measured as image distance minus paraxial back focal distance, the endpoint errors are +0.0014 mm at wide and −0.0014 mm at tele. At calculated positions below the gap limit, the magnitude is under 0.0001 mm. At the limit it is −0.0050 mm at 135.71 mm, −0.0257 mm at 138.52 mm and −0.0012 mm at 142.28 mm. Between positions the greatest absolute errors are 0.0191 mm below the limit and 0.0260 mm at the limit near 138.9 mm.

## Product Identification

The sole example is a probable match for the K-mount SMC Pentax Zoom 85–210 mm f/4.5, sold from 1975 to 1977. Figure 1(b) matches the catalog’s long-end section on p. 25 in element shape, the cemented pair in the moving group and the two broad air spaces in the rear triplet. Asahi specifies eleven elements in ten groups, a 29°–11° field and a 58 mm filter. Pentax Forums and Cinelens Wiki record six blades for the automatic diaphragm. The patent gives 85–205 mm at f/4.5 and its plots span 29.6°–11.8°. The catalog’s separate 85–210 mm f/3.5 has twelve elements in eleven groups. [Asahi lens catalog](https://hirokun.jp/pentax_lens_accessory_catalog_1976-77.pdf) · [Pentax Forums K-mount record](https://www.pentaxforums.com/lensreviews/SMC-Pentax-K-85-210mm-F4.5-Zoom-Lens.html) · [Cinelens Wiki, K-mount record](https://cinelenswiki.com/lens:pensmc85-210mmf4.5)

The K version and the 1971–75 M42 S-M-C Takumar share their optical design. Asahi’s two Takumar sections show the same front triplet, moving components and rear triplet. Both versions use a single zoom and focus ring. The K lens reaches 3.5 m on its ordinary scale and 1.9 m with the supplied close-up lens. The model covers infinity focus only. ## Corrections, 2026-11-25

- The f/8 aperture and 3° half-field limits gave way to the f/4.5 aperture of Figures 2(a–c) and the full 35 mm frame. Clear radii from the lower edges in Figure 1 replace the narrow estimates. All follow the figure and pass the axial beam. Moving the stop from the midpoint of the R17–R18 gap to the 8.00/7.00 mm division accounts for the printed coma coefficients.
- Table 1 retains its millimetre scale and its 205 mm long-end designation. The duplicate d2 = 1.50 mm line above R1 is omitted because Claim 1 starts with d1 and gives d2 only once. The 210 mm marking belongs to the production barrel.
- The patent describes Figure 1(b) as intermediate and 1(c) as tele. Their drawn d13 gaps put 1(b) at tele (about 0.1 mm) and 1(c) near mid-zoom (about 7.2 mm). The endpoint spacings follow Table 1.
- Four Table 2 coefficients differ from the values calculated from the prescription. The Petzval terms at R3 and R20 are +0.320 and +0.032 (printed +0.310 and +0.331). The R9 astigmatism term is +0.054 (printed −0.054) and its column sums to +0.067 (printed +0.670). These are errors in the derived coefficients. The radii, thicknesses and glass pairs stand as printed.
- The two zoom endpoints come from the printed limits. The original 127 intermediate positions are represented by 22 calculated positions that preserve the 70.600 mm total gap and keep infinity focus within 0.026 mm of the fixed image. The patent gives no finite-focus row. The four group labels follow Figure 1, with C1 marking the cemented L5/L6 doublet.
- The product metadata name the probable production lens and distinguish its 85–210 mm range from the 85–205 mm example. The shared optics appear in Pentax K and M42 for 35 mm film. The six blades and the f/4.5, 5.6, 8, 11, 16, 22 and 32 aperture scale describe the K version. Asahi identifies f/5.6 by a white dot between 4.5 and 8. The M42 diaphragm stops at f/22. 2026-11-25 rim review:

- L10 rear face, surface 19: clear radius 13.4 → 12.0 mm, with a flat land out to the unchanged 13.4 mm edge. All three panels of Figure 1 end the concave rear face short of the edge and join it to the rim with a short slanted stroke. Measured at 8.21 px per mm, the face ends at 0.86–0.89 of the edge radius, about 11.7 mm. The clear radius sits 0.3 mm outside that reading. This is the smallest value at which no field at any of the 24 zoom positions loses more than 3 points of illumination. Corner illumination at infinity falls from 55.3 to 54.4 % at 85 mm and from 52.4 to 51.4 % at 205 mm. The largest loss is 2.5 points at 9.3° at 111.3 mm. The f/4.5 axial beam reaches 11.46 mm on this face and passes at every zoom position.
- The other twenty rims were compared again with Figures 1(a–c) and none changed. Each lies within about 3 % of the drawn edge. The square-cut elements keep one radius on both faces, L5 and L6 share one edge, and L7 stays smaller than L8 as drawn.

## Remaining Questions

- The factory radii, spacings, glass suppliers and intermediate cam positions remain undocumented. The catalog drawing cannot resolve the difference between the 205 mm design and the 210 mm barrel marking.
- The clear radii are figure measurements, and the drawn bevel on L10 is represented by a flat land. The precise iris position comes from the Seidel coefficients. The production rim dimensions, iris offset and blade curvature remain unknown.
- The patent supplies no finite-focus spacings or reproduction ratio. The cited K-mount specifications give no maximum magnification or internal group travel at 3.5 m.
