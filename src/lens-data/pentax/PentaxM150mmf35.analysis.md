## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 4,134,645 A  
**Filed:** 1977-08-25  
**Granted:** 1979-01-16  
**Priority:** 1976-08-26 (JP 51-101957)  
**Inventors:** Takahiro Sugiyama; Toshifumi Kubota; Rensuke Adachi  
**Assignee:** Asahi Kogaku Kogyo Co., Ltd. (printed as Asahi Kogaku Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha)  
**Title:** Miniaturized telephoto camera lens  
**Example details:** Example 3, repeated in claim 3; section in Fig. 5, aberration plots in Fig. 6. The patent is normalized to F = 100; every printed length is multiplied by exactly 1.5 for f = 150 mm.

This is a patent design associated with the SMC Pentax-M 150mm f/3.5, not a confirmed factory prescription. The lens was introduced in 1977 for the Pentax K mount and made until 1986. Reference listings give five elements in five groups, a 1.8 m minimum focus, an f/3.5 to f/32 aperture range, a six-blade diaphragm, a 49 mm filter thread and a length of 75 mm. The patent names no product, and no Pentax construction diagram was found to compare with Fig. 5. The association is probable and rests on four points:

1. The patent's five single lenses in five air-spaced components match the retail construction of five elements in five groups.
2. At the 1.5 scale the example is a 150 mm f/3.5 lens. Its printed half-field of 8.2° is the half-angle of a 150 mm lens on the 43.3 mm diagonal of 35 mm film.
3. The patent's other two examples fit the rest of the series. Example 1 (f/2.8, ±10.2°) corresponds to a 120 mm lens and Example 2 (f/3.5, ±9.0°) to a 135 mm lens; the M series had a 120mm f/2.8 and a 135mm f/3.5. The PhotonsToPhotos Optical Bench lists all three lenses.
4. The August 1976 priority date precedes the 1977 introduction. At this scale the front vertex lies 73.5 mm ahead of the K-mount flange, close to the 75 mm length of the lens, and the 43 mm front clear diameter fits inside the 49 mm filter thread.

These points fix the type, scale and period of the design but not its radii, clear apertures or iris position. Examples 2 and 3 are separate designs: every radius differs, as do several glasses and spacings.

## Optical Architecture

The power sequence is positive, negative, positive, positive, negative. Three closely spaced front elements form a positive group with a combined focal length of 93.9 mm (62.60 at F = 100). A long air space, d6, separates them from a compact rear pair: a positive meniscus and a negative meniscus, both with the stronger curve toward the object. All five elements are air-spaced and every surface is spherical; there are no cemented surfaces or cover plates.

The patent's aim is a high "telescopic ratio", a short total length plus back focus for the focal length. Here the front vertex lies 119.01 mm ahead of the image, 0.793 times the focal length. Seven conditions set the layout, and Example 3 meets all of them:

| Condition | Range at F = 100 | Example 3 |
|---|---|---|
| (1) F1,2,3 | F/1.8 to F/1.2 (55.6 to 83.3) | F/1.597 = 62.60 |
| (2) F1,2,3,4 | F/2.5 to F/1.85 (40.0 to 54.1) | F/2.279 = 43.89 |
| (3) d6 | 22 to 32 | 23.99 |
| (4) ν2 | below 28 | 25.4 |
| (5) ν1, ν3 | above 58 | 60.5, 64.1 |
| (6) n5 | above 1.62 | 1.65160 |
| (7) d8 | 1.5 to 6.5 | 2.72 |

The patent explains the trade-offs. A weaker front group (condition (1)) loads L4 and lengthens the lens; a stronger one makes the field hard to cover. A stronger front-plus-L4 combination (condition (2)) or a longer d6 (condition (3)) shortens the lens but lowers the Petzval sum and upsets the aberration balance, so the glass conditions (4) to (6) exist to hold the Petzval sum up while the front group is achromatized. The ninth surface, the concave front of L5, carries negative astigmatism and spherical aberration, which the patent balances with the astigmatism of the seventh surface.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.51112, νd = 60.5. Glass: NSL7 (OHARA) equivalent, a crown. f = +107.40 mm.

The largest element, nearly plano-convex: its rear radius (642.8 mm) is more than twelve times the front one. It takes the first share of the front group's positive power, and its Abbe number satisfies condition (5).

### L2 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: S-TIH6 (OHARA) equivalent, a dense flint. f = −121.61 mm.

The only negative element of the front group, a thin biconcave lens between the two crowns. Condition (4) requires its Abbe number to stay below 28. The patent obtains achromatism mainly in the first three lenses and calls that correction "rather excessive", which leaves the correction of lateral color to L4.

### L3 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Glass: S-BSL7 (OHARA) equivalent, a borosilicate crown. f = +104.11 mm.

Convex to the object, with the stronger curvature on that side as the claims require. It sits 0.105 mm behind L2 on the axis and completes the front group. Its Abbe number also satisfies condition (5).

### L4 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.72825, νd = 28.5. Glass: S-TIH10 (OHARA) equivalent, a dense flint. f = +110.08 mm.

The first element behind the long air space, convex to the object. Because L4 and L5 sit close together (condition (7)), the patent calls for a low Abbe number in L4 to correct lateral color, and a flint or dense flint of high index to limit the fall in the Petzval sum. With the front group it gives the combined focal length of condition (2), 65.8 mm here.

### L5 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.65160, νd = 58.6. Glass: S-LAL7 (OHARA) equivalent, a lanthanum crown. f = −48.14 mm.

The strongest element and the telephoto negative, concave to the object and 2.72 mm (4.08 mm here) behind L4. The patent says a high Abbe number here prevents a decrease in the Petzval sum once the front group is achromatized, and prefers an index above 1.62 (condition (6)) for the same reason: a higher index weakens this negative element's contribution to the Petzval sum.

## Glass Identification

The patent prints an index and Abbe number for each lens; its aberration plots are for the d- and g-lines, and every pair matches a d-line catalog glass. The table gives OHARA equivalents, which the model traces on; the supplier of the production glass is not established.

| Element | Patent nd / νd | Catalog equivalent | Catalog nd / νd | Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | 1.51112 / 60.5 | NSL7 (OHARA) | 1.51112 / 60.49 | Crown |
| L2 | 1.80518 / 25.4 | S-TIH6 (OHARA) | 1.80518 / 25.43 | Dense flint |
| L3 | 1.51633 / 64.1 | S-BSL7 (OHARA) | 1.51633 / 64.14 | Borosilicate crown |
| L4 | 1.72825 / 28.5 | S-TIH10 (OHARA) | 1.72825 / 28.46 | Dense flint |
| L5 | 1.65160 / 58.6 | S-LAL7 (OHARA) | 1.65160 / 58.55 | Lanthanum crown |

The palette is conventional for its period: crowns around a dense flint in the front group, then a dense flint and a lanthanum crown in the rear pair. The patent prints no partial dispersions and claims no secondary-spectrum correction.

## Focus Mechanism and Image Plane

The patent names no focusing group or movement and prints the infinity state only, so no gap varies and the focus control stays at infinity. The 1.8 m minimum focus is the retail specification and is not traced.

The final gap of 60.6 mm is the printed back focus of 40.40 at F = 100. The computed paraxial back focus is 60.569 mm (40.38 at F = 100); the 0.031 mm difference is rounding in the printed table, and the model keeps the printed plane.

## Aperture Stop and Clear Apertures

The patent neither prints nor draws a stop, and Fig. 5 shows none. The model places it in the long air space d6, 6.0 mm (4.0 at F = 100) behind S6, a modeling choice. At f/3.5 the entrance pupil radius is 21.43 mm and the stop radius 17.32 mm.

The patent prints no semi-diameters, so all are inferred, with both faces of each element sharing one rim:

| Element | Model sd (mm) | Fig. 5 reading (mm) | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | 21.5 | 20.9 | Just above Fig. 5, at the f/3.5 marginal ray (21.43 mm at S1); 43 mm diameter, inside the 49 mm filter thread |
| L2 | 21.2 | 20.8 | Just above Fig. 5, at the f/3.5 marginal ray (21.13 mm at S3); drawn square-cut |
| L3 | 21.2 | 20.8 | Level with L2, as Fig. 5 draws it; the f/3.5 marginal ray is 20.55 mm high at S5 |
| L4, L5 | 11.25 | 10.7, 10.7 | Fig. 5, about half the front height |

Fig. 5 is drawn to scale along the axis, and its surface curves give the same scale across it. The front three elements sit within 3 % of its heights and the rear pair 5 % above them. The step order is the drawing's: L1 a little taller than L2 and L3, which are level with each other, and L4 and L5 level.

With these rims the full f/3.5 beam passes on the axis. Off the axis the rear pair trims the beam: about 95 % of it passes at 5.4°, 88 % at 6.8° and 80 % at the 8.1° frame corner. The chief ray reaches the corner unobstructed.

## Model Scope and Limitations

| Quantity | Patent, F = 100 | Patent × 1.5 | Model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Focal length | 100 | 150.0 | 149.998 |
| Back focus | 40.40 | 60.60 | 60.569 paraxial |
| S1 to S10 | 38.94 | 58.41 | 58.41 |
| F1,2,3 | 62.60 | 93.90 | 93.93 |
| F1,2,3,4 | 43.89 | 65.84 | 65.85 |

The patent prints a Petzval sum of 0.020 at F = 100; the surface-by-surface sum of the printed prescription is 0.018.

- The prescription is the patent's, not a confirmed factory one, and no maker diagram was available to check element shapes or the iris position.
- Stop position and all semi-diameters are inferred, so the off-axis illumination figures depend on them.
- The image plane is the printed back focus.
- The model is static at infinity and says nothing about performance at 1.8 m.
- The patent gives indices and Abbe numbers only, so behavior away from the d-line rests on the catalog equivalents.
