## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 7,843,651 B2
**Filed:** August 10, 2009
**Priority:** August 11, 2008 (JP 2008-207276)
**Granted:** November 30, 2010
**Inventor:** Yasuo Kanazashi
**Assignee:** Hoya Corporation
**Title:** Wide-angle lens system
**Example details:** Table 1, Fig. 1 and Figs. 2A–2E; millimetres as printed, no scaling.

Embodiment 1 is identified with the smc PENTAX-DA 15mm F4 ED AL Limited, announced on March 3, 2009 and reissued in 2013 as the HD PENTAX-DA 15mm F4 ED AL Limited with the same 8-element, 6-group construction. The correlation is probable rather than certain: the patent gives eight closely related embodiments and the production prescription is not published. The design values, 15.45 mm at F/4.1, differ slightly from the marketed 15 mm f/4 and are kept.

1. Construction: 8 elements in 6 groups, as Pentax lists for the lens.
2. Special elements: one hybrid aspherical element and one ED element. Ricoh's construction diagram for the HD version puts the asphere on the front meniscus and the ED glass in the positive member of the rear cemented doublet, where Embodiment 1 has nd 1.49700 / νd 81.6.
3. Field: half-field 42.96° (85.9° diagonal) at image height 14.24 mm, against the marketed 86° on APS-C.
4. Close focus: the printed near state (magnification −0.142) puts the object about 0.18 m from the image plane, matching the marketed 0.18 m minimum focus; Pentax quotes 0.15×.
5. Timing: the Japanese priority date precedes the lens's announcement by about seven months.

Embodiments 1–6 all form the front asphere as a resin layer on glass, as the production hybrid element does; Embodiments 7 and 8 put the asphere directly on the glass meniscus. Nothing published distinguishes among the first six, so Embodiment 1 stands as the representative.

The 0.18 mm resin layer is modeled as its own cemented element so its printed nd and νd are kept; the lens still counts eight elements. The patent publishes no semi-diameters or stop radius. The semi-diameters are rounded readings of Fig. 1, and the stop radius follows from F/4.1.

## Optical Construction

The design is a retrofocus wide-angle with negative–negative–positive power. The negative first group holds the hybrid front meniscus and a positive meniscus. The weakly negative second group holds a negative meniscus and a cemented positive/negative doublet. The positive third group holds a negative/positive cemented doublet and a positive rear element. Paraxial group focal lengths from the prescription are f1 = −43.8 mm, f2 = −156.7 mm and f3 = +24.6 mm. The patent splits the front negative power between the first two groups so that neither has to carry it alone.

The stop sits 3.13 mm ahead of S11 (patent text), so the printed S10–S11 gap of 5.19 mm divides into 2.06 mm before the stop and 3.13 mm after it. All 15 refracting surfaces are kept, including the glass/resin boundary and the resin's aspherical rear face. The paraxial EFL is 15.449 mm (printed 15.45), the rear vertex is 41.82 mm behind S1 and the image plane 79.31 mm behind S1.

The patent defines fB from the rear of the most image-side cover glass, but Table 1 and Fig. 1 show no plate. The paraxial air back focus of 37.490 mm matches the printed 37.49 mm, so the model uses air only. Table 1 leaves d15 blank; the last gap is that derived value.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 and L1r — Hybrid Negative Front Meniscus

L1: nd = 1.80400, νd = 46.6. Glass: S-LAH65V (OHARA) catalog equivalent — lanthanum flint. f = −29.8 mm.
L1r (resin layer): nd = 1.52972, νd = 42.7. Unmatched optical resin. f = −96.2 mm. L1 with its resin layer: f = −22.8 mm.

The glass substrate occupies S1–S2 and the resin S2–S3, 0.18 mm thick on axis. The resin's image-side face carries the aspherical profile; the patent describes the meniscus as a hybrid element made aspherical by a resin layer on its image side. This strong negative meniscus takes part of the retrofocus front's negative power, and the patent relies on the positive element behind it to correct the off-axis aberrations it introduces. No resin chemistry or supplier is given.

### L2 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.62004, νd = 36.3. Glass: E-F2 (HOYA) catalog equivalent — flint. f = +59.9 mm.

The positive meniscus, convex to the object, closes the negative first group. In the patent's account it corrects the off-axis aberrations that the strong front meniscus produces, before they reach the rear group.

### L3 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.83481, νd = 42.7. Glass: TAFD5F (HOYA) catalog equivalent — dense lanthanum flint. f = −14.1 mm.

This high-index negative meniscus, convex to the object, opens the second group and carries the rest of the front negative power. The patent pairs it with the following cemented doublet so that the second group as a whole stays weak (f2/f = −10.14). Fig. 1 ends the deep concave rear face at about 4.9 mm and carries a flat land out to the 5.7 mm edge; the model draws the same land.

### L4/L5 — Cemented Positive/Negative Doublet

L4: nd = 1.69895, νd = 30.1. Glass: E-FD15 (HOYA) catalog equivalent — dense flint. f = +8.47 mm.
L5: nd = 1.80400, νd = 46.6. Glass: S-LAH65V (OHARA) catalog equivalent — lanthanum flint. f = −13.6 mm. Doublet f = +18.8 mm.

The doublet runs S8–S10 with its junction at S9, immediately ahead of the stop. L4 is biconvex and L5 a negative meniscus concave to the object. The positive member is the more dispersive glass (νd 30.1 against 46.6), the reverse of an ordinary achromatic doublet.

### L6/L7 — Cemented Negative/Positive Doublet

L6: nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: FD60 (HOYA) catalog equivalent — dense flint. f = −15.0 mm.
L7: nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6. Glass: FCD1 (HOYA) catalog equivalent — ED fluorophosphate crown. f = +14.85 mm. Doublet f = +122 mm.

The doublet opens the positive third group behind the stop. Pairing the most dispersive glass in the lens with the ED crown gives strong color correction at weak net power. Ricoh's construction diagram marks this positive member as the lens's single ED element; the patent itself does not name the glass, so the ED identification is inferred from the maker's diagram.

### L8 — Positive Rear Element

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.2. Glass: S-FSL5 (OHARA) catalog equivalent — fluor crown. f = +33.5 mm.

Biconvex, with the stronger convex face toward the image that the patent's claims specify, this element completes the third group (f3/f = 1.59). It leaves the long back focus, 37.49 mm or 2.4 times the focal length, that an SLR mirror box needs.

The patent prints only d-line indices and Abbe numbers. Each glass element matches a catalog glass at that precision. HOYA rows are used where one matches, since Hoya was the applicant, and OHARA rows elsewhere. These are catalog equivalents, not confirmed production melts. The resin has no catalog match and keeps its printed nd and νd with an Abbe-number dispersion model.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent focuses by moving the first and second groups together toward the object while the third group and the stop move toward the object faster. At the close end the G2–G3 gap d10 shrinks from 5.19 to 4.57 mm and fB grows from 37.49 to 39.83 mm, at magnification −0.142 (Table 1). A paraxial trace of that state puts the object about 182 mm from the image plane, consistent with the marketed 0.18 m minimum focus and 0.15×. The data file models infinity focus only; the near state is not implemented.

## Aspherical Surfaces

S3, the resin/air surface, is the lens's only asphere. The patent's sag equation is x = cy²/(1 + √(1 − (1+K)c²y²)) + A4y⁴ + A6y⁶ + A8y⁸ + A10y¹⁰, the same (1+K) conic form the data file uses. The coefficients are K = −1 (paraboloid base), A4 = 2.64942 × 10⁻⁵, A6 = 1.0204 × 10⁻⁷ and A8 = −6.9952 × 10⁻¹⁰; unlisted terms are zero.

The surface is far shallower toward the rim than a sphere of the same 10.544 mm vertex radius: about 5.0 mm of sag at 10 mm height against 7.2 mm for the sphere. That weakens the meniscus's negative power at the edge of the field. This reads as distortion and sagittal-coma control, the aberrations the patent ties to this element's shape. The asphere is a composite (hybrid) type, a synthetic-resin layer formed on the glass meniscus.

## Conditional Expressions

The patent states five conditions. Table 9 prints its row and column headings transposed: the rows labelled "Cond." hold the embodiments. The Embodiment 1 prescription reproduces its values:

| Condition | Range | Embodiment 1 |
|---|---|---|
| (1) f3/f | 1.30 to 1.70 | 1.59 |
| (2) f2/f | −13.50 to −3.80 | −10.14 |
| (3) SF = (R2 + R1)/(R2 − R1) of the front meniscus | −2.20 to −1.60 | −1.98 |
| (4) fL1/f | −1.75 to −1.40 | −1.48 |
| (5) fL2/f | 2.90 to 5.90 | 3.88 |

SF uses the paraxial radius of the aspherical surface, as the patent specifies. fL1 is the focal length of the front meniscus with its resin layer, and fL2 that of L2.

## Model Scope and Limitations

- Infinity focus only; the patent's near state is described under Focus Mechanism but not modeled.
- The semi-diameters are rounded readings of Fig. 1, and the stop radius follows from F/4.1; the patent lists neither. The resin's aspherical face is the one exception: Fig. 1 ends it at about 10.4 mm, while the model keeps 11.0 mm, since the drawn value would narrow the traced field from 40.5° to 39.2°. With these rims the full-field beam is strongly vignetted at the corners (about 28 % geometric transmission at 42.8°, 56 % at 40°), which may not match the production lens.
- The full-field chief ray at 42.96° lands at 14.237 mm, matching the printed image height of 14.24 mm.
- At full aperture the real axial beam is slightly narrower than the paraxial pupil (entrance radius 1.817 mm against 1.884 mm). The model keeps the printed F/4.1 as its nominal aperture.
- The eight glass elements trace on catalog dispersion curves of the equivalents listed above. The resin layer uses an Abbe-number model from its printed nd and νd. On axis the d and C lines follow the patent's Fig. 2B within about 0.01 mm; the g line focuses about 0.03 mm shorter than plotted, more than any choice among the catalog equivalents can shift it.
