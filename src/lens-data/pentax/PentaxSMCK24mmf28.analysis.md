# PENTAX SMC PENTAX 24mm f/2.8: US 3,938,883

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [US 3,938,883](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3938883A/en)  
**Application Number:** 572,866  
**Priority:** 8 May 1974 (Japan 49-50954)  
**Filed:** 29 April 1975  
**Granted:** 17 February 1976  
**Inventor:** Yasuo Takahashi  
**Assignee:** Asahi Kogaku Kogyo Co., Ltd.  
**Title:** Wide Angle Lens System  
**Example details:** The single Example in column 3, repeated in the claim; lens arrangement in Fig. 1, aberrations in Fig. 2. Printed at F = 100; all lengths × 0.24, indices and Abbe numbers unchanged.

The patent names no product. This page describes a patent design associated with the SMC Pentax 24mm f/2.8, not a confirmed factory prescription. The association rests on these points:

1. **Construction count.** The patent's lens system "is composed of 9 elements in 8 groups", with one cemented pair. The SMC Pentax 24mm f/2.8 has 9 elements in 8 groups.
2. **Speed and field.** The patent describes "a bright wide angle lens having a brightness in the range of 1:2.8", and Fig. 2 plots its aberrations to a half field of 42°. The production lens is f/2.8 with an 84° angle of view.
3. **Maker and timing.** The assignee is Asahi Kogaku Kogyo, Pentax's maker. The Japanese priority is May 1974, and the lens was introduced in 1977 and made until 1984. Cine Lens Wiki states that its successor, the SMC Pentax-A 24mm f/2.8, kept the same optical formula.

No Pentax cross-section of this lens was available, so the element shapes could not be compared with the production lens. The association is therefore plausible to moderate.

The patent prints its prescription at F = 100. Every radius and spacing in the data file is the printed value × 0.24, the ratio of the marketed 24 mm to the printed F; the patent does not state that factor. Refractive indices and Abbe numbers are as printed, and all surfaces are spherical, so there are no aspheric coefficients to transform. The table itself traces to an EFL of 99.974 at F = 100, so the model's EFL is 23.994 mm rather than exactly 24 mm. Nothing has been refitted to remove that difference.

## Optical Architecture

The patent calls the design a retrofocus wide-angle lens. Nine spherical elements form eight groups: positive meniscus L1; negative menisci L2 and L3; positive meniscus L4, cemented to L3 as doublet C1; negative meniscus L5; the stop; biconvex L6; biconcave L7; positive meniscus L8; and the nearly plano-convex L9.

The patent states that the first to fifth lenses "comprise a front lens group of the negative retrofocus type". At this scale L1 to L5 combine to −15.67 mm and L1 to L6 to +35.76 mm; L7 to L9 then bring the system to 23.99 mm. The paraxial back focus is 39.98 mm, 1.666 times the focal length, against the abstract's "about 1.6 times". The glass and air from the first vertex to the last span 46.90 mm.

The weak positive L1 in front of the negative group is the design's distinctive feature. Condition (1) keeps its power within a narrow range: the patent says a stronger L1 would demand more negative power behind it to hold the back focus, reducing the Petzval sum, while a weaker one would leave distortion uncorrected.

The FRONT bracket on the diagram is the patent's front group, L1 to L5. The patent names no rear group; the REAR bracket covers the remaining elements behind the stop. The numerals 12 to 28 in the patent's Fig. 1 correspond to L1 to L9.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.71300, νd = 54.0. Glass: LAC8 (HOYA) catalog equivalent; supplier not established. f = +137.3 mm.

The large front element, convex to the object, with the patent's "face of larger curvature turned towards the subject side". The patent prints its focal length as F1 = 571.4 = F/0.175, inside condition (1); the table computes to 572.1 at F = 100. Its weak positive power trims the barrel distortion of the negative group behind it.

### L2 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.62041, νd = 60.3. Glass: S-BSM16 (OHARA) catalog equivalent; supplier not established. f = −30.3 mm.

The first negative meniscus of the front group, convex to the object. Its rear face r4 (10.46 mm) is the most strongly curved surface in the lens. Fig. 1 draws its rear rim much smaller than its front, and the model's front and rear clear apertures (13.2 and 9.12 mm) follow that taper.

### L3 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.78590, νd = 44.2. Glass: S-LAH51 (OHARA) catalog equivalent; supplier not established. f = −19.9 mm in air.

The front half of doublet C1 and the strongest negative element. Condition (3) sets its index and Abbe number against L4: 0.05 < n3 − n4 < 0.15 and 8 < ν3 − ν4 < 15, here 0.1132 and 12.1. The patent ties the condition to keeping the negative power of L3 while distributing colour correctly. A larger index step would cost negative lenses of high Abbe number and force low-Abbe positive lenses into the achromatization.

### L4 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.67270, νd = 32.1. Glass: SF5 (Schott) catalog equivalent; supplier not established. f = +22.2 mm in air.

The flint rear half of C1, cemented to the concave rear face r6 of L3. The pair is weakly negative in air, −129.8 mm. The junction carries the index and Abbe steps of condition (3).

### L5 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.64250, νd = 58.4. Glass: S-BSM36 (OHARA) catalog equivalent; supplier not established. f = −39.2 mm.

The last element of the front group, just ahead of the stop. It brings L1 to L5 to −65.28 at F = 100 (printed −65.4 = −F/1.53), inside condition (2), F/1.75 < |F1–5| < F/1.35. The patent says a stronger front group would overload the positive lenses behind it, so that a single sixth lens would no longer suffice, and a weaker one would need wider spacings to reach the back focus, against the aim of compactness.

### L6 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.74320, νd = 49.4. Glass: S-LAM60 (OHARA) catalog equivalent; supplier not established. f = +17.9 mm.

The strongest element, 10.37 mm thick on the axis (43.2 at F = 100), directly behind the stop. It brings L1 to L6 to +149.0 at F = 100 (printed 149.3 = F/0.67), inside condition (4), F/0.85 < F1–6 < F/0.6. The patent says a shorter F1–6 would overload L6, raising coma and lowering the Petzval sum, and a longer one would leave it too little positive effect.

### L7 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: SF6 (Schott) catalog equivalent; supplier not established. f = −21.2 mm.

The dense-flint negative of the rear group, with the lowest Abbe number in the lens. The patent sets no condition on it alone. As the only strongly dispersive negative element behind the stop, it is the probable achromatizing partner of L6; that reading is an interpretation, not the patent's statement. Fig. 1 draws L7 and L8 nearly touching at the rim, but the table separates them by 1.32 mm of air.

### L8 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.52307, νd = 58.4. Glass: C12 (HOYA) catalog equivalent; supplier not established. f = +33.6 mm.

A low-index positive meniscus whose steep rear face r15 (−15.67 mm) faces the image, as the patent's claim describes. With L9 it falls under condition (5).

### L9 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.69680, νd = 55.7. Glass: S-LAL14 (OHARA) catalog equivalent; supplier not established. f = +50.4 mm.

The last element. Its front radius r16 is 34680.0 at F = 100 (8323.2 mm here), so the element is nearly plano-convex; the model keeps the printed curvature. Condition (5) sets the mean index and Abbe number of L8 and L9: 1.5 < (n8 + n9)/2 < 1.75 and 50 < (ν8 + ν9)/2 < 62, here 1.6099 and 57.05. The patent ties these limits to spherical aberration and chromatic balance at 1:2.8: a lower mean index raises the Petzval sum, and a higher one costs Abbe number, ease of mass production and glass price.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent defines its indices as d-line values and prints Abbe numbers, but names no glass. The labels in the data file are catalog rows that match each printed nd to five decimals and νd within 0.3. They are not the production melts, and the supplier is not established.

SF5 and SF6 are lead-flint legacy rows, chosen for the patent's 1974 date as on the SMC Pentax 18mm f/3.5. L8's C12 is the only catalog row at nd 1.52307; its Abbe number differs from the printed value by 0.24. The model keeps every printed nd, and its chromatic calculations take dispersion from these catalog rows.

The palette is conventional for the period: lanthanum glasses in L1, L3, L6 and L9, two dense flints (L4, L7) as achromatizing partners, and barium crowns of νd 58 to 60 in the negative menisci L2 and L5. Neither the patent nor Pentax names an ED or anomalous-dispersion glass. Fig. 2(b) plots d- and g-line spherical aberration; the Abbe numbers alone do not establish partial-dispersion correction.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent says nothing about focusing: it names no focusing lens or direction and prints no close-focus spacings. The model is therefore static at infinity focus, with no variable gaps. Pentax's minimum focus distance of 0.25 m (0.12× magnification) is a catalog figure, not a modelled state.

## Stop, Clear Apertures and Image Plane

The patent prints no stop position, and Fig. 1 draws no iris. The model places the stop in the 4.44 mm air space d9 between L5 and L6, 3.33 mm behind r9 and 1.11 mm ahead of r10. That placement, three quarters of the way across the gap, keeps the chief rays clear of the front rims; it is a modelling choice, not a published diaphragm position. The stop radius of 5.76 mm follows from the paraxial entrance pupil at F/2.8 (4.28 mm radius). The production lens stops down to f/22 with five blades.

Clear apertures are not printed. All semi-diameters are estimates. L1 to L5 follow Fig. 1 closely: L1's 19.2 mm against about 19.3 mm drawn. L6 to L9 stand wider than the figure, at 7.92, 8.4, 10.08 and 12.0 mm against about 7.6, 7.6, 8.9 and 10.1 mm drawn. The figure's own rims would still pass the axial beam at F/2.8, but would cut more of the corner bundle. Corner illumination depends on these estimates, and the corner bundle is clipped mainly by L6 to L8.

The image plane is the paraxial back focus of the scaled table, 39.981 mm behind r17 and 86.877 mm from the first vertex. The patent prints no back focus beyond the abstract's ratio, so this plane is derived, not published.

The patent's 42° half field lands at 21.03 mm at this scale, 97 % of the 21.65 mm full-frame corner; the corner itself would lie at 42.8°. The data file declares that 42° field in `projection`. Distortion at 42° is −2.66 %, close to the roughly −2.7 % of Fig. 2(c). The model's longitudinal spherical aberration is +0.11 mm at full aperture and −0.18 mm at 0.7 of the pupil, the zonal undercorrection that Fig. 2(a) shows.

The table meets every condition. The printed focal lengths are rounded and differ from the computed ones by less than 0.6 %. Three small inconsistencies sit in the text: the claim's condition (2) drops the absolute-value bars that column 2 prints, the discussion of condition (4) gives its upper limit as F/0.55 instead of F/0.6, and the discussion of condition (5) gives its lower index limit as 1.55 instead of 1.5.
