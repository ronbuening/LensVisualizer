# PENTAX SMC PENTAX 18mm f/3.5: US 3,841,736, Table III

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 3,841,736
**Priority:** 11 May 1972 (Japan 47-46604)
**Filed:** 2 May 1973
**Granted:** 15 October 1974
**Inventors:** Yasuo Takahashi, Sadao Okudaira
**Assignee:** Asahi Kogaku Kogyo Co., Ltd.
**Title:** Wide Angle Objective Lens System with Large Back Focus
**Example details:** Second embodiment, Table III and Fig. 3 (F = 100, 1:3.5, half field 51°, back focus FB = 199.7), aberrations in Fig. 4. All lengths × 0.18; indices and Abbe numbers unchanged.

The patent names no product and gives its dimensions as relative values at F = 100. The 0.18× scale is inferred from the marketed focal length; it is not stated in the patent. This page describes a patent design associated with the SMC Pentax 18mm f/3.5 at family level, not a confirmed factory prescription. The association rests on these points:

1. **Construction count.** Pentax's lens table prints 11 groups, 12 elements. Table III has 11 elements in 10 groups, and the thin plate in Pentax's section drawing accounts for the extra element and group.
2. **Section drawing.** Pentax's drawing has the model's element order and shapes. L1 to L6, L10 and L11 fit Table III, including the meniscus L10 and biconvex L11 that separate Table III from Table I. It is not identical: its cemented L7/L8 is thinner than Table III's, and its L9 is about twice as thick, with a stepped rim.
3. **Speed and field.** The patent's 1:3.5 and 102° full field compare with the f/3.5 and 100° in Pentax's table.
4. **Timing.** The Japanese priority is May 1972 and the lens reached retail in 1975. No Takumar 18mm f/3.5 was found, so the design has no known screw-mount version.

The page prints Pentax's count of 12 elements in 11 groups and draws 11 elements in 10 groups, because Pentax's count includes a filter plate. Its brochure gives the lens four built-in filters and a provision for gelatin filters at the rear. The patent lists no plate, and its printed back focus excludes one: Table III computes to FB = 199.71 as tabulated. The plate's thickness and index are unknown, so it stays out of the prescription. The retail lens has that glass in the beam, which the model's focus and aberrations do not include.

## Optical Architecture

Eleven elements in ten groups, all spherical: negative L1, positive L2, negative L3 and L4, positive L5 and L6, the stop, cemented doublet C1 (L7, L8), positive L9, negative L10 and positive L11. The model adds no filter plate.

The power layout is a retrofocus. At this scale L1 to L4 combine to −10.08 mm and L1 to L6 to +20.50 mm. The back focus of 35.95 mm is 1.997 times the focal length, against the patent's stated aim of more than 1.85. The first embodiment (Table I, Fig. 1) has the same order, but with a biconcave L10 and a meniscus L11.

Every radius, axial spacing and the image distance use the same factor of 0.18; indices and Abbe numbers are as printed. The stop radius follows from the patent's 1:3.5, which matches the marketed f/3.5.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.64000, νd = 60.2. Glass: LACL60 (HOYA) catalog equivalent; supplier not established. f = −46.4 mm.

The large front meniscus, convex to the object. With L3 and L4 it supplies the negative power that lengthens the back focus. Condition (1) sets the combined power of L1 to L4, and the patent credits that group with reducing the steep field angle reaching L5 and the lenses behind it.

### L2 — Plano-Convex

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Glass: S-BSL7 (OHARA) catalog equivalent; supplier not established. f = +109.0 mm.

A thick positive lens, 7.18 mm on the axis at this scale, with a flat front face in Table III. The patent gives the second lens the job of keeping chromatic aberration and distortion from growing in the negative direction.

### L3 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.64000, νd = 60.2. Glass: LACL60 (HOYA) catalog equivalent; supplier not established. f = −26.5 mm.

The strongest negative lens of the front group. Its steeply curved rear face r6 has a large negative spherical term in Table IV (−16.00).

### L4 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Glass: S-BSL7 (OHARA) catalog equivalent; supplier not established. f = −38.6 mm.

The last negative meniscus of the front group, which it brings to |F1234| = F/1.786. Its rear face r8 has the largest negative spherical term in Table IV (−37.55).

### L5 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.64000, νd = 60.2. Glass: LACL60 (HOYA) catalog equivalent; supplier not established. f = +72.1 mm.

A thin, weak biconvex lens that opens the positive section. Pentax's drawing places the filter plate right behind it, in the 3.89 mm air space before L6.

### L6 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.62588, νd = 35.7. Glass: F1 (SUMITA) catalog equivalent; supplier not established. f = +18.7 mm.

The thick biconvex positive just ahead of the stop. It brings L1 to L6 to F123456 = F/0.878, the value that condition (2) ties to the correction of lateral colour.

### L7 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Glass: S-BSL7 (OHARA) catalog equivalent; supplier not established. f = +18.1 mm in air.

The front half of cemented doublet C1, directly behind the stop, and the low-index side of the r14 junction.

### L8 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.83400, νd = 37.2. Glass: S-LAH60 (OHARA) catalog equivalent; supplier not established. f = −10.1 mm in air.

The rear half of C1, which is −24.3 mm in air as a pair. The patent corrects the spherical aberration left by the rear lenses at the junction r14, using its index step n8 − n7 = 0.318. Table IV gives that face −34.75. The patent describes the doublet's ratio F7/|F8| = 1.791 as dividing the correction of colour, coma and spherical aberration.

### L9 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.1. Glass: S-FSL5 (OHARA) catalog equivalent; supplier not established. f = +28.0 mm.

A positive meniscus with a nearly flat front face. Its rear face r17 is one of the two surfaces where the patent says the long back focus concentrates spherical under-correction (26.60 in Table IV). Pentax's drawing shows this element thicker than Table III's 2.63 mm, with a stepped rim.

### L10 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: SF6 (Schott) catalog equivalent; supplier not established. f = −44.1 mm.

A thin dense-flint meniscus, 1.04 mm on the axis. Its rear face sets condition (5), r19 = 1.217F, which the patent ties to coma for rays of low incident height.

### L11 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Glass: S-BSL7 (OHARA) catalog equivalent; supplier not established. f = +33.9 mm.

The last element, nearly flat in front and strongly curved behind. Its rear face r21 is the other surface the patent names with r17 (16.22 in Table IV).

## Glass Identification and Selection

Table III prints d-line indices and Abbe numbers and names no glass. The labels in the data file are catalog equivalents chosen on those coordinates. They are not the production melts, and the supplier is not established.

| Elements | Printed nd / νd | Label | Catalog nd / νd |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L1, L3, L5 | 1.64000 / 60.2 | LACL60 (HOYA) | 1.64000 / 60.20 |
| L2, L4, L7, L11 | 1.51633 / 64.1 | S-BSL7 (OHARA) | 1.51633 / 64.14 |
| L6 | 1.62588 / 35.7 | F1 (SUMITA) | 1.62588 / 35.65 |
| L8 | 1.83400 / 37.2 | S-LAH60 (OHARA) | 1.83400 / 37.16 |
| L9 | 1.48749 / 70.1 | S-FSL5 (OHARA) | 1.48749 / 70.24 |
| L10 | 1.80518 / 25.4 | SF6 (Schott) | 1.80518 / 25.43 |

All 11 labels resolve in the catalog and match the printed nd. F1 and SF6 are lead-flint legacy rows, chosen for the patent's 1972 date. The model keeps every printed nd, and its chromatic calculations take dispersion from the catalog rows.

The patent's only Abbe-number discussion concerns the first two lenses: ν1 near 60 and ν2 near 64 here, against 40 and 48.9 in the first embodiment, leave a smaller chromatic residual through the twelfth surface. Neither the patent nor Pentax names ED or anomalous-dispersion glass. The Abbe numbers do not establish partial dispersion or apochromatic correction.

## Focus Mechanism

The model is static at infinity focus and has no variable gaps, because the patent prints no close-focus spacings. Pentax's table lists a minimum focus of 0.25 m, which the data file carries as `closeFocusM`. That distance is a catalog figure, not a modeled state.

## Stop, Clear Apertures and Image Plane

Column 2 of the patent says a diaphragm separates the seventh lens from the lenses before it, so the stop lies in the 2.82 mm gap between L6 and L7. No position is printed, but Table IV fixes one. That table's caption says "Table I", yet its values recompute from Table III. Its coma and astigmatism columns depend on the pupil position and reproduce with the pupil 5.36 behind r12 at F = 100, which is 0.965 mm at this scale. The figures draw no iris. The stop's 5.66 mm radius follows from the paraxial entrance pupil at F/3.5, and this placement does not establish room for the blades. Pentax's table gives f/22 as the smallest stop.

Clear apertures are not printed. All semi-diameters are estimates read from Fig. 3 by fitting Table III's arcs to the drawing, which is to scale within 4 % (vertical against axial). They are not factory clear apertures. L1 and L2 follow the figure, and L3 and L4 sit within 0.9 mm of it. From L5 back the rims stop 0.8 to 1.8 mm above the figure to keep corner light, since the figure's own rims would cut into the beam well inside the corner. Pentax's drawing is also fuller than Fig. 3 at the rear. Illumination is 48.1 % at 51°, and it depends on these estimates.

The image plane is the printed FB × 0.18: 35.946 mm behind r21 and 92.25 mm from the first vertex. The computed EFL is 17.998 mm and the computed paraxial back focus is 35.948 mm, which agree with F = 100 and FB = 199.7 to printed precision. No dimension is adjusted to remove the difference. The patent's back focus has no plate in it, so this plane is the patent's, not a retail measurement.

The patent's half field is 51°, the value on Figs. 2 and 4. It lands at 21.15 mm at this scale, so the model covers 98 % of the 21.65 mm full-frame corner and stops just short of it; the corner would lie at 51.6°. Pentax quotes 100°. The data file declares the 51° field in `projection` because the paraxial chief-ray estimate undercounts it for this design.
