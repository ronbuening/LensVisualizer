## Optical Architecture

Eleven powered elements in ten groups. The negative front subassembly creates long back focus; E7/E8 are cemented. E10 is a negative meniscus and E11 biconvex, distinguishing TableIII from TableI. All modeled faces are spherical; no aspheric coefficients, reflective path, camera stack or additional filter is introduced.

The source dimensions use a uniform factor 0.18; every radius, axial interval and image distance uses the same factor. Indices and Abbe numbers remain literal. The source f-number, rather than the retail label, controls the normal native paraxial entrance-pupil rule.

## Stop, Clear Apertures and Image Plane

The stop coordinate and surface clear apertures are not printed. The authored stop is the midpoint of the gap after S12, at z=38.982600000mm. Quarter- and three-quarter-gap planes are sensitivity hypotheses. All three passed normal native geometry and exact spherical-cap clearance across the entire source-f-number iris disk. This does not certify room for blades or a mount. The ordinary-prime stop radius is derived by the engine, not hand-authored.

The apertures are explicit modeling estimates. Initialization uses the independent paraxial entrance-pupil marginal envelope with10% clearance, plus coarse schematic envelope proportions. Cardinar uses approximate L2/L1=.91, cemented-pair/L1=.78 and rear/L1=.50 proportions. The600mm model retains front near-equal envelopes, an isolated fourth radius about.56 of the front, and rear pair about.36. These are not calibrated factory clear apertures.

For the ultra-wide K18, the source-field paraxial envelope was only an initialization aid. Its first apertures failed native edge-thickness and cross-gap checks. That rejected vector is retained. A geometry-only descendant reduced offending clear zones, with unequal front/rear radii on the front negative menisci; source radii, thicknesses, glasses, stop and image plane stayed unchanged. The final vector was frozen before any ray/MTF performance test. No aperture was selected for higher contrast.
