## Patent Identification and Product Correspondence

**Patent:** US 7,116,499 B2
**Filed:** 2005-04-22 (priority JP 2004-131996, 2004-04-27)
**Granted:** 2006-10-03
**Inventor:** Jun Hirakawa
**Assignee:** Pentax Corporation
**Title:** Wide-angle lens system
**Example details:** Table 4 and Fig. 10; infinity aberration plots Figs. 11A–E, near-state plots Figs. 12A–E

Embodiment 4 is associated with the smc PENTAX-DA 14mm F2.8 ED [IF], released in 2004. The evidence converges on several points:

1. The production lens has 12 elements in 11 groups with no aspherical surface; Embodiment 4 is the only example in the patent with that count (Embodiments 1–3 have 11 elements in 10 groups).
2. Ricoh's construction diagram matches Fig. 10 element by element: a weak positive front element, two negative menisci, a positive meniscus, the cemented pair and negative meniscus of G2, and the rear group around the stop.
3. The diagram marks one ED element at the position of L11 (nd 1.497, νd 81.6) and a high-index, low-dispersion element at L2.
4. f = 14.38 mm, F/2.9 and a 45.1° half field correspond to the retail 14 mm f/2.8 and its 90° angle of view on APS-C; the patent's background names a 23.5 × 15.7 mm sensor.
5. Focusing moves G2 and G3 with G1 fixed, consistent with the internal focusing the "[IF]" designation names.

The correlation is probable rather than confirmed: Ricoh does not publish the prescription. The data file uses the patent's millimetre values unscaled, so the design focal length is 14.38 mm rather than 14 mm.

## Optical Architecture

A negative–negative–positive retrofocus design in twelve spherical elements and eleven groups. G1 (L1–L4) is fixed; G2 (L5–L7) is negative; G3 (L8–L12) is positive and carries the stop between L9 and L10. The only cemented component is L5–L6. No cover glass or filter is modelled.

The patent's goal is a half field of about 45° with a back focus long enough for a mount shared with 35 mm film cameras: the printed back focus of 36.98 mm is 2.6 times the 14.24 mm image height, against the roughly 2.5 times the patent says a digital SLR on such a mount needs. Conditions (1)–(3) set the power split between G1, G2 and G3 as a double retrofocus arrangement (Table 5: 1.82, 1.65 and 1.10). Condition (6) (3.12) keeps G1 weak enough that G2 and G3 can focus without large spherical-aberration change.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.69680, νd = 55.5. Glass: S-LAL14 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +432.5 mm.

A very weak positive meniscus, convex to the object. The patent describes it as the one addition Embodiment 4 makes to the first group: a positive element ahead of the two negative menisci. The patent does not give its purpose; in a retrofocus wide-angle, such an element usually offsets part of the front group's barrel distortion (author's interpretation).

### L2 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.72916, νd = 54.7. Glass: S-LAL18 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = −50.2 mm.

The first of the two negative menisci, convex to the object, that carry G1's negative power. The patent chooses the meniscus shape because it produces strong negative power without large astigmatism and coma. Ricoh's diagram marks this element as high-index, low-dispersion glass; it is a lanthanum crown, not an anomalous-dispersion type.

### L3 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.78590, νd = 44.2. Glass: S-LAH51 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = −40.7 mm.

The second negative meniscus, also convex to the object, in lanthanum flint. It shares the negative power with L2; the patent notes that splitting this power over more menisci favours performance and back focus.

### L4 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.8. Glass: FDS90 (HOYA catalog equivalent). f = +69.8 mm.

A dense-flint positive meniscus, convex to the image, closing G1. Condition (4) asks this element for a refractive index above 1.75 and an Abbe number below 30 (Table 5: 1.85, 23.80). It collects the diverging beam from both menisci; the high index corrects their astigmatism and the high dispersion their lateral colour with few positive elements.

### L5 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.2. Glass: S-FSL5 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +27.9 mm.

Biconvex crown front member of the cemented doublet that opens G2. Cemented to L6 at R = −18.8 mm, it leaves the doublet positive overall (f = +36.5 mm) inside a negative group.

### L6 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.69100, νd = 54.8. Glass: S-LAL9 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = −129.3 mm.

Negative meniscus rear member of the G2 doublet, concave to the object. The cemented interface joins nd 1.487 to nd 1.691; the patent assigns the doublet no role beyond its place at the front of G2.

### L7 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.80400, νd = 46.6. Glass: S-LAH65V (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = −17.3 mm.

Negative meniscus, convex to the object, closing G2; the strongest negative element in the lens. The patent places a meniscus of this orientation at the rear of G2 in every embodiment.

### L8 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: S-TIH6 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +35.2 mm.

Dense-flint positive element opening G3. The patent's general description calls this element biconvex; in Embodiment 4 its rear radius (+2221 mm) is so weak that it is a nearly plano-convex meniscus, convex to the object.

### L9 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.2. Glass: S-FSL5 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +184.2 mm.

Weak positive meniscus, convex to the image, 2.0 mm ahead of the stop. Both faces are strongly curved and concave toward the object, so the element adds little power; the patent assigns it no separate role.

### L10 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.78472, νd = 25.7. Glass: S-TIH11 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = −28.2 mm.

Biconcave dense-flint element 4.35 mm behind the stop, the only negative element in G3. It is the flint partner of the low-dispersion crowns L11 and L12.

### L11 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6. Glass: FCD1 (HOYA catalog equivalent) — ED. f = +26.8 mm.

Biconvex positive element and the ED element of Ricoh's diagram; its nd/νd is that of a fluorophosphate ED crown. With L12 it forms the rear positive pair whose average Abbe number condition (5) keeps above 70 (Table 5: 75.90), mainly to correct lateral colour.

### L12 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.2. Glass: S-FSL5 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +45.9 mm.

Biconvex crown last element, the second member of the condition (5) pair.

The patent prints only d-line indices and Abbe numbers and names no supplier. Each label is a catalog row whose nd matches the printed value and whose νd lies within 0.05 of it; where several vendors matched equally, the closest OHARA or HOYA row was chosen. The labels supply catalog dispersion for colour calculations and are not claims about the production supplier.

The chromatic plan works on lateral colour at both ends. In front of the stop, the dense-flint L4 corrects the colour of the two front menisci (condition 4). Behind it, the dense-flint L10 is balanced by the ED crown L11 and the crown L12, whose average νd of 75.9 meets condition (5). L11 is the only anomalous-dispersion glass.

## Focus Mechanism and Image Plane

The lens focuses internally: G2 and G3 move together 2.27 mm toward the object, carrying the stop, while G1 stays fixed. The patent gives the reasons: the moving groups weigh less than the whole lens, and a stationary front group simplifies an autofocus mechanism. Table 4 prints both states.

| Quantity   | Infinity | Near state |
| ---------- | -------: | ---------: |
| D8 (G1–G2) |  4.43 mm |    2.16 mm |
| BF         | 36.98 mm |   39.25 mm |
| f          | 14.38 mm |   14.80 mm |
| m          |        0 |     −0.137 |
| F-number   |      2.9 |        3.0 |

The image plane stays 111.91 mm behind S1 in both states. The patent gives the near state's magnification but not its object distance. Traced paraxially, the model's near state focuses on an object 200.1 mm from the image plane (88.2 mm ahead of S1), with f = 14.797 mm and m = −0.1372, matching the printed values. The production lens focuses closer, to 0.17 m (0.19×). Table 4 gives F/3.0 for the near state, while Figs. 12A–B are labelled F3.7; the difference is unresolved, and the model keeps the infinity stop size in both states. Slider positions between the two printed states are linear interpolations, not a published focus cam.

The paraxial back focus at infinity is 36.978 mm against the printed 36.98 mm; the 0.002 mm difference is not applied.

## Stop and Semi-Diameters

The patent places the stop 2.0 mm behind surface 17 (56.95 mm behind S1) but does not print its diameter; the model's 5.51 mm stop radius follows from F/2.9 at infinity. No semi-diameter is printed. S1 and S2 are set at 30 mm, about 15 % above the 26.2 mm first-order height of the edge-of-field beam, and the rims narrow toward the rear as in Fig. 10 and Ricoh's section. Two rims are set by geometry: S4 at 17 mm, because the 22.8 mm of S3 would exceed its 21.25 mm radius, and S6 at 13.95 mm, because 17.4 mm would exceed the rim-slope limit and 14 mm would leave too little edge clearance across the 7.9 mm air space to S7. These are modelling estimates, not measured factory apertures.

## Model Scope and Limitations

Wide open at infinity, the model passes the full beam out to about 15° off axis and about 97 % of it at 22.5°. At the 45° field edge, geometric transmission falls to about 40 %, cut mainly by the rims behind the stop (S18–S23). Because those rims are estimates, the corner figure describes the model rather than the production lens. At the 45° field edge, the chief ray reaches an image height of 14.17 mm, essentially the 14.18 mm APS-C half-diagonal.

Only nd and νd are printed, so line indices and partial dispersion come from the catalog equivalents above. The near-state aperture is ambiguous (F/3.0 in Table 4, F3.7 on the plots), and the near state is not the production lens's minimum focus distance.
