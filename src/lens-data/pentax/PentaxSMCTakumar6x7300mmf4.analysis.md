## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 3,467,462 A  
**Filed:** 1965-07-09 (Ser. No. 470,711)  
**Granted:** 1969-09-16  
**Priority:** 1964-08-13 (JP 39-45,432)  
**Inventors:** Tomokazu Kazamaki, Tohru Matsumoto  
**Assignee:** Asahi Kogaku Kogyo Co., Ltd.  
**Title:** Lens system of low chromatic aberration  
**Example details:** The first of three embodiments: section in Fig. 1, table in column 3, repeated in claim 2 (column 6), aberration plots in Fig. 3. The patent is normalized to f = 100 mm; every length is multiplied by exactly 3 for f = 300 mm.

The model is associated with the Pentax 6x7 300mm f/4. Asahi introduced it in 1969 with the Asahi Pentax 6x7 as the Super-Takumar/6x7 300mm f/4. It became the Super-Multi-Coated Takumar/6x7 in 1971 and was later sold as the SMC Takumar 6x7 and the SMC Pentax 67 300mm f/4; references describe the optics as unchanged under the three names. Ricoh's catalog lists five elements in five groups, a 17° angle of view, f/4 to f/45, an automatic diaphragm, 5.0 m minimum focus, an 82 mm filter, and 92.5 × 186 mm for 1,430 g. The later SMC Pentax-M★ 67 300mm f/4 ED [IF] is a different design.

This is a patent design associated with the lens, not a confirmed factory prescription. Five points support the association:

1. The example's five air-spaced single elements match the retail construction of five elements in five groups.
2. Scaled by 3, it is a 300 mm f/4. Its half-field of 8° (Fig. 3) reaches 42.2 mm of image height, 94 % of the 44.8 mm half-diagonal of the 6x7 frame; Ricoh's 17° corresponds to the full diagonal.
3. The printed length from the first surface to the image is 89.86 at f = 100, or 269.58 mm here. With the 84.95 mm flange distance of the Pentax 67 mount, the first vertex lies 184.6 mm ahead of the flange, against the 186 mm barrel length in Ricoh's table. The 75 mm front clear aperture fits inside the 82 mm filter thread.
4. US 3,502,393, the same inventors' patent behind the catalog's SMC Takumar 6x7 600mm f/4, describes itself as a development of "the 5-group 5-lens system telephoto objective" of application Ser. No. 470,711, which is this patent.
5. The PhotonsToPhotos reconstruction of this example is titled for the SMC Takumar 6x7 300mm f/4.

The case has two weaknesses. The Japanese priority of August 1964 and the US filing of July 1965 come four to five years before the 6x7 system appeared, and the patent's background illustrates its aim with a 150 mm lens used in place of a 50 mm standard lens, a 35 mm setting. Scaled by 1.5 instead of 3, the same example is a 150 mm f/4 whose 16° field suits 35 mm film. Asahi's Super-Takumar 150mm f/4 appeared in 1965 with five elements; most references give five groups, though some give four for production before 1967. The example may therefore have been designed for that lens and scaled for the 6x7 later. No Pentax construction drawing of either lens and no designer credit for either was found. The example does not suit a 35 mm 300 mm f/4, which needs only half its field.

Both readings of the patent print a sign wrongly at one surface. The table prints r3 = −117.00 and r9 = +18.00 at f = 100, while claim 2 prints r3 = +1.1700F and r9 = −0.1800F. The patent's own Seidel table settles both: its Petzval terms for surfaces 3 and 9, −0.357 and −1.879, follow only from r3 = −117.00 and r9 = −18.00. With those signs all ten Petzval terms reproduce and their sum is 0.174, against 0.173 printed. They also match Fig. 1, which draws L2 biconcave and L5 as a thin element rather than the strong biconvex lens a positive r9 would make. Of the four sign combinations, only this one gives the stated f = 100 and back focus of 40.16 (computed 100.02 and 40.18); the others give focal lengths of 30 to 39. The model therefore takes r3 from the table and r9 from the claim. Examples 2 (f/2.8) and 3 (f/4.5, with fluorite first and third lenses) are not used.

## Optical Architecture

Five spherical singlets form a telephoto: a front group of L1 (biconvex crown), L2 (biconcave flint) and L3 (crown meniscus, convex to the object), a 60 mm air space, the isolated biconvex L4, and the rear negative meniscus L5, 33 mm behind L4. The power sequence is P-N-P, P, N. There are no cemented, aspheric or plane surfaces.

At this scale the front group has a composite focal length of +300.0 mm and L1 to L4 together +166.7 mm; L5 alone is −154.2 mm. The lens is 149.10 mm from S1 to S10, and the image lies 269.58 mm behind S1, 0.90 times the focal length.

The patent's account is chromatic and astigmatic. Each lens corrects the color left by those ahead of it in turn: L1 is under-corrected, L2 over-corrects it, L3 under-corrects the first two, L4 over-corrects the first three, and L5 corrects the color of the first four. A rear negative lens in a long-focus system usually has to correct the front's astigmatism with a strongly curved front face, which worsens coma. Here L4's front face is no stronger than aplanatic for the paraxial rays leaving L3, so L4 adds little or even negative astigmatism and L5's front radius can stay long: the patent requires at least 0.12F, and this example has 0.18F. The patent stresses that this is achieved with inexpensive glasses and small curvatures, and notes that the Rayleigh limit of a 1:4 lens is 0.19 mm.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.51630, νd = 64.0. Glass: S-BSL7 (OHARA catalog equivalent), a borosilicate crown. f = +200.0 mm.

The largest element and the strongest positive one in the front group, with a strong front face (R1 = 107.70 mm) and a nearly flat rear. In the patent's chromatic chain it is the under-corrected starting point.

### L2 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.71740, νd = 29.5. Glass: PBH1 (OHARA catalog equivalent), a lead dense flint of the period. f = −207.7 mm.

Placed 9 mm behind L1, it over-corrects L1's color. Its front face is the r3 whose sign the claim misprints; the Seidel table and Fig. 1 both require it concave to the object.

### L3 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.51630, νd = 64.0. Glass: S-BSL7 (OHARA catalog equivalent), the same crown as L1. f = +339.6 mm.

Convex to the object, 0.6 mm behind L2. The patent specifies a meniscus whose front face is more strongly curved than its rear and has it under-correct the over-corrected color of L1 and L2. It closes the front group, whose composite focal length is +300.0 mm.

### L4 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.60340, νd = 38.0. Glass: S-TIM5 (OHARA catalog equivalent), a flint. f = +242.3 mm.

Isolated 60 mm behind L3, with a weak front face (R7 = 156.00 mm) and an almost flat rear. The front face is the patent's key condition: it is no stronger than aplanatic for the converging paraxial beam leaving L3, which keeps L4's astigmatism very small or negative. In the chromatic chain the patent has it over-correct the color of the first three lenses.

### L5 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.51110, νd = 60.5. Glass: NSL7 (OHARA catalog equivalent), a crown. f = −154.2 mm.

The thin rear negative meniscus, concave to the object, 33 mm behind L4. It supplies the telephoto's negative power, corrects the astigmatism of the front elements and substantially cancels the remaining color. Because L4 adds little astigmatism, its front radius (r9 = 0.18F, 54.00 mm here) stays long enough to keep coma small; the patent's limit is 0.12F.

## Glass Identification

The patent gives d-line indices and Abbe numbers to four decimals and one decimal. Each pair matches a catalog glass within that precision; the supplier of the production glass is not established.

| Element | Patent nd / νd | Catalog equivalent | Catalog nd / νd | Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1, L3 | 1.5163 / 64.0 | S-BSL7 (OHARA) | 1.51633 / 64.14 | Borosilicate crown |
| L2 | 1.7174 / 29.5 | PBH1 (OHARA) | 1.71736 / 29.51 | Lead dense flint |
| L4 | 1.6034 / 38.0 | S-TIM5 (OHARA) | 1.60342 / 38.03 | Flint |
| L5 | 1.5111 / 60.5 | NSL7 (OHARA) | 1.51112 / 60.49 | Crown |

The palette is the patent's "inexpensive glass": three common crowns, a dense flint and an ordinary flint, with no special-dispersion material. The patent prints no partial dispersions, so behavior away from the d-line rests on the catalog equivalents.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent names no focusing group or movement and prints the infinity state only, so no gap varies and the focus control stays at infinity. Pentax's retail minimum focus of 5.0 m is not traced.

The final gap of 120.48 mm is the printed back focus of 40.16 at f = 100. The computed paraxial back focus is 120.531 mm (40.177 at f = 100); the 0.05 mm difference is rounding in the printed table, and the model keeps the printed plane.

## Aperture Stop and Clear Apertures

The patent neither prints nor draws a stop, and Fig. 1 shows none. The model places it 3 mm behind S8, at the front of the 33 mm air space before L5, following the PhotonsToPhotos reconstruction; this is a modeling choice. At f/4 the entrance pupil radius is 37.5 mm and the stop radius 21.98 mm. The aperture scale runs from f/4 to f/45, as in Ricoh's table.

The patent prints no semi-diameters, so all are inferred, from the PhotonsToPhotos clear diameters checked against Fig. 1. The figure keeps the prescription's axial spacings to scale, so its rim heights read at that scale are:

| Element | Model sd (mm) | Fig. 1 reading × 3 (mm) | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | 37.50 | 36.3 | f/4 entrance pupil |
| L2 | 35.43 | 33.8 | Just above the f/4 axial beam (35.01 mm at S3) |
| L3 | 35.43 | 33.9 | Drawn at L2's height |
| L4 | 25.47 | 26.1 | Fig. 1 |
| L5 | 17.25 | 17.4 | Fig. 1 |

The figure draws L1 to L3 a few percent inside the f/4 axial beam, so those rims follow the beam and the drawn step order, L1 above L2 and L3 at one height. L1's rim equals the f/4 entrance pupil, so the oblique beam is cut from the first surface on. Of the f/4 beam, about 77 % passes at 4°, about 46 % at the patent's 8° edge and about 41 % at the 8.4° frame corner.

## Model Scope and Limitations

| Quantity | Patent, f = 100 | Patent × 3 | Model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Focal length | 100 | 300 | 300.051 |
| Back focus | 40.16 | 120.48 | 120.531 paraxial |
| S1 to S10 | 49.70 | 149.10 | 149.10 |
| Half-field | 8° | 8° | 8.5° (traced to the 6x7 corner) |

- The prescription is the patent's, not a confirmed factory one, and the association with the 6x7 lens is probable rather than established (see above).
- Stop position and all semi-diameters are inferred.
- The image plane is the printed back focus.
- The model is static at infinity and says nothing about performance at 5.0 m.
- The model traces to 8.5°, past the 6x7 corner at 8.4° (Ricoh's 17° diagonal); the patent states 8°, so the outer 6 % of the frame diagonal lies beyond the field its plots cover.
- The patent gives d-line indices and Abbe numbers only, so chromatic results depend on the catalog equivalents.
