# PENTAX SUPER-TAKUMAR 24mm f/3.5 — Audit Log

## Source

Yasuo Takahashi, _Compact Wide Angle Objective Lens System with Long Back Focal Length_, assigned to Asahi Kogaku Kogyo Co., Ltd. The US application was filed July 10, 1968, with Japanese priority 42-47511 of July 24, 1967. US 3,545,845 was granted December 8, 1970. Figures 1 and 2 show the section and aberration curves (PDF p. 1). Table I gives the sole prescription in column 4 (p. 3) and again in the claim in column 6 (p. 4). Table II and the diaphragm position are in column 5 (p. 4). [US 3,545,845](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3545845A/en)

Asahi’s SL manual shows the original 24 mm Super-Takumar and specifies nine elements, f/3.5–16, an 84° field and a 0.25 m minimum focus. The Honeywell cutaway shows the later Super-Multi-Coated Takumar. [Asahi Pentax SL manual, printed p. 24](https://www.cameramanuals.org/pentax_pdf/asahi%20pentax_sl.pdf#page=27) · [Honeywell dimensioned cutaway](http://whitemetal.com/pentax/smct_24_35/smct_24_35_cutaway.gif)

## Transcription Check

The two printed tables account for all 17 distinct refracting surfaces, nine center thicknesses, seven air spaces and nine d-line index and Abbe-number pairs. The only ambiguous radius is r1, printed “22.1.0” in column 4 and clearly 221.0 in the claim. Both tables give n8 = 1.58487 and ν8 = 53.0.

Patent faces r12 and r13 share the radius −116.0 at zero separation. Table II counts their cemented interface as surface 12. Surfaces 13–17 correspond to patent faces r14–r18. L9 starts with a plane face. All other optical faces are spherical. At ×0.24 scale, column 5 places the stop 0.96 mm behind L5 in the 1.20 mm space before L6, leaving 0.24 mm behind the stop.

The film lies at the d-line paraxial image plane at infinity. With the lens wide open, the axial marginal ray and the full-frame corner chief ray clear the optical rims. The rear rims vignette part of the corner bundle. The elements have positive edge thickness and adjacent air-spaced faces stay clear of each other.

The 5.58 mm stop radius follows from the paraxial entrance pupil at f/3.5. The patent gives only the infinity layout and prints no image gap, finite-focus displacement or magnification. The near gap assumes the internal spacings stay fixed as the whole lens moves toward the subject. An object 250 mm from the film requires 38.7951 mm behind L9, an extension of 3.2983 mm. The film position minus paraxial focus is −0.000045 mm at infinity and +0.000032 mm at 0.25 m. At the near setting, the chief ray from the object through the stop center to the film corner clears each rim by at least 1.28 mm.

## Product Identification

The original M42 Super-Takumar section matches Fig. 1 in all nine element shapes and eight air-separated groups. The nearly flat rear face of L2, thick low-power L4, L6/L7 doublet immediately behind the iris and plano-convex L9 distinguish this construction. The later Honeywell cutaway shows the same sequence. Together the drawings strongly identify the sole example with the Super-Takumar. The maker’s drawings give neither glass names nor finite-focus spacings. [Asahi Pentax SL manual, p. 24](https://www.cameramanuals.org/pentax_pdf/asahi%20pentax_sl.pdf#page=27) · [Honeywell cutaway](http://whitemetal.com/pentax/smct_24_35/smct_24_35_cutaway.gif)

The Takumar Field Guide dates the original model 43961 to 1967–1971. Its multicoated M42 successor, 43962, retained the optics and added open-aperture metering on compatible bodies. Both have a five-blade automatic iris. The specified field is 84° across 35 mm film, compared with 83° in Fig. 2. [Takumar Field Guide, 43961](https://takumarguide.weebly.com/1--35--24-43961.html) · [Pentax Forums, M42 specifications](https://www.pentaxforums.com/lensreviews/S-M-C-Super-Takumar-24mm-F3.5.html)

## Corrections, 2026-11-25

- All lengths use ×0.24, the ratio of the marketed 24 mm to the patent’s nominal 100. The earlier factor of 24/98.565 forced the focal length to 24 mm and concealed the 1.4% shortfall. The claim’s r1 of 221.0 resolves the extra point in the body table and gives 53.04 mm at this scale.
- L8 retains the printed n8 of 1.58487. Its calculated spherical aberration coefficients for patent faces 16 and 18 are 4.79 and 8.18, compared with 4.61 and 8.00 in Table II. Substituting L6’s index of 1.56883 gives 4.61 and 8.01, a focal length of 100.004 and the tabulated Petzval sum of 0.09 to two decimal places. These values suggest an error but do not establish a replacement glass.
- The 5° half-field limit gave way to the full 36 × 24 mm frame. All 17 clear radii follow Fig. 1. The earlier estimates of 4.36–5.90 mm blocked the corner chief ray at surfaces 1–6 and 17. The drawn rims pass the axial beam at the patent’s full f/3.5 aperture and admit the 43.1° corner chief ray.
- The M42 Super-Takumar section identifies the 24 mm f/3.5 lens and its single cemented pair, D1. The aperture range extends from the former f/8 limit to the maker’s f/16 minimum, with f/3.5, 4, 5.6, 8, 11 and 16 available. The production specification supplies the five blades, 35 mm format and 0.25 m focus distance. [Asahi Pentax SL manual, p. 24](https://www.cameramanuals.org/pentax_pdf/asahi%20pentax_sl.pdf#page=27) · [Pentax Forums specifications](https://www.pentaxforums.com/lensreviews/S-M-C-Super-Takumar-24mm-F3.5.html)
- The infinity-only model now also reaches the marked 0.25 m. The infinity image gap follows from the paraxial back focus. The close position assumes unit focus and gives 0.139× magnification. Whole-block motion is documented on the later 43962. [Radojuva, focusing of the 43962](https://radojuva.com/en/2022/02/super-multi-coated-takumar-24mm-f-3-5/)
- Six elements have catalog equivalents for dispersion. The differences below are catalog minus patent. Each element retains its printed index and Abbe number.

| Elements | Catalog equivalent | Δnd        | Δνd   |
| -------- | ------------------ | ---------: | ----: |
| L1, L4   | Ohara S-BSL7       | −7.5 × 10⁻⁸ | +0.04 |
| L3       | Ohara S-BSM16      | +9.1 × 10⁻⁷ | −0.01 |
| L6       | Schott N-BAK4      | −2.5 × 10⁻⁶ | −0.02 |
| L7       | Sumita SF15        | +8.8 × 10⁻⁷ | +0.05 |
| L9       | Ohara S-TIL26      | +1.9 × 10⁻⁶ | +0.02 |

At infinity the model gives 23.656 mm EFL, f/3.50, 35.497 mm back focus and a 5.58 mm stop radius. The corner half-field is 43.1°.

## Remaining Questions

- At ×0.24 scale the printed n8 leaves the focal length 1.4% short of 24 mm. If the Table II Petzval terms were truncated to two decimals, they imply n8 of about 1.565–1.580. Ohara S-BAL3 (1.57135 / 52.95) also falls in that interval, preserves ν8 to the stated precision and gives 99.78 on the nominal 100 scale. Neither candidate uniquely explains the data. The intended L8 glass remains unknown.
- L2, L5 and L8 lack catalog matches at their printed index and Abbe number. The patent names no supplier for any element. The Honeywell cutaway suggests wider front rims than Fig. 1 and about 36.1 mm of back focus for the multicoated lens. The original Super-Takumar’s clear apertures remain undocumented. [Honeywell cutaway](http://whitemetal.com/pentax/smct_24_35/smct_24_35_cutaway.gif)
- The original’s mechanical focus travel is unknown. At the marked minimum distance, the calculated 3.30 mm extension gives 0.139× magnification. The production specification is 0.12×. [Pentax Forums specifications](https://www.pentaxforums.com/lensreviews/S-M-C-Super-Takumar-24mm-F3.5.html)
