# PENTAX SUPER-TAKUMAR 24mm f/3.5 — audit

## Source

Yasuo Takahashi, US 3,545,845, assigned to Asahi Kogaku Kogyo Co., Ltd., published December 8, 1970. Table I in column 4 and the claim in column 6 describe the same nine-element objective. Figure 1 gives its section, Fig. 2 its aberration curves and Table II its Seidel coefficients. [US 3,545,845](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3545845A/en)

## Transcription

The 17 distinct refracting surfaces, nine center thicknesses, seven air spaces and nine index/Abbe pairs agree with the two printings of Table I. All lengths use a factor of 0.24 from the stated focal length of 100 to the marked 24 mm. The claim gives r1 as 221.0, clarifying the extra point in “22.1.0” in the body table. This yields a front radius of 53.04 mm.

Patent faces r12 and r13 have the same radius, −116.0, and zero separation. They form the single L6/L7 cemented interface, surface 12. Surfaces 13–17 correspond to patent faces r14–r18. The diaphragm divides the 1.20 mm L5–L6 air gap into 0.96 mm in front of the stop and 0.24 mm behind it, as specified in column 5. The first face of L9 is plane. All other optical faces are spherical.

The physical stop radius of 5.58 mm follows from the paraxial f/3.5 entrance pupil. At full aperture the axial marginal ray clears the glass edges, as does the chief ray to the corner of the 36 × 24 mm frame. The edge thicknesses and spaces between adjacent surfaces remain positive. The rear rims vignette part of the corner bundle.

The infinity image gap is the d-line paraxial back focal distance. For the 0.25 m setting, the rear gap was calculated for an object and image 250 mm apart with all internal spacings fixed. The gap is 38.7951 mm, an extension of 3.2983 mm between the rounded positions. The signed differences between the film plane and paraxial focus are −0.000045 mm at infinity and +0.000032 mm at 0.25 m. A ray from the finite object through the stop center to the film corner clears every glass rim by at least 1.28 mm. Neither close-focus spacings nor magnification appear in the patent.

## Product Identification

The SL manual’s section of the M42 Super-Takumar agrees with Fig. 1 in all nine element shapes, the eight air-separated groups, the thick low-power L4, the L6/L7 cemented pair just behind the iris and the plano-convex L9. The manufacturer gives an 84° field, f/3.5–16 and 0.25 m minimum focus. The later Super-Multi-Coated Takumar cutaway independently shows the same layout. These distinctive features strongly associate the sole example with the product. The manufacturer’s drawings establish the construction but give no glass names or finite-focus prescription. ## Corrections

### 2026-11-25

The model retains the claim’s unambiguous r1 and the printed L8 index, 1.58487, at νd 53.0. At the nominal scale the focal length falls 1.44% short. For patent faces 16 and 18 the spherical aberration coefficients are 4.79 and 8.18, against Table II’s 4.61 and 8.00. With L8 at 1.56883 they become 4.61 and 8.01, the focal length becomes 100.004, and the Petzval sum agrees with the printed 0.09 to two decimal places. If the Petzval terms in Table II were truncated to two decimal places, they put the L8 index between about 1.565 and 1.580. An alternative with an Abbe number close to the printed 53.0 is Ohara S-BAL3 (1.57135 / 52.95), which gives a focal length of 99.78. Neither candidate accounts uniquely for the data. Both copies of Table I print 1.58487.

## Remaining Questions

The factory glass for L8 is unknown. L2 and L5 also lack catalog equivalents at the printed index and Abbe number. For the remaining six elements the catalog names describe optical equivalents. The patent identifies no supplier.

The later cutaway suggests wider front glass rims than Fig. 1 and about 36.1 mm of back focus. The original Super-Takumar’s clear apertures and mechanical focus travel have no published dimensions in these sources. The inferred extension gives 0.139× at the marked minimum distance, compared with the 0.12× listed for the production lens.
