## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [US 4,934,797 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4934797A/en)
**Filed:** 1989-09-26
**Granted:** 1990-06-19
**Inventor:** Jun Hirakawa
**Assignee:** Asahi Kogaku Kogyo Co., Ltd.
**Title:** Wide-angle lens system of a retrofocus type
**Example details:** Example 4, column 7 (PDF page 13); section Fig. 10, aberrations Figs. 11–12; priority JP 63-247146 (30 September 1988). All dimensions are scaled uniformly ×0.24 from the patent's f = 100.

The patent cover spells the assignee “Asahi Kogaku Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha.” The Google Patents record gives the same original assignee as “Asahi Kogaku Kogyo Co Ltd”, and its 1989-09-26 assignment entry keeps the cover spelling. The data file uses the corpus form “Asahi Kogaku Kogyo Co., Ltd.”, a rendering of the corporate suffix, not a different entity.

Example 4 is a probable production correlation for the smc PENTAX-FA★24mmF2 AL[IF] (1991), not a confirmed factory prescription. The [Ricoh/Pentax specification table](https://www.ricoh-imaging.co.jp/japan/products/filmcamera/lens/index35_others.html) gives 11 elements in 9 groups, an 84° angle of view, 0.3 m minimum focus, 0.13× maximum magnification, f/22 minimum aperture and a 67 mm filter, and the [maker's construction diagram](https://www.ricoh-imaging.co.jp/japan/products/filmcamera/lens/image/construct/w-fa24f2.jpg) marks one aspherical and one anomalous low-dispersion element. The evidence converges:

1. Example 4 has 11 glass elements in 9 groups, as listed.
2. The diagram's aspherical element is the biconcave negative behind the cemented pair that follows the iris, the position of the patent's resin-coated L8.
3. The diagram marks the rear positive meniscus as anomalous low-dispersion glass; Example 4 prints nd 1.61800 / νd 63.4 there, a phosphate-crown value.
4. Example 4 is f/2.0 with ω = 42.1° (2ω = 84.2°), matching F2 and 84°.
5. The patent focuses by moving only the rear group with the front group fixed, consistent with the [IF] designation, and its 1988 priority precedes the 1991 release.

Only Examples 3 and 4 use the 1.618/63.4 rear glass (Examples 1 and 2 print 1.69680/55.5 and Example 5 1.71300/53.8), and Example 3 is f/2.1, so Example 4 is the only f/2.0 example with that rear element. No radius, spacing or glass value is borrowed from another example.

## Optical Architecture

The retrofocus layout has a weakly divergent fixed front group (L1–L3, S1–S6; f ≈ −96.0 mm at this scale) and a convergent rear group (L4–L11, S7–S21; f ≈ +29.2 mm) that moves to focus; the diagram brackets them as Front and Rear, the patent's own names (subscripts F and R in its conditions). Eleven glass elements form nine groups: the front group holds three singlets; the rear group holds a negative meniscus, a thick biconvex positive, the iris, a cemented negative–positive pair (C1), the hybrid aspheric negative (H1), a second cemented negative–positive pair (C2) and a positive meniscus convex to the image. The example meets the patent's conditions (1) 1.1 < hR/hF < 1.3 and (2) −0.5 < f/fF < 0.0 with the printed values 1.200 and −0.250, which the model reproduces (1.2004 and −0.2500).

The hybrid asphere's resin layer occupies S14–S15, 1.22 thick at f = 100 or 0.2928 mm here, with the distinct printed radii −114.137 and −88.794 (f = 100). It is drawn as a separate medium bonded to L8, labelled L8r, and is not counted as an element. The patent numbers no elements; the labels L1–L11 follow the element order.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Focal lengths below are for each element alone in air at the 24 mm scale.

### L1: Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.72916, νd = 54.7. Glass: S-LAL18 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +394.782 mm.

Weak positive first element of the front group, nearly plano-convex. It adds little power; the front group as a whole is held near afocal, as condition (2) requires.

### L2: Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.83400, νd = 37.2. Glass: S-LAH60 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = −41.241 mm.

The main negative element of the front group, convex to the object, and the source of the retrofocus back focus. It raises the paraxial ray height at the rear group by the factor that condition (1) bounds.

### L3: Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.76180, νd = 27.1. Glass: PBH25 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +104.013 mm.

Biconvex positive closing the front group. The patent pairs it with L2 so that the front group stays nearly afocal; the paraxial ray height at the rear group then changes little as that group moves to focus, which keeps spherical aberration stable.

### L4: Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.72916, νd = 54.7. Glass: S-LAL18 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = −37.751 mm.

Negative meniscus, convex to the object, at the front of the focusing rear group, as the patent's description of the rear group specifies.

### L5: Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: PBH6 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +30.772 mm.

Thick biconvex positive just ahead of the iris; it supplies most of the rear group's positive power before the stop.

### L6: Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.74000, νd = 28.3. Glass: S-TIH3 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = −18.632 mm.

Front element of the cemented pair C1 behind the iris. The patent allows either a positive–negative or a negative–positive pair at this position; Example 4 uses negative–positive.

### L7: Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.80440, νd = 39.6. Glass: S-LAH63 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +13.948 mm.

Rear element of C1 and the strongest positive element in the rear group.

### L8r: Bonded Aspheric Resin Layer

nd = 1.52010, νd = 50.8. Glass: Unmatched (aspheric resin layer). f = +181.544 mm.

The patent states that, for economic reasons, its aspheric lenses are spherical lenses with a transparent resin coating applied, and that all-glass aspheres may be used instead. This 0.2928 mm layer carries the aspheric surface S14 on the front of L8 and is modeled as its own medium.

### L8: Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.78472, νd = 25.7. Glass: S-TIH11 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = −19.244 mm.

Glass substrate of the hybrid H1. The patent places this aspheric negative lens near the stop, where the asphere acts on axial rays while leaving off-axis rays largely unaffected, to correct the spherical aberration that a retrofocus rear group tends to leave undercorrected.

### L9: Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: PBH6 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = −106.557 mm.

Front element of the cemented pair C2, concave to the object; the cemented face is nearly flat (R = −1508.807 at f = 100).

### L10: Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.6. Glass: LAH66 (OHARA catalog equivalent). f = +36.168 mm.

Rear element of C2, convex to the image.

### L11: Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.61800, νd = 63.4. Glass: PCD4 (HOYA catalog equivalent) — inferred anomalous dispersion. f = +52.560 mm.

Rear positive meniscus, convex to the image, as the patent specifies. Ricoh's diagram marks this position as anomalous low-dispersion glass, and the printed pair matches phosphate crowns such as PCD4 (catalog θgF 0.540, slightly above the normal line); the patent itself prints no partial dispersion.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent prints d-line nd and νd only and names no glass. Each label is a catalog row matching the printed pair within its printed precision; these are catalog equivalents, not confirmed production melts. Where several rows tie, the row whose catalog νd rounds to the printed value was preferred, remaining ties follow the OHARA majority, and older OHARA names are used where the catalog holds them. The printed νd 37.2 and 25.4 and nd 1.76180 and 1.80440 fit OHARA rows rather than the HOYA alternatives (NBFD10 νd 37.3, FD60 νd 25.5, FD140 nd 1.76182, NBFD3 nd 1.80450), while νd 63.4 fits HOYA PCD4 rather than OHARA S-PHM52 (63.3).

The glass labels resolve to catalog dispersion formulas, so color is traced on the catalog curves; the resin layer uses an Abbe-number estimate. Without printed partial dispersions the data cannot establish secondary-spectrum performance.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent focuses by moving only the rear group toward the object while the front group stays fixed; the arrow in Fig. 10 shows the direction. Example 4 prints one near state, at 1/10 magnification:

| Spacing           | Infinity | 1/10     |
| ----------------- | -------- | -------- |
| d6 (front–rear), ×0.24 | 3.81 mm  | 1.19 mm  |
| Back focus, ×0.24 | 36.75 mm | 39.37 mm |

At f = 100 these are d6 15.87 → 4.95 and fB 153.12 → 164.04. The track (441.09 at f = 100, 105.86 mm here) stays constant, so the rear group travels 2.62 mm. A paraxial calculation from the printed values gives magnification −0.0999, with the object about 0.34 m from the image. This is not the marketed close-focus state: Pentax specifies a 0.3 m minimum focus distance and 0.13× maximum magnification. The model covers infinity focus only; the printed near state is not modeled.

## Aspherical Surfaces

Only S14, the object-side face of the resin layer on L8, is aspherical. The patent's equation is x = ch²/(1 + √(1 − (K + 1)c²h²)) + ΣAₙhⁿ, so its K is the standard conic constant; Example 4 prints K = 0, a spherical base, and no conversion is needed. Printed coefficients at f = 100: A4 = −7.869 × 10⁻⁷, A6 = −5.331 × 10⁻¹¹, A8 = +1.765 × 10⁻¹⁴, A10 = 0. With the uniform scale s = 0.24 each coefficient becomes A_p / s^(p−1); K is unchanged. The resin's spherical rear face (S15) on the glass keeps its own printed radius.

The negative A4 deepens the concave face toward the rim, so the surface's negative power increases outward, as the patent requires of an asphere near the stop. Condition (3), 3.7 < log(ΔX_H / ΔX_H/2) / log 2 < 4.3, measures how close the departure is to pure fourth order; the patent gives 4.0003 for this example.

## Model Scope and Limitations

At f = 100 the printed prescription traces to EFL 99.998 and back focus 153.122, against the printed 100.00 and 153.12. At the 24 mm scale the model has EFL 23.9996 mm, f/2.0 and its image plane 105.862 mm behind the first vertex. The patent's half-field is 42.1°; the model's chief ray reaches the 21.6 mm full-frame corner at 42.7° unobstructed.

The patent prints no stop position. The iris sits at the midpoint of d10, between L5 and C1, where the text's element order places the diaphragm; the exact station is inferred. Semi-diameters are estimated from Fig. 10 and checked by real-ray trace; they are not maker values, and they vignette the corner bundle strongly (up to about 64 % of one side at S21). As the figure draws them, the deep concave rear faces of L2 and L4 end at 16.7 mm and 10.5 mm, and flat lands continue to the 20 mm and 12 mm element edges.

The engine sets the f/2 iris paraxially (9.46 mm radius). A real ray at the full f/2 entrance radius meets the stop at about 10.1 mm and would graze S9–S11 (10 mm), so the real-ray working aperture at the paraxial iris is slightly slower than f/2. Residual zonal spherical aberration at the d line is about −0.08 mm at 0.7 of the pupil and −0.035 mm at the rim (24 mm scale), so best axial focus lies slightly ahead of the paraxial image plane; the model keeps the patent's plane.
