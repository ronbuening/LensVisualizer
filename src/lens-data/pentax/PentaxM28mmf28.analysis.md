## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 4,143,945 A  
**Filed:** 1977-07-28  
**Granted:** 1979-03-13  
**Priority:** 1976-07-29 (JP 51-90572)  
**Inventor:** Toshifumi Kubota  
**Assignee:** Asahi Kogaku Kogyo Co., Ltd.  
**Title:** Small retro-focus wide angle photographic lens system  
**Example details:** Example III, repeated in claim 3; section in Fig. 5, aberration plots in Fig. 6. The patent is normalized to F = 100; all lengths, including the inferred semi-diameters, are multiplied by exactly 0.28 for f = 28 mm.

This is a patent design associated with the first version of the SMC Pentax-M 28mm f/2.8, introduced in 1977, not a confirmed factory prescription. The association rests on four points:

1. The patent's seven single lenses in seven air-spaced components match the retail construction of seven elements in seven groups.
2. At the 0.28 scale the example is a 28 mm f/2.8, and its printed half-field of 37.5° gives the 75° angle of view of a 28 mm lens on 35 mm film.
3. The patent's stated aim is a retrofocus wide-angle lens "particularly suited to miniaturization", the brief of the compact M series.
4. The July 1976 priority date falls shortly before the lens's introduction.

The retail 49 mm filter, f/2.8 to f/22 range, five-blade diaphragm and 0.3 m minimum focus fit a lens of this kind, but none of them fixes the radii, clear apertures or iris position. A later version of the lens, from about 1982, is reported to use a different optical formula; this page models the first. The faster SMC Pentax-M 28mm f/2 belongs to a different patent family.

Example III is the one transcribed. Examples I and II share the layout but each carries a print inconsistency of its own (a positive final radius in Example II, a sign ambiguity between Example I's table and its claim), and neither is used here.

## Optical Architecture

Seven spherical singlets form a retrofocus lens: a negative front group of L1 (negative meniscus), L2 (positive meniscus) and L3 (negative meniscus), a long air space d6, and a positive rear group of L4 (thick biconvex), L5 (biconcave), L6 and L7 (positive menisci concave to the object). The power sequence is N-P-N, then P-N-P-P. There are no cemented surfaces, aspheric surfaces or cover plates.

The front group has a composite focal length of −26.14 mm (−93.37 at F = 100). Placed 9.285 mm ahead of the rear group, it pushes the back focus to 36.198 mm, 1.29 times the focal length, which leaves room for the reflex mirror. The lens is 34.810 mm from S1 to S14 and the image plane lies 71.008 mm behind S1.

The patent's text defines d6 as the distance between the front and rear groups, belonging to neither. Its six conditions set the layout, and Example III meets all of them:

| Condition | Range at F = 100 | Example III |
|---|---|---|
| (1) d6 | 25 to 38 | 33.16 |
| (2) \|F1,2,3\|, F1,2,3 < 0 | 76.9 to 142.9 | 93.37 |
| (3) F1,2,3,4 | 125 to 200 | 136.44 |
| (4) \|F1,2,3,4,5\|, F1,2,3,4,5 < 0 | 87.0 to 153.8 | 121.66 |
| (5) Σd | 100 to 150 | 124.32 |
| (6) df / db | 0.25 to 0.5 | 0.379 |

The patent explains that a shorter d6 would force stronger front negatives to keep the back focus, worsening coma and reducing the Petzval sum, while a longer d6 needs extra components. Condition (6) reflects the other trade: the front elements sit close together to keep the lens diameter small, which leaves the rear group comparatively long.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.49782, νd = 66.8. Glass: N-BK10 (SCHOTT catalog equivalent), a borosilicate crown (catalog 1.49782 / 66.95). f = −329.4 mm.

The weakest element, convex to the object, with its stronger curve (R2 = 24.62 mm) toward the image as the claims require of the first and third lenses. It is the largest element, at 12.04 mm semi-diameter.

### L2 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.67790, νd = 50.7. Glass: LACL9 (HOYA) equivalent, catalog νd 50.55. f = +63.18 mm.

The only positive element of the front group, convex to the object and 0.098 mm from L3 on the axis. It offsets part of the negative power of L1 and L3 and, in the usual retrofocus arrangement, part of the barrel distortion they introduce.

### L3 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.62041, νd = 60.3. Glass: S-BSM16 (OHARA) equivalent. f = −18.66 mm.

The strongest negative element and the source of most of the front group's power. Its deep rear surface (R6 = 9.43 mm) faces the long air space. Condition (2) bounds the front group's composite focal length.

### L4 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.80440, νd = 39.6. Glass: S-LAH63 (OHARA) equivalent. f = +21.00 mm.

The strongest element of the lens, 6.84 mm thick (24.42 at F = 100), directly behind the stop. Condition (3) sets its power together with the front group: the patent says it "uncorrects" the excess color correction of the front group while correcting the other aberrations.

### L5 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.9. Glass: PBH53 (OHARA) equivalent. f = −21.29 mm.

The only negative element of the rear group. Condition (4) fixes its power, and the patent credits it with correcting color, spherical aberration and coma. A weaker L5 would need a flint of still higher dispersion, which the patent says was not available, or extremely low-dispersion glass in L6 and L7 at the cost of aberration balance.

### L6 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.61800, νd = 63.4. Glass: PCD4 (HOYA) equivalent. f = +31.12 mm.

Concave to the object, with its stronger curve toward the image as claimed. With L7 it carries the final convergence to the image.

### L7 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.61800, νd = 63.4. Glass: PCD4 (HOYA) equivalent. f = +54.18 mm.

The same glass and form as L6, 0.246 mm behind it. It is the tallest rear element in Fig. 5, and its rim carries the oblique beam to the format corner.

## Glass Identification

The patent defines n as the d-line index. The table gives catalog equivalents by index and Abbe number; the supplier of the production glass is not established.

| Element | Patent nd / νd | Catalog equivalent | Catalog nd / νd | Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | 1.49782 / 66.8 | N-BK10 (SCHOTT) | 1.49782 / 66.95 | Borosilicate crown |
| L2 | 1.67790 / 50.7 | LACL9 (HOYA) | 1.67790 / 50.55 | Lanthanum crown |
| L3 | 1.62041 / 60.3 | S-BSM16 (OHARA) | 1.62041 / 60.29 | Dense barium crown |
| L4 | 1.80440 / 39.6 | S-LAH63 (OHARA) | 1.80440 / 39.59 | High-index lanthanum glass |
| L5 | 1.84666 / 23.9 | PBH53 (OHARA) | 1.84666 / 23.89 | Dense flint |
| L6, L7 | 1.61800 / 63.4 | PCD4 (HOYA) | 1.61800 / 63.40 | Dense phosphate crown |

L1 matches SCHOTT N-BK10 in nd exactly, with νd 0.15 higher; that row was added to the site's glass catalog for this lens, so all seven elements trace on catalog dispersion curves. The patent prints no partial dispersions. The palette is conventional: crowns in the front group, one high-index positive and one dense flint behind the stop, and two phosphate crowns at the rear.

## Focus Mechanism and Image Plane

The patent names no focusing group or movement and prints the infinity state only, so no gap varies and the focus control stays at infinity. The 0.3 m minimum focus is the retail specification and is not traced. The patent ties condition (5), on overall length, to the needs of the focusing mechanism.

The final gap of 36.198 mm is the printed back focus of 129.28 at F = 100. The computed paraxial back focus is 36.193 mm (129.26 at F = 100); the 0.006 mm difference is rounding in the printed table, and the model keeps the printed plane.

## Aperture Stop and Clear Apertures

The patent neither prints nor draws a stop, and Fig. 5 shows none. The model places it at the midpoint of d6, 11.656 mm behind S1, a modeling choice. At f/2.8 the entrance pupil radius is 5.00 mm and the stop radius 5.60 mm.

The patent prints no semi-diameters, so all are inferred. Fig. 5 read at its own axial scale gives:

The Petzval sum is 0.189 at F = 100.

- The prescription is the patent's, not a confirmed factory one, and it represents the first version of the lens only.
- Stop position and all semi-diameters are inferred.
- The image plane is the printed back focus.
- The model is static at infinity and says nothing about performance at 0.3 m.
- The 37.5° edge reaches 21.19 mm of image height, 0.46 mm short of the format corner; rays beyond 37.5° extend past the patent's stated field.
- The patent gives d-line indices and Abbe numbers only, so behavior away from the d-line rests on the catalog equivalents, and for L1 on its printed pair.
