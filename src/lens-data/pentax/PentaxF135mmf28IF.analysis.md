# Optical Analysis: smc PENTAX-F 135mm F2.8 [IF]

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 4,793,698  
**Filed:** 2 April 1987  
**Granted:** 27 December 1988  
**Priority:** 2 April 1986 (JP 61-75771)  
**Inventor:** Hiroyuki Hirano  
**Assignee:** Asahi Kogaku Kogyo Co., Ltd.  
**Title:** Telephoto Lens System  
**Example details:** Example 1, Figs. 1–3; all lengths uniformly scaled ×1.35 from the printed f = 100.

This is a patent design associated with the smc PENTAX-F 135mm F2.8 [IF], not a confirmed factory prescription. Published listings give that lens seven groups and eight elements, an F2.8–F32 aperture range, a 0.7 m minimum focus distance, a 52 mm filter, an 80 mm length and an 18° angle of view. The later FA 135mm F2.8 [IF] is listed with the same construction and a maximum magnification of 0.25×; that the two share their optics rests on report only. The ×1.35 scale is a modeling choice, since the patent states no production focal length.

The association rests on five points:

1. **Construction.** Example 1 has eight spherical elements in seven groups.
2. **Class.** f = 100, F2.8 and a 9.4° half-field scale to 135 mm, and the printed image height Y = 16.5 scales to 22.3 mm, which covers the 135-format corner.
3. **Focusing.** Two inner units move and the overall length stays fixed, as [IF] implies. The printed close state of 1/4 magnification matches the FA's listed 0.25×.
4. **Length.** S1 lies 125.8 mm from the image plane, so 80.3 mm ahead of the 45.46 mm K-mount flange, against the listed 80 mm.
5. **Timing.** The priority date is April 1986 and the lens is reported from 1987.

Example 1 is a fit, not proof. All five examples share f = 100, F2.8, the 9.4° half-field, the eight-element, seven-group layout and the 1/4 close state, so these points do not single one out. Examples 1 and 3 are the shortest, at 125.8 and 125.7 mm at this scale against 127.1–127.8 mm for the others. Pentax's drawing of the FA version shows a meniscus cemented doublet, which favours Examples 1 and 3 over the biconvex/biconcave doublets of Examples 2, 4 and 5. At the drawing's low resolution, its cemented interface also looks the steepest of the three doublet faces, as in Example 1 rather than Example 3.

## Optical Architecture

Eight spherical elements in seven groups form a telephoto with a positive, negative, negative, positive unit sequence and a telephoto ratio of 0.93. Unit 10 comprises L1–L4 (f ≈ +97 mm); unit 20 is the cemented L5/L6 doublet (f ≈ −99 mm); unit 30 is L7 (f ≈ −288 mm); unit 40 is L8 (f ≈ +97 mm). Units 10 and 40 stay fixed while units 20 and 30 move by different amounts to focus. Surface 10 is a cemented interface, not an air gap.

The prescription reproduces all five printed conditional values: f/f2,3 = −1.893, f2/f3 = 0.343, X3/X2 = 0.600, d2-3/f = 0.108 and d3-4/f = 0.141.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Element focal lengths below are isolated thick-element calculations in air. Values for cemented elements are not their in-assembly contributions.

### L1 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.1. Glass: S-FSL5 (Ohara) catalog equivalent. Isolated f = +165.386 mm.

A low-index, high-Abbe positive front meniscus supplies converging power at the front of unit 10. The patent calls for low-dispersion glass here to offset the chromatic weakness of a positive, positive, positive, negative front unit.

### L2 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.58913, νd = 61.0. Glass: S-BAL35 (Ohara) catalog equivalent. Isolated f = +126.660 mm.

The second positive meniscus contributes positive power within the fixed front unit, 0.18 mm behind L1.

### L3 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.71300, νd = 53.8. Glass: S-LAL8 (Ohara) catalog equivalent. Isolated f = +87.380 mm.

The third positive meniscus has the highest index and the strongest curvature of the three. It precedes the negative member that completes the front unit.

### L4 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.76182, νd = 27.5 as printed (see Glass Identification and Selection). Glass: S-TIH14 (Ohara), SF14 class. Isolated f = −45.025 mm.

The negative meniscus completes unit 10, with the iris behind it. Fig. 1 chamfers the rim of its strongly curved rear face, so S8's clear aperture sits below S7's.

### L5 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.6. Glass: S-LAH66 (Ohara) catalog equivalent. Isolated f = −54.065 mm.

The negative member of cemented unit 20 opens at S9 and shares S10 with L6. The patent wants this moving unit achromatic in itself, so that focusing disturbs chromatic correction as little as possible.

### L6 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.74077, νd = 27.8. Glass: S-TIH13 (Ohara) catalog equivalent. Isolated f = +110.578 mm.

The positive member of unit 20 shares the S10 interface. Its dense flint pairs with the higher-Abbe lanthanum glass of L5 to achromatize the unit.

### L7 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Glass: S-BSL7 (Ohara) catalog equivalent. Isolated f = −287.726 mm.

This weak negative meniscus forms moving unit 30. The patent gives it part of the correction of unit 10's spherical aberration and of the Petzval sum, and prefers the lowest practical dispersion when the unit is a single negative element.

### L8 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.53172, νd = 48.9. Glass: LLF6 (Sumita) catalog equivalent. Isolated f = +96.988 mm.

The final positive element forms fixed unit 40, which the patent uses to balance field curvature and distortion.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent prints Nd and νd at the d line and names no glass. Each element carries a catalog equivalent that matches those values; the production supplier is not established.

| Element | Catalog equivalent | nd | νd (printed) | Class |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | S-FSL5 (Ohara) | 1.48749 | 70.1 | FK5 fluor crown |
| L2 | S-BAL35 (Ohara) | 1.58913 | 61.0 | SK5 dense crown |
| L3 | S-LAL8 (Ohara) | 1.71300 | 53.8 | LaK8 lanthanum crown |
| L4 | S-TIH14 (Ohara) | 1.76182 | 27.5 | SF14 dense flint |
| L5 | S-LAH66 (Ohara) | 1.77250 | 49.6 | LaF34 lanthanum flint |
| L6 | S-TIH13 (Ohara) | 1.74077 | 27.8 | SF13 dense flint |
| L7 | S-BSL7 (Ohara) | 1.51633 | 64.1 | BK7 borosilicate crown |
| L8 | LLF6 (Sumita) | 1.53172 | 48.9 | LLF6 extra-light flint |

**Source erratum.** The table and claim 7 print νd 27.5 for L4, which disagrees with nd 1.76182, the SF14 class at about 26.5. Example 4 uses the same index and prints 26.5. Figs. 2 and 3 fit 26.5: their paraxial g-line offsets read +0.07 and +0.45 at f = 100, S-TIH14 traces +0.07 and +0.45, and νd 27.5 would give −0.16 and +0.14. The file keeps the printed value, and the chromatic trace uses S-TIH14's catalog dispersion, which corresponds to νd 26.5.

## Focus Mechanism and Image Plane

Focusing is internal with two moving units. Units 20 and 30 move toward the image by different amounts while units 10 and 40 and the iris stay fixed, so the overall length does not change. Unit 20 travels 24.78 mm and unit 30 travels 14.87 mm. The patent prints each gap at infinity and at 1/4 magnification:

| Gap | Printed (f = 100) | Infinity (mm) | 1/4 magnification (mm) |
|---|---|---|---|
| d8 | 7.247–25.603 | 9.783 | 34.564 |
| d11 | 10.753–3.410 | 14.517 | 4.604 |
| d13 | 14.075–3.061 | 19.001 | 4.132 |

The patent prints no object distance. With the image plane held fixed, the printed near gaps give a paraxial magnification of −0.2500 and an object-to-image distance of 725.0 mm, 599.2 mm from S1. That is about 3.6 % over the retail 0.7 m. The focal length falls from 135.0 mm to 112.4 mm at this state, the behaviour of internal focusing that the patent's background describes. Real-ray spherical aberration there dips to −0.43 at f = 100, against about −0.44 on Fig. 3. The near gaps sum 0.001 short at f = 100, a rounding that is left as printed. Positions between the two printed states are interpolated, not patent values.

The patent prints no back focus. The last gap, 46.385 mm, is the paraxial back focus computed at infinity. It places the image plane 125.800 mm from S1 at both states. EFL is 135.007 mm.

## Iris and Clear Apertures

The patent prints no stop position and Fig. 1 marks none. The iris sits at a fixed plane 75 % of the way through the infinity d8, 30.71 mm from S1 and 7.34 mm behind S8. The 75 % position is inferred, but Fig. 3 supports a fixed iris there. At Y = 16.5 its field curves read about −0.27 sagittal and −0.57 meridional at f = 100, and this iris traces −0.26 and −0.58. An iris moving with unit 20 traces −0.40 and −1.0 or worse, and at close focus it would need a beam about 33 mm high at L1, more than the front element offers. The wide-open radius, 14.10 mm, follows paraxial entrance pupil diameter = F/N at infinity focus, and the aperture scale runs to f/32.

The clear apertures are inferred, not measured from a lens. Fig. 1 at its axial scale gives 25.15 mm for L1, 23.98 mm for L2, 21.37 mm for L3, 16.10 mm for S8, 12.27 mm for L7 and 18.30 mm for L8. S7 (18.58 mm) and unit 20 (14.85 mm) keep earlier values within 3 % of the figure. Pentax's FA drawing also shows L8 taller than L7. L1's 50.3 mm diameter fits inside the 52 mm filter thread, and every rim clears the axial f/2.8 bundle.

## Coverage

The patent states a 9.4° half-field, or 18.8° in full. The model reaches the 21.65 mm corner of the 135 format at 9.2°, inside that field. At infinity focus and f/2.8, relative illumination is 93.1 % at 4.6°, 85.0 % at 6.1°, 75.7 % at 7.6° and 64.6 % at 9.2°. L7 does most of the corner clipping, followed by L1. These values follow from the inferred rims, so they describe this model rather than a measured lens.
