# PENTAX SMC ZOOM 28-50mm f/3.5-4.5

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 4,142,779\
**Application Number:** 794,589\
**Filed:** May 6, 1977\
**Priority:** Japan 51-52938, May 10, 1976\
**Granted:** March 6, 1979\
**Inventor:** Ryota Ogawa\
**Assignee:** Asahi Kogaku Kogyo Co., Ltd.\
**Title:** *Zoom Lens System*\
**Example details:** Example 3 table, column 6; FIG. 6; repeated unchanged in claim 3

US 4,142,779 describes compact wide-angle zooms of two groups, a negative front group and a positive rear group, and
gives four numerical examples. Examples 1 to 3 cover about 28.8-49 mm at F/3.5-4.5 with a 76°-48° angle of view;
Example 4 is a 24.5-34.2 mm design. The patent does not name a product. The data file transcribes Example 3 and
associates it with the **SMC PENTAX ZOOM 28mm f/3.5-50mm f/4.5** on the following evidence:

1. Asahi's lens catalogue lists the SMC PENTAX ZOOM 28mm f/3.5-50mm f/4.5 with 10 elements in 10 groups, a 75°-46°
   angle of view, 0.55 m minimum focus, a 52 mm filter, 315 g, and a barrel 65 mm wide and 60 mm long at 28 mm, 52 mm
   long at 50 mm. Example 3 is ten air-spaced singlets (10 elements in 10 groups) at f = 28.8-48.9 mm, F/3.5-4.5,
   76°-48°.
2. The catalogue's construction diagram shows four singlets in the front group, with a deep, flanged negative meniscus
   second, and six in the rear group. Examples 2 and 3 both have that layout. On the axis the diagram draws the third and
   fourth front elements almost touching; Example 3 separates them by 0.10 mm, Example 2 by 2.79 mm. Measured along the
   axis, the diagram's front-group vertex positions agree with Example 3 to within about 2.5 % of the group length;
   Example 2 misses by up to 10 %.
3. Example 3's total track is 98.46 mm at 28.8 mm and 90.08 mm at 48.9 mm. With the 45.46 mm K-mount flange distance,
   the front vertex sits 53.0 mm and 44.6 mm ahead of the flange, inside the catalogue's 60 mm and 52 mm barrel
   lengths. Example 2's track (109.52 and 102.11 mm) would put its front vertex 4.1-4.7 mm beyond the barrel at both
   ends. Example 3's track shortens by 8.4 mm from wide to tele; the barrel shortens by 8 mm.
4. The Japanese priority date (May 1976) precedes the lens, which secondary sources date to about 1977.

The later **SMC PENTAX-M ZOOM 28-50mm f/3.5-4.5** (about 1979-1985; 0.6 m minimum focus, 52 mm filter, 310 g) is
described by secondary sources as the same optical formula in a more compact barrel, with 10 elements in 10 groups. If
so, this file covers both versions; Asahi literature confirming the M version's optics was not found.

The prescription is used at the patent's own scale, which is already a 28.8-48.9 mm production scale; no scale factor
is applied.

## Optical Architecture

Example 3 is an all-spherical negative-positive zoom. The front group (L1-L4, focal length -47.73 mm, printed as
Fa = -47.730) is a weak positive element, a strong negative meniscus, a weak negative meniscus and a dense-flint positive
meniscus. The rear group (L5-L10, +32.16 mm) is two positive lanthanum-crown elements, a nearly afocal crown meniscus,
the biconcave dense-flint element the claims call the "double concave lens", and two positive elements behind it. All
ten elements are air-spaced; four of the air spaces (d2, d6, d10 and d18) are only 0.10 mm.

Both groups move during zooming, and only two spacings change: the group separation d8 and the back focus.

| Printed row | f (mm) | F | d8 (mm) | Back focus (mm) | Total track (mm) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wide | 28.8 | 3.5 | 26.35 | 38.035 | 98.455 |
| Middle | 40 | 4 | 11.42 | 45.582 | 91.072 |
| Tele | 48.9 | 4.5 | 4.43 | 51.579 | 90.079 |

From wide to tele the rear group moves 13.54 mm toward the object and the front group 8.38 mm toward the image, so the
lens is longest at 28 mm, as the catalogue's barrel lengths show. The front group is almost stationary near the long
end: it comes closest to the image near 47.7 mm and moves back about 0.02 mm toward the object by 48.9 mm. As in any
zoom of two rigid groups, d8 varies as a hyperbola in focal length (about -26.96 + 1535.2/f mm) and the back focus as
a straight line (about 18.63 + 0.6739 f mm, the slope being the ratio of the rear and front group focal lengths).

The patent's argument is about size: a strong negative front group lets the rear group travel farther for a given zoom
ratio, which shrinks the front diameter, but raises spherical aberration and coma at the long end. The six conditions
(below) set the strong negative power on L2's rear face, the air lens between L3 and L4, and the position of the
biconcave element in the rear group to hold those aberrations.

The patent prints no stop position and FIG. 6 draws none; its text places the stop in the rear group. The model sets the
iris midway in the 1.87 mm air space between L7 and L8. At that position one fixed iris radius gives F/3.5, F/4.06 and
F/4.50 at the three printed rows, which matches the printed F/3.5, 4 and 4.5 within 1.4 %; the variable maximum aperture
then comes from the change in pupil magnification, not from a moving iris.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.58913, νd = 61.1. Glass: S-BAL35 (OHARA) equivalent, barium crown. f = +135.8 mm (printed f1.2 = 135.798).

A weak, nearly plano-convex front element (r1 = 99.200, r2 = -408.000). Condition (2) bounds its power relative to the
front group, 2.0 < f1.2/|Fa| < 6.0; here the ratio is 2.845. The patent ties the upper limit to distortion: too weak an
L1 lets negative distortion grow at the wide end.

### L2 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.80610, νd = 40.9. Glass: LAH53 (OHARA) equivalent, lanthanum flint. f = -24.9 mm.

The front group's main negative element, convex to the object, with a deep rear face (r4 = 18.000). That face alone has
a focal length of -22.330 mm (printed f4), which condition (3) holds between 0.35 and 0.6 of Fa (0.468 here) to balance
wide-angle coma against long-end spherical aberration. FIG. 6 draws L2 with a flat-faced flange: the front surface runs
to about 17 mm, the concave rear surface ends at about 13.4 mm where a bevel meets L3.

### L3 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.63854, νd = 55.4. Glass: BSM18 (OHARA) equivalent, dense crown. f = -107.9 mm.

A weak meniscus, convex to the object (r5 = 245.958, r6 = 53.700), that shares the front group's negative power so L2
need not carry all of it. FIG. 6 draws it square-cut.

### L4 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: PBH6 (OHARA) equivalent, lead dense flint (SF6 type). f = +51.9 mm.

The front group's achromatizing positive element, convex to the object. Its front face (r7 = 25.294) stands 0.10 mm
behind L3's rear face (r6 = 53.700). The two faces form the air lens of condition (4): their combined focal length,
printed f6.7 = 50.050 mm, sits at 1.049 |Fa|, within 0.7-1.8. The patent uses this pair to balance spherical aberration
and coma at the long end.

### L5 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.7. Glass: TAF1 (HOYA) equivalent, lanthanum crown. f = +50.3 mm.

The leading element of the rear group, the "double convex lens on the object side" of the claims.

### L6 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.7. Glass: TAF1 (HOYA) equivalent, lanthanum crown. f = +33.1 mm.

The strongest positive element of the rear group, convex to the object, 0.10 mm behind L5. L5 and L6 together form the
converging front half of the rear group.

### L7 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Glass: S-BSL7 (OHARA) equivalent, borosilicate crown (BK7 type). f = -324.0 mm.

A thin meniscus of almost no power (r13 = 40.040, r14 = 31.900), concave toward the stop. It bends the rays without
adding power and closes the space ahead of the iris.

### L8 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: PBH6 (OHARA) equivalent, lead dense flint. f = -17.9 mm.

The negative element of the rear group, just behind the stop. Condition (5) fixes its position: the distance from L5's
front vertex to L8's front vertex is b = 8.16 mm against a rear-group length a = 17.97 mm, so b/a = 0.454 (0.35-0.55).
Moving it rearward shortens the back focus and raises coma; moving it forward raises spherical aberration.

### L9 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.56013, νd = 47.0. Glass: FEL3 (HOYA) equivalent, light flint. f = +36.8 mm.

The main positive element behind L8, with its stronger face (r18 = -23.537) toward the image.

### L10 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Glass: S-BSL7 (OHARA) equivalent, borosilicate crown. f = +166.2 mm.

A weak meniscus concave to the object, 0.10 mm behind L9, that completes the rear group's positive power.

## Glass Identification

The patent prints nd and νd only. Every pair matches a catalog row within 0.0001 in nd and 0.1 in νd; the labels name
that row as an equivalent, not as the glass Asahi bought. For L2, L4 and L8 the labels use OHARA's rows of the period,
LAH53 and the lead flint PBH6, rather than their later lead-free successors. FIG. 10 plots one dashed colour curve
beside the d-line spherical aberration; it is not labelled, but only the g line matches its sign and the way it shrinks
from wide to tele. With PBH6 the model's paraxial g-line focus sits 0.117, 0.085 and 0.050 mm short of the d-line focus
at 28.8, 40 and 48.9 mm, against about 0.122, 0.077 and 0.054 mm read from the plots; the lead-free S-TIH6 row would
miss by up to 0.027 mm. The model also reproduces the printed distortion: -4.6 % at 38° at the wide end, about -1 % at
29° at 40 mm and close to zero at 48.9 mm.

| Glass (equivalent) | nd | νd | Elements | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S-BAL35 (OHARA) | 1.58913 | 61.1 | L1 | Front positive crown |
| LAH53 (OHARA) | 1.80610 | 40.9 | L2 | High-index negative meniscus |
| BSM18 (OHARA) | 1.63854 | 55.4 | L3 | Negative crown meniscus |
| PBH6 (OHARA) | 1.80518 | 25.4 | L4, L8 | Lead dense flint: front-group achromat, rear negative element |
| TAF1 (HOYA) | 1.77250 | 49.7 | L5, L6 | Rear-group positive lanthanum crown |
| S-BSL7 (OHARA) | 1.51633 | 64.1 | L7, L10 | Low-power crown menisci |
| FEL3 (HOYA) | 1.56013 | 47.0 | L9 | Rear positive light flint |

The SF6-type dense flint appears in both groups with power opposite to its group's: as the positive L4 against the
negative L2 and L3 in front, and as the negative L8 against the lanthanum-crown positives L5 and L6 behind.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent prints the infinity state only and names no focusing method, so the model is infinity-only. Asahi gives a
minimum focus distance of 0.55 m for this lens. Secondary sources note that the front element rotates during focusing
on the M version, which suggests front-group helicoid focusing, the usual method for two-group zooms; this is an
inference and is not modelled.

## Conditional Expressions

The patent requires six conditions. Example 3's printed and computed values satisfy all of them.

| Condition | Range | Example 3 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (1) ΔfB / (ls · R) | 0.25-0.38 | 13.544 / (26.35 × 1.698) = 0.303 |
| (2) f1.2 / \|Fa\| | 2.0-6.0 | 135.798 / 47.730 = 2.845 |
| (3) f4 / Fa | 0.35-0.6 | -22.330 / -47.730 = 0.468 |
| (4) f6.7 / \|Fa\| | 0.7-1.8 | 50.050 / 47.730 = 1.049 |
| (5) b / a | 0.35-0.55 | 8.16 / 17.97 = 0.454 |
| (6) Σ / (2ω · Fs) | 0.040-0.056 | 98.455 / (76 × 28.8) = 0.0450 |

ΔfB is the change of back focus over the zoom, ls the group separation at the shortest focal length, R the zoom ratio,
Σ the total length including back focus, 2ω the largest angle of view in degrees and Fs the shortest focal length.

## Model Scope and Limitations

- The three printed rows are published stations. Five stations between them (31, 33.5, 36.5, 43 and 46 mm) are solved
  paraxially from the printed group structure, with d8 set for the focal length and the back focus set for focus.
  Linear interpolation between the printed rows alone would defocus the image by up to 0.62 mm near 34 mm; with the
  solved stations the largest defocus anywhere along the zoom is 0.054 mm.
- The iris position is inferred (see Optical Architecture). The solved stations carry the f-numbers the fixed iris
  gives (F/3.61 to F/4.36); the printed rows keep F/3.5, 4 and 4.5.
- No semi-diameter is printed. The rims are measured from FIG. 6, which is drawn at the 28.8 mm setting, and checked
  by real-ray trace: the full-aperture axial beam passes at every station, and the chief ray reaches the 21.6 mm
  corner of the 35 mm frame at every station (at 38.2° at the wide end, close to the patent's 76° full angle).
  Wide open at 28.8 mm the corner keeps about half of the axial illumination by geometric vignetting; at 40 and
  48.9 mm it keeps about 87 % and 90 %.
- Only infinity focus is modelled.
