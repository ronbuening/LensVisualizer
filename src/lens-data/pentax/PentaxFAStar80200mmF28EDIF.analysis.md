## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 5,572,276 A  
**Filed:** November 10, 1994  
**Granted:** November 5, 1996  
**Inventor:** Jun Hirakawa  
**Assignee:** Asahi Kogaku Kogyo Co., Ltd. (original cover: Asahi Kogaku Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha)  
**Title:** Inner Focus Type Telephoto Zoom Lens  
**Example details:** First Embodiment, Table 1 (col. 5–6); Figure 1 optical section; Figures 2–4 infinity aberrations; Figures 5–6 aberrations at 1.5 m. Unscaled millimetres, d-line indices.

The correlation with the production lens is qualified: it rests on matching construction, not on a confirmed factory prescription. Pentax lists the FA★ 80–200mm F2.8 ED [IF] with:

1. 16 elements in 13 groups. Table 1 has 16 elements, three cemented doublets and 13 air-separated groups.
2. Four ED elements. Table 1 has high-Abbe glass (νd 81.6 or 95.0) at L1, L3, L4 and L12, the positions marked ED in the maker's construction drawing.
3. Inner zoom and inner focus. Table 1's front-vertex-to-image length is 233.52 mm at all three printed focal lengths, and only the G1-2 subgroup moves to focus.
4. f/2.8 throughout. The patent prints f = 81.63–135.00–196.00 mm at F 2.8, against the marketed 80–200 mm.

Embodiment 5 also has 16 elements in 13 groups, but its front element is nd 1.69680 / νd 55.5 and it has only three high-Abbe elements. Embodiment 2 has 14 groups and Embodiments 3–4 have 17 elements in 15 groups. Embodiment 1 is therefore the closest fit. Matching ED positions does not identify the glass supplier or the production partial dispersion. Pentax rates the lens to 1.4 m and f/32; the model works at infinity only.

## Optical Architecture

The 16 glass elements form 13 air-separated groups, with cemented interfaces at S12, S19 and S24. The patent's four lens groups, positive–negative–positive–positive, are a different division from those 13 physical groups:

- **G1** (S1–8, f₁ = +104.57 mm) is split into a fixed front subgroup **G1-1** (L1, L2; S1–4) of weak negative power (f₁₋₁ = −849.26 mm) and a positive focusing subgroup **G1-2** (L3, L4; S5–8; f = +91.98 mm).
- **G2** (L5–L8; S9–15; f = −33.31 mm) is the negative variator.
- **G3** (L9–L11; S16–20; f = +79.68 mm) is the positive compensator.
- **G4** (L12–L16; S21–29; f = +150.72 mm) is the fixed positive relay, with the iris between L14 and L15.

From wide to tele, G2 moves 29.92 mm toward the image (D8 2.81 → 32.73 mm). At the printed focal lengths G3 moves 6.42 mm toward the image by 135 mm, then 6.76 mm back, ending 0.34 mm ahead of its wide position; in the interpolated stations its rearmost point, 6.71 mm behind the wide position, falls near 122 mm. D8 + D15 + D20 stays 43.04 mm, so G1, G4 and the 233.52 mm front-vertex-to-image length do not change. The patent prints spacings at the three focal lengths only; the 30 intermediate zoom positions in the model are interpolated to keep G4 fixed and paraxial focus 0.015–0.019 mm behind the printed image plane at every station, and are not the factory cam.

Table 6 gives f₁/f₁₋₁ = −0.123, inside the patent's condition −0.3 < f₁/f₁₋₁ < 0, and the two G1-2 lenses have a mean νd of 88.3, above the condition ν₁₋₂ > 75.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Element focal lengths are isolated thick-lens values calculated from Table 1; the patent does not print them.

### L1 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6. Glass: FCD1 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed) — ED position. f = +417.753 mm.

Positive ED meniscus at the front of the fixed G1-1 subgroup. The patent prefers an Abbe number above 75 for this positive lens so that the front group's chromatic aberration is corrected (col. 4).

### L2 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.78472, νd = 25.7. Glass: S-TIH11 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = −271.602 mm.

Negative meniscus, concave to the image, as the patent specifies for G1-1. Its power outweighs L1's, so G1-1 is weakly negative (f₁₋₁ = −849.26 mm).

### L3 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.43875, νd = 95.0. Glass: S-FPL53 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed) — ED position. f = +181.687 mm.

First positive lens of the G1-2 focusing subgroup, its stronger face toward the object. The patent uses only positive lenses here: the space a negative lens would take becomes focus travel, and the subgroup's strong positive power shortens that travel (col. 4).

### L4 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6. Glass: FCD1 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed) — ED position. f = +179.877 mm.

Second positive high-Abbe element in G1-2, convex to the object. With L3 it gives a mean νd of 88.3, which the patent ties to a small chromatic change as the subgroup moves for focus.

### L5 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Glass: S-BSL7 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = −71.465 mm.

Negative element beginning the G2 variator: nearly flat in front, with most of its power at the deep rear face (R = 37.251 mm).

### L6 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.69680, νd = 55.5. Glass: S-LAL14 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = −41.449 mm.

Negative front element of the first cemented pair, within G2.

### L7 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: S-TIH6 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = +50.320 mm.

Positive high-index flint partner of L6; common interface S12.

### L8 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.80400, νd = 46.6. Glass: S-LAH65 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = −108.190 mm.

Negative rear element of G2, concave to the object.

### L9 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.69680, νd = 55.5. Glass: S-LAL14 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = +116.858 mm.

Positive element beginning the G3 compensator.

### L10 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Glass: S-BSL7 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = +81.948 mm.

Positive crown in the G3 cemented pair.

### L11 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: S-TIH6 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = −119.864 mm.

Negative flint partner of L10; common interface S19.

### L12 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6. Glass: FCD1 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed) — ED position. f = +103.445 mm.

Positive high-Abbe element beginning the fixed G4 relay.

### L13 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.2. Glass: S-FSL5 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = +125.324 mm.

Positive low-index crown at the front of the third cemented pair, which sits just ahead of the iris.

### L14 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: S-TIH6 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = −3708.652 mm.

Flint partner of L13; common interface S24. Almost powerless on its own, it works mainly through the index and dispersion step at the cemented face.

### L15 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.83481, νd = 42.7. Glass: S-LAH55 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = −40.232 mm.

Strong negative meniscus just behind the iris, concave to the image. With L16 it forms the negative rear part of G4 (f = −130.36 mm for L15–L16).

### L16 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.2. Glass: S-FSL5 (catalog equivalent; supplier unconfirmed). f = +112.346 mm.

Positive rear element, 38.09 mm behind L15, completing the relay toward the image.

The patent prints nd and νd only. Each pair matches the listed catalog glass, whose dispersion data the chromatic analyses use; the production supplier is unconfirmed. L1, L3, L4 and L12 carry an inferred anomalous-dispersion tag from the maker's ED marking, not from a published partial-dispersion value. Three of the four ED elements sit in G1, where the patent sets its νd > 75 preferences. In line with the patent's aim of correcting each group on its own so that aberrations change little as the groups move (col. 4), each moving zoom group carries a crown–dense-flint cemented doublet (L6/L7 in G2, L10/L11 in G3).

## Focus Mechanism

Inner focus. The patent moves G1-2 (S5–8; L3 and L4) toward the object for closer distances, while G1-1, the zoom groups and G4 stay fixed (Figure 1). The condition −0.3 < f₁/f₁₋₁ < 0 is set so that the change in spherical aberration caused by the closer object is cancelled by the change caused by moving G1-2 (col. 4). Figures 5–6 show aberrations at 1.5 m, but the patent prints no close-focus spacings. The model is therefore **infinity-only**: both focus keyframes hold the same spacings and the focus control moves no group. Pentax rates the production lens to 1.4 m; that distance is catalog data, not a modeled state.

## Iris Position and Clear Apertures

Figure 1 places the iris between S25 and S26, where the patent prints only the 7.88 mm air space. Measured from the figure, the iris sits about 4.3–4.5 mm behind S25; the model uses 4.40 mm before it and 3.48 mm after it (**inferred**, not a factory dimension). The maker's construction drawing also puts the iris directly behind the L13/L14 doublet. The iris radius, about 14.87 mm, is derived from the f/2.8 design f-number rather than a published diameter; G4 does not move, so it holds f/2.8 at every focal length.

Clear radii are estimated from Figure 1; the patent prints none. Where an estimated rim was smaller than the f/2.8 beam, it was raised by the smallest tenth-millimetre step, keeping square rims square. The G2 cemented doublet (S11–S13) is square at 18.9 mm, as Figure 1 draws it, which leaves 0.21 mm of air at the rim of the 7.01 mm S10–S11 space; above about 19.17 mm that space would close at the edge. No radius, thickness or index was changed to fit the rims. Off-axis bundles are partly vignetted by the estimated rims.

## Image Plane and Model Scope

The printed 47.47 mm back focus is kept as the image distance. The prescription computes EFL 81.631 / 135.025 / 196.014 mm against the printed 81.63 / 135.00 / 196.00 mm, and its paraxial image lies 0.015 / 0.019 / 0.016 mm behind the printed plane. The cause of that small offset (rounding or a deliberate focus shift) is not established, and the plane is not moved to paraxial or best focus.

On-axis longitudinal spherical aberration of the d-line marginal ray is −0.037 mm at the wide end (−0.126 mm at the 0.7 zone), +0.161 mm at 135 mm and −0.225 mm at the tele end. Figures 2–4 show the same wide-negative / middle-positive / tele-negative trend; the 15–19 µm paraxial offset is about two pixels on their 0.5 mm scale and cannot be resolved there.

Everything else the patent does not print — the intermediate zoom positions, the iris position, the clear radii and the glass identities — is inferred as described above. The model has no close-focus state.
