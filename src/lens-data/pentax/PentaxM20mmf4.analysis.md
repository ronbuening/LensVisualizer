# SMC Pentax-M 20mm f/4

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 4,062,622 A  
**Filed:** 1976-05-19 (Appl. No. 687,814)  
**Granted:** 1977-12-13  
**Priority:** 1975-05-22 (Japan)  
**Inventor:** Takahiro Sugiyama  
**Assignee:** Asahi Kogaku Kogyo Co., Ltd.  
**Title:** Small size retrofocus wide angle photographic lens  
**Example details:** Example 2, section in Fig. 3, aberration plots in Fig. 4; repeated in claim 6. DE 2620571 C3 is the German family member. The patent is normalized to F = 100; every length, including the semi-diameters, is multiplied by exactly 0.2 for f = 20.0 mm.

This is a patent design associated with the SMC Pentax-M 20mm f/4, not a confirmed factory prescription. The association rests on five points:

1. Example 2 has eight singlets in eight groups, the retail construction. Example 1 cements its sixth and seventh lenses and would be eight elements in seven groups.
2. The example prints F/4 and a 94° field, the retail aperture and angle of view.
3. At the 0.2 scale it is a 20.0 mm lens with a 36.15 mm back focus, enough for a single-lens reflex mirror, from a first-to-last vertex length of 34.59 mm.
4. The May 1975 priority and May 1976 US filing precede the lens's 1977 launch. It was made for the Pentax K mount until 1984.
5. Pentax's lens catalog prints a section of eight singlets with a very thick fourth element and a separated negative-positive pair near the rear (p. 110), and its table lists eight elements in eight groups, f/4 and 94° (p. 125).

The retail 49 mm filter, f/4 to f/22 range and 0.25 m minimum focus fit a lens of this kind, but none of them fixes the radii, clear apertures or iris position.

The US cover prints the assignee as Asahi Kogaku Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha, the same company as the corpus form above. Claim 6 repeats Example 2 with r12 = 38.348 and d12 = 44.878. The descriptive Example 2 and both German occurrences print 138.348 and 4.878, and the file follows them.

## Optical Architecture

Eight spherical, air-spaced singlets form a retrofocus wide-angle lens: a weak positive front meniscus, two negative menisci convex to the object, a very thick biconvex fourth element, the diaphragm, then a positive meniscus, a separated negative-positive pair and a closing positive meniscus. There are no aspheric surfaces and no cover plates.

L1 to L3 together are strongly negative, with a composite focal length of −11.61 mm, and L1 to L4 remain negative at −48.13 mm. Everything behind the diaphragm supplies the positive power. This split gives a back focus of 1.81 f (36.15 mm at this scale) from a lens whose glass occupies only 34.59 mm. The patent's four conditions set the strength of these partial systems and the balance of lengths ahead of and behind the diaphragm, which it ties to the back focus, aberration balance and overall size.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.62041, νd = 60.3. Glass: S-BSM16 (OHARA) equivalent. f = +110.79 mm.

Weak front meniscus, convex to the object, and the largest element at 16 mm semi-diameter. It sits ahead of the negative front and contributes little power.

### L2 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.62041, νd = 60.3. Glass: S-BSM16 (OHARA) equivalent. f = −22.79 mm.

First negative meniscus, convex to the object. Its rear face S4 (R = 7.884 mm) is the most strongly curved surface in the lens. Fig. 3 draws L2 with a stepped rim, the S4 face ending well inside the outer land of S3.

### L3 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.62299, νd = 58.1. Glass: BACD15 (HOYA) equivalent. f = −21.58 mm.

Second negative meniscus, convex to the object, completing the negative front. Condition (1) bounds the composite focal length of L1 to L3, which the patent calls important for the back focus: weaker, and the spacings behind L3 and the diameters of L1 and L8 must grow; stronger, and the converging lenses behind it need shorter focal lengths, raising spherical aberration and coma.

### L4 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.71736, νd = 29.5. Glass: E-FD1L (HOYA) equivalent. f = +23.83 mm.

A dense flint 10.44 mm thick on the axis, directly ahead of the diaphragm, and the feature that most distinguishes this design. Condition (2) bounds the composite focal length of L1 to L4, which the patent uses to place the diaphragm within a small system while leaving the rear groups room to correct aberrations.

### L5 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.63854, νd = 55.4. Glass: S-BSM18 (OHARA) equivalent. f = +30.34 mm.

Convex to the image and directly behind the diaphragm; the first of the positive rear members.

### L6 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: K-SFLD6 (SUMITA) equivalent. f = −21.28 mm.

The dense-flint negative of the patent's sixth group, 0.976 mm ahead of L7 in this example.

### L7 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.1. Glass: S-FSL5 (OHARA) equivalent. f = +23.82 mm.

The low-dispersion positive of the sixth group. With L6 it forms a very weakly positive pair (f = +1213 mm), as the patent requires of that group.

### L8 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Glass: K-BK7 (SUMITA) equivalent. f = +101.20 mm.

Closing positive meniscus. Condition (3), on the composite focal length of L1 to L7, sets its strength: the patent says a stronger L8 would worsen wide-angle coma or spherical aberration and the sine condition, and a weaker one would shift the load to L5 and L7 and affect the back focus. Claim 1 calls the last lens a positive meniscus convex to the object, but both of Example 2's radii are negative, so as printed it is convex to the image.

## Glass Identification and Reference Convention

The table prints n and ν without naming a spectral line. The file reads them as d-line (587.6 nm): every pair matches a d-line catalog glass to the printed precision, and Fig. 4 plots the d and g lines. The equivalents below are current catalog rows chosen by index and Abbe number; they identify glass types, not the 1977 production melts.

| Element | Patent nd / νd | Catalog equivalent | Catalog nd / νd | Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1, L2 | 1.62041 / 60.3 | S-BSM16 (OHARA) | 1.62041 / 60.29 | SK16 dense crown |
| L3 | 1.62299 / 58.1 | BACD15 (HOYA) | 1.62299 / 58.12 | SK15 dense crown |
| L4 | 1.71736 / 29.5 | E-FD1L (HOYA) | 1.71736 / 29.50 | SF1 dense flint |
| L5 | 1.63854 / 55.4 | S-BSM18 (OHARA) | 1.63854 / 55.38 | SK18 dense crown |
| L6 | 1.80518 / 25.4 | K-SFLD6 (SUMITA) | 1.80518 / 25.40 | SF6 dense flint |
| L7 | 1.48749 / 70.1 | S-FSL5 (OHARA) | 1.48749 / 70.24 | FK5 fluor crown |
| L8 | 1.51633 / 64.1 | K-BK7 (SUMITA) | 1.51633 / 64.10 | BK7 borosilicate crown |

The only flints are the thick positive L4 and the rear negative L6; every other element, including the whole negative front, is a crown. The patent prints no partial dispersions, so behaviour away from the d-line rests on these catalog equivalents.

## Focus Mechanism and Image Plane

The patent names no focusing group or movement and prints the infinity state only, with no close-distance data, so no gap varies and the focus control stays at infinity. The 0.25 m minimum focus is the retail specification and is not traced.

The final gap of 36.154 mm is the printed back focus of 180.770 at F = 100, and it places the image plane 70.741 mm behind S1. This is the paraxial focus: the computed value is 36.1535 mm, and the 0.0005 mm difference is rounding in the printed table.

## Aperture Stop and Clear Apertures

The patent places diaphragm 80 between L4 and L5 and prints no position for it. The model splits the 1.9514 mm gap d8 at its midpoint, 0.9757 mm from each face. At f/4 the stop radius is 4.114 mm, set by the paraxial entrance pupil of 5.0 mm diameter (F/N), which lies 13.12 mm behind S1. The open iris clears the S8 face by 0.98 mm and the S9 face by 0.89 mm.

The patent prints no semi-diameters, so all are inferred from Fig. 3:

## Model Scope and Limitations

| Quantity | Patent, F = 100 | Patent × 0.2 | Model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Focal length | 100 | 20.0 | 20.000 |
| Back focus | 180.770 | 36.154 | 36.1535 paraxial |
| S1 to S16 | 172.937 | 34.587 | 34.587 |
| F1,2,3 | −58.038 | −11.608 | −11.606 |
| F1,2,3,4,5,6 | 153.846 | 30.769 | 30.760 |

- The prescription is the patent's, not a confirmed factory one, scaled by 0.2 from F = 100.
- The d-line reading of the index table, the stop position and all semi-diameters are inferred.
- The image plane is the printed paraxial focus, not best focus.
- The model is static at infinity and says nothing about performance at 0.25 m.
- The patent's 47° half field lands at 20.98 mm, 97 % of the 21.65 mm format corner, which lies at 47.7°. That corner is beyond the patent's stated field; with S4 at 7.06 mm its chief ray clears every rim.
- Distortion agrees with Fig. 4: the model gives −2.2 % at 47° and a largest value of −3.85 % near 40°, where the plot reads about −2 % at the edge and a peak near −4 %.
