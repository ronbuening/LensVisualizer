# PENTAX SMC FA 28-105mm f/4-5.6 POWER ZOOM: optical design

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 5,264,965 A  
**Application Number:** US 834,659  
**Priority:** Cover: February 15, 1991 and October 14, 1991, Japan 3-106952 and 3-331106; the description instead prints February 15, 1989 for the first application.  
**Filed:** February 12, 1992  
**Granted:** November 23, 1993  
**Certificate of Correction:** September 12, 1995  
**Inventor:** Jun Hirakawa  
**Assignee:** Asahi Kogaku Kogyo Co., Ltd.  
**Title:** Zoom Lens  
**Embodiment analyzed:** Embodiment 4, Figure 14 and Tables 7–8, in millimetres without scaling. Figures 15–17 are the three corresponding aberration diagrams.

The original **13-element/11-group Power Zoom** is a probable production correlation; its factory prescription is not confirmed. Ricoh's discontinued-lens entry and section image distinguish this version from the later 15-element/12-group [IF] design. The maker image agrees qualitatively with E4's four functional groups and two cemented doublets. The patent gives a 29.02–101.60 mm, f/4.1–5.8 design range; the retail lens is labeled 28–105 mm, f/4–5.6.

The original Power Zoom entry states 75–23.5° full field, 0.43 m minimum distance, 0.33 maximum magnification, 58 mm filter, 72.5 × 95.5 mm dimensions, 515 g and minimum aperture f/22–32. It describes four-group nonlinear movement and power zoom. These are version-specific product facts. The later IF entry's 0.5 m, 62 mm and 305 g do not apply. The model implements a conservative common f/22 endpoint, not the maker's entire zoom-dependent minimum-aperture mechanism.

The patent's correction certificate changes multiplication, punctuation and words in the claims, leaving the E4 numerical prescription unchanged. E1–E3 are different five-functional-group alternatives. The cover and description give different first-priority years; this bibliographic discrepancy does not affect the prescription.

## Optical Architecture

E4 has 13 glass elements, 11 air-separated optical groups and four moving functional groups with negative–positive–negative–positive powers. Group I contains four singlets; II has one positive singlet followed by a cemented positive/negative doublet; III has a cemented positive/negative doublet followed by a negative singlet; IV has three singlets. The shared cemented faces are r12 and r15. All 24 refracting faces are spherical.

The diagram uses Figure 14's group designations I–IV over r1–r8, r9–r13, r14–r18 and r19–r24. D1 brackets L6/L7 (r11–r13), and D2 brackets L8/L9 (r14–r16). Sequential L1–L13 element labels are editorial: the figure names groups rather than individual elements.

Calculated standalone component EFLs are I −39.648013 mm, II +29.283823 mm, III −28.402793 mm and IV +44.652076 mm. Their positions and interactions, rather than addition of isolated powers, determine the zoom EFL. The three tabulated stations give calculated EFLs of 29.017001717, 57.000854804 and 101.600968015 mm. Their Gaussian BFLs are 40.224463117, 53.046210987 and 76.721554758 mm. The image gaps remain the **printed** 40.22, 53.04 and 76.73 mm, leaving calculated-minus-printed residuals +0.004463117, +0.006210987 and −0.008445242 mm. The latter two exceed ±0.005 mm, half the last printed place; their cause is unresolved.

The source condition 0.4 < fI²/(fw·ft) < 0.7 gives 0.533152860 and is satisfied, with negative group I. Table 9's rounded −39.648 mm and 0.533 agree. The condition referring to a fifth group is not applicable to four-group E4. The source prefers high index in the first group, although L1's printed index is 1.69680, below 1.7. The source provides aberration diagrams rather than a numerical Seidel coefficient table; the element roles below are qualitative interpretations.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The table's un-subscripted N and ν do not explicitly specify a spectral line. Accordingly, every first line below retains **n(source), ν(source)**. Conventional d-line tracing is an interpretation, supported by the d/g/c aberration plots. Catalog equivalents describe compatible optical coordinates; the production melt and supplier are not identified for any element. Listed focal lengths are thick elements isolated in air, including air on the far side of each cemented member.

### L1 — Biconvex Positive Singlet

n(source) = 1.69680, ν(source) = 55.5. Glass: LAC14 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = +249.801924 mm.

This weak positive front collector precedes the two strong negatives. Its large front radius and near-flat rear radius provide modest positive power while the complete first component remains negative. Its printed index is below the general 1.7 design preference.

### L2 — Negative Meniscus Singlet

n(source) = 1.77250, ν(source) = 49.6. Glass: TAF1 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = −42.933226 mm.

The more strongly curved rear face makes this meniscus negative. Its adopted front/rear clear radii are 20/16.5 mm. Figure 14 draws the same stepped element: the deep rear face ends near 16.7 mm and a flat annulus steps out to a front rim near 20.4 mm; the viewer approximates that step with a straight join between the two rims. The wider front rim avoids an unnecessarily clipped front entrance. These are figure-based estimates, not source dimensions or a claim about the factory blank.

### L3 — Biconcave Negative Singlet

n(source) = 1.77250, ν(source) = 49.6. Glass: TAF1 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = −38.517588 mm.

L3 supplies a second strong negative contribution inside the first component. Although it repeats L2's coordinates, that does not independently identify a historical melt or establish that factory production used one shared batch.

### L4 — Positive Meniscus Singlet

n(source) = 1.84666, ν(source) = 23.8. Glass: FDS90 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = +48.855596 mm.

The high-index, high-dispersion positive rear member partly balances the negative front pair and redistributes color correction within the net-negative first group. The long-radius rear face leads into the changing D8 separation and the iris region.

### L5 — Biconvex Positive Singlet

n(source) = 1.61800, ν(source) = 63.4. Glass: PCD4 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = +45.789218 mm.

This relatively low-dispersion positive singlet starts group II immediately after the reconstructed iris. It cooperates with the following doublet in the group's net-positive action; no factory stop coordinate is inferred from its curvature alone.

### L6 — Biconvex Positive, Cemented to L7

n(source) = 1.71300, ν(source) = 53.8. Glass: S-LAL8 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = +22.938544 mm.

This strong positive member is cemented to a more dispersive negative element at r12. The paired powers and dispersion contrast support a chromatic-balancing interpretation, without proving exact cancellation of any specific aberration coefficient.

### L7 — Biconcave Negative, Cemented to L6

n(source) = 1.80518, ν(source) = 25.4. Glass: FD60 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = −28.381059 mm.

The high-dispersion negative member completes group II. Its actual in-situ cemented-interface power uses the index difference, not an air/glass surface substituted at r12. The isolated focal length above is deliberately distinguished from that coupled behavior.

### L8 — Positive Meniscus, Cemented to L9

n(source) = 1.80518, ν(source) = 25.4. Glass: FD60 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = +18.985679 mm.

The nearly flat negative-radius front face and strongly curved rear face form a positive meniscus. This element begins the second cemented pair, now inside the net-negative third component. Repeating L7's coordinates does not make their optical roles identical.

### L9 — Biconcave Negative, Cemented to L8

n(source) = 1.83400, ν(source) = 37.2. Glass: S-LAH60 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = −14.155188 mm.

L9 is a strong negative member. Its rear r16 leads into the short airspace before the smaller L10 clear aperture, where some off-axis rays can miss the following element.

### L10 — Biconcave Negative Singlet

n(source) = 1.83481, ν(source) = 42.7. Glass: TAFD5F (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = −59.008743 mm.

This negative singlet completes group III. Its 8.3 mm inferred clear radius is constrained by the short surrounding gaps. Rays missing both its spherical faces and its rim do not reach the image.

### L11 — Biconvex Positive Singlet

n(source) = 1.48749, ν(source) = 70.2. Glass: S-FSL5 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = +41.901891 mm.

The low-index, low-dispersion collector begins the positive fourth component. Its high Abbe number alone does not determine partial dispersion; the catalog equivalent supplies a qualified spectral proxy.

### L12 — Biconvex Positive Singlet

n(source) = 1.48749, ν(source) = 70.2. Glass: S-FSL5 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = +58.962395 mm.

A second low-dispersion positive singlet shares the rear component's collecting power. The close air separations follow the patent.

### L13 — Negative Meniscus Singlet

n(source) = 1.80400, ν(source) = 46.6. Glass: S-LAH65 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = −45.515438 mm.

The final negative meniscus counterbalances the two preceding positives while group IV remains net positive. Its influence on the final field and pupil depends on the assembled system; factory field-flattening performance is not established.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The glass labels use HOYA catalog equivalents LAC14, TAF1, FDS90, PCD4, FD60 and TAFD5F, and OHARA equivalents S-LAL8, S-LAH60, S-FSL5 and S-LAH65, at the positions listed above. The patent's N and ν values remain unchanged. These equivalents do not identify the production melt or supplier, or resolve the table's unspecified spectral line.

Reference-wavelength tracing interprets the printed indices as d-line values. Catalog dispersion curves provide qualified spectral proxies rather than measured production-glass properties. The maker does not designate special-dispersion positions for this lens, and no elements are tagged as such. Calculated MTF is not measured production performance.

## Zoom and Aperture Reconstruction

The three tabulated zoom stations are connected by a calculated cam path. D13 + D18 = 17.51 mm at all three stations, supporting the patent's rigid II/IV coupling. The reconstruction uses piecewise-linear D8 and a linearly interpolated **calculated Gaussian BFL target**, solves D13, and obtains D18 from that coupling. Printed image gaps are interpolated independently and remain unchanged at the three published stations. Of 257 positions, only positions 0, 128 and 256 are published. Their focal labels retain the printed 29.02, 57.00 and 101.60 mm.

The interpolated path has maximum Gaussian-target residual 0.000817670 mm and printed-plane residual 0.009107936 mm. EFL is monotone. Positions are continuous, but smooth velocity and the factory cam are unknown. In fixed-image coordinates, group I moves 15.97 mm imageward to the middle station, then 12.48 mm objectward to tele. Groups II and IV move together monotonically objectward by 36.51 mm overall. Group III moves 23.82 mm objectward overall, with a calculated 0.323 mm imageward reversal between approximately 49.79 and 57 mm. The three E4 table stations place III monotonically objectward; Figure 1's general schematic instead depicts an initial imageward excursion. Table 8 governs the numerical reconstruction, and no gap order was reversed to match that schematic.

The iris is inferred 1 mm before r9, inside D8, and moves with group II as the source states qualitatively. Its exact offset is not published. A fixed-diameter iris at a constant offset before group II within the minimum D8 gap cannot reproduce all three printed f-numbers. The model calculates the iris radius from the nominal f-number at each zoom position: 8.143954022, 9.150411048 and 11.270312969 mm at the published stations. The corresponding physical axial launch radii are 3.538658746, 5.816413756 and 8.758704139 mm, giving f/4.1, f/4.9 and f/5.8. The viewer's paraxial entrance-pupil radii are slightly larger: 3.551374084, 5.839280938 and 8.775409942 mm, corresponding to f/4.085320, f/4.880811 and f/5.788959. This wider launch clips marginal rays at the physical iris.

The patent prints no clear apertures. The rims are estimates from the wide-position Figure 14, constrained by the real-ray axial beam and corner chief ray. Apart from L2's 20/16.5 mm front/rear step, paired element rims are 25, 16.5, 16, 12, 12, 12, 8.4, 8.4, 8.3, 13.3, 13.7 and 14.0 mm in element order. Independent second-review measurements give approximately 13.32/13.70/14.22 mm for L11/L12/L13. The revised square L11/L12 rims restore the drawn increasing height order; L13 remains at 14.0 mm rather than changing the clearance policy. Figure 14 draws its concave front meeting L12's rear face at the rim. The r22/r23 clearance is now 0.161987 mm at their shared 13.7 mm height; the existing 0.98 gap-sag allowance is retained. Equal 16.5 mm front/rear rims on L2 would lose the entire wide 38° bundle. Extending its rear curve to 20 mm would reach 14.719 mm behind r1, beyond L3's 13.600 mm front vertex; the smaller rear curve-end is retained to avoid wrapping L3.

The rear-rim changes lower the engine's aperture-based middle half-field estimate from 26.888964° to 26.742480°, while wide/tele estimates remain 33.969655°/21.591687°. This figure-driven change does not reduce the traced format-corner coverage at any of the 257 zoom states or change the nominal f/4.1/4.9/5.8 schedule. Wide geometric transmission at 29.3°/32.2°/35.1° changes from 99.0/95.3/82.3% to 94.8/87.5/78.6%; the 38.1° edge remains 49.5%. These are sampled geometric results, not measured illumination.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent states that E4 reaches 0.43 m at every focal length with approximately 5 mm first-group travel. Exact finite spacings and a complete focus law are not published. The model retains infinity-focus geometry at every zoom position; finite-conjugate MTF is outside its scope.

A separate paraxial calculation interprets 0.43 m as object-to-image-plane distance and moves only group I toward the object, holding the other groups at their zoom positions. The three published zooms require travel of 4.994682, 4.745366 and 4.929456 mm, consistent with the patent's approximate statement. Measuring distance from the first vertex would give materially different travel. These calculated positions are not published focus stations and do not establish off-axis close-focus performance or factory motion.

## Aspherical Surfaces

None. Every selected surface is spherical. No artificial asphere, correction plate, sensor stack or unprescribed glass was added.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The focus control does not change the infinity-focus geometry. Clear apertures are inferred from the figure, and the model includes physical off-axis clipping. The minimum common-band edge thickness is 1.37 mm (L8), the maximum gap-sag fraction is 0.927 (r22 to r23 at 13.7 mm), and the maximum rim angle is 40.9° (r4). L2's stepped edge is approximated by a straight join between unequal front and rear rims. The inferred iris position, variable iris diameter and interpolated cam path are not factory measurements.

The paraxial entrance-pupil estimate slightly overfills the physical iris. Marginal-ray losses from that estimate are distinct from internal surface intersections. Strong wide-angle vignetting also follows the figure-based L2 rear rim and L13 aperture.

The printed half-fields of 38°, 20.7° and 11.9° give calculated chief-ray image heights of 21.608137487, 21.635610200 and 21.701164383 mm. The viewer's nominal 135-format image radius is 21.65 mm, so the telephoto source angle lies just outside that boundary, at a normalized field of 1.002363251. Ray sampling does not establish every possible ray path or uniqueness of the chief-ray solution. The model does not establish measured illumination, full spectral correction or finite-field production performance. Source rounding and bibliographic discrepancies remain unresolved.
