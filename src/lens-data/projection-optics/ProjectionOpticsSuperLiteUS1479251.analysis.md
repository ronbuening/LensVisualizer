# PROJECTION OPTICS SUPER-LITE 100mm f/2.2 (US 1,479,251 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 1,479,251, *Projecting lens*  
**Application Number:** Serial No. 578,695  
**Filed:** July 31, 1922  
**Granted:** January 1, 1924  
**Inventor:** William H. Repp, of Rochester, New York  
**Assignee:** none printed; the patent issued to the inventor  
**Example details:** sole numerical example, typeset table on printed page 3 (E.F. 100 mm, F/2.2); repeated in hand lettering on the drawing sheet; millimetres

US 1,479,251 describes a moving-picture projection objective built from two cemented doublets. Each doublet is an
equiconvex crown cemented to a plano-concave flint. The convex sides face the screen and the plano sides face the film.
The patent gives one worked example, for an equivalent focal length of 100 mm at F/2.2.

The PROJECTION OPTICS SUPER-LITE 100mm f/2.2 (US 1,479,251 Design) follows that example; it is not a measured
production lens. The link to the Super-Lite is nevertheless strong:

1. Repp co-founded the Projection Optics Company of Rochester, New York, in 1918 (Delta Lenses history). His earlier
   projection-lens patent, US 1,415,002 (1922), is assigned to Projection Optics Company, Inc. This patent prints no
   assignee, but the company was the inventor's own firm.
2. A 1923 *Exhibitors Trade Review* notice, reproduced by Delta Lenses, presents the new Super-Lite lens "manufactured by
   the Projection Optics Co." with a description supplied by Repp. That description repeats this patent's central
   argument: the design cuts the secondary reflected images from fifteen to six. The pictured Series III lens is
   engraved as patent applied for, which fits a 1922 filing and a 1924 grant.
3. Projection Optics' own Pro-Jex Super-Lite advertisement shows a cutaway of two widely separated doublets in a long
   barrel. The patent has the same layout: a separation about equal to the focal length and a short back focus.
4. Super-Lite lenses were sold in many focal lengths, first at about f/2.2 and later at f/2 (Delta Lenses lists the
   post-war Series III C at 4 to 10 inches). No source publishes a production prescription. The patent therefore
   describes the Super-Lite design principle, but it does not establish that any particular production focal length
   used this example's exact radii.

## Optical Architecture

The objective has 4 elements in 2 groups. A weak positive front doublet A (f = +197.82 mm) and a stronger positive rear
doublet B (f = +98.05 mm) are separated by 94.54 mm. These values match the patent's own description. The front
combination's focal length is almost twice the E.F., the rear combination's is nearly the E.F., and the separation is
approximately equal to the E.F.

The patent presents the design as a simplification of the Petzval-type projection lens in Repp's earlier patent. That
lens has a cemented front pair and an air-spaced rear pair, which leaves six free glass-air surfaces. Cementing the
rear pair leaves four. Using Fresnel's reflection estimate with an average index of 1.56, the patent puts the
transmission of six free surfaces at 74 % and of four at 82 %. That is a gain of about one tenth in screen brightness.
The number of secondary reflected images, m(m − 1)/2 for m free surfaces, falls from fifteen to six.

The construction uses only two curvatures besides the plano faces: ±82.57 mm in front and ±41.28 mm behind, in a 2:1
ratio. The crowns are 8.54 mm thick and the flints 2.85 mm in both doublets, so the same blanks and moulds serve front
and rear. The patent also states an assembly rule, the proportion 2 : 1 :: 1 : 1 between the front focal length, the
rear focal length, the separation and the E.F. An assembler can measure a rear doublet, pick a front doublet of twice
its focal length, and space them by the shorter focal length. The patent notes that this absorbs melt-to-melt index
variation.

First-order values of the transcribed table: paraxial EFL 99.9965 mm against the printed 100 mm. The printed 45.4 mm
free aperture gives f/2.2026, against the printed F/2.2. The paraxial back focus is 39.8225 mm, which matches the
printed 39.82 mm. The lens is 117.32 mm long from first to last vertex, and the total track to the image is 157.14 mm.

The patent projects from the film at the right to the screen at the left. The model traces in the opposite sense, from
a screen-side object at infinity toward the film. The convex faces therefore lead, and the plano faces point at the
film as they do in the drawing.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The patent numbers the lenses I to IV from the screen side and calls the two cemented combinations A and B; the
diagram uses the same designations.

### Front doublet A (I + II)

**I — Biconvex Positive (equiconvex).** `nd = 1.5160, νd = 63.8. Glass: Unmatched (borosilicate crown, nD 1.5160 / V 63.8). f = +81.44 mm.`

**II — Plano-Concave Negative.** `nd = 1.6182, νd = 36.4. Glass: Unmatched (dense flint, nD 1.6182 / V 36.4). f = −133.57 mm.`

The low-index crown and high-index flint are cemented at R2 = −82.57 mm, and the flint's plano face points at the film.
The patent says this doublet is corrected for colour and spherical aberration on its own. Used alone, though, it covers
only a small central field well, because coma and field curvature remain. Its radii are shallow because its focal
length is about twice the system's. The patent says they are 1.45 times longer than the corresponding curves of the
earlier Petzval-type design.

### Rear doublet B (III + IV)

**III — Biconvex Positive (equiconvex).** `nd = 1.5160, νd = 63.8. Glass: Unmatched (borosilicate crown, nD 1.5160 / V 63.8). f = +41.46 mm.`

**IV — Plano-Concave Negative.** `nd = 1.6182, νd = 36.4. Glass: Unmatched (dense flint, nD 1.6182 / V 36.4). f = −66.77 mm.`

The rear doublet reproduces the front one at half scale, except that the element thicknesses are unchanged. Its
cemented interface is R5 = −41.28 mm, the strongest curve in the system. The patent compares this curve with the
38.314 mm strongest radius of the earlier design. Taken alone, the rear doublet has the same faults as the front one.
The patent states that the pair, spaced as specified, is free of the defects that would lower the quality of the screen
image. It allows the rear pair to be separated by a thin spacing ring instead of cemented. The model follows the
cemented form of the table.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent specifies each glass by nD, the index for the yellow (sodium D) line of the solar spectrum, and by Abbe's
V = (nD − 1)/(nF − nC). The model keeps both numbers with the D-line index reference. Only two glasses are used:

| Glass (patent name) | nD | V | Elements | Role |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Borosilicate crown | 1.5160 | 63.8 | I, III | Positive collective elements |
| Dense flint | 1.6182 | 36.4 | II, IV | Achromatizing partners |

The patent names no maker or catalogue glass. No modern catalogue row matches either pair exactly; the nearest,
BK7-type crowns and F2-type flints, miss the printed indices by more than 0.0001. The model
therefore carries no catalogue substitution. Its dispersion is built from nD and V alone, so partial dispersion and
secondary spectrum are approximations. With these values each doublet is close to achromatic.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent describes no focusing mechanism. The model is fixed at infinity on the screen side. The patent places the
film slightly farther than 39.82 mm from the last plano surface, which corresponds to a finite screen distance. It does
not give that distance, so no finite projection throw is modelled.

## Aperture, Field and Clear Radii

The patent states the free aperture of the front system as 45.4 mm, so 100 : 45.4 gives F/2.2. It draws no separate
iris. The model places the aperture stop at the front vertex plane with that 45.4 mm diameter. The front doublet's
clear radius is 22.7 mm.

The patent prints no rear clear aperture and no field. The rear doublet's clear radius of 17.5 mm on all three faces
follows the drawing. Scaled to the printed front aperture, the drawn rear doublet is 0.78 of the front's height
(17.7 mm), and its flint is drawn square-cut at the crown's height. The patent says its figure shows the lens at full
size (printed page 2). Read at the scale of the printed spacings, the drawn front doublet is about 47.6 mm across,
2.2 mm more than the free aperture, and the rear doublet about 37.2 mm; the same margin leaves a rear clear radius of
17.5 mm.

The lens is catalogued as a projection objective for 35 mm cinema film. The 27.2 mm format diagonal corresponds to a
7.9° half-field at this focal length. The model's 16° full field (8° half) is the smallest whole-degree field that
reaches that corner; it is not a patent value. A period 35 mm projector gate, about 14.4 mm in half diagonal,
corresponds to about 8.4°.

At both angles the chief ray clears every rim, but the rear doublet cuts most of one side of the oblique beam (about
71–84 %). Geometric transmission falls from 98.9 % at 4° to 60.5 % at 7.9°.

## Drawing Sheet and Table

The drawing sheet letters the same example by hand, and two of its labels disagree with the typeset table. It gives
the front radius as 88.57 (table +82.57; the second front label reads 82.57) and the flint V as 30.4 (table 36.4). Its
flint thickness labels are illegible.

The other labels agree with the table: 8.54, 94.54, 39.82, 41.28, nD 1.5160 and 1.6182, and V 63.8. The table values are
used. They reproduce the printed 100 mm E.F. and the 39.82 mm back focus, while an 88.57 mm front radius would put the
back focus at 44.05 mm. With V = 36.4 the thin-lens longitudinal colour of the front doublet is about 0.5 mm; the
drawing's 30.4 would raise it to about 2.1 mm. The specification text also cites 41.28 mm as the strongest curve, as in
the table.

## Design Heritage and Context

According to the Delta Lenses history, the Super-Lite name first appears in a 1922 advertisement. The lens launched in
1923 in several barrel sizes, the standard one being Series III. Later versions include the Bi-Focal Super-Lite
(1929), the coated Series III C at f/2, and the Hilux line that succeeded it in 1953. The same history describes the
Super-Lite as a Petzval derivative with as many plano and identically curved surfaces as possible. That matches this
patent's two-curvature construction.
