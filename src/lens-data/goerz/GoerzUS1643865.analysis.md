# GOERZ 100mm f/9 (US 1,643,865 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 1,643,865 A, *Photographic Two-Lens Objective*  
**Filed:** August 5, 1924 (German priority August 13, 1923)  
**Granted:** September 27, 1927  
**Inventor:** Franz Weidert  
**Assignee:** Optische Anstalt C. P. Goerz Aktiengesellschaft  
**Example details:** Example 1, Fig. 1; one of three numerical examples, all at fD = 100 mm

The model is the first numerical example of US 1,643,865. The patent concerns the cheapest class of camera lens: the cemented two-lens achromat. It describes the older "French landscape lens", which carries its diaphragm and shutter in front of the glass, and proposes instead a two-lens objective with the diaphragm behind it, so that the shutter is protected, the camera is shorter, and the aperture can be larger than before.

Example 1 is printed at a focal length of 100 mm with an effective aperture of f/9, and the model uses those printed values.

The patent names no product, and none is tied to this example. The nearest Goerz lens of the period is the Frontar of the Box-Tengor camera, recorded on a 1925 Goerz Box-Tengor and listed by collectors as a two-element 105 mm f/11 lens. That aperture fits the patent's f/11 Example 2 at least as well as the f/9 Example 1 modelled here. No source links the Frontar to this patent, and no Frontar construction diagram was found to compare.

## Optical Architecture

The objective is a single cemented group of two spherical elements: a biconvex crown in front and a biconcave flint behind it, together forming a meniscus that is convex toward the subject. The diaphragm stands in air 11.5 mm behind the last glass surface.

The patent sets out four conditions and Example 1 meets each:

1. The rear diaphragm is at least 8 % of the focal length behind the last vertex (here 11.5 %).
2. The objective is left spherically under-corrected by at least 2 % of the focal length for a ray entering at 1/18 of the focal length from the axis. This is deliberate: it reduces coma with the stop behind the lens.
3. The cemented surface is weak. The product of its curvature and its refractive power must lie below 60 at 100 mm focal length; the patent prints +32.28 for this example.
4. The two glasses differ in ν by at least 10 units (here 16.3).

Computed from the printed values, the focal length is 100.00 mm, and the 9.1 mm diaphragm gives an aperture of f/8.99.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### Element 1 — Biconvex Positive Crown

`nD = 1.5096, ν = 63.4.` Radii +21 and −35 mm, centre thickness 3 mm. Standalone focal length in air +26.23 mm. It supplies all of the positive power.

### Element 2 — Biconcave Negative Flint

`nD = 1.5490, ν = 47.1.` Radii −35 (cemented to Element 1) and +38.82 mm, centre thickness 1.25 mm. Standalone focal length in air −33.33 mm. It corrects colour; the small index step at the cemented surface (1.5096 to 1.5490) keeps that surface nearly neutral, as the patent requires.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent names both glasses by their numbers in the catalogue of Sendlinger Optische Glaswerke G. m. b. H., Berlin-Zehlendorf: crown 510/634 and flint 549/471. For each it prints nD, nF, nG′ and ν, with ν defined as (nD − 1)/(nF − nC):

| Glass | nD | nF | nG′ | ν |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Crown 510/634 | 1.5096 | 1.5153 | 1.5197 | 63.4 |
| Flint 549/471 | 1.5490 | 1.5573 | 1.5641 | 47.1 |

The model uses the printed nD and ν at the sodium D line. No modern catalogue glass is substituted. The patent stresses that both are cheap glasses with a wide choice of melts.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent gives no focusing data. The model is focused at infinity only: the image plane is the computed paraxial focus at the D line, 81.81 mm behind the diaphragm (93.31 mm behind the last glass surface).

## Patent Conditions and Model Scope

| Quantity | Patent | Model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Focal length | 100 mm | 100.00 mm |
| Aperture | f/9 | f/8.99 |
| Spherical undercorrection at 5.56 mm incidence | 3.1 mm | 3.09 mm |
| Curvature of the cemented surface | −28.57 | −28.57 |
| Refractive powers at the cemented surface | +14.56 / −15.69 | +14.56 / −15.69 |

The lens diameters are the patent's: a free diameter of 14.7 mm and a diaphragm of 9.1 mm. The full f/9 axial beam passes. The patent states no field angle. With the printed diameters, the share of the meridional beam that passes falls to about 0.9 at 10°, 0.6 at 20° and 0.4 at 27° off axis, so the model darkens toward the corners as a lens of this type does.

What is printed: every radius, thickness, index and ν, the stop position, the diaphragm diameter and the free lens diameter. What is inferred: only the image distance.
