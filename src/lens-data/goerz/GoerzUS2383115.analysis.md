# GOERZ 100mm f/6 (US 2,383,115 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [US 2,383,115 A](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/2383115), _Photographic Lens_

**Inventors:** Milo A. Durand

**Assignee:** C. P. Goerz American Optical Company

**Filed:** 17 April 1943

**Published:** 21 August 1945

**Example details:** Example I, Figure 1; numerical table on PDF p. 2.

The patent gives three numerical examples. Example I is the symmetric four-element design with an equivalent focal length of 100 and an aperture of f/6 or greater. Its length unit is implicit; the model interprets the dimensions in millimetres. Example II is asymmetric, with a focal length of about 153.5 mm, while Example III adds a plate and gives 138 mm at f/10. C. P. Goerz American Optical Company was the US Goerz firm.

The [company catalogue of about 1959](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00259/00259.pdf#page=5) describes the Rectagon as an f/6 wide-angle anastigmat covering 90°, originally intended for aerial mapping. Its section shows four separate menisci. These similarities support a related family, but the catalogue does not identify this patent. The asymmetric, approximately six-inch Example II is a more plausible mapping-lens candidate than Example I; the production prescription remains undocumented.

## Optical Architecture

Four spherical, air-spaced menisci form four groups, with positive–negative–negative–positive power. Both halves are symmetric about the central air space. The outer positive menisci provide converging power, and the more dispersive inner negative menisci balance that power. All four menisci have their concave sides facing the centre. There are no cemented interfaces or aspherical surfaces.

The patent uses positive and negative signs for convex and concave surfaces. The Example I table, oriented by Figure 1 in the direction of light travel, gives the model's signed radius sequence +20.118, +55.801, +25.089, +15.196, −15.196, −25.089, −55.801 and −20.118 mm. The two 0.316 mm air gaps and the central 7.366 mm interval retain the printed values.

The computed EFL is 99.9807 mm, 0.0193% below the printed 100, at paraxial f/6. The computed back focus is 90.5361 mm; the patent supplies no back-focus value. The distance between the first and last vertices is 19.782 mm, placing the paraxial image plane 110.3181 mm behind the first vertex.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### Element 1 — Positive Meniscus

nD = 1.588, νD = 53.4. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.588 / ν 53.4). Focal length in air: +50.95 mm.

The front outer meniscus supplies positive power. Its stronger convex front surface faces the object, and its concave rear surface faces the centre. It forms a symmetric pair with Element 4.

### Element 2 — Negative Meniscus

nD = 1.636, νD = 34.8. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.636 / ν 34.8). Focal length in air: −63.21 mm.

The front inner meniscus has stronger curvature on its concave rear surface and contributes negative power. Its higher dispersion provides the opposing chromatic contribution to the outer positive elements.

### Element 3 — Negative Meniscus

nD = 1.636, νD = 34.8. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.636 / ν 34.8). Focal length in air: −63.21 mm.

The rear inner meniscus mirrors Element 2 about the central gap. Its concave front surface faces the stop, preserving the symmetric negative inner pair.

### Element 4 — Positive Meniscus

nD = 1.588, νD = 53.4. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.588 / ν 53.4). Focal length in air: +50.95 mm.

The rear outer meniscus mirrors Element 1 and supplies positive power toward the image. Its stronger convex rear surface faces away from the central gap.

## Glass Identification / Selection

The table identifies the indices as D light, taken as sodium at 589.3 nm. The printed pairs are nD 1.588 / νD 53.4 for the outer positive elements and nD 1.636 / νD 34.8 for the inner negative elements. Neither pair has an exact catalogue match, so all four glass identities remain unmatched.

The crown-like positive and flint-like negative pairs provide the chromatic power balance. The patent does not supply C-, F- or g-line indices or partial dispersion. Spectral results derived from the Abbe numbers are estimates; they do not establish the dispersion of the original glass or measured broadband image quality.

## Focus Mechanism

Infinity focus only; the patent gives no close-focus data or focusing movement. The final image gap is the computed paraxial back focus. No mechanical minimum focus distance is documented for this example.

No diaphragm is drawn. The inferred stop lies halfway through the central 7.366 mm gap, with 3.683 mm on each side, at 9.891 mm behind the first vertex. Its radius of 7.1305 mm follows from the paraxial f/6 aperture. Real rays through that opening give an entrance-pupil equivalent of about f/5.965 and an image-sine value of about f/5.952. These definitions differ slightly from the paraxial f-number. The stop has about 0.387 mm clearance from each adjacent inner rim. The f/6–f/32 stop sequence is a model setting rather than a published diaphragm specification.

## Field, Aperture and Model Limits

The specification describes a field reaching or exceeding 90°, and claim 5 repeats a field of 90° or more (PDF pp. 2 and 4). The model therefore uses a 90° full field and a 45° half-field. At 45°, the real chief ray reaches an image height of 101.745 mm at the paraxial plane. Twice this height gives the model's 203.49 mm image circle; the patent does not print that diameter or specify a film format.

The chief ray clears every surface at 45°. With the inferred f/6 iris and the current rims, about 59.54% of the uniformly sampled stop area reaches the image, corresponding to 40.46% geometric vignetting. This describes pupil clipping, not measured brightness or image quality. Coating and absorption data are absent.

All clear semi-diameters are inferred. The outer elements use 12 mm rims; the inner elements use 11 and 9.45 mm. Figure 1 appears to extend the outer elements to roughly 15 mm and the inner elements to about 9.4 mm. Those differences remain unresolved. The current glass and air spaces retain positive edge clearance, and no rim crosses the inferred stop plane. The patent's wide-field claims and the Rectagon catalogue description do not establish the edge resolution or broadband performance of this particular example.
