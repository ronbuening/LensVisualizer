# GOERZ 100mm f/6 (US 2,383,115 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,383,115 A, _Photographic Lens_](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/2383115), by Milo A. Durand, assigned to C. P. Goerz American Optical Company, was filed on 17 April 1943 and published on 21 August 1945. No earlier priority date is stated. Example I is the symmetric four-meniscus design: Figure 1 is on PDF p. 1 and its numerical table and bibliography are on p. 2. The patent contains three numerical examples; the others are on p. 3, and claim 5 is on p. 4.

The [C. P. Goerz American Optical Co. catalogue, about 1959](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00259/00259.pdf#page=5), describes and illustrates the Rectagon on PDF p. 5. Its approximate date follows from 64 years of US lensmaking since 1895. It supplies historical product context, without identifying this patent or Example I.

## Transcription Check

All eight radii, seven internal intervals and four glass index/dispersion pairs agree with the Example I page images at printed precision. No numerical discrepancy was found. The table gives convex/concave radius signs; Figure 1 establishes the direction of light travel, giving the Cartesian sequence +20.118, +55.801, +25.089, +15.196, −15.196, −25.089, −55.801 and −20.118 mm.

Both 0.316 mm air gaps remain. The central 7.366 mm interval is split into 3.683 mm on each side of the inferred stop. Four separate spherical menisci, four groups, positive outer elements and negative inner elements agree with the patent. There are no cemented interfaces or aspherical surfaces.

The table prints “Index (D light)”, taken as sodium at 589.3 nm. The outer pair remains 1.588 / 53.4 and the inner pair 1.636 / 34.8. The focal length of 100 has no explicit length unit; dimensions are interpreted as millimetres without rescaling.

## First-Order Check

The calculated paraxial EFL is 99.98 mm, 0.02 mm below the printed 100. The paraxial aperture is f/6.00, consistent with the printed f/6 or greater. Calculated BFD is 90.54 mm. The first-to-last vertex distance is 19.78 mm and the first-vertex-to-image distance is 110.32 mm. The image plane is at paraxial infinity focus, 90.54 mm behind surface 8; the patent prints no back focus.

The inferred stop radius is 7.13 mm. Real-ray entrance-pupil and image-sine equivalents are about f/5.97 and f/5.95. The stop has about 0.387 mm clearance from each adjacent inner rim. Glass and air edge clearances remain positive, and the iris sets the full aperture without rim limitation.

The 45.0° chief ray clears every surface and reaches an image height of 101.745 mm at the paraxial plane, covering the declared 203.49 mm circle. At that field, 59.5% of the stop area reaches the image, corresponding to 40.5% geometric vignetting. The outer surfaces account for the meridional clipping. These are geometric properties at the D line; they do not establish measured Rectagon brightness or resolution.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

No diaphragm is drawn. The stop is placed halfway through the central gap, 9.891 mm behind the first vertex; its radius follows from paraxial f/6. All clear semi-diameters are inferred: 12 mm on the outer elements and 11/9.45 mm on the inner elements. The final image gap is calculated, and the f/6–f/32 sequence and f/32 endpoint are model settings without a published minimum iris opening.

The field is printed: the specification describes 90° or more, and claim 5 repeats that extent. The model uses a 90.0° full field and 45.0° half-field. The 203.49 mm image circle is twice the real chief-ray image height at that half-field, rather than a printed diameter or a production film format.

The model is focused at infinity. `focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. No close-focus state or focusing movement is supplied.

All four glasses remain unmatched. No catalogue curve is assigned; dispersion follows the printed Abbe numbers. The nearest cited outer-element row, Ohara BAL7, differs by ΔnD +0.000649 and Δν −2.23. The inner-element row Ohara S-TIM6 differs by ΔnD +0.000201 and Δν +0.58. Neither pair agrees within the printed precision. The patent gives no C-, F- or g-line indices, partial dispersion, anomalous-dispersion designation, supplier, melt, coating or absorption data.

## Product Identification

The Goerz catalogue presents the Rectagon as a marketed f/6 wide-angle anastigmat covering 90°, originally intended for aerial mapping. Its four separate menisci and distortion-correction aim support a related family. They do not establish Example I as a production prescription. The asymmetric, approximately six-inch Example II is a more plausible mapping-lens candidate; this remains an inference.

No production product is documented for Example I. The Goerz name follows the patent assignee. Mount, film format, diaphragm blade count and shape, marketed focal length and aperture, and marketed minimum focus distance remain omitted.

## Corrections, 2026-11-21

- The inventor-and-example identity was replaced by the maker-and-patent title and key. The maker changed from unspecified to Goerz, and the lens became visible in the catalogue.
- The patent number gained the publication suffix A; Milo Durand became Milo A. Durand; the abbreviated assignee became C. P. Goerz American Optical Company. The 1945 publication year and Example I are retained.
- The subtitle is “US 2,383,115 A, Example I — Goerz / Durand”. The specifications are “4 ELEMENTS / 4 GROUPS”, “ALL SPHERICAL”, “DESIGN f = 100 mm” and “DESIGN F/6”, distinguishing the printed design scale from the calculated EFL.
- The catalogue design focal length changed from 100 to 99.98 mm to reflect the calculated EFL. The prescription retains its original dimensional scale.
- Element labels became Element 1–Element 4. Generic shape descriptions became Positive Meniscus for Elements 1/4 and Negative Meniscus for Elements 2/3. Optical roles and in-air focal lengths of +50.95, −63.21, −63.21 and +50.95 mm were added to describe the positive–negative–negative–positive arrangement.
- General glass descriptions became `Unmatched (nD 1.588 / ν 53.4)` on Elements 1/4 and `Unmatched (nD 1.636 / ν 34.8)` on Elements 2/3. Each label change has Δn = 0 and Δν = 0. Sodium D remains the index reference; no catalogue identity or additional spectral values were assigned.
- The full field increased from 20.0° to 90.0° and the maximum traced half-field from 10.0° to 45.0°, following the specification and claim 5. The 203.49 mm image circle was added from the real 45.0° chief-ray height. Chief-ray coverage does not imply full edge-pupil illumination.
- The Rectagon identification changed from an experimental lens to a documented marketed family, following the company catalogue. The exact production use of Example I remains unknown.

The resulting first-order values are EFL 99.98 mm, paraxial f/6.00 and BFD 90.54 mm, with a 7.13 mm stop radius and a 45.0° half-field. Every radius, thickness, air space, printed glass pair, stop position, clear semi-diameter and image-plane distance is retained.

## Remaining Questions

- Figure 1 appears to extend the outer elements to about 15 mm and the inner elements to about 9.4 mm, against the model's 12 mm and 11/9.45 mm rims. These drawing differences remain unresolved.
- Original glass identities, suppliers, melts and partial dispersion are unknown. Abbe-based spectral estimates do not establish measured broadband image quality.
- No source ties Example I to a particular production Rectagon or supplies its mount, film format, diaphragm blade count or shape, stop range, marketed specifications, minimum focus distance or focusing mechanism.
- The source does not establish edge resolution, broadband performance, coatings or absorption for this example. Its printed wide-field claim and the catalogue's product description do not resolve these limits.
