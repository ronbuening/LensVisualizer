# GOERZ 100mm f/6 (US 2,383,115 Design) — Audit

## Source

[US 2,383,115 A, _Photographic Lens_](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/2383115), filed 17 April 1943 and published 21 August 1945, names Milo A. Durand and C. P. Goerz American Optical Company. The assignee was the US Goerz firm. Example I and Figure 1 are the symmetric design; the patent contains three numerical examples.

## Transcription

Two independent readings of the page images agree on all eight radii, seven internal intervals and four glass index/dispersion pairs. The table's convex/concave radius convention converts to the Cartesian sequence +20.118, +55.801, +25.089, +15.196, −15.196, −25.089, −55.801 and −20.118 mm. Both 0.316 mm air gaps remain, and the 7.366 mm central gap is split into 3.683 mm on each side of the inferred stop. No numerical transcription discrepancy was found.

The table prints D-light indices. The D line is taken as sodium at 589.3 nm; the original pairs, 1.588 / 53.4 and 1.636 / 34.8, remain unchanged. The length unit for the printed focal length of 100 is implicit and is interpreted as millimetres.

## First-order

The printed focal length of 100 compares with a computed EFL of 99.980685777 mm, a −0.0193% difference. The paraxial aperture is f/6.000, consistent with the printed f/6 or greater. Computed BFD is 90.536134447 mm, the first-to-last vertex distance is 19.782 mm, and total track is 110.318134447 mm. The image plane is paraxial; the patent prints no back focus.

The inferred stop radius is 7.130497330 mm. Real-ray entrance-pupil and image-sine equivalents are about f/5.965 and f/5.952. The stop remains at the midpoint, with approximately 0.387 mm clearance from the adjacent inner rims. Glass and air edge clearances remain positive.

## Not printed

The stop position and radius, all clear semi-diameters, the final image gap and the f-stop ladder are inferred or computed. No close-focus state, film format, mount or glass supplier is specified. The wide field is printed: the text describes 90° or more and claim 5 repeats that extent. The 203.49 mm model image circle is twice the real chief-ray image height at the stated 45° half-field, not a printed diameter.

## Product

The [C. P. Goerz American Optical Co. catalogue of about 1959](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00259/00259.pdf#page=5) supports a Rectagon family relationship through its f/6 aperture, 90° field, distortion-correction aim and four separate menisci. It describes a marketed lens originally intended for aerial mapping, but does not identify this patent. Example II's asymmetric form and approximately six-inch focal length are more plausible for that use than Example I. No production prescription is documented for Example I.

## Corrections

The attribution and field have been brought into agreement with the patent. The previous claim that only an illustrative ±10° field was supported has been replaced by the printed ±45° field and its computed pupil clipping. The unsupported description of the Rectagon as experimental has been replaced by the company catalogue's product evidence. The prescription, inferred stop and clear semi-diameters are retained.

## Remaining questions

Figure 1 appears to show outer rims of about 15 mm and inner rims of about 9.4 mm, compared with the model's outer 12 mm and inner 11/9.45 mm. These are unresolved drawing comparisons. Neither original glass supplier nor partial dispersion is known, and no source ties this numerical example to a particular Rectagon production lens.
