# GOERZ 100mm f/6 (US 2,383,115 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,383,115 A, _Photographic Lens_](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/2383115), by Milo A. Durand, assigned to C. P. Goerz American Optical Company, was filed on 17 April 1943 and published on 21 August 1945. No earlier priority date is stated. Example I is the symmetric four-meniscus design: Figure 1 is on PDF p. 1 and its numerical table and bibliography are on p. 2. The patent contains three numerical examples; the others are on p. 3, and claim 5 is on p. 4.

The [C. P. Goerz American Optical Co. catalogue, about 1959](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00259/00259.pdf#page=5), describes and illustrates the Rectagon on PDF p. 5. Its approximate date follows from 64 years of US lensmaking since 1895. It supplies historical product context, without identifying this patent or Example I.

## Transcription Check

All eight radii, seven internal intervals and four glass index/dispersion pairs agree with the Example I page images at printed precision. No numerical discrepancy was found. The table gives convex/concave radius signs; Figure 1 establishes the direction of light travel, giving the Cartesian sequence +20.118, +55.801, +25.089, +15.196, −15.196, −25.089, −55.801 and −20.118 mm.

Both 0.316 mm air gaps remain. The central 7.366 mm interval is split into 3.683 mm on each side of the inferred stop. Four separate spherical menisci, four groups, positive outer elements and negative inner elements agree with the patent. There are no cemented interfaces or aspherical surfaces.

The table prints “Index (D light)”, taken as sodium at 589.3 nm. The outer pair remains 1.588 / 53.4 and the inner pair 1.636 / 34.8. The focal length of 100 has no explicit length unit; dimensions are interpreted as millimetres without rescaling.

## First-Order Check

The calculated paraxial EFL is 99.98 mm, 0.02 mm below the printed 100. The paraxial aperture is f/6.00, consistent with the printed f/6 or greater. Calculated BFD is 90.54 mm. The first-to-last vertex distance is 19.78 mm and the first-vertex-to-image distance is 110.32 mm. The image plane is at paraxial infinity focus, 90.54 mm behind surface 8; the patent prints no back focus.

The inferred stop radius is 7.13 mm. Real-ray entrance-pupil and image-sine equivalents are about f/5.97 and f/5.95. The stop has about 0.387 mm clearance from each adjacent inner rim. Glass and air edge clearances remain positive, and the iris sets the full aperture without rim limitation.

The 45.0° chief ray clears every surface and reaches an image height of 101.745 mm at the paraxial plane, covering the declared 203.49 mm circle. At that field, 79.4% of the stop area reaches the image, corresponding to 20.6% geometric vignetting. The outer faces of the inner menisci account for the meridional clipping. These are geometric properties at the D line; they do not establish measured Rectagon brightness or resolution.

## Product Identification

The Goerz catalogue presents the Rectagon as a marketed f/6 wide-angle anastigmat covering 90°, originally intended for aerial mapping. Its four separate menisci and distortion-correction aim support a related family. They do not establish Example I as a production prescription. The asymmetric, approximately six-inch Example II is a more plausible mapping-lens candidate; this remains an inference.

No production product is documented for Example I. The Goerz name follows the patent assignee. Mount, film format, diaphragm blade count and shape, marketed focal length and aperture, and marketed minimum focus distance remain omitted.
