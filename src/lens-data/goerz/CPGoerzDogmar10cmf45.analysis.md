# C. P. Goerz Dogmar 10cm f/4.5

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 1,108,307 A\
**Inventor:** Walther Zschokke  
**Assignee:** Optische Anstalt C. P. Goerz Aktiengesellschaft  
**Filed:** 17 January 1913  
**Granted:** 25 August 1914  
**Example details:** Sole numerical example, focal length 100 and aperture ratio 1:4.5.

Walther Zschokke's patent supplies the complete spherical prescription but does not name Dogmar. A Goerz American Optical advertisement in Camera Work No. 47 (July 1914, PDF page 92) names the new Dogmar f/4.5–5.5 and describes its three-foci use. The 1916 Goerz catalogue gives commercial stock context. A later Goerz American catalogue gives front- and rear-cell focal lengths of about 1.92× and 1.52× the complete lens; the patent prescription gives 1.924× and 1.582×, respectively.

W. J. Smith's Modern Lens Design, Fig. 13.22, p. 464, identifies US 1,108,307 and its focal-length-100 prescription as the Dogmar. The Science Museum Group records a Goerz Tenax with a 10 cm f/4.5 Dogmar, confirming that commercial focal length and aperture. Smith's separate enlarger example in Fig. 14.15 supplies none of this model's numerical data.

These sources connect the patent to the Dogmar family without establishing the exact prescription or glass melt of every 10 cm retail version. Source f = 100 and all source lengths are retained numerically in millimetres, with scale factor 1 and no fitting.

## Optical Architecture

Four separate spherical elements form four air-separated components: positive outer elements and negative inner elements. The patent calls the positive glass highly refractive baryta crown and describes meniscus-shaped air spaces between each positive/negative pair. Its asymmetric inner curvatures are directed toward the central diaphragm to improve the corrected double objective.

The central source gap is 5.4 mm. The drawing locates a diaphragm within it, but neither an axial split nor a hole diameter is tabulated. The model uses a disclosed midpoint hypothesis, not a source measurement.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1: Biconvex Positive

Source nD = 1.61412; derived νD = 56.3412844; baryta-crown glass with BACD6 (Hoya catalog row) as a spectral proxy under the site's proxy tolerance; historical supplier and melt unspecified. The positive front element participates in the collecting front cell; no exact catalogue glass is identified. Its isolated thick-element focal length in air is +35.99 mm at sodium D.

### L2: Biconcave Negative

Source nD = 1.56890; derived νD = 42.6142322; S-TIL26 (Ohara catalog row) spectral proxy under the site's proxy tolerance; historical supplier and melt unspecified. The surface facing the diaphragm is more strongly curved than its outer surface, consistent with the patent's arrangement. Its air separation from L1 is 1.8 mm. Its isolated thick-element focal length in air is −38.77 mm at sodium D.

### L3: Biconcave Negative

Source nD = 1.54820; derived νD = 46.1447811; Unmatched glass. The diaphragm-facing surface is the less strongly curved surface of this negative element. The two negative elements have different curvatures and glasses. Its isolated thick-element focal length in air is −40.94 mm at sodium D.

### L4: Biconvex Positive

Source nD = 1.61412; derived νD = 56.3412844; baryta-crown glass with BACD6 (Hoya catalog row) as a spectral proxy under the site's proxy tolerance; historical supplier and melt unspecified. Its source thickness is 3.6 mm rather than the front element's 4.2 mm; the two cells are asymmetric. Its isolated thick-element focal length in air is +35.32 mm at sodium D.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The source prints nD, nG′ and positive values labeled C–F. The positive C–F entries are interpreted as dispersion magnitudes nF − nC, not negative signed subtraction. The νD values in the glass labels are calculated as (nD − 1)/(nF − nC), rather than printed Abbe numbers. Smith's rounded glass codes corroborate this interpretation, but no exact melt or full dispersion curve is identified.

All four elements retain the sodium nD reference. The estimated nd − nD difference of about 1.0–1.2 × 10⁻⁴ exceeds the patent's five-decimal print precision, so the source values are not treated as helium d-line indices. BACD6 supplies the wavelength dependence for L1/L4 and S-TIL26 for L2. Each proxy curve is anchored at the printed sodium-D index; the stored source nD and derived νD values stay unchanged.

These rows meet the site's proxy tolerance: Abbe number within about 0.25 of the source-derived value and index within about 0.002. The tabulated index differences compare catalog helium-d indices with source sodium-D indices; they are selection differences, not substitutions for the printed nD. These are qualified spectral proxies, not exact matches or identifications of the historical glass, supplier or melt. L3 remains Unmatched because no row in the site's catalog lies within that Abbe window, and its wavelength dependence uses the source nD/νD Abbe approximation.

The patent's nG′ values, 1.62803 for L1/L4, 1.58637 for L2 and 1.56364 for L3, are not fitted by the proxy curves or L3's Abbe approximation; chromatic results therefore do not reproduce the complete published dispersion data.

## Focus Mechanism

The model is infinity-only. Its terminal image interval, 88.708856474 mm, is calculated from the source prescription, not a printed back focal distance. No source radius, thickness or refractive index was altered to focus the lens.

The patent describes use on a bellows camera, including each cell separately with additional extension and stopping down. The cited Tenax Press Camera uses rack-and-pinion focusing on the body. The model includes neither finite-focus spacings nor separate-cell configurations; no retail minimum focus distance is established.

## Aperture and Iris Assumptions

All eight optical surfaces use an inferred common semi-diameter of 11.5 mm, with equal front and rear rim heights on each element. The patent drawing supports these square-cut, comparable-height outlines. Its roughly 12 mm rim estimates differ by about 5–7%, within the uncertainty of a schematic whose individual axial spacings do not all match the table. The rims clear the nominal f/4.5 axial beam and the 6 × 9 cm corner chief ray; they are not published manufacturing dimensions. The rear element has approximately 0.438 mm edge thickness at this radius.

At f/4.5 and infinity, modeled geometric pupil transmission is 81.4% at 20.8°, 73.4% at 23.8° and 63.4% at the 26.8° format corner. These are pupil-clipping fractions, not total image illumination including angular falloff or coating losses.

The iris is placed 2.7 mm into the source's 5.4 mm central gap. At the full inferred rim, its plane has 0.905003 mm clearance from the front adjacent surface and 1.646454 mm from the rear adjacent surface. The f/4.5 entrance pupil gives an iris radius of 9.550146968 mm. Both the midpoint and this radius are inferred, not published dimensions. The f/36 endpoint follows the Science Museum Group's 10 cm f/4.5–36 Tenax Press Camera specimen, rather than a minimum aperture stated in the patent.

All eight source radii and seven axial intervals are retained, without added spacers or altered element thicknesses.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The prescription gives EFL 99.996511940 mm at f/4.5, with 20.000 mm from the first to last glass surface and calculated back focus of 88.708856474 mm. Stop position and clear apertures remain inferred.

The patent and catalogue association does not establish factory tolerances, an exact production glass melt or measured image quality. Separate-cell use and finite-focus performance are outside this model.
