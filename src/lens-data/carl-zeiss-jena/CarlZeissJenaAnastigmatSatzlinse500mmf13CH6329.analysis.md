# Carl Zeiss Jena Anastigmat-Satzlinse 500mm f/13 — CH 6329

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** CH 6329 (Eidgenössisches Amt für geistiges Eigentum, Patentschrift Nr. 6329, Klasse 49)<br>
**Filed:** 15 February 1893 ("15. Februar 1893, 7 Uhr, p.")<br>
**Published:** 15 August 1893<br>
**Inventor:** Paul Rudolph<br>
**Applicant:** Dr. Paul Rudolph, in Jena (Deutschland), the patentee; agent E. Blum & Cie., Zürich<br>
**Title:** *Dreifache Linse für photographische Objektive*<br>
**Example details:** Beispiel 2 of two (page 2), given in units of the focal length and drawn as Fig. 2 "für die
Brennweite 500 mm"; every length is scaled ×500 here

The patent sets out to bring into one cemented lens the correction that the anastigmat doublets reached with two
separate members. In such double objectives, "in bekannter Weise", one member is an achromat whose positive lens has the higher index,
the other one whose indices are graded the opposite way, so that the cemented surface of one acts as a collecting
surface and that of the other as a dispersing one, both for the focal length and for the spherical aberration. The
patent says the same opposed action can be had inside a single cemented lens if it is made of three parts: either a
collective lens between two dispersing lenses, one of higher and one of lower index than it, or a dispersing lens
between two collective lenses graded the same way. Either way one cemented surface collects and the other disperses.

The patent prints one example of each form, both in units of the focal length and both stated for f = 500 mm.
Beispiel 1 (Fig. 1) has a collective middle lens and is rated 1:16; Beispiel 2 (Fig. 2), this file, has a dispersing
middle lens, a largest effective opening of 0.077 (1:13) and a field angle of about 90°. Indices are given for the
Fraunhofer D line and for the hydrogen line Hγ. The text requires the cemented lens to be a meniscus or a very unequal
biconvex lens with its weaker surface toward the diaphragm, and it allows the outer lens on the stop side to take
either the lower or the higher index. It names three uses: the single lens as a landscape objective, two equal lenses
set symmetrically about the diaphragm in the manner of the aplanats, and a lens combined with a second, different one
or with an ordinary achromat into an unsymmetrical anastigmat, where the elements must be recomputed so that the
residues of one member cancel those of the other. The single claim protects a cemented achromatic lens of three parts
whose outer lenses have one a lower and one a higher index than the middle lens.

Rudolph, at the Zeiss works in Jena, filed it in his own name. Moritz von Rohr (1899) dates the type of Beispiel 2 to
the end of 1891 and records that Zeiss issued it in 1893 as the Anastigmat-Satzlinse, Series VI, at 1:14.5; he cites
Rudolph's British patent 4692 of 22 April 1893 for it. The Swiss filing is two months earlier. The example's own
ratings, 500 mm and 1:13, are not those of a catalogued lens, so the file carries no mount or format and names the
example in its title.

## Optical Architecture

The lens is a single cemented group of three elements behind a front stop: a positive meniscus, a thin biconcave lens
and a biconvex lens, the group as a whole concave toward the stop and convex toward the image. The stop is 5.20 mm in
front of the first surface, the vertex length is 17.90 mm and the paraxial back focus 546.46 mm. The table traces to an
EFL of 500.251 mm, 0.05 % above the stated 500 mm.

| Surface | Index step | Surface power (mm⁻¹) | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| r1 = −71.15 | air → 1.52246 | −0.00734 | concave front face toward the stop |
| r2 = −27.35 | 1.52246 → 1.56724 | −0.00164 | strongly curved junction, crown to denser flint |
| r3 = +103.65 | 1.56724 → 1.61120 | +0.00042 | weak junction, flint to denser crown |
| r4 = −63.00 | 1.61120 → air | +0.00970 | rear face, the main collective surface |

The paraxial power is concentrated in the two outer faces; the junctions carry the correction. With thick-lens focal
lengths of +79.8, −37.9 and +65.1 mm, the three members are individually six to thirteen times stronger than the whole
lens, and nearly all of their power cancels. At r2 the low-index crown L1 meets the higher-index flint L2, the order
of the old achromats; at r3 the flint meets the heaviest baryta crown L3, the order the Jena baryta glasses made
possible. One old and one new achromat share the middle lens, the opposed gradation of the claim inside one cemented
lens. Von Rohr files the type under "2aα" in his scheme of single lenses derived from the anastigmat doublet.

The Petzval sum is 6.80 × 10⁻⁴ mm⁻¹, about half that of a thin single crown lens of the same focal length, a Petzval
radius of 1471 mm or 2.9 f. The concave front face is the main reason: its negative Petzval contribution offsets
most of the rear face's.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Positive Meniscus, concave to the stop

nD = 1.52246 (sodium D), nG₁ = 1.53373; no nC or nF, so no νD. Glass: Unmatched (patent nD/nG₁ only); von Rohr's
ordinary silicate crown. f = +79.8 mm.

The front member and the lowest-index glass. Its front face is the strongest negative surface in the lens and the
main source of its low Petzval sum; its rear face is the deeply curved junction r2, whose sag at the 22.5 mm rim is
11.8 mm. The lens reaches a knife edge at 22.68 mm and keeps 0.20 mm at the rim.

### L2 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.56724, nG₁ = 1.58092; no nC or nF, so no νD. Glass: Unmatched (patent nD/nG₁ only); von Rohr's baryta light
flint. f = −37.9 mm.

The thin middle lens, 2.60 mm on the axis and 16.9 mm at the rim, that both achromats share. The index rises by 0.045
at r2 and by 0.044 at r3, so inside the cemented group neither junction has much focusing power, and their work is the
correction of aberrations and colour. Its index is the value von Rohr footnotes as misprinted in the *British Journal
of Photography*; the Swiss patent prints it as he corrected it.

### L3 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.61120, nG₁ = 1.62483; no nC or nF, so no νD. Glass: Unmatched (patent nD/nG₁ only); von Rohr's heaviest
baryta crown. f = +65.1 mm.

The rear collective lens and the highest-index glass. Its rear face is the strongest positive surface in the lens and
fixes the focal length most sensitively. It reaches a knife edge at 23.03 mm and keeps 0.32 mm at the rim.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent defines each glass by its index for the Fraunhofer D line and for "die Linie H1 γ des Wasserstoffspectrums",
the hydrogen line Hγ at about 434.0 nm, and names no glass types.

| Element | nD | nG₁ | nG₁ − nD | Von Rohr's name (p. 373) | ν̄ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | 1.52246 | 1.53373 | 0.01127 | gewöhnliches Silikat-Crown (ordinary silicate crown) | 46.4 |
| L2 | 1.56724 | 1.58092 | 0.01368 | Baryt-Leichtflint (baryta light flint) | 41.5 |
| L3 | 1.61120 | 1.62483 | 0.01363 | schwerstes Baryt-Crown (heaviest baryta crown) | 44.8 |

Hγ is neither the F line nor the mercury g line the schema stores as ng, and the patent prints no nC, so no Abbe number
can be formed. The data file keeps nD and records nG₁ in comments only, with no catalog glass as a proxy; the model
traces at nD alone and shows no colour error. Von Rohr's ν̄ = (nD − 1)/(nG′ − nD) comes from exactly these pairs
(46.36, 41.46, 44.84) and is given only as context. Its ordering shows the chromatic logic: L3 has a higher index
than L2 and a lower dispersion toward the blue, the new-achromat pairing.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent gives only the infinity prescription. The model is infinity-only: the last gap is the computed paraxial
back focus, and no focusing motion is modelled.

## Aperture, Field and Rims

The stop is a plain diaphragm 5.20 mm in front of the lens, so it is also the entrance pupil. At the patent's 1:13 its
radius is 19.24 mm, and the axial beam reaches 21.18 mm at the rear face, inside the 22.5 mm rims. Traced on axis, the
longitudinal spherical aberration is +3.00 mm at the rim of the pupil, −4.43 mm at 0.85, −5.67 mm at 0.7 and −3.97 mm
at 0.5; the marginal ray crosses zero near 1:13.55, and the deepest zone is −5.70 mm at a height of 13.9 mm. Reduced to
f = 100 the zone is −1.14 mm, against von Rohr's "about −1.1 mm".

The patent gives a field angle of about 90°, and the model traces to ω = 45°. The chief ray passes there at heights of
5.0, 9.0, 11.7 and 14.2 mm on r1 to r4 and lands 440.4 mm from the axis, 12 % short of f·tan ω: a front-stop meniscus
of this kind has marked barrel distortion. A meridional trace through the model passes about 92 % of the stop-filling
beam at 20°, 82 % at 34° and 73 % at 45°; the paraxial rim limit is 52.6°, at r4.

Coddington traces along the real chief ray, measured along the axis from the paraxial image plane (positive away from
the lens), put the tangential and sagittal images at +11.7 and −12.3 mm at 34°: a difference of 24.0 mm with their
mean 0.3 mm inside the image plane. Reduced to f = 100 the difference is 4.79 mm, von Rohr's "about 4.8 mm" at 34°,
and the flattening he describes. Beyond that the astigmatism grows fast: at 45° the two images lie 79.2 and 4.4 mm
beyond the image plane. Neither the patent nor von Rohr says at what stop the 90° is meant; f/16 and f/22 are provided.

The clear radii are measured from Fig. 2, which is close to scale along the axis: the stop sits about 0.011 f in front
of r1 for a printed 0.0104, and r1 is drawn with its printed curvature, while r4 is drawn flatter than printed. The
figure draws the cemented lens square-ended at about 0.048 f in semi-height, 24 mm at this scale, with L1 and L3 running
out at the rim. At the printed radii those two lenses reach knife edges at 22.68 and 23.03 mm, so the drawn height
cannot be reached; the model takes 22.5 mm on all four faces.

## Design Heritage and Context

Moritz von Rohr's *Theorie und Geschichte des photographischen Objektivs* (1899) prints this example reduced to
f = 100 as Fig. 137, "P. Rudolph: Anastigmat-Satzlinse": r = 14.2, 5.5, 20.7 and 12.6, d = 1.7, 0.5 and 1.4, a stop
distance of 1.0 and the patent's three nD values, computed for 1:14.4 and ω = 34°. Every value is the patent's table
×100 rounded to 0.1. His caption gives the source as "P. Rudolph. 1.", which in his bibliography (p. 424) is the
German patent 56109 of 1890 on the Anastigmat doublet; his text on pp. 373 and 377 cites entry 6, the British patent
4692 of 1893, for these lenses, so the caption's 1 reads as a misprint for 6. He reports full correction of the margin at about 1:12.5 with a zone of about −1.1 mm (the patent table corrects the
margin near 1:13.55 and is +1.41 mm long at 1:12.5, reduced to f = 100), and an
astigmatic difference of about 4.8 mm at 34°.

Von Rohr's account (pp. 373–379) gives the history. Rudolph found the type at the end of 1891 but at first gave
priority to the new f/4.5, f/6.3 and f/9 doublets. When news of Goerz's Doppel-Anastigmat application reached him, he
filed the single lenses for patents in several countries; under German patent law the German application was refused, but a right of prior use remained, and the 1891
construction was made as Series VI at 1:14.5. Von Rohr concludes that Rudolph (end of 1891) and E. von Höegh (end of
1892) found the astigmatically corrected single lens of this type independently, and that Rudolph's construction data
were the first to be printed. He also judges it the first landscape lens since Thomas Grubb's to claim spherical
correction in the strict sense, with the sine condition also fulfilled.

The Series VI single lens and the Series VIa sets made from two of them were issued in 1893. Von Rohr shows the set lens
as Fig. 139, two of these lenses in symmetric opposition computed for 1:6.8 and ω = 29°, the symmetrical doublet the
patent describes, and lists the issued combinations as holosymmetric at 1:6.9 and hemisymmetric at 1:7.7 and 1:8.5.
From spring 1895 the series was replaced by the four-lens cemented Anastigmatlinse of Series VII and its paired form,
Series VIIa. The other form in the patent, Beispiel 1, with a collective middle lens, is the type von Rohr describes as
two achromats of normal glass pairs; its correction, especially of astigmatism, was worse, and it was not introduced.
