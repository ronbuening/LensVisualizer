# CARL ZEISS JENA 100mm f/2 (DE 700,699 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[DE 700,699 C, _Photographisches Objektiv_](https://patents.google.com/patent/DE700699C/de), sole example, prints F = 100 mm and 1:2. The front page names Ludwig Bertele and Zeiss Ikon Akt.-Ges., Dresden. Protection dates from 9 January 1938; the grant was announced on 28 November 1940 and the Patentschrift issued on 3 January 1941. The table prints lowercase nd.

The [historical Sonnar account](https://www.zeissikonveb.de/start/objektive/normalobjektive/sonnar.html), section 5.2, associates DRP 700.699 with a lower-distortion Sonnar 2/5 cm that did not enter series production. Its [distortion plot](https://impro.usercontent.one/appid/oneComWsb/domain/zeissikonveb.de/media/zeissikonveb.de/onewebmedia/Marco/Carl%20Zeiss%20Jena/Sonnar%202,0%20verbesserte%20Verzeichnung.jpg) extends to 22.0° half-field and about +0.98% distortion.

## Transcription Check

All ten radii, seven glass thicknesses and seven nd/νd pairs agree with the patent page images examined at 300 and 600 dpi. The first air space is 0.38 mm. The second is 9.44 mm, divided at the iris into 7.50 mm after R6 and 1.94 mm before R7. No transcription mismatch was found. Dimensions retain the printed 100 mm scale and millimetre units. There are no aspherical surfaces or variable spacings.

The drawing supports clear radii of approximately 28 mm at R1/R2 and 23.7 mm at R3–R5 and R8–R10. These measurements used vertex spacings to establish about 12.1 pixels/mm. The roughly 16 mm drawing readings at R6/R7 are smaller than the retained clear radii of 16.8 and 17 mm; axial marginal-ray heights there are about 16.59 and 16.38 mm.

## First-Order Check

The calculated paraxial EFL is 100.012 mm, about 0.012% above the printed 100 mm. The paraxial f-number is 2.00, with a stop radius of 16.304 mm. Calculated BFD is 54.535 mm and the first-vertex-to-image distance is 122.105 mm.

Finite-aperture tracing gives approximately f/2.02 with the retained iris. An exact f/2 entrance radius of 25.003 mm requires a stop radius of about 16.49 mm; the present iris admits an entrance radius of about 24.729 mm. The image plane remains at paraxial focus. Only infinity focus is described; all groups retain fixed relative spacings.

The surfaces stay clear of each other; the iris sets the full aperture. The 22.0° chief ray reaches the approximately 40.80 mm image radius without blockage.

Chief-ray distortion is approximately +0.27% at 10.0°, +0.58% at 15.0° and +0.98% at 22.0°, consistent with the historical curve. This establishes chief-ray coverage, while off-axis marginal rays remain vignetted.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The patent gives the iris position, but no iris diameter, back focus, field angle, image format or clear radii. The stop radius follows paraxial f/2; the image plane is calculated paraxially. The clear radii combine drawing measurements with the axial-beam clearances at R6/R7, so no single description of their origin applies to every surface.

The 22.0° field follows the historical distortion plot. The traced chief-ray image height is about 40.805 mm, giving an inferred image-circle diameter of 81.61 mm at the printed 100 mm scale. This is not a published production image format or a claim of a fully unvignetted edge bundle. The modeled iris spans f/2 to f/16; the source supplies no minimum iris opening.

The printed glass coordinates are retained. Catalog curves approximate dispersion for L1/L5, L2, L3 and L6; L4/L7 use an Abbe-based estimate. No measured partial dispersion, transmission or production polychromatic performance is established. Catalog names identify matching optical properties, without establishing historical suppliers or melts.

## Product Identification

No production product is documented for this prescription. The 1–3–3 layout resembles the Contax Sonnar 85/2 and Jupiter-9, but this does not establish their use of this patent. US 2,254,511 is a different eight-element f/1.4 design, rather than another publication of DE 700,699.

The title retains the patent's 100 mm design scale. The patent was filed by [Zeiss Ikon AG](https://patents.google.com/patent/DE700699C/de), the [Carl Zeiss Foundation's camera company](https://rolleigraphy.eu/name.php), where Bertele designed the [Sonnar lenses made by Carl Zeiss Jena](https://www.zeissikonveb.de/start/objektive/normalobjektive/sonnar.html) and sold to Zeiss Ikon; this site lists his Zeiss Ikon patent designs under [Carl Zeiss Jena](https://surfaceandstop.com/authors/ludwig-bertele/). No mount, production image format, diaphragm blade count or shape, marketed focal length, marketed f-number or marketed minimum focus distance is assigned.

## Corrections, 2026-11-20

- The patent designation changed from DE 700,699 A to DE 700,699 C to identify the issued Patentschrift. The assignee's spelling was shortened to Zeiss Ikon AG. Ludwig Bertele, the sole example and the 1941 publication year were confirmed and retained.
- The subtitle reads “DE 700,699 C, sole example — Zeiss Ikon / Bertele.” The specifications are “7 ELEMENTS / 3 GROUPS”, “DESIGN f = 100 mm”, “DESIGN F/2” and “ALL SPHERICAL”.
- All seven index references changed from sodium D to helium d. Five of seven printed index/dispersion pairs match catalog d-line rows; none match sodium D. Lowercase nd alone does not establish the reference wavelength.
- Repeated shape labels became Element 1 through Element 7. Short optical roles and calculated in-air focal lengths were added: +131.30, +95.27, +104.00, −33.27, +104.04, −41.07 and +45.23 mm. Shape descriptions already agreed with the radius signs. C1 now identifies L2–L4 and C2 identifies L5–L7; each cemented triplet has a spanning annotation.
- R1/R2 clear radii changed from 26.5/26.5 to 28/28 mm. R3–R5 changed from 24.5/23/22.5 to 23.7/23.7/23.7 mm. R8–R10 changed from 17/17/17 to 23.7/23.7/23.7 mm. These drawing-based changes preserve the axial beam and reduce rear-rim vignetting. R6/R7 remain at 16.8/17 mm because smaller drawing readings would clip the f/2 axial beam.
- The image plane is 54.5352 mm behind R10. The field increased from 5.0° to 22.0°, and the inferred 81.61 mm image circle was added using the historical plot and traced chief ray. The one-sided R10 bundle-width clipping estimate at 22.0° decreased from about 80% to 40% with the larger rear rims; this is not an area-weighted transmission measurement.
- `focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m to represent infinity; it is not a measured minimum focus distance. No near-focus state is given.
- The explicit `aperture.maxFNumber` value of 16 was removed because the patent supplies no minimum iris opening. The design f/2 and selectable f-stops [2, 2.8, 4, 5.6, 8, 11, 16] remain.

### 2026-11-20 second review on the local site

- The live diagram was compared with the drawing sheet (PDF page 3) at 600 dpi. The 67.57 mm span from R1 to R10 measures 1,632 px, or 24.15 px/mm. The sheet is slightly sheared, so each surface's upper half sits up to 0.7 mm behind its lower half; heights and sags were therefore read as the mean of the two halves. Read that way, the drawn sags of R1 and R10 at about 23 mm height (4.06 and 3.00 mm) agree with the printed radii (3.96 and 3.01 mm), so the scale holds across the axis as well as along it.
- Rims read again at the centres of the ink lines: front lens 28.15 mm (model 28), middle triplet 23.6 mm and rear triplet 23.7 mm (model 23.7 for both). The R6 curve ends at 15.6–15.9 mm and the R7 curve at 15.2–16.0 mm; the model's 16.8 and 17 mm stay on the f/2 axial beam (16.59 and 16.38 mm), 7% and 9% above the drawing. No clear radius changed.
- Chamfers: the drawing carries the middle triplet at full height about 3.7 mm past R5 before chamfering to the R6 curve end, and the rear triplet about 2.6 mm ahead of R8 before chamfering to the R7 curve end. The model joins the R6 and R7 rims to the 23.7 mm edges with straight lines. A flat land carrying R6 out to 23.7 mm was tried on the page. It costs no light and narrows the opening between the two triplets at their rims from 15.8 mm to 6.5 mm (about 8.6 mm drawn), but it squares off a corner the drawing chamfers. Placed by its vertices, the drawing departs from the straight edge by at most 3.0 mm and from the land by 3.8 mm; placed by the R6 curve end, by 3.6 and 3.0 mm. The two outlines are equally close within the reading error, so the straight edge stays and no land was added. A land at R7 would put the rear triplet's corner 2.9 mm ahead of the drawn corner, against 2.2 mm behind it now, so none was added there either.
- No face sits below its starting value and the lens has no lands or rim cuts. Wide-open transmission stays 98.6, 92.8, 77.3 and 64.9% at 8.3, 13.8, 19.3 and 22.0°; the 22.0° half-field, f/2 and the clear corner chief ray are unchanged.
- Group brackets changed from “G1 (1)”, “G2 (3 cemented)” and “G3 (3 cemented)” to “G1 (+)”, “G2 (−)” and “G3 (+)”. The old suffixes counted elements and repeated the C1 and C2 brackets, and “G1 (1)” overran the front lens so that the first two labels were staggered on the page. The new suffixes give the sign of each component's focal length (+131.30, −203.41 and +111.27 mm), matching the patent's description of two collecting members enclosing a dispersing meniscus; the patent prints no group numbers.
- Found correct on the page: names L1–L7 are the patent's own lens designations; the element types agree with the radius signs; the inspector shows the in-air focal lengths +131.3, +95.27, +104.00, −33.27, +104.04, −41.07 and +45.23 mm, which a paraxial check reproduces, with TRIPLET C1 on L2–L4 and TRIPLET C2 on L5–L7. The stop sits in the printed 7.50/1.94 mm split of the 9.44 mm Blendenraum. No surface is aspheric, no element carries an anomalous-dispersion tag, and the patent names no special glass. The claim's condition holds: L5 and L7 exceed L6's index by 0.103 and 0.152, at least one unit in the first decimal.
- Movement: the lens is infinity only. The page shows the focus control disabled and marked “Not modeled” with the plain infinity description, and no zoom control.
- Glass: the table, read again at 300 dpi, confirms L4 at 1.6883 / 31.2 (the SF8 type at 1.68893 misses by 6.3e-4) and L7 at 1.6689 / 48.8. The labels match at the d line: N-SK16 (Schott) on L1 and L5, BAFD7 (Hoya) on L2, BAK2 (Sumita) on L3 and S-NSL36 (Ohara) on L6, all within 6e-5 in nd and 0.08 in νd. N-SK16 is the only Schott row that matches and stays as the period maker's glass. For L2, L3 and L6 no Schott row in the catalog matches, and the Ohara, Hoya and Sumita rows that do tie (S-BAH27 and BASF7 for L2, S-BAL12 for L3), so those labels stay. L4 and L7 stay unmatched: the patent prints only nd and ν for its single example, and no Schott, Hoya (including obsolete), Ohara, Sumita or Hikari row lies inside the exact window. L7's pair recurs as L7 of Bertele's US 2,254,511, which points to a real glass of the period rather than a fitted value. Five elements use catalog curves and two use Abbe estimates; none lacks dispersion data, and the colour analysis runs. The patent prints no aberration curves to compare colour against.
- The front page names Ludwig Bertele as inventor and Zeiss Ikon Akt.-Ges., Dresden, as applicant, issued 3 January 1941. The assignee spelling Zeiss Ikon AG matches the other Zeiss Ikon entries on this site.

## Remaining Questions

- L4/L7 lack matching catalog curves. All historical glass suppliers and melt identities remain unknown.
- The inferred 81.61 mm image circle is not printed by the patent. At 22.0°, the chief ray and one marginal extreme were found, while the opposite marginal extreme was not solved; a complete unvignetted edge bundle is not established.
- No source documents a production lens, mount, image format, diaphragm blade count or shape, minimum iris opening, marketed specifications or close-focus mechanism. These values remain omitted; the f/16 endpoint is not a published limit.
