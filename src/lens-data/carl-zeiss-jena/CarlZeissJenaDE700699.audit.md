# CARL ZEISS JENA 100mm f/2 (DE 700,699 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[DE 700,699 C, _Photographisches Objektiv_](https://patents.google.com/patent/DE700699C/de), sole example, prints F = 100 mm and 1:2. The front page names Ludwig Bertele and Zeiss Ikon Akt.-Ges., Dresden. Protection dates from 9 January 1938; the grant was announced on 28 November 1940 and the Patentschrift issued on 3 January 1941. The table prints lowercase nd.

The [historical Sonnar account](https://www.zeissikonveb.de/start/objektive/normalobjektive/sonnar.html), section 5.2, associates DRP 700.699 with a lower-distortion Sonnar 2/5 cm that did not enter series production. Its [distortion plot](https://impro.usercontent.one/appid/oneComWsb/domain/zeissikonveb.de/media/zeissikonveb.de/onewebmedia/Marco/Carl%20Zeiss%20Jena/Sonnar%202,0%20verbesserte%20Verzeichnung.jpg) extends to 22.0° half-field and about +0.98% distortion.

## Transcription Check

All ten radii, seven glass thicknesses and seven nd/νd pairs agree with the patent page images examined at 300 and 600 dpi. The first air space is 0.38 mm. The second is 9.44 mm, divided at the iris into 7.50 mm after R6 and 1.94 mm before R7. No transcription mismatch was found. Dimensions retain the printed 100 mm scale and millimetre units. There are no aspherical surfaces or variable spacings.

The drawing supports clear radii of approximately 28 mm at R1/R2 and 23.7 mm at R3–R5 and R8–R10. These measurements used vertex spacings to establish about 12.1 pixels/mm. The roughly 16 mm drawing readings at R6/R7 are smaller than the retained clear radii of 16.8 and 17 mm; axial marginal-ray heights there are about 16.59 and 16.38 mm.

## First-Order Check

The calculated paraxial EFL is 100.012 mm, about 0.012% above the printed 100 mm. The paraxial f-number is 2.00, with a stop radius of 16.304 mm. Calculated BFD is 54.535 mm and the first-vertex-to-image distance is 122.105 mm. The stored last gap is less than 0.0001 mm beyond the calculated paraxial image plane.

Finite-aperture tracing gives approximately f/2.02 with the retained iris. An exact f/2 entrance radius of 25.003 mm requires a stop radius of about 16.49 mm; the present iris admits an entrance radius of about 24.729 mm. The image plane remains at paraxial focus. Only infinity focus is described; all groups retain fixed relative spacings.

Surface geometry has no edge-thickness, air-gap intrusion or stop-clearance errors. The finite-aperture f-number is within 3% of f/2, with no axial rim, iris or tracing limitation and no failed result. Image-circle checks find no undersized surfaces or omitted results. The field-coverage check at a 1.01 ratio threshold reaches 100%: the 22.0° chief ray reaches the approximately 40.80 mm image radius without blockage.

Chief-ray distortion is approximately +0.27% at 10.0°, +0.58% at 15.0° and +0.98% at 22.0°, consistent with the historical curve. This establishes chief-ray coverage, while off-axis marginal rays remain vignetted.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The patent gives the iris position, but no iris diameter, back focus, field angle, image format or clear radii. The stop radius follows paraxial f/2; the image plane is calculated paraxially. The clear radii combine drawing measurements with the axial-beam clearances at R6/R7, so no single description of their origin applies to every surface.

The 22.0° field follows the historical distortion plot. The traced chief-ray image height is about 40.805 mm, giving an inferred image-circle diameter of 81.61 mm at the printed 100 mm scale. This is not a published production image format or a claim of a fully unvignetted edge bundle. The selectable stops from f/2 to f/16 are a display convention; the source supplies no minimum iris opening.

The printed glass coordinates are retained. Catalog curves approximate dispersion for L1/L5, L2, L3 and L6; L4/L7 use an Abbe-based estimate. No measured partial dispersion, transmission or production polychromatic performance is established. Catalog names identify matching optical properties, without establishing historical suppliers or melts.

## Product Identification

No production product is documented for this prescription. The 1–3–3 layout resembles the Contax Sonnar 85/2 and Jupiter-9, but this does not establish their use of this patent. US 2,254,511 is a different eight-element f/1.4 design, rather than another publication of DE 700,699.

The title retains the patent's 100 mm design scale. The optical maker is Carl Zeiss Jena and the patent assignee is Zeiss Ikon AG. No mount, production image format, diaphragm blade count or shape, marketed focal length, marketed f-number or marketed minimum focus distance is assigned.

## Remaining Questions

- R6/R7 clear radii exceed the drawing readings to preserve the axial f/2 beam. Their exact optical rim and land shapes remain uncertain.
- L4/L7 lack matching catalog curves. All historical glass suppliers and melt identities remain unknown.
- The inferred 81.61 mm image circle is not printed by the patent. At 22.0°, the chief ray and one marginal extreme were found, while the opposite marginal extreme was not solved; a complete unvignetted edge bundle is not established.
- No source documents a production lens, mount, image format, diaphragm blade count or shape, minimum iris opening, marketed specifications or close-focus mechanism. These values remain omitted; the f/16 endpoint is a default rather than a published limit.
