# CARL ZEISS JENA 100mm f/2 (US 2,188,523 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [US 2,188,523 A, Photographic Objective](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2188523A/en)\
**Inventors:** Ludwig Bertele\
**Assignee:** Zeiss Ikon AG\
**Published:** January 30, 1940\
**Filed:** September 14, 1938\
**German priority:** January 8, 1938\
**Example details:** Sole numerical example, PDF page 2; section drawing on PDF page 1.

The patent describes a seven-element, three-group photographic objective at 100 mm f/2. Its ten signed radii, nine axial distances, seven refractive indices and seven Abbe numbers are preserved. The index heading is lowercase nd, interpreted as the helium d line; the patent does not state a numerical wavelength.

DE 700,699 C covers the same invention, with the same inventor, applicant and claim but a different numerical example. Marco Kröger identifies that German design as an attempt to reduce the distortion of the Sonnar 2/5 cm and reports that the improvement never entered production ([Sonnar history, §5.2](https://www.zeissikonveb.de/start/objektive/normalobjektive/sonnar.html)). No production lens is documented for this US prescription.

## Optical Architecture

The layout is a positive front singlet, a cemented middle triplet and a cemented rear triplet: 1 | 3 | 3. All surfaces are spherical. The front singlet has an in-air focal length of +133.12 mm, the middle triplet is net negative at −207.17 mm, and the rear triplet is positive at +110.29 mm. These group powers combine to give a computed EFL of 100.05 mm, only 0.05 mm above the printed 100 mm.

The four cemented interfaces are R4, R5, R8 and R9. The rear triplet places a low-index biconcave element between two higher-index positive elements. All three claims state this index relationship; claims 2 and 3 add the nearly equal magnitudes of its concave radii, 44.84 and 41.79 mm.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below describe each element alone in air, rather than its power within a cemented assembly. The patent gives no glass trade names. Element 2 matches a present-day catalog glass; elements 1, 3 and 5 use spectral proxies, while elements 4, 6 and 7 remain unmatched.

### Element 1 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.6200, νd = 60.3. Glass: S-BSM16 (Ohara), spectral proxy. f = +133.12 mm. The front singlet collects light for the two cemented groups. Its convex face points toward the object.

### Elements 2–4 — Middle Cemented Triplet C1

Element 2: nd = 1.7015, νd = 41.2. Glass: BAFD7 (Hoya), catalog match. f = +96.12 mm. This positive meniscus forms the front of the triplet.

Element 3: nd = 1.5487, νd = 45.8. Glass: S-TIL1 (Ohara), spectral proxy. f = +99.68 mm. This biconvex element shares both faces with its neighbors.

Element 4: nd = 1.6883, νd = 30.0. Glass: Unmatched. f = −33.01 mm. Its strong negative power makes the middle triplet net diverging despite the two positive elements ahead of it.

### Elements 5–7 — Rear Cemented Triplet C2

Element 5: nd = 1.6200, νd = 60.3. Glass: S-BSM16 (Ohara), spectral proxy. f = +105.71 mm. This positive meniscus has its concave face toward the stop space.

Element 6: nd = 1.5150, νd = 50.0. Glass: Unmatched. f = −41.81 mm. This biconcave element has a lower index than either neighboring element, as specified in the claims.

Element 7: nd = 1.6682, νd = 48.8. Glass: Unmatched. f = +45.63 mm. This thick biconvex element completes the converging rear group.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The printed nd and Abbe numbers are retained. The model uses the d-line convention, approximately 587.6 nm, to match the table heading. Element 2 matches BAFD7 (Hoya) within the printed precision: Δn = +0.00004 and Δν = −0.05, catalog minus printed at the d line. This match does not identify the historical supplier or melt.

Elements 1 and 5 use S-BSM16 (Ohara) as a spectral proxy, with Δn = +0.00041 and Δν = −0.01. Element 3 uses S-TIL1 (Ohara) as a spectral proxy, with Δn = −0.00056 and Δν = −0.02. These differences are catalog minus printed at the d line and lie within 0.002 in index and 0.25 in Abbe number. The present-day rows supply only the shape of the dispersion curve, so colour analysis follows real glass curves rather than Abbe-number estimates; they do not identify Bertele's historical glasses.

Elements 4, 6 and 7 remain unmatched because no catalog row lies within those tolerances. Their dispersion away from the reference line is estimated from the printed index and Abbe number. Element 4 has the lowest Abbe number and is the most dispersive glass in the prescription. The patent supplies no measured spectral curves or partial-dispersion data, so chromatic and polychromatic performance remain uncertain.

## Focus Mechanism

The model is focused at infinity. The patent gives no close-focus data or group motion, and no marketed minimum focusing distance is assigned.

## Aperture, Image Plane and Field Limits

The US patent gives no iris position or diameter. DE 700,699 C divides its 9.44 mm stop space into 7.50 and 1.94 mm. Applying that ratio to the US example's 9.54 mm gap gives the rounded split 7.58/1.96 mm: the stop is 7.58 mm behind R6 and 1.96 mm ahead of R7. This is an inference from the German member, rather than a US-patent dimension.

The paraxial f/2 rule gives a stop radius of 16.315 mm. Exact axial tracing accepts an entrance radius of 24.74 mm, corresponding to f/2.02 by EFL divided by entrance diameter. The iris limits this beam; the optical rims pass it.

The computed paraxial back focus is 54.63 mm. The image plane is within 0.00005 mm of paraxial focus, with no adjustment for minimum spot size. Total track is 122.76 mm and the first-to-last-surface length is 68.13 mm.

The 10.0° half-field is illustrative. The patent states neither a field angle nor a camera format. At this illustrative field, the real chief ray reaches an image height of 17.69 mm. Peripheral rays can be clipped by the inferred rims; this does not establish a measured transmission or a factory coverage specification.

## Model Scope and Design Heritage

The clear semi-diameters, from R1 to R10, are inferred as 30, 29.6, 24, 24, 24, 17.4, 17.3, 24, 24 and 24 mm. The section drawing supports nearly cylindrical triplets with chamfered inner ends. Its unequal axial scales make the drawing evidence for proportions rather than exact dimensions. The 24 mm outer rims follow those proportions; the R6/R7 curve ends remain above the f/2 axial ray heights of 16.61/16.39 mm. Straight edges join these curve ends to the outer rims; no flat lands or rim cuts are present. Wide-open geometric transmission is 99.4% at 8.3° and 98.3% at the illustrative 10.0° edge. The precise optical rim and chamfer dimensions remain uncertain.

The German and US examples share the invention but differ numerically; for example, the German table gives R1 = 67.54 mm instead of 68.41 mm. The seven-element Sonnar and Jupiter-9 family resemblance does not establish that this particular prescription was manufactured. Factory clear apertures, baffles, tolerances, measured MTF and finite focusing remain undocumented.
