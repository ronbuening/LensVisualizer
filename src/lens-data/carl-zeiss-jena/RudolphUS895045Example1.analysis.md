# Rudolph US895045, Example 1 (1908)

## Identity and source
Patent-only numerical reference, not a verified retail or factory prescription. Paul Rudolph assigned the patent to Carl Zeiss, Jena. Filed July 12, 1907; published August 4, 1908. The patent calls it an improvement of US444714. No commercial Protar identification is claimed.
Primary facsimile: https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/69/d0/a3/fa7ec817756a83/US895045.pdf , PDF page 1 drawing, page 2 Example 1. Both were visually inspected. Source nominal focal length 100 and f/12.5; four elements in two cemented doublets. Exact stated air split b1=b2=1.5 establishes the stop plane.

## Literal prescription
Radii: +17.5, +5.8, +18.6, -12.8, +18.6, -14.3 mm. Glass thicknesses: 2.9, 1.3, 1.1, 1.8 mm. Air gap: 3 mm, split equally at the stop.
D indices: 1.6489, 1.6031, 1.5154, 1.6112. Source also reports hydrogen G-prime indices 1.6744, 1.6239, 1.5275, 1.6260. Its relative dispersion is (nGprime-nD)/(nD-1), NOT conventional Abbe Vd. Model is explicitly D-line only and has no invented Vd or chromatic claims.
