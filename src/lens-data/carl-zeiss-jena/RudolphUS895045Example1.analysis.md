# Rudolph Unsymmetrical Double Objective f/12.5 — US 895,045 Example 1

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 895,045 A  
**Filed:** July 12, 1907 (Serial No. 383,411)  
**Granted:** August 4, 1908  
**Inventor:** Paul Rudolph  
**Assignee:** The firm of Carl Zeiss, Jena (Carl-Zeiss-Stiftung)  
**Title:** Photographic Objective  
**Example details:** Example 1 of two, the one shown in the single drawing; tabulated for a focal length of 100 at a relative aperture of 1:12.5

The patent describes itself as an improvement of "certain of the Zeiss double objectives" of Rudolph's earlier US 444,714, the 1890 Zeiss Anastigmat that was renamed Protar in 1900. Both designs are unsymmetrical double objectives with one cemented pair on each side of the diaphragm. The improvement is a single glass choice: the collective lens of the front pair is made of a glass with a refractive index of at least 1.54, where the earlier constructions used a very low index there.

The model transcribes Example 1 as printed. It is a patent design, not a measured production lens. The layout of four elements in two cemented groups and the 1:12.5 aperture are those of the Zeiss Anastigmat (Protar) Series IV, which period catalogues describe as a non-convertible four-element, two-group wide-angle lens of that aperture. That is a family resemblance only. No source was found that ties this 1907 prescription to a production series or a production date, so no product identity is claimed.

An Austrian family member, AT 35513 B ("Unsymmetrisches Doppelobjektiv", Carl Zeiss), carries the same two examples.

## Optical Architecture

The objective has four elements in two cemented groups, with the diaphragm midway in a 3.0 mm air space between them (the patent prints b1 = b2 = 1.5).

- **Front group (L1 + L2):** a thick negative meniscus cemented to a positive meniscus. The cemented surface is dispersive and concave toward the diaphragm. The pair has almost no power: its focal length is about 5,900 mm.
- **Rear group (L3 + L4):** a biconcave lens cemented to a biconvex lens. The cemented surface is collective and convex toward the diaphragm. This pair carries nearly all the power, with a focal length of 93.5 mm.

The complete system computes to a focal length of 101.11 mm with a back focus of 98.28 mm. The glass is only 10.1 mm long from first to last vertex, so the lens is very compact for its focal length.

This is the "old achromat plus new achromat" principle of the 1890 Anastigmat: one pair with a dispersive cemented surface, one with a collective cemented surface, so that astigmatism and field curvature can be corrected together.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The patent prints the D-line index and the G′ (hydrogen Hγ) index of each glass. It prints no Abbe numbers, so none are given here.

### L1 — Negative Meniscus, convex to object

nD = 1.6489, nG′ = 1.6744. Glass: unmatched dense flint type. f = −14.81 mm.

The front dispersive lens. It has the highest relative dispersion in the system (0.0393 in the patent's measure) and is 2.9 mm thick, the thickest element. Its steep rear surface (r2 = +5.8) is the dispersive cemented surface.

### L2 — Positive Meniscus, convex to object

nD = 1.6031, nG′ = 1.6239. Glass: unmatched. f = +13.46 mm.

The collective lens of the front pair and the subject of the patent. Its index of 1.6031 is well above the 1.54 minimum that the claims set. The index step across the cemented surface is only 0.046, much smaller than in the earlier constructions. The patent explains that the height of this index, not the size of the step, is what matters for correction.

### L3 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.5154, nG′ = 1.5275. Glass: unmatched crown type. f = −14.54 mm.

The dispersive lens of the rear pair, in a low-index, low-dispersion glass. In Example 1 it has slightly lower relative dispersion (0.0235) than its collective partner (0.0242). The patent points this out: only the front pair has the usual arrangement of a more dispersive negative lens.

### L4 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.6112, nG′ = 1.6260. Glass: unmatched dense barium crown type. f = +13.51 mm.

The collective lens of the rear pair. Its index is 0.096 higher than that of L3, which makes the cemented surface (r5 = +18.6) collective.

## Glass Identification and Selection

| Element | nD | nG′ | (nG′ − nD) / (nD − 1) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | 1.6489 | 1.6744 | 0.0393 |
| L2 | 1.6031 | 1.6239 | 0.0345 |
| L3 | 1.5154 | 1.5275 | 0.0235 |
| L4 | 1.6112 | 1.6260 | 0.0242 |

The last column is the patent's own measure of relative dispersion. It is not the reciprocal of the Abbe number, and it cannot be converted to one without a catalogue of the period. All four printed ratios agree with the printed indices.

No glass has been matched to a catalogue type. The model therefore uses the D-line indices only and shows no colour error. Tracing the same radii with the G′ indices gives a focal length of 100.24 mm, 0.87 mm shorter than at the D line.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent gives no focusing data. A lens of this kind was focused by moving the whole objective. The model is set to infinity focus only.

## Patent Conditions and Model Verification

**What is printed.** All six radii with their signs, the four glass thicknesses, the two air spaces on either side of the diaphragm, the D and G′ indices, the focal length of 100 and the aperture of 1:12.5. Every one of these is used unchanged.

**What is inferred.** The stop diameter (3.68 mm semi-diameter, from 1:12.5), the clear semi-diameters of the glass (4.0 to 4.2 mm, estimates), the back focus (the computed paraxial value) and the 5° half-field used for tracing. The patent states no field angle. A trace of the chief ray shows it still passes the estimated rims at about 40° off axis, so the 5° limit is a cautious setting and not a statement of coverage.

**Focal length.** The printed table computes to 101.11 mm, 1.11 % above the stated 100. Example 2 of the same patent computes to 100.00 mm, so the table conventions are sound. A search for a single misprinted value in Example 1 was not conclusive. Changing r2 from 5.8 to 5.9 or d3 from 1.1 to 1.2 would restore 100, but the page prints 5.8 and 1.1 clearly and the Austrian family member reads the same. The radii are given to one decimal, and a change of only 0.05 in r1 moves the focal length by about 1 %. The printed values are therefore kept and the difference is put down to rounding.

**Claim condition.** Claim 1 requires the collective lens of the pair with the dispersive cemented surface to have an index of at least 1.54. L2 has 1.6031. Satisfied.
