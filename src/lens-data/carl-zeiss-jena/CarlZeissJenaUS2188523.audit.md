# CARL ZEISS JENA 100mm f/2 (US 2,188,523 Design) — Audit

## Source

US 2,188,523 A, _Photographic Objective_, names Ludwig Bertele and Zeiss Ikon AG. The application was filed September 14, 1938, with German priority January 8, 1938; publication was January 30, 1940. The section is on PDF page 1 and the sole numerical example is on PDF page 2.

## Transcription

Two independent readings of the page images agree on all ten signed radii, nine axial distances, seven indices and seven Abbe numbers. All 33 numerical values match the prescription. Both readings identify the lowercase nd heading; the original sodium-D interpretation was incorrect. Radius signs are printed explicitly. R4/R5 and R8/R9 are shared cemented interfaces, giving the 1 | 3 | 3 arrangement.

## First-order Properties

The printed 100 mm f/2 example computes to EFL 100.047854508 mm and paraxial back focus 54.625853960 mm. The 0.04785% focal-length difference is consistent with the printed precision. The original iris radius was 16.320631399 mm, with a real accepted entrance radius of 24.741468384 mm, equivalent to f/2.021865739. Earlier independent vector refraction and application traces agreed; their results do not establish measured factory performance.

The original inferred rims passed surface-clearance checks. The minimum sampled glass edge thickness was 1.2763 mm; the R6–R7 shared-edge clearance was 1.1332 mm. Off-axis peripheral clipping was present, while the chief rays in the chosen field completed. These observations support retaining the rims pending a separate comparison with the figure.

## Values Not Printed

The US patent prints no stop position, stop diameter, clear semi-diameters, back focus, field angle, image height or camera format. The original stop split was 7.5366/2.0034 mm inside the printed 9.54 mm gap. The illustrative 10° half-field, aperture series through f/16 and infinity-only focus setting are modeling choices. No finite focusing law is supplied.

DE 700,699 C is the German member of the same invention, with the same inventor, applicant and claim but a different numerical example. Its printed stop-space split, 7.50/1.94 mm in 9.44 mm, supports an inferred split of 7.58/1.96 mm in this US example. Only the ratio is transferred; no German prescription values replace the US table.

## Product Evidence

Marco Kröger's [Sonnar history, §5.2](https://www.zeissikonveb.de/start/objektive/normalobjektive/sonnar.html) identifies DRP 700.699 as a distortion improvement to the Sonnar 2/5 cm that did not enter series production. This supports the Sonnar family connection, but no production lens is documented for the US numerical example. Its seven-element arrangement alone does not identify a Jupiter-9.

## Corrections

The source table requires no numerical correction and no source erratum. The spectral-line correction concerns the model's interpretation of the printed heading. The stop remains inferred from the German member; the original US air gap is preserved. The last image distance remains the paraxial back focus, rounded only for readability.

## Remaining Questions

Open: the figure depicts triplets about 23.7 mm in semi-height, with inner chamfers near 16 mm. The model instead tapers from R3 25.4 to R6 17.4 mm and from R7 17.3 to R10 21.1 mm. Review optical rims and chamfers separately; no rim is changed here.

Open: the historical glass suppliers and spectral curves, US-example iris dimensions, factory field, baffles, finite focusing, tolerances and measured performance remain unknown. The German stop ratio is supporting evidence for an inference, not a dimension printed in the US patent.
