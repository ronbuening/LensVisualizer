# CARL ZEISS JENA 100mm f/6.3 (US 886,416 Design) — US 886,416 Example 2

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 886,416 A, *Photographic Objective*  
**Application Number:** Serial No. 383,412  
**Filed:** July 12, 1907  
**Granted:** May 5, 1908  
**Inventors:** Paul Rudolph  
**Assignee:** Carl-Zeiss-Stiftung (the patent's wording is "the firm of Carl Zeiss, of Jena")  
**Example details:** Example 2, Fig. 2; table on specification page 1, tabulated for a focal length of 100

The CARL ZEISS JENA 100mm f/6.3 (US 886,416 Design) is the second of the patent's two numerical examples. Example 1 (Fig. 1) is a single cemented triplet of relative aperture 1:11 with the diaphragm in front. Example 2 (Fig. 2) places two equal triplets symmetrically in front of and behind the diaphragm to make a double objective of relative aperture 1:6.3, which the patent describes as spherically, astigmatically and chromatically corrected. Example 2 is the one modelled here.

The patent names no product and none is documented for this design. Collectors have linked the patent to the Zeiss Doppel-Amatar 1:6.8, a symmetric lens of two cemented triplets from about 1910, but that suggestion comes from a forum discussion that could be read only as search-result summaries, the product is f/6.8 where the patent example is f/6.3, and the link is unproven. The design is therefore shown at the patent's own focal length of 100.

## Optical Architecture

The objective is a symmetric double anastigmat: two identical cemented triplets facing each other across a central diaphragm, six spherical elements in two groups, with no aspherical surface. Each triplet, read from the diaphragm outward, is a biconcave negative element (I), a biconvex positive element (II) and a negative meniscus (III). The positive element is enclosed between the two negative ones.

The patent starts from a known form, which it cites in US 528,155 (Fig. 2) and British patent 4692 of 1893 (Fig. 1): a cemented triplet whose collective cemented surface is near the diaphragm and convex toward it, and whose dispersive cemented surface is farther away and concave toward it. Rudolph's change is in the choice of glass. The index step at the collective cemented surface is made at least twice the step at the dispersive one. In Example 2 the steps are 0.1206 (between glasses I and II) and 0.0408 (between glasses II and III), a ratio of about 3.0, so the claimed condition is met with margin. The patent says this gives better correction of spherical aberration or of astigmatism, and that halves like those of Example 2, which are not corrected for the sine condition, must be combined into a double objective to give a satisfactory image.

Computed from the printed table, each triplet has a focal length of 173.58 mm and the whole objective 99.87 mm. The diaphragm sits 2.7 mm from the inner vertex of each half, as printed. The length from the first to the last vertex is 19.8 mm and the computed back focus is 91.18 mm, so the lens is short and compact for its focal length.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Focal lengths are for each element alone in air. The rear half repeats the front half in reverse order.

### L1 and L6 — outer negative meniscus (patent glass III)

`nD = 1.6240`, `f = −34.93 mm`. Radii 22.6 and 10.7 mm, centre thickness 2.1 mm. The convex side faces outward. Its cemented face is the dispersive surface of the triplet, concave toward the diaphragm.

### L2 and L5 — biconvex positive element (patent glass II)

`nD = 1.5832`, `f = +15.71 mm`. Radii 10.7 and 54.8 mm, centre thickness 4.1 mm. This is the only positive element of each half and carries all of its converging power. With the inferred 7.8 mm clear radius its edge is about 0.17 mm thick, which agrees with the near knife-edge drawn in Fig. 2.

### L3 and L4 — biconcave negative element (patent glass I)

`nD = 1.4626`, `f = −31.89 mm`. Radii 54.8 and 20.3 mm, centre thickness 1.0 mm. This low-index element faces the diaphragm. Its cemented face is the collective surface, convex toward the diaphragm, where the large index step that the patent claims is applied.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent identifies each glass by two refractive indices: nD for the solar D line and nG′ for the hydrogen Hγ line.

| Patent glass | Elements | nD | nG′ |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| I | L3, L4 | 1.4626 | 1.4714 |
| II | L2, L5 | 1.5832 | 1.5972 |
| III | L1, L6 | 1.6240 | 1.6448 |

No Abbe number and no glass type or maker is printed. The data file stores the printed nD values with the sodium D reference and gives no dispersion, so the model is monochromatic: it shows the geometry and the D-line ray paths but not the colour correction that the patent claims. No modern catalogue glass matches any of the three and no period glass was identified, so each element is labelled as unmatched with its two printed indices.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent gives no focusing data, object distance or close-focus state. A lens of this kind was focused by moving the whole objective, but the model holds only the infinity state and no spacing moves.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The model uses the printed radii, thicknesses, diaphragm distance and indices, each twice because the objective is symmetric. The table prints radii without signs; the signs in the data file follow Fig. 2 and the wording of the claims.

| Quantity | Patent | Model |
|---|---:|---:|
| Focal length | 100 | 99.87 mm |
| Relative aperture | 1:6.3 | f/6.3 |
| Back focus | not printed | 91.18 mm (computed, paraxial) |
| Vertex length | 19.8 (sum of printed values) | 19.8 mm |

The focal length differs from the stated 100 by 0.13 %, which is within the rounding of a table printed to one decimal place.

Three things in the model are not printed in the patent. The clear radii, 8.5 mm on the outer faces and 7.8 mm inside, are estimates that pass the full f/6.3 axial beam; Fig. 2 scales to roughly 7.4 mm. The image plane is the computed paraxial focus for an object at infinity. The 5° trace field is a working value only, because the patent states no field angle or image size.
