# CARL ZEISS JENA TESSAR 100mm f/3.5 (US 1,741,947 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 1,741,947 A, “Photographic lens, corrected spherically, chromatically, astigmatically, and for coma”](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/1741947), by Willy Merté, was filed on 11 June 1927, with German priority of 26 June 1926, and published on 31 December 1929. It names the assignee as the firm Carl Zeiss, Jena; the catalog records Carl-Zeiss-Stiftung. The sole numerical example appears in the drawing on PDF page 1 and in the typed prescription on page 2. It specifies focal length 100 units, f/3.5 and a full field of 55.0°. The model takes those units as millimetres.

H. H. Nasse's [“Tessar,” Carl Zeiss Camera Lens Division, March 2011](https://lenspire.zeiss.com/photo/app/uploads/2018/02/cln39_en_web_special_tessar.pdf), pp. 2–4 and 7, documents the architecture and historical production lenses. Merté and Ernst Wandersleb's later [US 1,849,681 A, “Photographic three-lens objective”](https://patents.google.com/patent/US1849681A/en), filed on 10 July 1931, with German priority of 17 July 1930, and published on 15 March 1932, contains different numerical examples.

## Transcription Check

All seven refracting boundaries, their radius signs, seven printed axial thicknesses and separations, four D-line indices and four typed Abbe values agree with the page images. R2 and R5 are optical flats. The diaphragm lies 4.42 mm behind R4 and 2.29 mm ahead of R5; R6 is the cemented interface. Every finite radius sign is printed. No transcription mismatch was found. There are no aspheres or variable gaps.

Element III's Abbe number is 51 in the drawing and 51.4 in the typed table. The model retains the more precise typed value. The drawing agrees with the four-element, three-group sequence I | II | III+IV and the listed element shapes.

## First-Order Check

The D-line paraxial EFL is 100.05 mm, 0.05 mm above the printed 100 mm. The paraxial aperture is f/3.50, matching the printed f/3.5. Calculated BFD is 85.284 mm; the stored image plane agrees with paraxial infinity focus to better than 0.001 mm. The first-to-last vertex track is 29.99 mm, and the first-vertex-to-image distance is 115.274 mm. The patent prints no back focus.

The stop radius is 11.900 mm. Exact axial tracing gives a real-ray entrance equivalent of f/3.55; a nominal f/3.50 marginal ray requires a stop radius of 12.072 mm. The iris sets the aperture, with no rim limiting the axial opening. The surfaces stay clear of each other.

The 27.5° chief ray reaches an image height of 51.64 mm without blockage, giving approximately 103.28 mm image-circle diameter. Chief rays clear the rims through the stated field, but peripheral pupil rays are vignetted. This does not establish uniform illumination or a fully unvignetted image circle.

The stated curvature conditions hold: the outer absolute radii sum to 79.30 mm against a 75 mm minimum; the middle element's absolute radii sum to 99.98 mm against 90 mm; and the cemented radius of 28.50 mm is below five sixths of the rear radius magnitude, 39.35 mm.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The diaphragm position is printed, but its diameter is not. The stop radius follows the paraxial f/3.50 aperture. The image plane is calculated, and the approximately 103.28 mm image circle follows the printed 55.0° field and the chief-ray image height. It is not a published film format.

All clear semi-diameters are estimates: R1 16.2, R2 15.8, R3 16.0, R4/R5 15.2, R6 15.7 and R7 16.5 mm. The drawing suggests outer clear radii of about 13.7 mm and clear faces near R4/R5 of about 11.7 mm before the chamfers. The larger model rims leave an approximately 0.24 mm edge on element I and a pointed edge on IV, whose rear rim extends beyond the cemented interface. Conversely, the drawn front rim is about 4.0% smaller than the nominal f/3.5 axial beam's 14.29 mm radius. These conflicting rim constraints remain unresolved.

`focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. No close-focus distances, movement law or production focusing mechanism are given. The selectable aperture range extends from f/3.5 to f/22; the patent gives no minimum iris opening.

Historical glass suppliers, melt identities, additional spectral indices and partial-dispersion data are unprinted. K-SK7 (Sumita) and FL57 (Hoya) supply catalog dispersion curves for I and II without establishing their historical identities. III and IV use Abbe-based estimates. Calculated colour correction and simulated MTF are not measured performance of a production lens; the patent supplies no MTF curve.

## Product Identification

The positive front singlet, biconcave middle singlet and rear cemented pair match the Tessar architecture described by ZEISS. No cited source links this numerical prescription to a specific production Tessar. The Tessar name identifies the design family. US 1,849,681 A is a later design with different prescriptions.

No production mount, image format, diaphragm blade count or shape, marketed focal length, marketed f-number or marketed minimum focus distance is assigned. No special-dispersion designation is established by the source.

## Corrections, 2026-11-21

- The inventor-based identity became the maker-and-patent identity shown above. The maker changed from unspecified to Carl Zeiss Jena, and the lens became publicly listed. The patent number, 1929 publication year, Willy Merté attribution, sole example and Carl-Zeiss-Stiftung assignee entry were retained.
- The subtitle is “US 1,741,947 A, sole example — Zeiss / Merté.” The specifications are “4 ELEMENTS / 3 GROUPS”, “ALL SPHERICAL”, “DESIGN f = 100 mm” and “DESIGN F/3.5”, distinguishing the printed scale and aperture from any production specifications.
- The catalog design focal length changed from 100 to 100.05 mm to describe the calculated EFL. The four-element/three-group counts and f/3.5 design aperture agree with the patent.
- Element labels became Element 1 through Element 4, with the drawing's numerals I–IV. Optical roles and standalone focal lengths of +52.84, −35.16, −54.13 and +29.70 mm were added. C1 identifies III and IV as the rear cemented doublet spanning R5–R7; its net focal length is +60.97 mm. The shape descriptions were retained.
- Element I's glass label changed from unmatched to K-SK7 (Sumita). At sodium D, catalog minus printed differences are ΔnD = +0.0000271 and ΔνD = −0.04. Element II changed from unmatched to FL57 (Hoya), with ΔnD = +0.0000812 and ΔνD = +0.08. These are close optical-property matches, without establishing historical suppliers; the printed indices and Abbe numbers were retained.
- Elements III and IV received explicit unmatched labels carrying their printed pairs, nD 1.52648 / ν 51.4 and nD 1.62377 / ν 56.9. No catalog curve meets the same matching limits for either. Their coordinates changed by Δn = 0 and Δν = 0.
- The front clear semi-diameter decreased from 19 to 16.2 mm to reduce excess overhang while preserving the accepted rays. The other estimated rims were retained. No printed radius, thickness, separation or glass coordinate was changed to alter image quality.
- The focus description states infinity focus only because the patent gives no close-focus data. No production minimum focus distance is claimed.

The resulting model has EFL 100.05 mm, paraxial f/3.50, BFD 85.284 mm, stop radius 11.900 mm and half-field 27.5°. The printed diaphragm station and field are retained. The calculated image plane is approximately 85.284 mm behind R7, and the inferred image-circle diameter is approximately 103.28 mm.

## Remaining Questions

- The clear rims remain larger than the drawing, particularly the thin front-element edge and pointed rear-element edge. The drawing's smaller front rim conflicts with the required f/3.5 axial beam, and the R4/R5 chamfers have no numerical dimensions.
- The historical identities of all four glasses, matching catalog curves for III and IV, and measured spectral performance remain unknown.
- No source establishes a specific production lens, mount, image format, diaphragm blade count or shape, minimum iris opening, marketed specifications or close-focus mechanism.
