# CARL ZEISS JENA TESSAR 100mm f/3.5 (US 1,741,947 Design)

## Patent and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 1,741,947 A

**Inventors:** Willy Merté

**Assignee:** Carl-Zeiss-Stiftung (the patent names the firm Carl Zeiss, Jena)

**Published:** 31 December 1929

**Filed:** 11 June 1927; German priority 26 June 1926

**Example details:** Sole numerical example; drawing on PDF page 1 and typed prescription on PDF page 2.

The patent describes a photographic and projection objective with four elements in three groups, a nominal focal length of 100, an aperture of f/3.5 and a full field of 55.0°. The model uses millimetres. Its D-line effective focal length is 100.05 mm, 0.05 mm above the printed value; the calculated paraxial back focal distance is 85.284 mm. The patent gives no back focal distance. [Original patent](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/1741947).

The layout is Tessar-type: a positive front singlet, a negative middle singlet and a rear cemented pair. ZEISS documents this architecture and production Tessars at f/3.5, but no source ties this particular prescription to a production Tessar. The later Merté–Wandersleb patent US 1,849,681 A, with German priority in 1930, contains different prescriptions. The Tessar name here identifies the design family. [ZEISS Tessar history](https://lenspire.zeiss.com/photo/app/uploads/2018/02/cln39_en_web_special_tessar.pdf), [later patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US1849681A/en).

## Optical Architecture

Elements I and II are air-spaced singlets. Elements III and IV form the rear cemented doublet C1, with a shared interface at R6. R2 and R5 are optical flats; the remaining refracting surfaces are spherical. There are seven refracting boundaries and a separate diaphragm between II and III.

The printed diaphragm station is 4.42 mm behind R4 and 2.29 mm ahead of R5. Its axial coordinate is 18.20 mm from the first vertex. The first-to-last vertex track is 29.99 mm, and the image plane lies 115.274 mm behind the first vertex.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### I / Element 1 — Plano-Convex Positive

nD = 1.60717, νD = 59.5. Glass: K-SK7 (Sumita), a catalogue match to the printed coordinates; historical supplier unknown. Standalone focal length in air: +52.84 mm.

The +32.08 mm front surface and flat rear surface provide the front group's positive power. Both estimated clear semi-diameters are 15.8 mm, giving a square-cut rim.

### II / Element 2 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.57596, νD = 41.3. Glass: FL57 (Hoya), a catalogue match to the printed coordinates; historical supplier unknown. Standalone focal length in air: −35.16 mm.

The −71.13 and +28.85 mm surfaces supply negative power between the converging front and rear groups. The centre thickness is 3.73 mm; both estimated clear semi-diameters are 13.7 mm.

### III / Element 3 — Plano-Concave Negative

nD = 1.52648, νD = 51.4. Glass: Unmatched. Standalone focal length in air: −54.13 mm.

The flat front surface is 2.29 mm behind the diaphragm. This 1.50 mm thick negative element is cemented to IV at R6, whose radius is +28.50 mm. The typed table prints ν = 51.4, while the drawing prints 51; the model retains the typed value. Estimated clear semi-diameters are 13.7 and 15.5 mm.

### IV / Element 4 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.62377, νD = 56.9. Glass: Unmatched. Standalone focal length in air: +29.70 mm.

The shared +28.50 mm cemented interface and −47.22 mm rear surface provide positive power. Its centre thickness is 8.00 mm and both clear semi-diameters are estimated at 15.5 mm, giving a square-cut rear rim. The cemented III–IV group has a net positive focal length of +60.97 mm.

## Glass Identification and Spectral Scope

The patent prints nD and Abbe numbers. The model retains those coordinates and uses the D line (taken as sodium, 589.3 nm). Catalogue comparisons at that line give the following differences, calculated as catalogue minus patent:

| Element | Catalogue disposition           |        ΔnD |     ΔνD |
| ------- | ------------------------------- | ---------: | ------: |
| I       | K-SK7 (Sumita)                  | +0.0000271 |   −0.04 |
| II      | FL57 (Hoya)                     | +0.0000812 |   +0.08 |
| III     | Unmatched (nD 1.52648 / ν 51.4) |          — |       — |
| IV      | Unmatched (nD 1.62377 / ν 56.9) |          — |       — |

The first two labels supply catalogue dispersion curves without establishing the identity of the historical melts. No catalogue row meets the same matching limits for III or IV; their spectral behaviour is estimated from the printed Abbe numbers. The patent gives no additional spectral indices or partial-dispersion data, so calculated colour correction is not a measured property of a production lens.

## Focus Mechanism

The model is focused at infinity. The patent gives no close-focus distances, focus-state table or movement law. All elements remain fixed, and the image plane is the calculated paraxial infinity plane, 85.284 mm behind R7. No production minimum focus distance is assigned.

## Aperture and Field Coverage

The patent specifies the diaphragm's position but not its diameter. The paraxial f/3.50 aperture gives a stop radius of 11.900 mm. Exact axial tracing gives a real-ray entrance equivalent of f/3.55 at that opening; a nominal f/3.50 marginal ray would require a stop radius of 12.072 mm.

The patent's stated full field of 55.0° gives a 27.5° half-field. Its real chief ray reaches an image height of 51.64 mm at the paraxial image plane, giving an approximately 103.28 mm image circle. This diameter is derived from the stated field, rather than a published film format. Chief rays clear the estimated rims through 27.5°, while peripheral pupil rays are vignetted; chief-ray coverage does not imply full-pupil illumination.

Every clear semi-diameter is inferred from the sole drawing on PDF page 1 and checked against the nominal f/3.5 axial beam. The drawing supports roughly 13.7 mm outer rims, with undimensioned chamfers near R4 and R5. R3–R5 use 13.7 mm; R1/R2 use 15.8 mm and R6/R7 use 15.5 mm to preserve peripheral illumination. The front singlet and rear positive element have square-cut rims. The drawn front rim is smaller than the nominal axial beam's 14.29 mm radius. Wide-open geometric pupil transmission is approximately 91.0% at 16.5°, 80.7% at 22.0° and 64.7% at 27.5°; these figures exclude absorption and obliquity falloff.

## Patent Conditions and Model Limits

The numerical example satisfies the reported curvature conditions:

| Condition                                                                       | Values at the model dimensions   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Sum of the outer absolute radii at least three quarters of focal length         | 32.08 + 47.22 = 79.30 mm ≥ 75 mm |
| Sum of the middle element's absolute radii at least nine tenths of focal length | 71.13 + 28.85 = 99.98 mm ≥ 90 mm |
| Cemented radius no greater than five sixths of the rear radius magnitude        | 28.50 mm ≤ 39.35 mm              |

The surfaces stay clear of each other. Image-quality calculations depend on the inferred rims, unprinted iris diameter and calculated image plane. The patent supplies no MTF curve for this prescription; simulated MTF is not a measurement of a historical lens.
