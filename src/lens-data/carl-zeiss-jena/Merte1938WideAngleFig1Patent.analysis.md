# Merté / Zeiss US2126126 Figure1, 1938

Primary patent https://patents.google.com/patent/US2126126A/en ; original PDF https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/a4/b6/10/af9cd55c13c1d1/US2126126.pdf . PDF pages3-4 were rendered and visually read. THIS model is only Fig1/Table1; Fig2 is neither built nor counted.

Four glass elements in three groups: plano-negative front, positive middle meniscus and negative rear cemented doublet. Literal signed radii, thicknesses, airspaces and explicit n_d/V retained. Nominal100mm f8 patent example. Native and independent paraxial EFL100.11860590080387mm (+0.1186059% versus source100); calculated paraxial BFD157.8809275731288mm. No source-value adjustment, scale fitting or glass substitution to erase the residual. Last distance is derived BFD, not factory value or optimized polychromatic focus.

The physical iris is explicitly located110.28mm after the first element's back vertex and2.36mm before the second element's front. Those are SOURCE distances, not estimates. Glass rim semi-diameters30mm front and10mm remaining groups ARE estimates. Initial12mm rear rims failed native gap-sag validation; reducing only those heuristic rims to10mm passed without overriding geometry tolerance. These rims do not establish the patent's full field. The image plane and infinity-only reciprocal convention are computational modeling choices, not measurements from a production lens.

Sphaerogon design-family association is plausible from historical illustrations, but no exact product prescription match was proved. Do not identify this normalized100mm model as a19mm retail Sphaerogon. Later8-element f6.8 V1936 No18 is specifically NOT this4-element f8 formula. Maker is documented design assignee Carl Zeiss Jena, not claimed factory identity. Hidden patent reference.
