# DE 630,224 C — Audit

## Source

The DPMA facsimile identifies Zeiss Ikon Akt.-Ges. in Dresden and _Photographisches Objektiv_, patented from 11 February 1934, grant announced 30 April 1936, published 23 May 1936. No inventor is printed. The sole numerical example states F = 100 and 1:2.

## Transcription

Two independent readings of the page images agree on all eleven radii, seven glass thicknesses, three air separations, seven refractive indices and seven Abbe numbers. No aspheres or variable gaps are printed. The scanned table has its printed plus signs at R7 and R11 struck through and minus signs written in; −298.6 and −297.0 mm follow those corrections and agree with the drawing. L4's ν is clearly 31.0. The earlier uncertainty about these readings is resolved.

The drawing shows seven elements in four groups: 1 | 2+3+4 | 5+6 | 7. Cemented surfaces are R4, R5 and R8.

## First-order

The unchanged prescription computes to EFL 99.860188267 mm against the printed F = 100, a −0.139812% difference. The computed paraxial BFD is 41.319610882 mm, the first-to-last vertex distance is 72.5 mm and the first-vertex-to-image distance is 113.819610882 mm. The nominal f-number is 2.000 and the inferred paraxial iris radius is 15.738134444 mm. Both focus endpoints retain the same optical state.

The R6 clear radius limits the largest passing axial entrance height to about 24.123835 mm, or geometric f/2.069741. Measured against the beam the paraxial iris admits, the R6 limit is f/2.06 (+3.1%). These two aperture figures use different reference beams.

## Not printed

The source supplies no stop station, iris opening, clear semi-diameters, back focus, field angle, film format, close-focus distance or focus movement. The model's stop splits the published 7.2 mm air space into 6.33 + 0.87 mm; the image plane is paraxial. The ±6° field is illustrative. Clear radii combine estimates with measurements from the drawing, so no single semi-diameter source category is assigned.

The patent prints n_d without defining the wavelength. Lowercase d follows its notation, while the intended wavelength remains uncertain. Catalogue comparisons cannot resolve that uncertainty; the numerical n/ν values are retained.

## Product

The 1 + 3 + 2 + 1 construction is Sonnar-related: it resembles the Contax Sonnar 5 cm f/2 with a positive rear singlet added. No documented product uses this prescription. The name therefore identifies the patent design rather than a production Sonnar.

## Corrections

The R7/R11 negative signs and L4 ν = 31.0 already followed the page images and remain unchanged. The stop station, computed image plane, radii, spacings and printed glass constants are retained. R6 remains at 15.3 mm because the drawing's clear zone ends at approximately 15.0 mm before a chamfer; enlarging it to pass the nominal f/2 marginal would go beyond that evidence.

## Remaining questions

Open: complete the comparison of the remaining estimated clear rims with the drawing, especially R3–R5 and R8–R9. The spectral line, historical glass identities, exact iris position and any product connection remain unresolved. The source does not establish performance over a specified field.
