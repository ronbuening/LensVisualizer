# CARL ZEISS JENA 100mm f/2 (DE 630,224 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[DE 630,224 C, _Photographisches Objektiv_](https://depatisnet.dpma.de/DepatisNet/depatisnet?window=1&space=menu&content=treffer&action=pdf&docid=DE000000630224A), assigned to Zeiss Ikon AG, printed as “Zeiss Ikon Akt.-Ges. in Dresden”. No individual inventor is printed. Protection dates from 11 February 1934; the grant was announced on 30 April 1936 and the Patentschrift issued on 23 May 1936. No separate priority or filing date is identified on these pages. The sole numerical example is on specification p. 1 (PDF p. 2); the construction drawing is PDF p. 3. It prints F = 100 and 1:2 without a length unit; the model uses a 100 mm scale.

[US 1,998,704 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US1998704A/en), Figure 1, supplies a comparison with the six-element, three-group Sonnar arrangement. [Sumita optical-glass data](https://www.sumita-opt.co.jp/en/download/), K-BK7, and [Ohara S-TIM28 data](https://www.ohara-gmbh.com/fileadmin/user_upload/export-data/pdf/product_datasheets/S-TIM28_English_.pdf) supply modern dispersion comparisons. No source documents a production lens using this exact prescription.

## Transcription Check

All eleven signed radii, seven glass thicknesses, three air separations and seven index/Abbe pairs agree with the patent page images. No numerical discrepancy remains. The printed plus signs at R7 and R11 are struck through and minus signs written in; −298.6 and −297.0 follow those corrections and agree with the drawing. Element 4's Abbe number is 31.0. There are no aspherical surfaces or variable gaps.

The drawing shows seven elements in four air-spaced components: 1 | 2+3+4 | 5+6 | 7. The cemented interfaces are R4, R5 and R8. The published air spaces are 0.6, 7.2 and 15.6 mm at the chosen scale. The middle space contains the inferred stop and retains its total length.

## First-Order Check

The calculated EFL is 99.86 mm against the nominal 100 mm scale, a −0.1% difference. The nominal f-number is 2.00, matching the printed 1:2, with a paraxial stop radius of 15.738 mm. Calculated BFD is 41.32 mm; the patent prints none. The first-to-last vertex distance is 72.50 mm, and the paraxial image plane is 113.82 mm behind the first vertex. Both focus endpoints describe the same infinity state.

R6 limits the largest passing axial entrance radius to about 24.124 mm, giving a geometric entrance aperture of f/2.07. Measured against the beam admitted by the paraxial iris, the same rim gives f/2.06, 3.1% above nominal. These figures use different reference beams. The surfaces stay clear of each other, and the iris lies in air.

At the illustrative 6.0° half-field, the chief ray clears every rim and reaches 10.51 mm image height. The 23.0° chief ray also clears every rim and reaches about 42.10 mm. Neither result establishes full-bundle illumination or adequate correction at that angle; some marginal rays are clipped within the illustrative field.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The patent supplies no stop station, iris opening, clear radii, back focus, field angle, film format, close-focus distance or focus movement. The inferred stop is 6.33 mm after R6 and 0.87 mm before R7, 31.63 mm from the first vertex. Its station clears the concave rear face of the triplet; the midpoint of the gap would intersect that element. Its radius follows paraxial f/2, and the image plane follows paraxial infinity focus.

The clear radii combine estimates with drawing measurements. R7 is 17.8 mm and both rear-singlet radii are 25 mm from the drawing. R6 retains 15.3 mm: the drawn clear zone ends at approximately 15.0 mm before a chamfer, whereas the nominal f/2 marginal ray reaches 15.774 mm. The other rims are estimates. No single description of rim origins applies to every surface.

The ±6.0° field is illustrative. Its 10.51 mm chief-ray height does not establish a source-backed image circle, so no image-circle diameter or format is assigned. The selectable f-stops extend from f/2 to f/16; the patent gives no minimum iris opening or blade geometry. `focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. No finite-focus movement is supplied.

The table prints n_d and ν without defining a wavelength. Lowercase d follows the printed notation; catalogue comparisons do not settle the spectral line. Catalogue curves approximate dispersion for elements 3 and 4; the remaining elements use Abbe-based estimates. The patent supplies no individual C-, F- or g-line indices or partial-dispersion data, and establishes no measured MTF, coatings, transmission or apochromatic performance. Modern glass names do not establish historical suppliers or melts.

## Product Identification

The 1 + 3 + 2 + 1 construction resembles the Contax Sonnar 5 cm f/2's 1 + 3 + 2 arrangement with a positive rear singlet added. This is a structural relationship; no product is documented for the seven-element prescription. The title identifies the patent design at its chosen scale, listed under Carl Zeiss Jena, while Zeiss Ikon AG remains the patent assignee. No marketed focal length, aperture, minimum focus distance, mount, image format or diaphragm blade count is assigned.

## Corrections, 2026-11-21

- The design received the maker-and-patent identity “CARL ZEISS JENA 100mm f/2 (DE 630,224 Design)” under Carl Zeiss Jena and became publicly listed. The name makes no production-lens claim.
- The patent designation changed from DE 630224 to DE 630,224 C, the assignee from Zeiss Ikon to Zeiss Ikon AG, and the sole example was explicitly identified. The empty inventor list and 1936 publication year follow the front page.
- The subtitle is “DE 630,224 C, sole example — Zeiss Ikon”. The specifications are “7 ELEMENTS / 4 GROUPS”, “ALL SPHERICAL”, “DESIGN f = 100 mm” and “DESIGN F/2”, identifying the construction and chosen scale.
- The design focal-length entry changed from 100 to 99.86 mm to state calculated EFL. The inferred 21.01 mm image-circle entry was removed because the field has no source. A single estimate-based description of rim origins was removed because some radii follow the drawing.
- L1–L7 received the labels Element 1–Element 7, shape descriptions and optical roles. Elements 1–3 and 6 are positive menisci, element 4 a negative meniscus, element 5 biconcave negative and element 7 biconvex positive. Their calculated individual focal lengths in air were added: +114.68, +84.13, +165.45, −33.48, −54.45, +49.44 and +124.15 mm.
- All seven index references changed from D to the printed lowercase d, with the undefined wavelength explicitly recorded. The numerical indices and Abbe numbers remain as printed; the notation does not resolve the historical spectral line.
- Element 3's unidentified glass label changed to K-BK7 (Sumita), listed at 1.51633/64.1, with catalogue-minus-printed differences Δn +0.000030 and Δν +0.10. Its dispersion curve gives ν about 64.062, or Δν +0.062. Element 4's changed to S-TIM28 (Ohara), about 1.68893/31.075, with Δn −0.000069 and Δν +0.075. These supply approximate dispersion curves. Elements 1, 2, 5, 6 and 7 retain unmatched labels stating their printed coordinates; no historical supplier, anomalous dispersion or extra spectral indices are inferred.
- C1 identifies the cemented triplet, elements 2–4, spanning R3–R6. C2 identifies the cemented doublet, elements 5–6, spanning R7–R9. Each component has one cemented annotation; the four component brackets identify the front singlet, triplet, doublet and rear singlet.
- The R7 clear radius changed from 15.3 to 17.8 mm, and R10/R11 from 17 to 25 mm, following the drawing. Geometric transmission at ±6.0° changes from 93.0% to 93.1%, with larger gains beyond 6.0°. R6 remains at 15.3 mm because enlarging it to pass nominal f/2 would extend beyond the drawn clear zone. The corrected R7/R11 signs, stop station, image plane, radii of curvature, thicknesses, air spaces and ±6.0° field are retained.

With these values, EFL is 99.86 mm, nominal f-number 2.00, BFD 41.32 mm, stop radius 15.738 mm and half-field 6.0°. The image plane remains at paraxial infinity focus, 113.82 mm behind the first vertex; the geometric axial entrance aperture remains about f/2.07.

## Remaining Questions

- The exact stop station and opening, intended spectral wavelength and historical glass suppliers and melts remain unknown. Elements 1, 2, 5, 6 and 7 have no matching catalogue curve.
- The clear radii beyond R6, R7 and the rear singlet remain estimates, especially R3–R5 and R8–R9. R6 limits axial acceptance at nominal f/2; the source gives no iris dimensions to explain the difference.
- No source establishes a useful field, image circle, production product, mount, image format, blade count or shape, minimum iris opening, marketed specifications or close-focus mechanism.
