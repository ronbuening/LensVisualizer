# CARL ZEISS JENA R-BIOTAR 100mm f/0.9 (US 1,967,836 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 1,967,836 A, _Photographic Lens_  
**Inventors:** Willy Merté  
**Assignee:** Carl-Zeiss-Stiftung  
**Published:** July 24, 1934  
**Filed:** December 29, 1933; German priority December 30, 1932  
**Example details:** Sole numerical example; drawing and prescription on PDF page 1, repeated table and claims on PDF page 2.

The patent describes a five-element, four-group photographic objective at f = 100 mm and f/0.90, covering an image field approximately 25 mm in diameter. It names the firm Carl Zeiss, Jena, Germany as assignee, given above as Carl-Zeiss-Stiftung. All printed radii, axial intervals, refractive indices and Abbe values are retained. [US 1,967,836 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US1967836A/en)

The R-Biotar family association rests on Edward K. Kaprelian's July 1949 account of Merté's f/0.85 X-ray cine objective. He describes a Petzval arrangement with an air space between the first two elements and an added collective element; Fig. 2a, labelled “MERTE f/0.85,” has the same five-element, four-group form. This supports a family connection. The production R-Biotar was a 45 mm f/0.85 lens, whereas this example is 100 mm f/0.90; the exact production prescription is not established. A surviving 4.5 cm f/0.85 lens is documented by Flints Auctions. [Kaprelian, _Objective Lenses of f/1 Aperture and Greater_](https://doi.org/10.5594/J17813), [Flints, lot 264](https://www.flintsauctions.com/auction/lot/lot-264---a-carl-jena-zeiss-r-biotar-45cm-f085-camera-lens/?lot=22893&sd=1)

## Optical Architecture and Orientation

The four air-separated members are L1, L2, L3 and cemented L4+L5, in that order. The first, third and fourth members are converging, and the second is negative. Diaphragm A lies between the second and third members. All curved faces are spherical, R6 is plane, and R8 is the cemented interface. The first air lens is a dispersive meniscus, while the other two air lenses are convergent, as the patent describes.

The printed stop intervals are 43.80 mm behind R4 and 16.59 mm before R5, placing A 95.26 mm behind the first vertex. The first-to-last vertex length is 176.81 mm, within the patent's preferred range of three quarters to twice the focal length. The stop station is printed; its physical opening is inferred.

The patent defines the front toward the object for reduction and toward the image for enlargement. The model follows the reduction direction with an object at infinity. The calculated EFL is 99.998 mm. No back focal distance is printed, so the image plane is the paraxial infinity plane, 26.00 mm behind R9 and 202.81 mm behind R1.

The stated image diameter is 25 mm. A real chief ray reaches the 12.5 mm image radius at the derived half-field of 7.2°. The patent prints the image diameter rather than a field angle. It permits larger image fields when reduced definition is acceptable, without giving a second numerical field.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.63753, νd = 56.1. Glass: Unmatched. Focal length in air: +126.56 mm.

The front collector has radii +135.14 and −182.95 mm and a center thickness of 29.98 mm. Its inferred front and rear clear radii are both 61 mm, giving a square rim with about 5 mm of edge thickness.

### L2 — Negative Meniscus, Concave to Object

nd = 1.75823, νd = 27.4. Glass: Unmatched. Focal length in air: −183.65 mm.

The −128.96 mm front face is more strongly curved than the −1813.8 mm rear face. This 11.70 mm thick negative member uses the patent's high-dispersion glass, with νd below 35, ahead of the diaphragm. Its inferred clear radii are 61 and 52.6 mm. The front curve continues to the outer edge in the model. At the rear, an opaque land extends to 61 mm. The outline thus retains the drawn full-height shoulder with square corners where the drawing shows short chamfers.

### L3 — Plano-Convex Positive

nd = 1.46449, νd = 65.8. Glass: FK3 (Schott), a catalog match. Focal length in air: +210.23 mm.

The +97.65 mm front face and plane rear face form the added positive collector behind the diaphragm. Its center thickness is 23.82 mm; both inferred clear radii are 45 mm, giving a square rim. The low index and high Abbe number distinguish it from the denser front collector and rear doublet glasses.

### L4 — Biconvex Positive in the Rear Doublet

nd = 1.62203, νd = 53.1. Glass: SSK2 (Sumita), spectral proxy. Focal length in air: +50.67 mm.

The +60.22 mm front face and −59.78 mm cemented face enclose 15.10 mm of glass. L4 supplies the positive power of the rear doublet. Its inferred front and cemented clear radii are both 26 mm.

### L5 — Negative Meniscus, Concave to Object, in the Rear Doublet

nd = 1.75823, νd = 27.4. Glass: Unmatched. Focal length in air: −94.52 mm.

L5 shares L4's −59.78 mm cemented face and has a weak −369.17 mm rear face. It is 3.40 mm thick on axis, with an inferred rear clear radius of 23.4 mm and a bevel toward the 26 mm cemented edge. Its dispersive glass repeats L2's printed pair, consistent with the patent's discussion of a dispersive element in the final converging member. L4+L5 form cemented group C1 with a combined focal length of +99.07 mm; the individual element focal lengths above are calculated in air.

## Glass Identification and Spectral Limits

Both tables print nd with a lowercase d, and claim 3 explicitly names the d-line. All five elements therefore use helium d, approximately 587.6 nm. The patent gives an index and Abbe value for each glass, without naming suppliers, melts or additional spectral indices.

L3 matches the Schott FK3 catalog row at nd = 1.46450 and νd = 65.77: catalog minus printed differences are Δnd +0.00001 and Δνd −0.03. It uses the catalog dispersion curve.

L4 uses Sumita SSK2 as a spectral proxy, with catalog minus printed differences of Δnd +0.00027 and Δνd +0.04 at helium d. These differences fall within the site's spectral-proxy tolerance of 0.002 in index and 0.25 in Abbe number. The printed index and Abbe number are retained. The catalog row supplies the shape of the dispersion curve, and the historical supplier and melt are unknown for both catalog assignments.

L1, L2 and L5 retain Unmatched labels because no catalog row falls within those tolerances. Their spectral behavior is estimated from the printed index and Abbe number; measured partial dispersion and broadband performance remain unknown.

## Focus Mechanism

Infinity focus only; the patent gives no close-focus data. There is no group motion or finite-conjugate prescription. The reduction/enlargement orientation statement supplies no working object and image distances. No production minimum focus distance is assigned.

## Aperture, Rims and Model Limits

The printed aperture is f/0.90. The inferred iris radius is 37.27 mm from paraxial pupil scaling; exact axial tracing at that opening admits an entrance radius of 52.29 mm, equivalent to f/0.96. The full f/0.90 axial marginal ray would require an iris radius of 39.99 mm. The source aperture and printed stop station remain the basis of the model.

No numerical clear radii are printed. The inferred radii at R1–R9 are 61, 61, 61, 52.6, 45, 45, 26, 26 and 23.4 mm. They follow the sole drawing on page 1, whose axial spacings and front-surface sag agree with the printed prescription at a single common scale. R4 has an opaque land to 61 mm, approximating the drawn shoulder without continuing the optical curve. The rear optical face is slightly larger than its roughly 23 mm drawn extent to retain corner illumination. Glass thickness and air clearance remain positive, and the stop plane lies entirely in air.

Every optical rim passes the axial beam and the chief ray at 7.2°, while the rear doublet vignettes part of the off-axis bundle. Wide-open geometric pupil transmission is about 98.3% at 4.8°, 91.7% at 6.0° and 82.9% at 7.2°. Upper-side meridional clipping of the nominal f/0.90 bundle is approximately 23%, 27% and 30% at R7, R8 and R9; these widths are not radiometric illumination measurements. The opposite marginal extreme is not established. The drawn diaphragm opening is schematic and does not independently determine the iris diameter.

The patent supplies no MTF curve or measured performance for comparison. The inferred apertures, limited glass dispersion data and infinity-only conjugate limit comparisons with a manufactured X-ray objective.
