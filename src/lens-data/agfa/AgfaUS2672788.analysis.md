# AGFA 100mm f/2.5 (US 2,672,788 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,672,788 A

**Inventors:** Theodor Brendel

**Assignee:** Agfa Camera-Werk AG

**Filed:** July 19, 1951

**Published:** March 23, 1954

**Priority:** Germany, July 26, 1950

**Example details:** Example 2, prescription in column 3 on PDF page 4; Figure 1 shows its arrangement.

The patent describes a five-element photographic objective derived from the double-Gauss form. It gives three numerical examples: Example 1 at f/2.8 and Examples 2 and 3 at f/2.5. All three have a printed focal length of 100 mm, and the description states a 55.0° picture field (PDF p. 3, columns 1–2). Example 2 computes to an effective focal length of 100.00 mm.

No production lens is documented for this prescription.

## Optical Architecture

Five elements form four air-spaced groups: L1 | L2+L3 | L4 | L5. Their group powers are positive–negative–negative–positive. The two inner negative menisci face the diaphragm with their concave surfaces; the front one is a cemented pair. The outer members are positive singlets.

The L2/L3 junction at R4 is plane and cemented. Its small index change, 1.62425 to 1.62356, gives little monochromatic power, while the different Abbe numbers make the pair useful for color correction. R8 is the separate plane front face of the rear positive element. Seven curved boundaries are spherical; the other two optical boundaries are plane.

The glass and air spaces total 46.655 mm from the first to the last optical vertex. The final image distance, absent from the patent, is the computed paraxial back focus of 70.93 mm, giving a first-surface-to-image distance of 117.59 mm.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### Element 1 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.62425, νd = 56.8. Glass: Unmatched. Standalone focal length: +107.27 mm.

The front positive meniscus collects light ahead of the negative inner groups. R1 = +41.132 mm and R2 = +101.677 mm give its positive power. Its axial thickness is 4.972 mm.

### Element 2 — Plano-Convex Positive

nd = 1.62425, νd = 56.8. Glass: Unmatched. Standalone focal length: +55.83 mm.

The positive front member of C1 has a curved entrance face, R3 = +34.851 mm, and a plane cemented rear face. Its 11.392 mm thickness makes it the thickest glass element. Its focal length is 55.8% of the complete objective's nominal focal length, within the patent's 45–65% condition.

### Element 3 — Plano-Concave Negative

nd = 1.62356, νd = 36.8. Glass: Unmatched. Standalone focal length: −34.36 mm.

The thinner, more dispersive rear member of C1 is cemented to Element 2 at plane R4 and ends at R5 = +21.425 mm. It is 1.905 mm thick. The assembled pair is a negative meniscus with a focal length of −144.42 mm, despite the positive power of its front member.

### Element 4 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.62356, νd = 36.8. Glass: Unmatched. Standalone focal length: −131.39 mm.

The rear inner meniscus has R6 = −25.708 mm and R7 = −40.801 mm. Its concave front surface faces the diaphragm. The 5.963 mm axial thickness is 0.448 times the thickness of the front compound meniscus, within the patent's 0.25–0.60 interval.

### Element 5 — Plano-Convex Positive

nd = 1.62309, νd = 58.1. Glass: Unmatched. Standalone focal length: +68.28 mm.

The rear collecting element has plane R8 and convex exit face R9 = −42.546 mm. It is 7.945 mm thick and follows the preceding meniscus across a 0.304 mm air gap.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent prints nd and explicitly names the d-line. The model uses the helium d line at 587.6 nm. The lower-dispersion 1.62425/56.8 pair is used for L1 and L2, the more dispersive 1.62356/36.8 pair for L3 and L4, and 1.62309/58.1 for L5.

All five elements remain unmatched. For L1/L2, the nearby Schott N-SK10 row differs by about −0.00147 in index. For L3/L4, the nearby CDGM H-F6 row differs by about +0.00139 in index and −1.21 in Abbe number. These are different glasses from the printed pairs.

The patent supplies no individual C-, F- or g-line indices or partial-dispersion values. Chromatic calculations for the unmatched glasses therefore depend on estimates from their index/Abbe pairs.

## Focus Mechanism and Aperture

The patent gives no close-focus data. The model is focused at infinity, with fixed optical spacings.

Figure 1 places the diaphragm in the 13.870 mm air space between R5 and R6. The inferred split is 7.68 mm after R5 and 6.19 mm before R6, placing the stop 26.253 mm behind the first vertex. A measurement of the drawing gives about 7.72/6.15 mm, consistent with that estimate.

The modeled iris radius is 13.94 mm, corresponding to paraxial f/2.50. An axial ray entering at a height of 20 mm reaches a stop height of 13.80 mm. The real-ray entrance-equivalent f-number is about 2.48, reflecting pupil aberration. Neither the split nor the iris bore is numerically specified by the patent.

## Field Coverage and Clear Apertures

The printed 55.0° picture field gives a half-field of 27.5°. Figures 3 and 4 plot astigmatism and distortion through 25.0°. At 27.5°, the chief ray reaches an image height of 51.70 mm without clipping. The model's 103.41 mm image circle is twice that height at the computed infinity plane; it follows the patent's field, with no camera format specified.

The field edge is vignetted. Full-pupil transmission at 27.5° is unresolved despite chief-ray clearance. At 25.0°, the front and rear faces clip the sides of the meridional bundle by about 44% and 32%, respectively; these widths are not transmitted-flux percentages.

All clear semi-diameters are inferred: R1/R2 22 mm, R3 20 mm, R4 19 mm, R5/R6 15 mm, R7 19 mm, and R8/R9 20 mm. Figure 1 suggests smaller values, especially at the rear. Its approximately 18.1 mm R3 and 13.6 mm R5 rims would clip the axial f/2.5 beam, which requires about 18.82 and 14.04 mm there. The model retains the larger clear apertures.

The minimum modeled glass thickness is about 1.003 mm. The curved central air space retains about 2.913 mm of clearance at the shared rim; the greatest rim angle is about 44.4°. No measured mount or bevel dimensions are available.

## Aberration Correction and Model Limits

The patent links improved spherical correction and field flatness to the thicknesses and powers of the inner menisci. Example 2's compound front meniscus occupies 28.5% of the optical length, within the stated 25–35% range. Its cemented index difference is 0.00069, below the patent's 0.01 ceiling. The computed Petzval sum is 0.002162 mm⁻¹.

The patent states a spherical-zone error of about 0.25% of focal length at f/2.5; the modeled longitudinal zone is about −0.275 mm. The source plots and these first-order results do not establish measured MTF or production-lens performance. The inferred diaphragm and clear apertures affect off-axis clipping, and the unmatched glasses limit spectral conclusions.
