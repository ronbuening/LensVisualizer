# Agfa Color-Solagon 55 mm f/2 (Agfa Ambiflex)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,784,646
**Filed:** 1953-10-21
**Granted:** 1957-03-12
**Priority:** 1952-10-28, Germany
**Inventors:** Theodor Brendel; Hans Lautenbacher
**Assignee:** Agfa Camera-Werk AG
**Title:** Four Component Photographic Objective Formed of Six Lens Elements
**Example details:** Tables on PDF pages 3–4; Figure 4.

Example 1 has a normalized focal length of 1. Every printed radius, thickness and air space is multiplied by 55 to obtain millimetres, while the refractive indices and Abbe numbers remain unchanged. The patent explicitly defines the d line as 587.6 nm. The printed assignee name is Agfa Camera-Werk Aktiengesellschaft, abbreviated above as Agfa Camera-Werk AG.

The association with the Color-Solagon 55 mm f/2 rests on two sources:

1. The [Agfa sales brochure](https://www.cameramanuals.org/agfa_ansco/agfa_agfaflex.pdf) confirms a 55 mm f/2 Color-Solagon with six elements.
2. A [historical account](https://www.zeissikonveb.de/start/objektive/normalobjektive/pancolar2.html) associates Brendel and Lautenbacher's DE 1,063,824 with the Color-Solagon offered for the Ambiflex and Selectaflex. Its reproduced numerical prescription agrees with US Example 1.

These sources support the production-lens association, but do not establish the exact retail prescription, production scale or complete German patent-family relationship. The [Agfa Solagon 50 mm f/2](/lens/agfa-solagon-50mm-f2) uses a different patent.

## Optical Architecture

The six elements form four air-separated components: a positive front singlet, the cemented L2/L3 doublet, the cemented L4/L5 doublet and a positive rear singlet. This is a Gaussian-type arrangement with the two inner compound menisci facing the central diaphragm. The patent describes the inner components as having small negative power and the outer components as positive (PDF page 3).

The inner doublets provide the opposed concave surfaces around the stop, while the outer positive elements supply the collecting power. The patent discusses this arrangement in relation to spherical and chromatic aberration, coma, astigmatism, field curvature, distortion and vignetting. Its correction claims do not establish measured performance for the reconstructed model or a production lens. All powered surfaces are spherical; the cemented interfaces are planar, and no aspheric coefficients are supplied.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The shapes below follow the transcribed radii. Individual element focal lengths are not published in the supplied source evidence and are not assigned here. The glass supplier and factory glass identities remain unknown.

### L1 — Positive meniscus, convex toward the object

nd = 1.72253, νd = 37.8. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.72253 / νd 37.8).

L1 is the front positive singlet. Its stronger front curvature and weaker rear curvature begin convergence before the front cemented component. It uses the same published index and Abbe number as L6.

### L2 + L3 — Front cemented component

L2: nd = 1.66972, νd = 47.4. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.66972 / νd 47.4).

L3: nd = 1.65456, νd = 33.9. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.65456 / νd 33.9).

L2 is plano-convex and L3 plano-concave, sharing the flat S4 cemented interface in C1. L3's concave rear surface S5 faces the diaphragm. The patent describes this compound meniscus as weakly negative. The positive member has the higher Abbe number, providing a dispersion contrast with its negative partner; this is a qualitative reading of the prescription rather than a measured chromatic result.

### L4 + L5 — Rear cemented component

L4: nd = 1.69915, νd = 30.1. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.69915 / νd 30.1).

L5: nd = 1.69086, νd = 54.7. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.69086 / νd 54.7).

L4 is plano-concave and L5 plano-convex, joined at the flat S7 interface in C2. The concave S6 surface faces the stop, opposing S5 across the central air space. This is the second weakly negative compound meniscus described by the patent. Its negative member again has the lower Abbe number; no partial-dispersion measurements establish the residual secondary spectrum.

### L6 — Positive biconvex rear singlet

nd = 1.72253, νd = 37.8. Glass: Unmatched (nd 1.72253 / νd 37.8).

L6 closes the system with a weakly curved front surface and a more strongly curved rear surface. It provides the rear positive component of the patent's four-component architecture. The image plane lies behind this element at a calculated, unprinted back-focus distance.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The prescription retains all six printed d-line index/Abbe pairs. No Schott row in the available catalog matches within |Δnd| ≤ 0.0001 and |Δνd| ≤ 0.15, so every element is labeled Unmatched. The source supplies no measured glass curves, established historical catalog identities or glass-vendor assignments. Modern catalog comparisons cannot establish the historical glass identity or override the patent's explicit 587.6 nm reference wavelength.

The two cemented components pair positive members of higher Abbe number with negative members of lower Abbe number. This supports a qualitative chromatic-correction interpretation, but the published index/Abbe pairs alone do not establish anomalous partial dispersion, apochromatic performance or full spectral accuracy. No measured glass curves or full spectral validation are claimed.

## Focus Mechanism

Only the infinity prescription is modeled. The source evidence supplies no close-focus station, focus travel or near-focus spacing sequence, so no manufactured focusing motion or minimum focusing distance is assigned. The very large object-distance setting represents infinity, rather than a measured close-focus limit.

The final gap, 39.8881649722 mm, is the calculated paraxial back focal distance and is not printed in the patent. The model holds the image plane at this distance.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The calculated effective focal length is 54.9985181967 mm at f/2, compared with the 55 mm target. The printed thicknesses and spaces total 35.67905 mm; including the calculated back focus gives a 75.56721497223845 mm track. These are model calculations, not factory measurements.

### Inferred rims and diaphragm

The source supplies neither clear-aperture semi-diameters nor an exact stop split. The inferred rims are S1/S2 20 mm, S3/S4 12.9 mm, S5 10.2 mm, S6 9.4 mm, S7/S8 11.5 mm and S9/S10 14.5 mm. L2 has equal front and rear rims; the shorter inner concave faces S5/S6 preserve the step toward the diaphragm shown in Figure 4. Their radii also admit the axial f/2 beam. The drawing is schematic: its individual axial thicknesses do not consistently match the prescription, so its proportions support the relative rim heights rather than factory aperture measurements.

The inferred iris position is 8.376374101204 mm after S5 and 4.803825898796 mm before S6, preserving the 13.1802 mm central air space. At the modeled cap edges, the axial distances to this plane are 3.958208458 mm from S5 and 2.159769020 mm from S6. These clearances do not establish manufactured shutter or mounting clearance or an exact factory stop station. The stop radius, 9.632180754601764 mm, is derived from f/2.

### Field coverage and image quality

There is no axial rim limit at f/2. The calculated geometric pupil transmission is approximately 51.7% at the full-frame corner, with substantial off-axis loss also present at the patent's 25° half-field. Unknown factory rims prevent attributing these losses to a production lens or treating them as measured illumination.

The modeled chief ray reaches the 21.65 mm full-frame corner at 21.7°. This establishes chief-ray coverage, not illumination across the whole pupil. The model supplies no measured illumination, measured glass curves, near-focus prescription or exact factory-performance data; no MTF claim follows from these calculations.
