# MIRANDA AUTO E 50mm f/1.4

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 3,634,001 A\
**Application Number:** 44,193\
**Filed:** 1970-06-08\
**Granted:** 1972-01-11\
**Priority:** 1969-06-07, Japan 44/44531\
**Inventor:** Kunio Shimada\
**Assignee:** Miranda Camera Co., Ltd. (printed “Miranda Camera Company Limited”)\
**Title:** Modified Gauss-type lens system having a long back focal length\
**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 1 (printed “EXAMPLE I”)

The prescription is the first numerical example on PDF page 4, printed column 3.
It describes a modified Gauss system with seven glass elements in five components.
The associated construction appears in Figure 1; Figure 2 is expressly associated with the first example.

The connection to the Auto Miranda-E 50mm f/1.4 is plausible but unconfirmed:

1. The patent is assigned to Miranda Camera Company Limited.
2. Miranda's Auto Sensorex EE manual identifies the Auto Miranda-E 50mm f/1.4 as a seven-element, five-group modified Gauss lens.
3. The published aperture ratio is 1:1.4; scaling the patent's nominal 100 mm design by 0.5 gives the marketed 50 mm class.

The manual establishes the Miranda four-claw bayonet interface and 35 mm film application.
Its marketed full angle is 46°, while the patent's aberration chart reaches a half-angle of 22.5°.
The manual calls this seven-element family “Auto Miranda-E” and specifies a minimum aperture of f/16.
The undated manual does not establish an exact release year, and no primary statement identifies Example 1 as the production prescription.

## Optical Architecture

The front-to-rear physical sequence is L1, the cemented pair L2–L3, the central stop space, the cemented pair L4–L5, L6 and L7.
The two cemented junctions are patent R4 and R7.
Each cemented pair shares a glass-to-glass refracting surface.
All refracting surfaces are spherical, and there are no published rear plates, filters or aspheres in this example.

The five air-separated components have positive–negative–negative–positive–positive net power when assessed individually in air.
The L2–L3 doublet has a standalone EFL of -89.970 mm, and L4–L5 has -123.618 mm at the 50 mm scale.
These compound powers differ from the powers of their positive and negative constituent elements.
Spacing and interaction between the components yield a positive complete system; isolated power signs do not establish the aberration contribution of each element.

The patent emphasizes control of its strongly negative central refracting surfaces and the curvature of field with a limited use of its historical “new-type” glass category.
Those statements describe the inventor's optimization rationale, not an independently reproduced performance measurement.
Despite the title's long-back-focus description, this numerical model does not meet BFD > EFL; it is not classified as retrofocus here.
Its track-to-EFL ratio also exceeds unity, so it is not classified as telephoto.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Positive meniscus, convex to object

nd = 1.72342, νd = 37.9. Glass: J-BASF8 (Hikari spectral proxy for patent BaSF8; historical supplier/melt unconfirmed). f = +74.610 mm.

The first element is an isolated positive meniscus ahead of the first cemented pair. Its rear radius is substantially weaker than its front radius. It contributes positive collection power, but its individual role in spherical aberration cannot be inferred from that sign alone. The patent distinguishes it from the more strongly powered second and fifth elements when discussing condition (1).

### L2 — Biconvex positive, first cemented pair

nd = 1.72342, νd = 37.9. Glass: J-BASF8 (Hikari spectral proxy for patent BaSF8; historical supplier/melt unconfirmed). f = +31.599 mm.

L2 is the positive member of D1 and meets L3 at R4. Condition (1) requires its refractive index to exceed 1.69. The patent identifies the second and fifth elements as the stronger converging members involved in its zonal spherical-aberration and field-curvature strategy. L2 has positive power in isolation, while the complete D1 doublet has negative net power.

### L3 — Biconcave negative, first cemented pair

nd = 1.71736, νd = 29.5. Glass: SF1 (Schott spectral proxy for patent SF1; historical supplier/melt unconfirmed). f = -19.733 mm.

L3 ends at the strongly concave central surface R5. Conditions (2) and (4) constrain that surface power and the low Abbe number of this negative element. Its interface with L2 is a weak positive refracting boundary because the adjacent indices are close; its final glass-to-air surface carries the strong negative surface power.

### L4 — Biconcave negative, second cemented pair

nd = 1.71736, νd = 29.5. Glass: SF1 (Schott spectral proxy for patent SF1; historical supplier/melt unconfirmed). f = -19.789 mm.

L4 begins at the opposing central surface R6 and is cemented to L5 at R7. Conditions (3) and (4) constrain its front-surface power and dispersion coordinate. The wide central air space separates the two negative elements. The patent discusses that space through condition (7), rather than publishing a movable focusing group.

### L5 — Biconvex positive, second cemented pair

nd = 1.73400, νd = 51.3. Glass: J-LAK09 (Hikari spectral proxy for patent LaK09; historical supplier/melt unconfirmed). f = +29.637 mm.

L5 is the positive member of D2. The patent explicitly places the first example’s sole “new-type” glass in this slot and lists LaK09. Conditions (1), (6) and (8) involve its index or neighboring surface curvatures. Neither that historical class label nor the positive focal power establishes a modern glass supplier or an anomalous-dispersion specification.

### L6 — Positive meniscus, convex toward image

nd = 1.66672, νd = 48.3. Glass: J-BAF11 (Hikari spectral proxy for patent BaF11; historical supplier/melt unconfirmed). f = +62.600 mm.

L6 is an isolated positive meniscus after the second cemented pair. Both tabulated radii are negative, with the stronger curvature on the rear surface. The element is separated from its neighbors by thin but nonzero air gaps. The independent calculation identifies its positive standalone power; no separate source-supported claim that it is a dedicated field flattener is made.

### L7 — Biconvex positive rear element

nd = 1.66672, νd = 48.3. Glass: J-BAF11 (Hikari spectral proxy for patent BaF11; historical supplier/melt unconfirmed). f = +94.955 mm.

L7 is the final isolated converging element. Its rear curvature is relatively weak, and the image plane lies behind that rear refracting vertex. Its positive standalone focal length describes the isolated element in air, not its in-situ contribution to the system focal length or a separately established aberration correction.

The element focal lengths above are standalone thick-lens values in air at the 50 mm scale.
The cemented compounds and complete model are computed separately using the actual glass-to-glass boundaries.
The patent's component discussions are in printed columns 1–4; the numerical example controls the radii and indices.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The palette contains four distinct printed coordinates: BaSF8 at 1.72342/37.9, SF1 at 1.71736/29.5, LaK09 at 1.734/51.3 and BaF11 at 1.66672/48.3.
The d-line convention is explicit in the patent; the original values are retained without e-to-d conversion.

Modern official Hikari rows provide J-BASF8 at 1.723420/38.03, J-SF1 at 1.717360/29.57, J-LAK09 at 1.734000/51.51 and J-BAF11 at 1.666720/48.33.
Those exact index matches still differ from the source in Abbe number by +0.13, +0.07, +0.21 and +0.03 respectively.
Schott's SF1 is 1.71736/29.51; HOYA alternatives include E-FD1L, discontinued BAF11 and TAC4 at 1.73400/51.05.
These comparisons demonstrate that a coordinate match is not unique supplier evidence.

Dispersion is approximated with J-BASF8 for L1–L2, Schott SF1 for L3–L4, J-LAK09 for L5 and J-BAF11 for L6–L7.
The Hikari curves use the manufacturer's published nine-term power-series coefficients; the Schott SF1 curve uses its published Sellmeier coefficients.
Each curve is anchored to the patent's d-line refractive index, and the original patent nd/νd values are retained.
J-LAK09 is distinct from J-LAK9 (1.691/54.93), despite their similar names.
These curves are spectral proxies: the original supplier and melt remain unconfirmed, and the patent publishes no C/F/g line indices or partial-dispersion measurements for its glasses.
No apochromatic or anomalous-partial-dispersion performance is established by the available evidence.

## Focus Mechanism

The production manual describes manual focusing down to 0.43 m.
The patent provides only the infinity prescription and gives no spacing changes for closer focus, so only infinity focus is represented here.

The stop is shown in the central D5 space, but its precise axial coordinate and diameter are unpublished.
Its modeled location is inferred at the midpoint of that gap.
Its radius of 11.492907 mm is calibrated by exact on-axis tracing at the entrance-pupil radius corresponding to f/1.4.
This reproduces the nominal f/1.4 aperture, but does not establish the production diaphragm's diameter.
A paraxial pupil calculation with the same iris gives f/1.447282, illustrating the effect of pupil aberration and the calculation convention.
The Seidel table also lists t₁ or l₁ = 0.59394 at f = 1 without defining the parameter; it does not establish the stop's physical location.

## Conditional Expressions

The patent gives eight design conditions in printed column 1.
Using the published f = 100.0 normalization, their evaluated quantities are:

- (1) N2 = 1.72342 and N5 = 1.734, both above 1.69.
- (2) f × |(1 − N3)/R5| = 2.304401, between 2.1 and 2.6.
- (3) f × |(N4 − 1)/R6| = 2.395992, between 2.2 and 2.6.
- (4) ν3 = ν4 = 29.5, below 32.
- (5) f/(−R4) = 0.305689, between 0.1 and 1.0.
- (6) f/R7 = 0.173223, between 0.03 and 0.5.
- (7) D5/f = 0.316700, between 0.29 and 0.33.
- (8) f × (1 − N5)/R8 = 1.577477, between 1.4 and 1.8.

All inequalities hold for the literal source prescription.
The signed surface powers in the printed example remain negative for conditions (2) and (3); the inequalities use their magnitudes.
The source-normalized surface-by-surface Petzval sum is 0.144152, consistent with its printed 0.144.
This reproduces the Petzval quantity, not the complete published Seidel table or plotted image quality.

## Calculated Optical Properties

All dimensional prescription entries are scaled uniformly by s = 0.5; indices and Abbe numbers are unchanged.
The semi-diameters are estimated from Figure 1, including its square front-element rim and stepped central doublets.
The drawing does not dimension those rims; the inferred apertures are also constrained by exact marginal rays, edge thickness, rim slope and shared-band air-gap clearance.
They are not measured production dimensions.

At infinity, the scaled prescription gives:

- EFL: 50.003665 mm.
- BFD to paraxial focus from the final glass vertex: 35.545147 mm.
- First-to-last glass-vertex track: 49.290000 mm.
- First vertex to computed paraxial focus: 84.835147 mm.
- Front principal plane relative to the first vertex: 49.227065 mm, imageward positive.
- Rear principal plane relative to the final vertex: -14.458518 mm, imageward positive.
- BFD/EFL: 0.710851; total optical track/EFL: 1.696579.
- Surface-by-surface Petzval sum: 0.002883042 mm⁻¹.

The image plane is placed 35.53 mm behind the last glass surface, following the patent's scaled BF value.
The literal native prescription computes BF = 71.090294 mm rather than the printed 71.06 mm.
That residual is larger than the printed BF's last-place rounding interval and is not claimed as an exact reproduction.
N5 is printed to only three decimal places; a sensitivity-only value within that rounding cell can reproduce the printed BF while the EFL still rounds to 100.0.
No such value is substituted in the model, and the scaled paraxial-focus offset remains approximately 0.015147 mm.

The on-axis f/1.4 marginal ray clears every glass rim.
Off-axis vignetting remains: at fields through 13.5°, some rays at 75% of the entrance-pupil radius clip at S1, S9 or S10.
The sampled rays within that field and pupil-fraction range clear the lens at f/2–f/16.
At wider fields and larger pupil fractions, additional rays are clipped or miss the front element's clear aperture.
The declared patent field reaches 20.36 mm image height, about 94% of the 21.65 mm full-frame corner; the corner chief ray clears at approximately 23.8° but exceeds the source's declared half-field.
The model therefore does not establish the production lens's full-field illumination.
