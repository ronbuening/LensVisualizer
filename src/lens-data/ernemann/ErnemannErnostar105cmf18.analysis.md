# Ernemann Ernostar 10.5 cm f/1.8: DE 436,260 C, Example II

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** DE 436,260 C  
**Designer (historical attribution):** Ludwig Bertele; no inventor is printed in the patent  
**Assignee:** Ernemann-Werke AG  
**Published:** 28 October 1926\
**Effective date stated:** 6 December 1924  
**Example details:** Example II on page 2; Abb. 2 on page 4; f/1.8, approximately 50° field.

Example II describes the design family of the Ernemann Ernostar 10.5 cm f/1.8 for the Ermanox 4.5 × 6 cm. A uniform scale factor of 1.05 maps the patent's f = 100 mm normalisation to nominal 105 mm. Every radius, thickness, gap, image-side distance and inferred semi-diameter receives this factor; the derived iris radius scales with them. Values remain in millimetres without units conversion; refractive indices, Abbe numbers, angles and f-numbers are unchanged. The complete four-page patent supplies the prescription on page 2 and Abb. 2 on page 4. The f/1.8 Ernostar lenses were sold at 8.5, 10.5, 12.5 and 16.5 cm; the commercial 10 cm Ernostar was the four-element f/2 lens. Bertele is a historical attribution, not a printed patent inventor.

Bertele's original article, published in 1926 and dated 5 November 1925, states that Ernemann manufactures the commercial Ernostar and describes f/1.8, approximately 50° field and a six-element/four-component section. It lists retail 85 mm, 125 mm and 165 mm applications. The article contains no complete numerical prescription and does not uniquely identify Example II. Its normalized curves do not prove a factory 100 mm variant.

The public [10.5 cm f/1.8 section](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d0/Ernostar_10%2C5cm_1%2C8_%281924%29.jpg) agrees with Example II in its six-element/four-group layout, including the second-component cemented triplet and iris behind the negative singlet. The 8.5, 12.5 and 16.5 cm f/1.8 lenses share this design family. This correspondence does not establish the exact factory radii, glasses or clear apertures. Examples I and III are distinct designs. The catalog records marketing focal length 105 mm and computed paraxial design EFL 104.71 mm, rounded to two decimals.

The [Science Museum Group's Ermanox record](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co15054/ermanox-camera-with-ernostar-f1-8-lens), object 1970-372, documents an Ernemann camera with an Ernostar f/1.8 lens dated 1925. It supports the camera and lens-family association without specifying focal length or a numerical prescription. The [Swiss Camera Museum](https://www.cameramuseum.ch/en/discover/permanent-exhibition/the-plate-era/the-ermanox-plays-with-the-shadows/) attributes the Ernostar to Ludwig Bertele and describes the Ermanox's helical lens focusing.

## Optical Architecture

Six spherical elements form four air-separated components: a positive front meniscus, a cemented triplet, a negative singlet and a positive rear singlet. The two cemented interfaces are within the second component, not added air spaces. The source's triplet modification is preserved rather than replaced by a simpler Ernostar-type formula.

The front meniscus and compound positive component precede the negative member, followed by the rear positive element. The diaphragm is after the negative member, at the source's explicit 5.87/6.62 mm split of the 12.49 mm gap, now 6.1635/6.951 mm within a 13.1145 mm gap. L2–L4 form cemented triplet C1 in group 2, bounded by surfaces 3 and 6.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1: front positive meniscus

Source nD = 1.5638, interpreted νd = 60.75. Glass: Schott N-SK11, a catalog equivalent rather than an identified historical melt. The more strongly curved front surface supplies positive power. Its source 7.26 mm center thickness becomes 7.623 mm.

### L2: positive front element of the triplet

Source nD = 1.6053, interpreted νd = 59.4. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.6053 / V 59.4). This biconvex element shares its rear boundary with L3. The relatively thick source 11.74 mm element becomes 12.327 mm.

### L3: middle positive meniscus of the triplet

Source nD = 1.6485, interpreted νd = 33.8. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.6485 / V 33.8). Its two negative radii form a positive meniscus in isolation. Both interfaces are cemented to the adjacent glasses.

### L4: negative rear element of the triplet

Source nD = 1.6270, interpreted νd = 39.0. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.6270 / V 39.0). This biconcave element has a source center thickness of 1.07 mm, now 1.1235 mm. Its front interface is cemented to L3.

### L5: negative singlet before the diaphragm

Source nD = 1.6223, interpreted νd = 36.0. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.6223 / V 36.0). The biconcave member precedes the source-defined stop. Its inferred clear aperture is constrained by clearance to that fixed stop plane.

### L6: rear positive singlet

Source nD = 1.6220, interpreted νd = 53.15. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.6220 / V 53.15). The biconvex rear element completes the positive-negative-positive power sequence. The source does not supply a terminal image gap or factory clear aperture.

Isolated-element focal lengths are not tabulated in the patent. The glass labels distinguish the L1 catalog equivalent from the five unmatched numerical pairs.

## Glass Identification and Spectral Convention

Patent ν values are interpreted as conventional νd using contemporary optical notation; the formula is not expressly defined in DE 436,260 C. No historical glass identification or measured historical dispersion curve is claimed.

This interpretation is supported by the original 1899 von Rohr text, printed page 59, which defines plain ν as (nD − 1)/(nF − nC), separately from a specially barred D–G′ convention elsewhere. Bertele's later US1975678 also uses nd/v dispersion notation. These sources support the conventional reading; they do not turn the omitted definition in the inspected German patent into an explicit source statement.

The patent nD reference is treated as historical sodium D. Its use at the helium-d reference is an approximation. Schott N-SK11 has catalog nd = 1.56384 and νd = 60.80, differing from L1 by +0.00004 and +0.05. Its catalog curve supplies a spectral equivalent while the patent's numerical nd/νd pair remains unchanged; it does not identify a historical supplier or melt.

The remaining pairs have no catalog match within |Δnd| ≤ 0.0001 and |Δνd| ≤ 0.15. In particular, Schott SF2 differs from L3 by −0.00081 in nd despite its similar νd = 33.85. Schott N-SSK2 differs from L6 by +0.00029 and +0.12; the patent prints 1.6220 to four decimal places, so this is not an exact match. Partial dispersion, measured historical spectral curves and quantitative color performance remain unknown. No source glass value is adjusted.

## Focus Mechanism

The model is infinity-only. Paraxial calculation gives EFL 104.706063452 mm and BFD 59.677024127 mm, compared with 99.720060431 mm and 56.835261070 mm at the patent's normalisation. The stored image-side gap is rounded to 59.677024 mm. Total track is 113.122024 mm, compared with 107.73526107 mm at the original scale. The small −0.280% EFL difference from nominal remains at 105 mm. The image interval is calculated, not a printed back gap.

The Swiss Camera Museum describes production Ermanox lenses in helical focusing tubes. The patent supplies no focus movement or minimum object distance, so only infinity is shown, with 1e15 m as the infinity placeholder. No finite-focus prescription is supplied.

## Inferred Apertures and Source Stop

Clear apertures are not tabulated. At the patent's normalisation, the inferred common semi-diameters by air-separated component were 28 mm, 25 mm, 17.5 mm and 20 mm. A 17.5 mm front rim on L5 limited the nominal f/1.8 beam to f/1.87; 18.4 mm cleared the axial beam. The present component semi-diameters are 29.4 mm, 26.25 mm, 18.375 mm and 21 mm, with L5's front surface 7 at 19.32 mm and rear surface 8 at 18.375 mm. The axial beam is limited by the iris at f/1.8. Abb. 2 supports unequal component heights qualitatively, not calibrated dimensions; all clear apertures remain inferred.

The negative component's 18.375 mm rear rim has approximately 0.344186 mm clearance to the stop plane; the rear component has approximately 9.444691 mm clearance. At f/1.8, the calculated iris radius is 17.444848259 mm, compared with 16.614141199 mm at the original scale. The axial split comes from the source; the hole radius and clear apertures are inferred. The displayed range through f/22 is not a documented retail limit.

## Model Scope and Field Limitations

For the catalog's 645 frame (56 × 41.5 mm, 69.7 mm diagonal), the corner chief ray reaches the 34.85 mm semi-diagonal at a half-angle of 18.0800° without rim obstruction. This chief-ray result does not establish full-pupil illumination or the patent's approximately 50° full field. Off-axis vignetting, image-plane performance and MTF remain unquantified. The product association does not establish an exact factory prescription.
