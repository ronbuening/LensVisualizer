# ERNEMANN 100mm f/2 (DE 428,657 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** DE 428,657 C, _Objektiv_

**Inventors:** Ludwig Bertele

**Assignee:** Ernemann-Werke AG

**Published:** 10 May 1926

**Example details:** “Beispiel nach Abb. 2”; Figure 1 is the other numerical example.

The patent describes an f/2 objective whose split component reduces coma while allowing a large rear member to collect light from a film or plate. Figure 2 uses five air-separated elements at the printed focal length of 100 mm. The patent is effective from 5 March 1925; its heading names Ernemann-Werke A.-G. in Dresden and its footnote credits Bertele in Dresden-Blasewitz ([patent, p. 1](https://depatisnet.dpma.de/DepatisNet/depatisnet?window=1&space=menu&content=treffer&action=pdf&docid=DE000000428657A)).

This is an Ernostar-type design with the rear positive component split into a negative meniscus and a biconvex lens. It differs from the DE 436,260 C Ernostar, which has six elements in four groups, including a cemented triplet. No production lens is documented for this prescription.

## Optical Architecture

The sequence from R1 to R10 is positive–positive–negative–negative–positive. The five center thicknesses are 8.57, 9.52, 2.86, 2.86 and 14.29 mm; the four air gaps are 0.48, 5.71, 19.05 and 0.48 mm. Both narrow gaps are air spaces, so all five elements are separate groups. All ten refracting surfaces are spherical.

The patent's description places the film behind R10 and the screen on the R1 side. The model is focused at infinity and traces collimated light through R1 toward an image behind R10. Actual projection reverses this direction. The first radius is explicitly +48.57 mm. Ray paths are reciprocal through the same surfaces and iris, but infinity focus in the two directions gives different back focal distances: 56.18 mm toward R10 and 64.62 mm toward R1. Their paraxial EFL is the same.

The printed prescription gives EFL 100.03 mm, 0.03 mm above the stated 100 mm, with paraxial f/2.00. The calculated image plane is 56.18 mm after R10; back focus is not printed. The first-to-last surface track is 63.82 mm and the distance from R1 to that plane is 120.00 mm. No cover plate, filter or baffle is specified.

The rear negative meniscus and positive biconvex lens enclose an air lens with converging action. The patent identifies this split as the means of reducing coma while permitting the large rear member needed for projection ([patent, pp. 1–2](https://depatisnet.dpma.de/DepatisNet/depatisnet?window=1&space=menu&content=treffer&action=pdf&docid=DE000000428657A)).

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 / Element 1 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.6068, ν = 59.5. Glass: Unmatched. Standalone focal length = +76.41 mm.

The front converging element is 8.57 mm thick, with radii +48.57 and −952.45 mm. Its clear radii are estimated at 25.5 mm on both surfaces. The weak rear curvature makes it nearly plano-convex in appearance; its minimum edge thickness is about 1.00 mm.

### L2 / Element 2 — Positive Meniscus

nD = 1.6068, ν = 59.5. Glass: Unmatched. Standalone focal length = +221.10 mm.

This second converging element precedes the central diverging lens. Its thickness is 9.52 mm and its radii are +38.10 and +48.19 mm. Clear radii of 23.5 and 20.5 mm follow Figure 2 approximately, with the rear curve ending below the front rim; they do not establish a factory rim shape.

### L3 / Element 3 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.6477, ν = 33.9. Glass: Unmatched. Standalone focal length = −37.56 mm.

The central diverging element is the more dispersive of the two glass types. It is 2.86 mm thick with radii −223.83 and +27.43 mm. Its clear radii are 19.5 and 17.5 mm, following the shorter rear curve in Figure 2. The following 19.05 mm air gap contains the inferred stop.

### L4 / Element 4 — Negative Meniscus

nD = 1.6068, ν = 59.5. Glass: Unmatched. Standalone focal length = −129.75 mm.

This is the negative part of the split rear component. Its radii are +200.01 and +56.19 mm and its thickness is 2.86 mm. Both clear radii are 23.5 mm. A 0.48 mm air gap separates it from L5; together their facing surfaces enclose the converging air lens.

### L5 / Element 5 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.6068, ν = 59.5. Glass: Unmatched. Standalone focal length = +46.57 mm.

The positive part of the rear component faces the film. It is 14.29 mm thick with radii +51.43 and −56.15 mm. Both clear radii are 24 mm, consistent with the large rear member shown in Figure 2. Its converging power is stronger than that of either front positive element.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The table prints nD and ν. The D line is taken as sodium, 589.3 nm. L1, L2, L4 and L5 share 1.6068 / 59.5, while L3 uses 1.6477 / 33.9. These printed values are retained, and no exact catalog row has been assigned to either pair.

[Schott SF2](https://us.shop.schott.com/advanced-optics/en/Optical-Glass/SF2/c/glass-SF2) is close to L3, but its catalog curve gives nD = 1.64752 and νD = 33.84: differences of −0.00018 and −0.06 from the patent. Its D-line index does not reproduce the printed 1.6477, even though its helium d-line index, 1.64769, is close. L3 therefore remains Unmatched. The patent provides no individual C-, F- or g-line indices, partial dispersion, transmission or glass supplier; spectral calculations based on nD and ν are estimates.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent gives no close-focus data, projection distance, magnification or focus movement. All spacings remain fixed, with the image plane at the calculated paraxial back focus. A finite film-to-screen projection state is not specified.

## Inferred Iris and Illustrative Field

No iris is drawn or dimensioned. The stop is placed halfway through the 19.05 mm R6–R7 gap, at 36.665 mm from the R1 vertex, leaving 9.525 mm on either side. Its calculated diameter is 30.77 mm for paraxial f/2.00. Exact axial tracing through this iris admits an entrance radius of 24.57 mm, equivalent to approximately f/2.04. This difference comes from the real-ray pupil geometry; the rims do not limit the axial aperture.

The ±5.0° field is illustrative because the patent gives neither a numerical field angle nor a format. Its 5.0° chief ray reaches an image height of 8.75 mm and clears every rim. No catalog image circle is assigned from that chosen field.

The clear radii follow Figure 2 approximately: 25.5 mm at R1/R2, 23.5/20.5 mm at R3/R4, 19.5/17.5 mm at R5/R6, 23.5 mm at R7/R8 and 24 mm at R9/R10. The shorter R4 and R6 curves preserve the drawn taper of L2 and L3. At f/2 and the illustrative 5.0° edge, their full-bundle extremes are 19.90 and 16.25 mm, leaving about 0.6 and 1.3 mm of rim clearance. Geometric transmission is about 93.4% at that edge; front-rim vignetting remains. This construction does not establish full-field illumination or the dimensions of a manufactured barrel. All element edges and adjacent clearances remain positive, and no glass crosses the stop plane.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The signed radii, center thicknesses, air gaps and glass pairs are printed. The iris station and size, clear radii, illustrative field and paraxial image distance are inferred. The patent supports the film-to-screen orientation but does not supply finite projection distances or an iris design. Glass identities, spectral performance, mechanical dimensions and production identity remain unresolved.
