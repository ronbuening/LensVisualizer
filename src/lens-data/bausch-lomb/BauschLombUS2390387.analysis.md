# BAUSCH & LOMB 100mm f/5.6 (US 2,390,387 Design)

## Patent and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,390,387 A, “Telephoto Objective”

**Inventors:** Wilbur B. Rayton and Lena M. Hudson

**Assignee:** Bausch & Lomb Optical Company

**Filed:** 31 January 1944

**Published:** 4 December 1945

**Example details:** Figure 2; matching prescription tables on PDF pages 1 and 3.

Figure 2 describes a 100 mm f/5.6 telephoto objective with four elements in two cemented doublets. Figure 1 is a separate air-spaced design. No production lens is documented as using the Figure 2 prescription.

The tables print radius magnitudes, four center thicknesses, the 21.08 mm separation, and four D-line index/Abbe-number pairs. Radius signs follow the drawing. The images print the second element’s index as 1.6210; the text extraction’s 1.620 is incorrect.

## Optical Architecture

A positive front doublet and a negative rear doublet flank the diaphragm. Their computed focal lengths are +53.12 mm and −64.07 mm, respectively. This power distribution gives a first-vertex-to-image distance of 80.18 mm, shorter than the 100.00 mm effective focal length. All surfaces are spherical.

The patent labels eight element faces, but R2 = R3 and R6 = R7 are cemented interfaces. The model therefore has six distinct refracting surfaces: its surfaces 2 and 5 each represent one shared glass-to-glass interface. C1 joins elements 1–2 and C2 joins elements 3–4; the 21.08 mm air space lies between the doublets.

The patent emphasizes the rear elements’ index difference, which is 1.7200 − 1.5286 = 0.1914, exceeding its stated minimum of 0.160. The rear cemented radius has magnitude 28.50 mm, or 28.5% of the stated focal length, below the 35% upper limit discussed for Figure 2 (PDF pages 2–3).

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### Element 1 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.5170, ν = 64.5. Glass: Unmatched. Standalone focal length in air: +24.47 mm.

The strongly curved front surface supplies positive collecting power. The element is cemented to the negative second element, and their combined power remains positive.

### Element 2 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.6210, ν = 36.2. Glass: S-TIM2 (Ohara), spectral proxy. Standalone focal length in air: −40.62 mm.

This more dispersive member opposes part of the first element’s positive power. Its front face is the shared cemented interface in C1; its rear face opens into the diaphragm space.

### Element 3 — Negative Meniscus

nD = 1.5286, ν = 51.6. Glass: SBF2 (Hoya), spectral proxy. Standalone focal length in air: −33.44 mm.

The object-facing concave surface supplies the rear component’s principal negative power. The element is cemented to the higher-index positive fourth element at the 28.50 mm interface.

### Element 4 — Positive Meniscus

nD = 1.7200, ν = 29.3. Glass: FD20 (Hoya), catalog match at the printed precision. Standalone focal length in air: +79.02 mm.

This positive member moderates the third element’s negative power while preserving a net negative rear doublet. Its index difference from element 3 is a central condition of the patent’s correction of field curvature, coma and astigmatism.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent labels the index N_D and gives V for each element. The model retains the printed indices and Abbe numbers at the sodium D line, 589.3 nm. Element 2 uses S-TIM2 (Ohara, Δn = −0.00111 and Δν = +0.05) and element 3 uses SBF2 (Hoya, Δn = +0.00075 and Δν = +0.03). Both meet the site's spectral-proxy tolerance of 0.002 in index and 0.25 in Abbe number. For element 4, Hoya FD20 gives nD = 1.720011 and νD = 29.2917, which round to the printed 1.7200 and 29.3. Differences are catalog minus printed at the D line.

The catalog curves supply the wavelength dependence for elements 2–4. Element 1 uses an Abbe-number estimate because no catalog row falls within the site's spectral-proxy tolerance. Figure 1 repeats its 1.5170/64.5 pair. The patent supplies no glass names, suppliers, melts, additional line indices or partial-dispersion data. The historical glass identities and detailed chromatic performance remain unknown.

## Focus Mechanism

The model uses fixed spacings at infinity. The patent supplies no focusing mechanism, close-focus distances or focus spacings.

## Aperture, Field and Image Plane

The calculated D-line EFL is 100.003 mm, 0.003 mm above the printed 100 mm. The patent gives no back focus. The image plane is the calculated paraxial infinity plane, 50.30 mm behind the last vertex, with a 29.88 mm first-to-last refracting-surface distance.

The diaphragm is drawn between the components but has no numerical station or diameter. The estimated location divides the 21.08 mm air space into 10.9 mm before the stop and 10.18 mm after it, placing the stop 16.7 mm behind the first vertex. Its calculated radius of 6.085 mm gives a paraxial f-number of 5.60. An exact spherical trace gives an entrance-equivalent f/5.71 and image-space sine f/5.75 for the admitted axial beam. A nominal f/5.6 marginal ray would require a 6.215 mm stop radius at the same station. These differences arise from the estimated iris and the aperture convention; the patent supplies no diameter for comparison.

The front and rear clear semi-diameters are estimated at 9.9 and 7.2 mm, with square rims on both cemented doublets. Figure 2 suggests approximately 9.4 mm front and 5.5 mm rear semi-diameters; the rear aperture remains an estimate larger than the drawing to preserve off-axis light. The smallest computed glass edge thickness is about 0.216 mm, and the minimum axial air clearance between the components is about 17.86 mm. Wide-open geometric transmission is about 99.2% at 5.0°, 96.4% at 6.7°, 92.9% at 8.3° and 85.6% at the illustrative 10.0° limit. The axial beam and the 10.0° chief ray clear every optical rim.

The ±10.0° field is illustrative. The patent’s discussion of a 30.0° field at f/5.6 concerns prior art and does not specify Figure 2’s coverage. Chief rays reach the image plane throughout the modeled field, although some off-axis pupil rays meet the front or rear rims. The calculated chief-ray image height at the illustrative 10.0° limit is about 17.92 mm; this is not a published format or coverage claim. Geometric image-quality calculations depend on these estimated apertures and the computed image plane; the patent supplies no measured MTF for comparison.
