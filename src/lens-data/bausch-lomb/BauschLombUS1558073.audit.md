# BAUSCH & LOMB 188mm f/4.5 (US 1,558,073 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 1,558,073 A, _Objective-lens system_](https://patents.google.com/patent/US1558073A/en), by William F. Bielicke, assigned to Bausch & Lomb Optical Company, Rochester, New York. The application was filed on 18 January 1922, serial number 530,160, and the patent was published on 20 October 1925. Its sole, unnumbered numerical example gives 188 mm f/4.5. The drawing is on PDF page 1; the title, inventor, assignee and filing date are on PDF page 2; the prescription, spectral definitions and claim are on PDF page 3 (printed specification page 2).

Bielicke's [US 1,540,752 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US1540752A/en) is cited only for comparison with the nominal focal length at the G′ line. It describes a different four-element, four-group f/2 design. No source documents a production lens using the prescription of US 1,558,073 A.

## Transcription Check

All seven signed radii, seven axial distances and four sets of D-line index, G′ index and Abbe number agree with the patent page images. Dimensions retain the printed millimetre scale. No numerical transcription mismatch was found. The poorly printed third glass thickness is read as 3.8 mm: its final digit has a closed lower bowl resembling the 8 in the first thickness, 7.8 mm. The second thickness is 3.3 mm and the fourth Abbe number is 55.0.

The diaphragm is explicitly 8.7 mm after R4 and 6.2 mm before R5, dividing a 14.9 mm air space. Its station is therefore 25.2 mm from the first vertex. R6 is the cemented interface between elements III and IV. The figure shows biconvex elements I and IV, biconcave elements II and III, and three air-separated groups. All powered surfaces are spherical.

The index reference is sodium D at 589.3 nm. The printed G′ indices are 1.6226, 1.5763, 1.5763 and 1.6309 for elements I–IV; the printed Abbe definition uses D, F and C. G′ remains distinct from the mercury g line. No historical glass supplier or melt is identified, and the four glass labels remain intentionally unmatched.

## First-Order Check

The D-line paraxial EFL is 188.64 mm, 0.3% above the printed 188 mm. The paraxial f-number is 4.50, matching the printed f/4.5, with an inferred iris radius of 17.975 mm and entrance-pupil radius of 20.960 mm. The image plane lies 166.98 mm after R7 at the computed D-line paraxial back focus. The patent prints no back-focus value. The powered-vertex track is 44.90 mm and the first-vertex-to-image distance is 211.88 mm.

The printed G′ indices give EFL 188.21 mm, 0.1% above the stated focal length, and back focus 166.48 mm. Their closer agreement does not establish why the D-line result differs. Reading the third glass thickness as 3.3 mm would instead give D-line EFL about 188.97 mm, less consistent with the printed glyph and stated focal length.

Exact axial tracing through the assumed iris gives an entrance-equivalent aperture of f/4.55 and entrance radius 20.708 mm. An iris radius of 18.201 mm would admit the exact f/4.5 marginal ray. The model uses the paraxial opening; the patent supplies no physical iris diameter. These geometric estimates establish neither a measured T-stop nor an image-space sine f-number.

The rear-pair index difference is 0.0563, inside the patent's 0.05–0.06 range. Element IV's index of 1.6165 exceeds the text's preferred minimum of 1.615 and the claim's minimum of 1.61. The calculated power ratio of the air-spaced front pair to the rear cemented pair is about 0.171 against the stated 0.16, within the broader stated range of 0.10–0.35.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The physical iris opening, clear radii, back focus, field angle, image circle, close-focus distance, focus travel and f-stop range are unprinted. The iris radius follows paraxial f/4.5 and the image plane follows the computed D-line back focus. The clear semi-diameters are estimates: 23.5 mm at R1/R2, 25 mm at R3, 24 mm at R4/R5 and 21.5 mm at R6/R7. The drawing suggests a common semi-diameter near 22 mm, with axial scale about 15.4 pixels/mm and curvature-based estimates of 14–18 pixels/mm; it does not establish exact manufactured apertures.

At these rims, the minimum glass edge thicknesses are about 1.22 mm for element I and 1.34 mm for element IV. The two air intervals remain open, with minimum clearances of 3.54 and 6.81 mm; surface sag consumes about 34.5% and 54.3% of their axial spaces. At the shared central clear radius of 24 mm, the diaphragm plane clears the adjacent glass by about 1.67 mm in front and 5.14 mm behind. At the assumed iris radius these clearances are about 4.91 and 5.61 mm. No glass crosses the diaphragm plane, and the iris sets the axial aperture. The surfaces remain clear of each other when the estimated radii vary by ±0.1 mm.

The modeled 12.0° half-field is illustrative. Its chief ray clears every rim and reaches image height 40.05 mm at the fixed image plane, while some marginal off-axis rays are vignetted. No image-circle diameter is assigned. A ±2% variation in the assumed iris radius gives entrance-equivalent apertures from about f/4.65 to f/4.47; this sensitivity does not establish manufacturing dimensions.

`focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. No near-focus state or movement law is given. The selectable f-stops are 4.5, 5.6, 8, 11, 16 and 22; `aperture.maxFNumber` is 22, without a published minimum iris opening. The glass dispersion follows the printed D-line indices and Abbe numbers; separate C/F indices, measured partial dispersion, actual spectral curves, measured MTF and complete spectral performance remain unknown.

## Product Identification

The four-element, three-group arrangement is Tessar-type: a positive front singlet, a negative middle singlet and a positive cemented rear doublet. This family resemblance does not identify a production Bausch & Lomb lens using the numerical example. The name retains the patent's 188 mm design scale. No production mount, image format, diaphragm blade count or shape, marketed focal length, marketed aperture or minimum focus distance is assigned.

- The element labels became Element 1–Element 4. The third element's generic cemented-member description became Biconcave Negative, matching its two radius signs and the drawing.
- Individual in-air D-line focal lengths were added: +73.44, −56.75, −71.37 and +48.28 mm. The optical roles identify the positive front collector, negative middle singlet, negative front member of the rear doublet and positive rear member.

Each change has ΔnD = 0 and ΔνD = 0. No catalog glass name is assigned because a historical identity is unestablished and no sufficiently close row is documented.
- The inferred 80.10 mm image-circle value was removed because the patent prints neither a field angle nor a format. The illustrative 12.0° half-field remains.
- The subtitle identifies the patent, sole example, Bausch & Lomb and Bielicke. The specification chips state “4 ELEMENTS / 3 GROUPS”, “ALL SPHERICAL”, “DESIGN F/4.5” and “DESIGN f = 188 mm”, retaining the source's nominal design values.

## Remaining Questions

- The cause of the nominal focal-length discrepancy is unresolved. The third glass thickness remains a difficult printed reading, accepted as 3.8 mm.
- Exact clear radii and their relation to the drawing's common lens height remain uncertain. The illustrative field does not establish a published image circle or fully unvignetted coverage.
- The physical iris diameter is unknown; the present opening gives a real-ray entrance-equivalent f/4.55. The source supplies no minimum iris opening or f-stop range.
- Historical glass suppliers, melts, complete dispersion curves and the wavelength intended by G′ remain unestablished.
- No source confirms a production lens, mount, image format, blade count or shape, marketed specifications, minimum focus distance, focusing mechanism or converter compatibility for this prescription.
