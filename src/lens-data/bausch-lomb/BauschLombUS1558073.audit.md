# BAUSCH & LOMB 188mm f/4.5 (US 1,558,073 Design) — Audit

## Source

US 1,558,073 A names William F. Bielicke as inventor and Bausch & Lomb Optical Company as assignee. The application was filed on 18 January 1922 and the patent was published on 20 October 1925. It contains one unnumbered numerical example, with the drawing on PDF page 1 and the table on PDF page 3.

## Transcription

Two independent readings of the page images agree on all seven signed radii, seven axial distances and four sets of D-line index, G′ index and Abbe number. The poorly printed d3 is accepted as 3.8 mm because its closed lower bowl resembles the 8 in 7.8. The diaphragm split is printed as 8.7 mm after R4 and 6.2 mm before R5; its derived station is 25.2 mm from the first vertex. R6 is cemented.

The D line is taken as sodium at 589.3 nm. The printed G′ indices are 1.6226, 1.5763, 1.5763 and 1.6309 for elements 1–4. They remain separate from the mercury g-line designation. The printed radii, distances and glass values are retained.

## First-order

The D-line EFL is 188.640798 mm and the computed paraxial back focus is 166.977796 mm. The powered-vertex track is 44.9 mm and the first-vertex-to-image distance is 211.877796 mm. The aperture calculation gives f/4.5 and an iris radius of 17.975139 mm.

The printed 188 mm is closer to the G′ result, 188.210679 mm with back focus 166.483031 mm, than the D-line result. The remaining focal-length discrepancy has no established cause and is recorded as unresolved. A 3.3 mm reading of d3 would give EFL about 188.97 mm and is less consistent with both the printed glyph and the stated focus.

Exact axial tracing gives an entrance-equivalent aperture of f/4.554866 at the assumed iris, rather than exact real-ray f/4.5. The latter would require an iris radius of 18.200810 mm. The printed aperture and diaphragm position are retained; neither physical opening is a factory measurement.

The rear-pair index difference is 0.0563, within the stated 0.05–0.06 range, and the rear positive element's index of 1.6165 exceeds the text's 1.615 minimum. The independently computed front-to-rear power ratio is about 0.171 against the stated 0.16, within the broader 0.10–0.35 range.

## Not printed

The patent supplies no back focus, physical iris diameter, clear rims, field angle, image circle, close-focus prescription or f-stop ladder. The final image distance is the D-line paraxial result. The rims are estimates, and the selected 12° half-field is illustrative; no image circle is stored.

The minimum modeled glass edge thicknesses are about 1.221 mm for element 1 and 1.338 mm for element 4. Both air intervals stay open, with minimum clearances of about 3.535 and 6.812 mm and sag-consumption fractions of 0.3453 and 0.5428. At the shared 24 mm central clear radius, the diaphragm plane has about 1.673 mm front and 5.139 mm rear clearance; at the assumed iris opening these become 4.908 and 5.605 mm. No glass crosses the diaphragm plane.

The optical geometry remains valid with the clear radii varied by ±0.1 mm. A ±2% change in assumed iris radius gives real-ray entrance equivalents of approximately f/4.645 to f/4.468. These sensitivities describe the estimates and do not establish manufacturing dimensions.

## Product

The four-element, three-group layout is Tessar-type. No source ties this numerical example to a production Bausch & Lomb lens. Bielicke's US 1,540,752 is a different four-element, four-group f/2 design.

## Corrections

The source prescription has no confirmed numerical transcription error. The incomplete patent citation and assignee name are corrected below. The third element is biconcave negative, and both members of the rear cemented pair belong to C1.

## Remaining questions

Open: the drawing suggests four lenses at a common semi-diameter near 22 mm, while the retained semi-diameters range from 21.5 to 25 mm. Their relation to the drawn optical edges remains to be resolved. The physical iris size, historical glass suppliers, cause of the nominal focal-length residual and production use remain unknown. The 12° field is illustrative.
