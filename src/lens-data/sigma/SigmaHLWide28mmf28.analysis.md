## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [JP S60-181715 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/JPS60181715A/en)

**Filed:** 1984-02-29 (application S59-36317)

**Published:** 1985-09-17

**Inventor:** Takehisa Koyama (小山武久)

**Applicant:** Sigma Corporation (株式会社シグマ)

**Title:** レトロフォーカス型広角レンズ (retrofocus wide-angle lens)

**Example details:** 実施例1; table on printed page 75, Fig. 1 section and Fig. 2 aberrations on printed page 76; d-line indices stated on printed page 74.

JP S60-181715 A describes a retrofocus wide-angle lens for single-lens reflex cameras with an aperture ratio of 1:2.8, a field of 75° or more and a long back focus. Its background notes that lenses of about 75° commonly limited aperture efficiency to 70 % or less, darkening the corners at full aperture; its stated aim (printed page 74) is to hold down coma flare while keeping aperture efficiency at 95 % or more over the whole field. Three numerical examples share one 8-element, 7-group layout; Fig. 2 gives Example 1 a half-field of 37.6° (2ω = 75.2°).

The link to the Sigma NON VIGNETTING 28mm F2.8, released in 1985 and later renamed H.L. (Highlight) WIDE 28mm F2.8, rests on converging evidence:

1. Applicant and timing: Sigma filed in February 1984; the publication and the lens's release both fall in 1985.
2. Construction: Sigma's booklet lists 8 elements in 7 groups; the patent claims a 7-group, 8-element system.
3. Aperture and field: F2.8 and 75° in the booklet; F2.8 and 2ω = 75.2° in Example 1.
4. Design purpose: the booklet presents the lens as the first wide angle free of corner light loss at full aperture, with about twice the light of conventional wide angles wide open; the patent's purpose is aperture efficiency of 95 % or more at every field angle.
5. Focal length: read in millimetres, the table traces to f = 28.34 mm.

Sigma published no optical section for this lens, so the production example cannot be identified. Examples 1 (2ω = 75.2°) and 3 (2ω = 75.4°) both match the catalog's 75°; Example 2 (2ω = 76.8°) is slightly wider. Example 1 is modeled as printed, and the other examples are not mixed into it.

The table prints no focal length, units, back focus or object distance. Read as millimetres at infinity, Example 1 has f = 28.34 mm, a paraxial back focus of 38.85 mm and 87.42 mm from surface 1 to the image. The back focus is derived, not printed.

## Optical Structure

A retrofocus lens: a negative front group of a weak positive element (L1) and two negative menisci convex to the object (L2, L3), then a positive rear group of a positive element strongly convex to the object (L4), a cemented negative doublet (L5–L6), a positive meniscus concave to the object (L7) and a biconvex element (L8). All eight elements are spherical, in seven air-separated groups. The patent's text counts the cemented doublet as one lens (its L5), so its L6 and L7 are L7 and L8 here; Fig. 1 labels the eight elements G1–G8, in the same order as L1–L8. Row 9 of the table is a flat air plane, the aperture stop, 28.60 mm behind surface 1 with 2.00 mm on either side.

The patent explains the layout on printed pages 73–74. In a retrofocus lens the negative front group adds positive coma that grows toward the edge of the field and spreads the outer rays of the sagittal bundle. The usual remedy is to split the strong concave power of the diverging part and strengthen the rear group, but a rear group used for sagittal coma alone leaves excessive negative spherical aberration and distortion. The patent balances the two through the power split set by four conditions, with subscripts in the patent's lens numbering (f5 is the cemented doublet; f4567 is the whole rear group):

| Condition | Range | Example 1 (printed) | Model |
|---|---|---|---|
| (1) \|f123 / f\| | 1.0–1.5 | 1.10 | 1.103 |
| (2) f1 / f | 5.0–8.0 | 5.38 | 5.379 |
| (3) f4 / f4567 | 1.2–1.9 | 1.67 | 1.670 |
| (4) \|f5 / f4567\| | 2.5–4.5 | 3.48 | 3.481 |

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Element focal lengths are thick-lens values in air. The roles below follow the patent's group descriptions and condition explanations; the per-element reading is interpretation.

- L1: positive meniscus, convex to the object; nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.6. Glass: TAF1 (HOYA) equivalent. f = +152.4 mm. Weak positive front element. Condition (2) keeps its focal length between 5 and 8 times f (5.38 here); the patent ties the lower limit to growing negative distortion and sagittal coma.
- L2: negative meniscus, convex to the object; nd = 1.51742, νd = 52.2. Glass: E-CF6 (HOYA) equivalent. f = −38.6 mm. The stronger of the two front negatives.
- L3: negative meniscus, convex to the object; nd = 1.51680, νd = 64.2. Glass: BSC7 (HOYA) equivalent. f = −83.5 mm. Shares the front group's concave power with L2, the split the patent uses against sagittal coma flare. Together L1–L3 have |f123 / f| = 1.10 (condition 1).
- L4: positive meniscus, strongly convex to the object; nd = 1.78472, νd = 25.7. Glass: FD110 (HOYA) equivalent. f = +44.7 mm. A 7.85 mm thick dense flint just ahead of the stop and the first positive lens of the rear group. Condition (3) bounds its power: below the lower limit (a stronger L4) negative spherical aberration grows and the following elements must be stronger; above the upper limit negative distortion and total length grow.
- L5: biconvex; nd = 1.78590, νd = 43.9. Glass: NBFD11 (HOYA) equivalent. f = +17.3 mm. Front element of the cemented doublet behind the stop.
- L6: biconcave; nd = 1.76182, νd = 26.6. Glass: FD140 (HOYA) equivalent. f = −13.7 mm. Cemented to L5 at surface 11; the doublet is negative overall (f = −93.2 mm). Condition (4) sets this negative component of the rear group: stronger, spherical aberration and coma are overcorrected; weaker, the lens grows longer.
- L7: positive meniscus, concave to the object; nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.6. Glass: TAF1 (HOYA) equivalent. f = +46.9 mm. Rear converging element.
- L8: biconvex; nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.6. Glass: TAF1 (HOYA) equivalent. f = +61.5 mm. Last element.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent states that n and ν are d-line values (printed page 74). Every printed pair matches a HOYA catalog row (|Δnd| = 0, |Δνd| ≤ 0.05):

| Glass (HOYA equivalent) | nd | νd | Elements | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TAF1 | 1.77250 | 49.6 | L1, L7, L8 | High-index lanthanum glass for the positive elements outside the doublet |
| E-CF6 | 1.51742 | 52.2 | L2 | Crown flint for the stronger front negative meniscus |
| BSC7 | 1.51680 | 64.2 | L3 | Borosilicate crown for the second front meniscus |
| FD110 | 1.78472 | 25.7 | L4 | Dense flint for the thick positive element before the stop |
| NBFD11 | 1.78590 | 43.9 | L5 | High-index lanthanum flint for the doublet's positive element |
| FD140 | 1.76182 | 26.6 | L6 | Dense flint for the doublet's negative element |

NBFD11 is the only exact catalog match for L5 (OHARA S-LAH51 has νd = 44.2), which points toward a HOYA palette, but Sigma's glass supplier is not documented; the labels are catalog equivalents. The patent prints no partial dispersions or line indices, so colour is traced on the catalog curves of these rows. The low-index glasses sit in the diverging front menisci and the high-index glasses in the converging rear group, where the doublet pairs νd 43.9 with νd 26.6 behind the stop.

## Focusing Mechanism

The patent tabulates the infinity state only and describes no focusing method, so the model is infinity-only. Sigma lists a 0.22 m minimum focus distance and 1:4.5 maximum magnification; how the production lens focuses is not documented.

## Model Scope and Limitations

Semi-diameters and the diaphragm diameter are not printed. The model's semi-diameters are read from Fig. 1 at its axial scale and checked by real-ray trace: L2 and L3 nearly meet at a 12.3 mm rim as drawn, L3's rear face ends at 10.9 mm with a flat land out to that rim, the cemented doublet is square at 10.0 mm, and L6's rear face ends at 9.5 mm where it meets L7. L6's wider drawn flange is not modeled. Fig. 1 draws the strongly curved faces flatter than their printed radii, so where its rim contacts would make the faces cross, the model follows the prescription. The stop radius (6.90 mm) follows from F2.8. Through these rims the model passes about 88 % of the axial geometric transmission at the 37.8° format corner; that is a ray count through estimated rims, not a reproduction of the patent's 95 % aperture-efficiency figure.
