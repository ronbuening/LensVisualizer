# SIGMA 19mm f/2.8 DN | Art — Audit Log

## Source

[JP 2013-029658 A, _Imaging optical system_](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP2013029658A/ja) names Atsushi Yamazaki as inventor and Sigma Corporation as applicant. Application JP 2011-165433 was filed July 28, 2011, with no earlier priority listed, and published February 7, 2013. Numerical Example 4 is on PDF pp. 14–15, the sag equation on p. 11, and the infinity section in Fig. 16 on p. 20.

Product specifications and construction drawings are published for both versions. The announcements describe the aspheres and autofocus drive. [Sigma EX DN](https://www.sigma-global.com/en/lenses/19_28/), [Sigma DN Art](https://www.sigma-global.com/en/lenses/a_19_28/), [Sigma's 2012 announcement](https://press.sigmaphoto.com/corporate/01/sigma-corporation-of-america-announces-prime-lenses-for-mirrorless-interchangeable-lens-cameras-at-ces-2012/), [Sigma France's 2013 announcement](https://www.sigma-photo.fr/content/111-nouveautes-cp-sigma-19mm-f28-dn).

## Transcription Check

All 15 surface rows agree with the page images, including the radii, thicknesses, air spaces, helium d-line glass pairs and the iris at S7. S9 and S14 are cemented interfaces. The sag equation uses 1 + K. The three printed conics have K = 0, and all twelve aspheric coefficients agree with the table. The fixed image plane is 22.2884 mm behind S15 in both printed configurations. The example lists no cover plate.

## Product Identification

Sigma announced the 19mm F2.8 EX DN in January 2012 and the 19mm F2.8 DN | Art in January 2013. Both use a linear motor for inner focus, consistent with the movement of G2 in the patent. [Sigma's 2012 announcement](https://press.sigmaphoto.com/corporate/01/sigma-corporation-of-america-announces-prime-lenses-for-mirrorless-interchangeable-lens-cameras-at-ces-2012/), [Sigma France's 2013 announcement](https://www.sigma-photo.fr/content/111-nouveautes-cp-sigma-19mm-f28-dn).

They share an eight-element, six-group layout with aspheres on L1, L6 and L8 and cemented pairs at L4–L5 and L7–L8. Both versions were sold in Sony E and Micro Four Thirds mounts and cover APS-C. Both are 19 mm, f/2.8–22, with a seven-blade rounded diaphragm and a 0.20 m minimum focus from the image plane at 1:7.4. Sigma gives diagonal fields of 73.5° on Sony E and 59.3° on Micro Four Thirds. [Sigma EX DN](https://www.sigma-global.com/en/lenses/19_28/), [Sigma DN Art](https://www.sigma-global.com/en/lenses/a_19_28/), [Sigma's focus-distance definition](https://www.sigma-global.com/en/shooting-with-sigma/a020-105-28-imp/).

Examples 3 and 4 are equally close to the production drawing. L3 thickness favours Example 4, and L6 thickness favours Example 3. The production drawing places the G2 doublet and meniscus about 1–1.5 mm farther back than either. Examples 1 and 2 have seven optical groups. Example 5 cements L2–L3 and separates the rear pair. Example 6 has a 21.94 mm focal length. Example 4 gives consistent gap labels. Example 3's spacing table calls its variable gaps d9 and d11, while the surface table places them after S7 and S12. [Patent tables](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP2013029658A/ja), [Sigma DN Art construction](https://www.sigma-global.com/en/lenses/a_19_28/).

## Corrections, 2026-11-24

- The modeled aperture expanded from f/4 to the patent's full f/2.88. The physical iris stays fixed through focus, giving a paraxial working f/2.93 at 400 mm versus the printed f/2.94.
- The modeled half-field increased from 8.0° to the printed 37.6°. The narrow estimated rims gave way to the Fig. 16 radii in the table above so the axial beam and corner chief ray clear the lens. The iris radius follows f/2.88 because Fig. 16 gives only an approximate opening.
- The fixed infinity prescription gained the printed 400 mm state and a calculated 800 mm position to preserve focus near the middle of the travel. The same image plane serves all three states.
- The model identifies the shared EX DN and DN Art optical design and uses the Art name. Product specifications give 19 mm, f/2.8, a minimum aperture of f/22, seven rounded blades, both mounts, APS-C coverage and a 0.20 m minimum focus. The model reaches the patent's 0.40 m state. [Sigma EX DN](https://www.sigma-global.com/en/lenses/19_28/), [Sigma DN Art](https://www.sigma-global.com/en/lenses/a_19_28/), [Imaging Resource's full-stop series](https://www.imaging-resource.com/lenses/sigma-19mm-f-2-8-ex-dn/).
- L1–L8 carry their optical shapes, roles and calculated in-air focal lengths. The brackets mark G1–G3 and the two cemented pairs. Surfaces S1, S12 and S15 are marked as aspherical.

The printed glass pairs are retained. Added Hoya labels supply dispersion curves for seven elements. ## Remaining Questions

- The factory prescription, production glass identities and melt data remain unpublished. L6 uses dispersion estimated from its printed nd of 1.77010 and νd of 49.34 because no catalog curve matches that pair.
- The patent supplies no tabulated rim or iris diameters. The 14.20 mm printed image height corresponds to a 28.40 mm diameter, slightly wider than the 28.35 mm APS-C diagonal used for this model.
- The production focus travel from 0.40 m to 0.20 m and the corresponding optical behaviour are undocumented in the patent. Sigma's specifications list no compatible teleconverter. [Sigma DN Art](https://www.sigma-global.com/en/lenses/a_19_28/).
