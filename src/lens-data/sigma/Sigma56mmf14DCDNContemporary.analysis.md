## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP 2020-030383 A
**Application Number:** JP 2018-157378
**Filed:** 2018-08-24
**Published:** 2020-02-27
**Inventor:** Takeshi Asakura
**Applicant:** Sigma Corporation
**Title:** Imaging optical system (結像光学系)
**Example details:** Numerical Example 1, original pp. 15–16; Figure 1, p. 26; asphere conventions, p. 14.

This is an unscaled transcription of the patent example, a **related design**,
not a confirmed factory prescription of the SIGMA 56mm f/1.4 DC DN | Contemporary
(C018). The numbers come from the 31-page published application; the family's
grant, JP 7094550 B2, has not been compared with it.

Sigma's C018 specification and construction drawing give several convergent
criteria, none of them unique to Example 1:

1. Ten elements in six air-separated groups.
2. Two front singletons, a front doublet, a negative focus singleton, a rear
   triplet and a final doublet.
3. One SLD and two aspherical elements. The drawing marks the second element as
   both, which fits double-aspheric L12 with νd 71.68, and marks the last element
   as aspherical, which fits surface 17 on L35.
4. A 50 cm minimum focus and 1:7.4 maximum magnification, against the patent's
   500 mm state (500.0002 mm from d0 plus lens length) at a paraxial 1:7.50.
5. A marketed 56 mm f/1.4 and 28.5° APS-C field, against design 53.95 mm, F/1.46
   and 28.62°. The design focal length is 3.7% below the marketed figure.
6. Sigma's lens lengths plus each mount's flange distance come to about 77.5 mm
   on all seven mounts, against a 74.93 mm Example 1.

All seven examples were compared. Examples 2–5 have nine elements. Example 6 has
ten, but a meniscus L12, a much thicker L34 and an 82.61 mm length that does not
fit the barrel. **Example 7 fits equally well:** it has the same ten-element,
six-group layout, a biconvex L12 and f = 53.70 mm. Measured on Sigma's drawing,
Example 1 is closer at R1, R2 and R6 and Example 7 at R11, R12 and R16. Neither
reproduces the drawing's thicker L32 (about 1.5 mm more) or narrower L11–L12 air
gap (about 1.4 mm less), so the production lens reads as a sibling of these
examples rather than either one. The mount list describes the C018 products; it
does not establish identical prescriptions or camera stacks on every mount. The
28.4 mm image circle is the patent's (Y = 14.20 mm) and exceeds the smaller Micro
Four Thirds and Canon formats.

## Optical Architecture

The source describes positive G1, stop, negative G2 and positive G3 in order.
G1, the stop and G3 are fixed to the image plane in the mechanism description;
only the single negative G2 moves imageward for close focus. The design reduces
the moving optical mass to one element. This is an internal-focusing medium
telephoto architecture, not unit focusing.

G1 spans S1–S7 with published group power f = +43.50 mm. G2 spans S9–S10,
f = −37.32 mm. G3 spans S11–S17, f = +42.42 mm; its triplet C2 (G3a) and doublet
C3 (G3b) have published focal lengths +102.19 and +60.65 mm. The six air-separated
groups of the element count are not these three patent groups. The seventeen lens
surfaces include the plane stop S8.

The source also lists camera-side plane filter F at S18–S19: thickness
2.0000 mm, nd 1.51680, νd 64.20, preceded by 14.5792 mm air and followed by
1.0000 mm air. It is modeled as a rear plate, excluded from the ten photographic
elements and not replaced by air-equivalent thickness. The patent does not
identify a particular commercial camera stack or a filter clear aperture.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Element focal lengths below are isolated thick-element d-line paraxial values
in air. They are not additive powers for cemented assemblies. Role descriptions
are positional optical interpretations, not measured allocations of aberration
correction or assertions about manufacturing technique.

### L11 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.94595, νd = 17.98. Glass: FDS18 (HOYA) equivalent. f = +130.49 mm.

The front positive meniscus has the highest index and lowest Abbe number in G1. It collects the large axial bundle and participates in the positive front group. The patent prints its PgF as 0.6544, which pins the FDS18 row (catalog 0.6546) and lies well above the normal line.

### L12 — Biconvex Positive (Asph)

nd = 1.55332, νd = 71.68. Glass: M-FCD500 (HOYA) equivalent, the SLD position on Sigma's drawing. f = +67.82 mm.

The second, positive biconvex element has two independently specified aspheric faces and the highest Abbe number in the lens. Sigma's drawing marks this position as both SLD and aspherical. The same location and shape occur in Example 7. The weakly curved negative rear radius is kept as printed.

### L13 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.85478, νd = 24.80. Glass: S-NBH56 (OHARA) equivalent. f = -28.31 mm.

This negative meniscus is the front member of doublet C1. It shares cemented S6 with L14 and introduces lower-Abbe material opposite a positive, higher-Abbe partner.

### L14 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.72916, νd = 54.67. Glass: TAC8 (HOYA) equivalent. f = +26.73 mm.

The positive rear member of C1 completes G1. Its printed PgF of 0.5452 lies slightly below the normal line and separates TAC8 (catalog 0.5453) from the same-index S-LAL18 (0.5445). At 10.37 mm it is the thickest element in the lens.

### L21 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.56384, νd = 60.67. Glass: S-BAL41 (OHARA) equivalent. f = -37.32 mm.

The negative singleton is the entire moving focus group G2. It lies after the stop and has a strongly curved rear face. Its imageward travel changes both adjacent air gaps; it is not translated together with the stop or rear group.

### L31 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.64769, νd = 33.84. Glass: E-FD2 (HOYA) equivalent. f = -154.98 mm.

The first, negative member of rear triplet C2 shares S12 with L32. It supplies opposing power within an overall positive cemented group. The patent prints no PgF for it, so its partial dispersion is the catalog equivalent's.

### L32 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.90043, νd = 37.37. Glass: TAFD37 (HOYA) equivalent. f = +17.12 mm.

This positive biconvex central member of C2 has high index and is cemented on both faces to negative members. It has the strongest power of any element in the lens.

### L33 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.59270, νd = 35.44. Glass: FF5 (HOYA) equivalent. f = -21.35 mm.

The final negative member of C2 shares S13 and ends at S14. That concave rear face is the surface behind the patent's condition (8) value of −0.0199 and lowers the Petzval sum (¶0056). The small triplet rim inferred from Figure 1 dominates off-axis clipping and is not a measurement of factory vignetting.

### L34 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.51742, νd = 52.15. Glass: E-CF6 (HOYA) equivalent. f = -35.45 mm.

This thin negative member begins final doublet C3 at S15 and shares S16 with L35. Its 0.80 mm thickness separates this design from the 7.12 mm member of Example 6, but not from Example 7 (0.99 mm).

### L35 — Biconvex Positive (Asph)

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.50. Glass: M-TAF105 (HOYA) equivalent. f = +22.69 mm.

The positive rear member of C3 terminates in aspheric S17, the position Sigma's drawing marks as the second aspherical element. It supplies positive rear power and additional surface-shape freedom near the image-side field bundles. M-TAF105 is a moulding glass, which suits a moulded asphere, but the patent does not state the method.

## Glass Identification and Selection

Each printed nd/νd pair matches a catalog glass and recurs across the patent's
examples, so these are real glass types rather than optimizer values. The labels
are coordinate equivalents: the patent names no supplier or melt. It references
the d line at 587.56 nm and prints PgF for only the three positive G1 elements,
each within 0.0002 of its catalog row. Condition (7) uses the patent's own normal
line, ΔPgF = PgF − 0.6483 + 0.0018 νd. The model holds the same printed PgF against
its normal line, 0.6438 − 0.001682 νd, so those values differ.

Positive/negative cemented combinations provide chromatic-design freedom; no
apochromatic performance is claimed. Dispersion remains an estimate: seven of
the ten partial dispersions are catalog values for equivalent glasses, not
source data, and the filter plate traces on the BSC7 catalog curve, which matches its printed nd and νd.

## Focus Mechanism

The negative singleton G2 travels 5.5189 mm imageward. Printed d10 decreases by
5.5188 mm, a 0.0001 mm rounding difference although G3 is fixed. Both printed
states are kept as printed.

| Quantity                     | Infinity | 500 mm state |
| ---------------------------- | -------: | -----------: |
| d8: stop to G2 (mm)          |   2.7902 |       8.3091 |
| d10: G2 to G3 (mm)           |  11.3404 |       5.8216 |
| First surface to image (mm)  |  74.9278 |      74.9279 |
| Object to first surface (mm) | infinity |     425.0723 |
| Object to image (mm)         | infinity |     500.0002 |
| Printed EFL (mm)             |    53.95 |        52.16 |
| Printed F-number             |     1.46 |         1.62 |
| Model working f/#            |     1.53 |         1.70 |

The 500 mm state uses the printed d0 from the first surface, which sets the
object 500.0002 mm from the image plane. The printed spacings leave a paraxial
focus residual of about 0.0002 mm at both states, and the image plane is not
moved to remove it.

Sigma's focus cam is not published. A straight line between the two printed
states would miss focus by 0.48 mm at mid travel, so the model uses 65 focus
positions that keep both printed states and hold each intermediate object in
focus within 0.0003 mm. The 63 positions between the printed states are inferred.
MTF is available at the two printed states only, and distance labels close to
infinity, above the first inferred position at 32 m, are approximate.

## Aspherical Surfaces

The original equation uses the standard (1+K) conic convention and even powers.
K = 0 is explicitly printed on all three aspheres; it is not an omitted default.
A4 through A12 are preserved at the original scale, including every A12 term.

| Surface | Element face |           K |           A4 |           A6 |          A8 |          A10 |         A12 |
| ------- | ------------ | ----------: | -----------: | -----------: | ----------: | -----------: | ----------: |
| S3      | L12 front    | 0.00000E+00 | -2.34453E-06 | -5.31720E-09 | 1.20622E-11 | -2.01173E-14 | 1.16443E-17 |
| S4      | L12 rear     | 0.00000E+00 |  3.09778E-06 | -7.37705E-09 | 2.61383E-11 | -5.23565E-14 | 4.15742E-17 |
| S17     | L35 rear     | 0.00000E+00 |  7.09747E-06 | -1.64875E-08 | 3.00160E-10 | -2.16479E-12 | 6.45973E-15 |

S3 has negative quartic departure; S4 and S17 have positive quartic departure.
Higher terms alter the outer zone, so A4 sign alone does not quantify the net
surface departure or an aberration budget. The coefficients are unscaled, and the
patent does not state how the aspheres are made.

## Aperture, Rim and Field Qualifications

The patent's tables give no clear semi-diameters. The rims were read from
Figure 1, whose arcs fit the printed radii at 7.62 px/mm along the axis and
7.57 px/mm across it, and rounded to 0.1 mm; about 0.2 mm of reading uncertainty
remains, and the mechanical boundaries are unknown. Elements use square rims, and
the concave rear faces of L21 and L11 end in flat lands where Figure 1 ends the
curves, at 11.08 mm and 18.4 mm. L21's land lies outside the wide-open beams, while
L11's is a real aperture that trims them off axis. Figure 1 also ends L33's rear
curve at about 8.9 mm, but the model carries its 10.3 mm rim without a land, because
that height would lower the corner by a further 8–11 points. Sigma's drawing shows
larger triplet rims, about 11.2 mm on L31 and L32 and 12.3 mm on L33 against
10.3 mm, and L14 stepped down to about 15.6 mm against 17.3 mm, so the model's
vignetting is not a production measurement.

The model sizes every stop by one rule: a paraxial entrance pupil of EFL / F, here
36.95 mm at F/1.46, which gives a 12.14 mm stop radius. The printed F-numbers
follow the real marginal ray instead, and this design has strong pupil aberration.
At the full 18.48 mm entrance height the real ray reaches the stop plane at 12.81 mm,
where the paraxial ray reaches 12.14 mm. A 12.81 mm stop gives working values of
1.460 and 1.615 against the printed 1.46 and 1.62, and Figure 1 draws about that
radius. At the model's stop, the working f/# (1 / (2 n′ sin θ′) of the real marginal
ray) is 1.53 at infinity and 1.70 at the 500 mm state, and the paraxial working
values are 1.46 and 1.63. Wide open, the model therefore passes a beam about 5%
narrower than the printed F-number implies. This is a property of the model's stop
rule, not of the prescription, and the design F-number stays 1.46. The manufacturer's
nine-blade count is kept, but MTF uses a circular iris.

The chief ray to Y = 14.20 mm leaves at 14.31° at infinity, matching the printed
2ω of 28.62°. At the 500 mm state its launch slope gives a full angle of 25.05°,
matching the print, while the angle from the object point to the first vertex is
26.56°. These are different definitions and are not interchangeable. Wide open the
model passes 48.3% of the beam at the 14.3° corner at infinity and 72.2% at the
12.5° corner at the 500 mm state, limited mainly by the inferred rims of L11 and
triplet C2.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The model reproduces the printed focal lengths, 53.948 and 52.160 mm, and the five
printed group focal lengths. Its results describe this patent example at inferred
rims, not a production lens.

At the model's wide-open stop on the authored image plane, reference-line
diffraction MTF at 40 lp/mm is S/T 0.92/0.92 on axis, 0.32/0.88 at half field and
0.37/0.35 at the edge at infinity. At the 500 mm state it falls to 0.23/0.23 on
axis, 0.07/0.31 at half field and 0.20/0.15 at the edge, with a photopic edge of
0.22/0.03. A best-axial focus shift of −0.088 mm lifts the 500 mm axis to 0.58 but
lowers the reference edge to 0.01/0.01, and it is not applied. The weak 500 mm
result follows from the printed spacings and is not a quality measure of the product.

The model's +3.2% corner pincushion is close to the roughly 2.8% Photozone reports
for uncorrected files. Sigma notes camera corrections for distortion and peripheral
illumination, so corrected product results cannot validate this simulation.
