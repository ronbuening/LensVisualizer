# SIGMA APO 135-400mm f/4.5-5.6 ASPHERICAL RF — Audit Log

## Source

[JP H10-039215 A, Rear-focus telephoto zoom lens](https://patents.google.com/patent/JPH1039215A/en) (特開平10-39215), applicant Sigma Corporation (株式会社シグマ), inventor 上村 豊. The application, JP H08-207594, was filed on 19 July 1996 and published on 13 February 1998. Example 2 (数値実施例2) is on printed pages 5–6: Table 3 gives the surfaces and the variable spacings at three infinity settings, and Table 4 the asphere. Figure 2 on page 8 shows the lens section and group motions. Figures 10–15 give the Example 2 aberrations at infinity and at 1:40 for the wide, middle and telephoto settings.

Sigma’s September 2004 catalogues give the product name, the construction drawing with its SLD and aspherical markings, the description of a five-group zoom with rear focusing, the mount list and the production specifications. Impress Watch reports the specifications of the 2005 DG version. [Sigma Japanese catalogue, p. 8](https://w5.fuji.com.tw/pdf/SIGMA-lens-Japanese.pdf#page=8) · [Sigma English catalogue, p. 12](https://w5.fuji.com.tw/pdf/SIGMA-lens-English.pdf#page=12) · [Impress Watch](https://dc.watch.impress.co.jp/cda/lens/2005/11/11/2684.html)

## Transcription Check

All 26 rows of Table 3 were reread against the page images. The radii, axial thicknesses, d-line indices and Abbe numbers agree, at the patent’s millimetre scale. Surface 19 is the iris. Surfaces 7–9 and 14–16 form the two glass doublets. Surfaces 20–22 form the hybrid element, with 0.10 mm of resin ahead of the glass substrate. The index and Abbe number of each medium are kept separately. The table ends at surface 26; it prints no cover plate and no back focus.

Table 3 prints five variable spacings at each setting, all kept:

| Infinity setting | d6 (mm) | d9 (mm) | d13 (mm) | d18 (mm) | d19 (mm) |
| ---------------- | ------: | ------: | -------: | -------: | -------: |
| Wide             |    3.30 |   56.98 |     1.50 |    16.47 |    22.39 |
| Middle           |   52.01 |   28.99 |    12.54 |    11.33 |    11.98 |
| Telephoto        |   90.02 |   12.24 |    24.47 |     1.50 |     4.10 |

The patent’s sag formula places its conic factor A inside the square root as A(y/r)², so the printed A = 42.9 is the conic constant K = 41.9. Table 4 gives A₄ = 8.697 × 10⁻⁶, A₆ = 3.938 × 10⁻⁹, A₈ = −1.993 × 10⁻¹¹ and A₁₀ = 0 on surface 20, the exposed resin face immediately behind the iris; the model labels it 20A. Figure 2 shows the wide setting, and every drawn vertex lies within 3 px of the prescription’s positions at the figure’s scale. No printed value disagrees with the figure or the tables.

The wide and middle focal lengths agree with the printed values to 0.003%. At telephoto the printed spacings give 389.647 mm, 0.26% longer than the printed 388.64 mm; this is the d6 erratum below. The surfaces keep positive edge thickness and stay clear of each other at every setting. Each setting passes the full axial beam, and the chief ray reaches the 21.63 mm frame corner. Sigma’s catalogue section is drawn to the same proportions: its 26 vertices match Example 2 at the wide setting at 2.66 px per mm. The clear radii follow Figure 2, with the front group held inside the filter thread and surfaces 24–25 limited by their air gap.

With the drawn front rims (42.7 and 39.1 mm), the telephoto corner transmission would be 66.1% instead of 57.6%. The smaller front radii limit no axial ray at any setting.

**Iris.** The iris position follows d18 and d19. The short stop mark in Figure 2 is schematic. The paraxial entrance pupil required by each printed f-number gives iris radii of 12.012, 11.944 and 11.949 mm at the wide, middle and telephoto settings. The 30 calculated settings take an f-number interpolated between the printed rows.

**Image plane and zoom motion.** With the printed spacings, paraxial focus lies 185.826, 185.810 and 186.573 mm behind the rear face of L2. Figure 2 draws L2 stationary, and the wide and middle distances confirm it. The mean of those two, 185.818 mm, fixes L2 relative to the image plane at wide, middle and all 30 calculated settings, which run to 386.86 mm. The four rear spacings d9, d13, d18 and d19 interpolate in the same proportion between printed rows, and at each calculated setting d6 brings parallel rays to focus on the image plane. After rounding, the largest focus error at a calculated setting is 0.0001 mm. Linear travel between neighbouring settings leaves at most 0.020 mm of defocus.

At the printed telephoto setting the image gap is the paraxial back focus. From 386.86 mm to that setting the L2-to-image distance grows by 0.755 mm, the only interval in which the model departs from the stationary L2 of Figure 2.

**Focus.** `focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. The patent states that the negative rear group L5 moves toward the image for closer subjects, but prints no finite-focus spacings; Figures 11, 13 and 15 show the aberrations at 1:40 without the L5 travel.

**Glasses.** The patent prints no glass names. Thirteen glass members match catalogue rows within the printed precision; the resin layer, 1.52071 / 51.7, matches none. The table is under Corrections.

## Product Identification

Sigma’s catalogue drawing of the APO 135-400mm F4.5-5.6 ASPHERICAL RF shows the wide setting: three front elements, the stationary doublet close behind them, two air-spaced elements of L3, the L4 doublet and singlet, the iris, and three rear elements. It marks the second and third front elements and the first element of L4 as SLD glass, and the first element behind the iris as aspherical. Example 2 has the same sequence: its three nd 1.497 / νd 81.6 elements sit at those three positions, and its only asphere is the resin face on L5-1. The resin layer and its substrate count as one element, so the 14 media of Table 3 give Sigma’s 13 elements in 11 groups. Sigma describes a five-group zoom with rear focusing, which matches the five groups and the moving L5 of the patent. [Sigma Japanese catalogue, p. 8](https://w5.fuji.com.tw/pdf/SIGMA-lens-Japanese.pdf#page=8)

Example 1 (102.65–389.02 mm) has two aspheres and its iris at surface 11; Example 3 has Example 2’s layout but starts at 154.44 mm. Example 2 is the closest of the three to the production lens. Sigma does not identify the production prescription by example number. [Patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/JPH1039215A/en)

The catalogue specifies 135–400 mm, f/4.5–5.6, an 18.2°–6.2° diagonal field, nine blades, an f/32 minimum aperture, 2.0–2.2 m minimum focus measured from the film plane, 1:5.3 maximum magnification, a 77 mm filter, 88.5 × 183.6 mm and 1,245 g. It lists Sigma, Minolta A, Nikon AF (D type), Pentax and Canon mounts and no APO teleconverter compatibility. Example 2 is 2.9% longer than 135 mm at the short end and 2.8% shorter than 400 mm at the long end as printed, its printed half-fields give 17.8°–6.4°, and its aperture is f/4.63–5.78. The patent’s closest plotted state is 1:40. [Sigma English catalogue, p. 12](https://w5.fuji.com.tw/pdf/SIGMA-lens-English.pdf#page=12)

Sigma released the APO 135-400mm F4.5-5.6 DG on 27 November 2005 in Canon, Sigma and Nikon mounts, with Konica Minolta and Pentax versions then undecided. It has 13 elements in 11 groups, three SLD elements, one aspherical element, rear focusing, 2.0–2.2 m, 1:5.3, a 77 mm filter, 1,245 g and nine blades, with coatings meant to reduce flare and ghosting on digital SLRs. Whether its prescription differs from the ASPHERICAL RF is undocumented. [Impress Watch](https://dc.watch.impress.co.jp/cda/lens/2005/11/11/2684.html)

## Corrections, 2026-11-25

- The field extends from the former ±0.4° central limit to the 35 mm frame corner at every setting: 8.9°, 4.9° and 3.1° half-field at the printed settings, against the printed 8.9°, 4.9° and 3.2°. The iris keeps the printed full aperture, f/4.63, f/5.51 and f/5.78.
- The original clear radii of 36 mm (surfaces 1–6), 20 mm (7–13), 17.5 mm (14–18) and 11.5 mm (20–26) were sized for the narrow central beam. They were replaced by the Figure 2 readings in the table above, with two departures from the drawing: surfaces 1–6 are set to 37 mm, inside the 77 mm filter thread, instead of the drawn 39.1–42.7 mm, and surfaces 24–25 to 11.7 mm, below the drawn 12.45 mm, so that their faces keep an air gap.
- The original 129 zoom settings, 126 of them interpolated between printed rows, let L2 drift up to 7.7 mm from the stationary position that Figure 2 and the printed rows show. They were replaced by the three printed settings and 30 calculated ones that hold L2 185.818 mm from the image plane through 386.86 mm. The printed spacings at the three printed settings are unchanged.
- The image plane at wide and middle moved from each setting’s own paraxial focus to the fixed L2 distance: the image gaps are 50.228 and 82.728 mm, 0.008 mm short of and beyond paraxial focus. At telephoto it stays at the paraxial back focus, 106.013 mm.
- The telephoto d6 stays at the printed 90.02 mm. The resulting 389.647 mm focal length is recorded as the design value in place of the printed 388.64 mm, and the discrepancy is listed as an unresolved erratum.
- `focus.closeFocusM` changed from 10⁹ m to 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. No finite focus is modelled because the patent prints no focus spacings.
- The doublets at surfaces 7–9 and 14–16 and the hybrid element at 20–22 are flagged as cemented pairs C1, C2 and C3, and the five groups are labelled L1–L5. The elements are named by group, L1-1 to L5-3 with the resin layer R1, and carry plain roles and shapes in place of generic names.
- The lens was identified as Sigma’s APO 135-400mm F4.5-5.6 ASPHERICAL RF, with a subtitle naming the patent, example, assignee and inventor, and specification chips for the construction, the aspherical and SLD elements, the design focal lengths and rear focusing.
- Product metadata follows Sigma’s 2004 catalogue: 135–400 mm, f/4.5, 2.0 m minimum focus, full-frame 35 mm format, 13 elements in 11 groups, nine blades, and an f-stop scale of f/4.5, 5.6, 8, 11, 16, 22 and 32 with f/32 as the smallest aperture, in place of f/4.63, 5.51, 5.78 and 8. The mounts are Sigma SA, Canon EF, Nikon F, Pentax K and Sony A (Minolta A). The three SLD positions marked in Sigma’s drawing carry that designation.
- Catalogue equivalents give the glass dispersion, keeping the printed nd and νd. The differences are catalogue minus patent; the largest are 0.0000044 in nd and 0.05 in νd. The resin stays unmatched at 1.52071 / 51.7.

## Remaining Questions

- Table 3 may transpose the digits of the telephoto d6. Substituting 90.20 mm gives a focal length of 388.641 mm and places paraxial focus 185.895 mm behind L2, closer to the wide and middle distances. It also moves the telephoto marginal spherical aberration from −0.183 to −0.153 mm, against about −0.14 mm read from Figure 14. No single change fits every check exactly, so the printed 90.02 mm is kept.
- The production clear diameters (Sigma’s section gives proportions, not dimensions), the iris diameters, the zoom cam, the f-numbers between the marked ends and the rear-group positions for close focus are unpublished.
- The original glass suppliers and the resin formulation are unknown.
- The gazette prints the inventor’s name in kanji only. 上村 can be read Uemura or Kamimura; Google Patents gives Kamimura, the reading used here and in Sigma's other patents by the same inventor.
- No readable maker source dates the introduction of the ASPHERICAL RF; Sigma’s catalogue lists it in September 2004. Whether the 2005 DG version keeps the same prescription is undocumented.
