## Transcription

The 26 surface rows in ¶0023 agree with the printed radii, thicknesses, indices and Abbe numbers, at the patent’s millimetre scale. Surface 19 is the iris. Surfaces 7–9 and 14–16 form the two glass doublets. Surfaces 20–22 form the hybrid element, with 0.10 mm of resin before the glass substrate. The d-line index and Abbe number of each medium are preserved separately. The prescription ends at surface 26 without a cover plate.

All fifteen intergroup and iris spacings in Table 3 are preserved. Table 4’s asphere on surface 20 uses A = 42.9, equivalent to K = 41.9. The figure and the surface table agree on its location immediately behind the iris. The model labels this face 20A.

The ratios in ¶0026 give a separate check on the group powers. With the printed 388.64 mm telephoto focal length, the four calculated ratios are 0.1938, 0.1552, 1.7696 and 0.5160, against 0.1938, 0.1555, 1.7697 and 0.5160 in the patent. All satisfy the claimed ranges.

The surfaces have positive edge clearance throughout the zoom. Each setting passes the full axial beam and the chief ray to the 21.63 mm frame corner. Partial shading of the off-axis pupil leaves about 71%, 82% and 60% geometric transmission there at wide, middle and telephoto respectively.

**Iris.** The location of the iris follows d18 and d19. The short stop mark in Figure 2 is schematic. The paraxial entrance pupil required by each design f-number gives physical stop radii of 12.012, 11.944 and 11.949 mm at the printed settings.

**Film plane and zoom motion.** With the printed spacings, paraxial focus is 185.8260, 185.8105 and 186.5730 mm behind the rear face of L2. The mean of the first two distances, 185.8182 mm, fixes L2 relative to the film at wide, middle and all 30 calculated settings. The image distance follows by subtracting the L2-to-last-vertex track. The four rear spacings and the nominal f-number interpolate in the same proportion between printed rows. At each intermediate setting d6 places paraxial infinity focus on the film. After rounding, the largest focus error at a calculated setting is 0.00013 mm. Linear travel between settings gives a maximum error of 0.01956 mm.

At the printed telephoto setting the image gap is the paraxial back focus. From 386.86 mm to that endpoint the L2-to-film distance increases by 0.755 mm. This is the sole interval in which the model departs from the stationary L2 drawn in Figure 2. The infinity spacings are the only ones tabulated. Figures 11, 13 and 15 describe finite focus at 1:40 without giving L5 travel.

## Product Identification

The original Sigma catalogue and Figure 2 agree on the five-group zoom, rear focus, the two glass doublets, the three SLD positions and the asphere on the first rear-group element. The separately specified resin accounts for the extra refracting medium in a construction of 13 elements in 11 groups. Example 1 has a different stop location and two aspheres. Example 3 starts at 154.44 mm. The evidence strongly favours Example 2 for the pre-DG lens. The catalogue specifies 135–400 mm at f/4.5–5.6, nine blades, f/32 at the wide end, 2.0–2.2 m closest focus and 1:5.3 maximum magnification. [Sigma catalogue](https://w5.fuji.com.tw/pdf/SIGMA-lens-English.pdf)

## Corrections

### 2026-11-25

The surface prescription and all three printed spacing rows are reproduced as printed. The design range uses the focal lengths calculated from those radii and spacings. At telephoto the 389.65 mm result exceeds the printed 388.64 mm by 0.26%. The catalogue’s 400 mm marking is the production focal length.

## Remaining Questions

- Table 3 may transpose the digits of the telephoto d6. Substituting 90.20 mm gives a focal length of 388.641 mm and places paraxial focus 185.895 mm behind L2, closer to the wide and middle distances. The printed 90.02 mm is retained in the absence of a correction from Sigma.
- The actual clear diameters inside the 77 mm filter thread, the iris diameters, the zoom cam and the rear-group positions for close focus are unpublished.
- The original glass suppliers, the resin formulation and the inventor’s preferred romanization are unconfirmed. The Japanese gazette gives the name in kanji only.
