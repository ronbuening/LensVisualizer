## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP 2004-061605 A  
**Filed:** 25 July 2002  
**Published:** 26 February 2004  
**Inventor:** Tomoki Kono  
**Applicant:** Sigma Corporation  
**Embodiment analyzed:** Numerical Example 1, Figure 1, paragraphs 0015–0020.

Example 1 is probably the original non-DG SIGMA APO 80–400mm F4.5–5.6 EX OS announced at Photokina 2002, about two months after filing. Its 20 elements in 14 groups match the published construction of that lens; Examples 2 and 3 have 19 elements in 13 groups, and JP 2003-241098 A is a separate design. Identity with the later DG version is not asserted.

The patent labels the focal lengths 81.24, 180.52 and 389.46 mm; the prescription gives 81.237, 180.517 and 389.461 mm, and no scaling to the marketed 80–400 mm is applied. The printed F-numbers are f/4.46 and f/5.83 at the ends; the middle station uses f/5.3 from the Fig. 3 aberration-graph label, which is given to one decimal only.

## Optical Architecture

Seven groups follow positive, positive, negative, positive, negative, positive, negative power order. G1 has two singlets, G2 one singlet, G3 two cemented doublets plus a negative singlet (split, as in Figure 1, into G3A, the front doublet, and G3B, the rest), G4 a positive singlet plus a cemented doublet, G5 a doublet, G6 a singlet/doublet/singlet, and G7 a singlet plus doublet. The stop (row 20) sits 3.228 mm ahead of surface 21.

From wide to telephoto, the printed spacings move G1 by 92.5 mm, G2 by 76.5 mm, G4 by 27.2 mm, G5 by 19.3 mm, G6 by 26.4 mm and G7 by 35.0 mm toward the object, while G3 moves 13.2 mm toward the image; the arrows in Figure 1 show the same directions. Claim 1 and paragraph 0005 list G1 and G3–G7 as the groups that move toward the object, which the printed spacings and the figure contradict for G2 and G3. In the model's solved intermediate stations G6 turns back toward the image by about 0.1 mm above about 370 mm. The rear part of G3, G3B (L6–L8), is the stabilizing group: it shifts across the axis to correct image shake. The patent also tabulates a 0.5 mm shifted state, but the model is centered and does not simulate the shift. G7 moves along the axis for rear focusing.

Front element of G1, a weak flint meniscus convex to the object that pairs with the SLD element L2 behind a 1.23 mm air space to correct the front group's color.

### L2 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6. Glass: FCD1 (Hoya equivalent). Isolated-in-air focal length ≈ 212.319 mm.

Positive SLD element of G1; it carries the group's power with low dispersion.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The HOYA names are catalog equivalents that match the printed nd / νd and supply dispersion; they do not identify the actual supplier or melt. The patent's one-decimal Abbe numbers are kept. The three 1.497 / 81.6 elements (L2, L3, L9) are FCD1-class glass and sit where published reviews place the lens's three SLD elements (two front, one rear); that identity is inferred, since the patent does not name the glasses. Sigma's "APO" designation is a product name and is not evaluated from partial dispersion here. The example is all spherical.

## Zoom, Focus and Stabilization

The patent prints the six variable gaps and the back focus at 81.24, 180.52 and 389.46 mm, at infinity and at 3000 mm; those three zoom stations are marked published. The model adds 38 intermediate stations: the G4 and G6 positions are solved paraxially to hold focus, and the other gaps are interpolated. To focus from infinity to 3000 mm, G7 moves toward the image by 1.02, 3.91 and 12.32 mm at the three printed focal lengths; the intermediate focus positions are derived. These paths are reconstructions, not Sigma's cam data.

The patent calls 3000 mm a shooting distance without naming its reference plane. With the printed rows, the model puts the object 3000.57, 3000.23 and 3000.26 mm from the image plane (2768.0, 2719.3 and 2675.2 mm ahead of the first surface), so the 3000 mm is read as object-to-image distance, within the rounding of the printed back focus. The focus control's 3 m end is this patent state, not the lens's 1.8 m minimum focus distance. No finite-distance MTF reference is given, because the reference plane is inferred rather than stated.

The patent also tabulates aberrations with G3B shifted by 0.5 mm. The model keeps G3B centered and does not reproduce those shifted states.

## Aperture and Clear Apertures

The stop radius is calculated from the printed f-number at each zoom station. Clear radii are estimated from the wide-end Figure 1 (element widths measured at about 0.18 mm per pixel; the L5 rear face and the L7 rear face end where the drawing shows a chamfer and a flat step), checked against the axial marginal ray and the corner chief ray, and kept free of negative edge thickness and overlapping elements. The full-frame corner chief ray reaches the image at every zoom station, but the corner bundle is partly vignetted, most strongly by the front rims at the wide end. Some near-telephoto rays also clip at the stop or at the inferred rims; these are physical clips and are left in place rather than hidden by widening rims or changing the prescription. With the printed back focus, which is rounded to two decimals, the traced image-plane residual stays below 0.048 mm.
