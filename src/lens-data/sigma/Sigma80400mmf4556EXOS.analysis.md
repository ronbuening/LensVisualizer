## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP 2004-061605 A  
**Filed:** 25 July 2002  
**Published:** 26 February 2004  
**Inventor:** Tomoki Kono  
**Applicant:** Sigma Corporation  
**Embodiment analyzed:** Numerical Example 1, Figure 1, paragraphs 0015–0020.

This is a probable reconstruction of the original non-DG Sigma 80–400mm f/4.5–5.6 EX OS. The original manufacturer section and 20-element/14-component structure distinguish Example 1 from the two 19/13 alternatives and the separate JP2003241098A design. Later DG coating/version equivalence is not asserted.

Source focal labels are 81.24, 180.52 and 389.46 mm. Literal first-order values are approximately 81.2366, 180.5172 and 389.4614 mm. No scaling to marketing endpoints is applied. Aperture endpoints are source f/4.46 and f/5.83; the middle station uses f/5.3 from the published aberration-graph label. The latter is only one-decimal source precision.

## Optical Architecture

Seven groups follow positive, positive, negative, positive, negative, positive, negative power order. G1 has two singlets, G2 one singlet, G3 two cemented doublets plus a negative singlet, G4 a positive singlet plus a cemented doublet, G5 a doublet, G6 a singlet/doublet/singlet, and G7 a singlet plus doublet. Stop20 sits 3.228 mm ahead of surface21.

The rear portion of G3 is the stabilization unit. The source also tabulates a 0.5 mm displaced state, but the delivered model is centered and does not simulate lateral OS movement. G7 performs rear focusing.

## Glass Identification and Selection

Public catalog equivalents provide wavelength dispersion where their coordinates match. They do not establish actual supplier or melt identity. The source's one-decimal Abbe values remain unchanged. No partial-dispersion/APO claim is inferred from marketing or Abbe number alone. No aspheric coefficients are published for this example.

## Zoom, Focus and Stabilization

All six published gap rows are retained. Forty-one zoom stations include a calculated intermediate G4/G6 compensation cam; the other group coordinates follow source interpolation. Only the original three zoom stations are marked published. G7 movement is derived between the infinity and printed 3000 mm states, with nine focus keyframes. These trajectories are reconstructions, not factory mechanics.

The source calls 3000 mm a shooting distance without explicitly naming its reference plane in the table. Native first-order calculations put the conjugate approximately 3002.17, 2999.12 and 3000.08 mm from the image plane, supporting the image-plane interpretation within rounded BF precision. The control uses 3 m with that inference disclosed. No source-certified finite-conjugate MTF metadata is authored, because the reference is interpreted rather than explicitly certified. Native near endpoints are not replaced by the derived solutions.

The 0.5 mm OS-decenter table is retained in the original evidence only. A centered sequential prescription cannot claim validation of those displaced states.

## Aperture, Clear Apertures and Validation

The stop is calculated from the source f-number schedule. Clear radii are inferred from Figure 1 and constrained by positive glass edges and normal nonoverlap checks. The 0.90 gap-sag rule is unchanged. A central display fan uses 0.3 of the engine-computed field; this does not certify an unvignetted full entrance pupil.

Some sampled near-telephoto rays clip at the iris or inferred lens rims. These physical clips are retained in the validation output, not counted as successful rays or hidden by changing printed prescription values. Full-frame corner-chief transmission and complete pupil transmission are different tests. The sampled image-plane residual is below 0.048 mm, with source-rounded BF retained.
