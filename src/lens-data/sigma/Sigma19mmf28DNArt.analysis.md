# SIGMA 19mm f/2.8 DN | Art

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP 2013-029658 A\
**Application Number:** JP 2011-165433\
**Filed:** July 28, 2011\
**Published:** February 7, 2013\
**Inventors:** Atsushi Yamazaki\
**Assignee:** Sigma Corporation\
**Title:** 結像光学系 (Imaging optical system)\
**Example details:** 数値実施例4, PDF pp. 14–15. Figure 16 shows the lens section at infinity, and Figures 17–20 show aberrations at infinity and at a 400 mm shooting distance.

Sigma announced the 19mm F2.8 EX DN in January 2012 and announced the 19mm F2.8 DN | Art in January 2013. The Art version retained the optics. Sigma's construction drawings for the two versions show the same section. [Sigma's EX DN announcement](https://www.dpreview.com/articles/4688001151/sigmadigitalneo/), [Sigma's Art announcement](https://www.sigma-photo.fr/content/111-nouveautes-cp-sigma-19mm-f28-dn), [Sigma's EX DN drawing](https://www.sigma-global.com/lenses/19_28_specification_01_01.jpg), [Sigma's Art drawing](https://www.sigma-global.com/lenses/a_19_28_specification_01_01.jpg).

1. **Construction.** Both drawings show eight elements in six groups, with three single elements ahead of the iris, a cemented pair and a singlet behind it, and a cemented rear pair. Examples 3 and 4 share this layout and place the cemented interfaces between elements 4 and 5 and between elements 7 and 8. Examples 1 and 2 have seven optical groups, and Example 5 cements elements 2 and 3 and separates the rear pair.
2. **Aspheres and focus.** The drawings mark elements 1, 6 and 8 as aspherical, matching Examples 3 and 4. Sigma describes three glass-moulded aspherical elements and internal focus driven by a linear motor. The patent moves the doublet immediately behind the iris together with the following aspherical meniscus. Sigma's drawing shows the same two parts, and the announcement confirms internal focusing. The drawing alone gives no travel dimensions. [Sigma construction and specifications](https://www.sigma-global.com/en/lenses/a_19_28/), [Sigma's January 2013 press release](https://www.sigma-foto.de/fileadmin/content/products/lenses/19mm_F2.8_DN/PR_DE_A_19_28_DN.pdf).
3. **Focal length and field.** Example 4 gives 18.91 mm, f/2.88 and a 75.2° full field at an image height of 14.20 mm. Sigma specifies 19 mm, f/2.8 and a diagonal field of 73.5° on Sony E and 59.3° on Micro Four Thirds. Example 6 gives 21.94 mm. [Sigma specifications](https://www.sigma-global.com/en/lenses/a_19_28/).

The shared construction and field strongly link the patent to this lens. Examples 3 and 4 fit Sigma's drawing about equally. The thickness of element 3 favours Example 4, while that of element 6 favours Example 3. Sigma's drawing places the focusing doublet and the following meniscus roughly 1–1.5 mm farther back than either example. Example 4 supplies the prescription here because its variable-gap labels agree with its surface table. The factory prescription remains unpublished.

## Optical Architecture

The wide-angle design has a negative–positive–negative power distribution across the patent's three groups. These are distinct from the six groups in the construction count, which treats each air-spaced singlet or cemented pair as one group.

| Patent group | Elements | Focal length | Function                                                                                          |
| ------------ | -------- | -----------: | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| L1           | E1–E3    |    −38.05 mm | Fixed diverging front group, with two negative menisci and a positive meniscus ahead of the iris. |
| L2           | E4–E6    |    +15.85 mm | Moving positive group, comprising the cemented doublet L2F and the aspherical meniscus L2R.       |
| L3           | E7–E8    |    −69.78 mm | Fixed rear doublet with net negative power.                                                       |

The negative front group and the positive central group give a back focus longer than the focal length. The patent splits the central power between a +48.55 mm doublet and a +19.63 mm meniscus. This division limits the power required at either component and helps control changes in off-axis aberrations as L2 moves (¶0036–0044).

The patent gives the radii, axial spacings, glass constants, aspheric coefficients, stop position and 22.2884 mm image gap. At infinity the model gives an effective focal length of 18.9085 mm, a paraxial back focus of 22.2886 mm and a total track of 61.3642 mm. Clear semi-diameters are estimated from Fig. 16. The physical iris radius of 4.164 mm follows from the printed f/2.88. At 37.6° the chief ray reaches the printed image height of 14.20 mm to the precision of the table. The axial beam and the APS-C corner chief ray clear the rims at both printed focus positions. At the edge of the infinity field about 75% of the geometrically sampled pupil is transmitted at full aperture.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below describe the individual elements in air. E1–E8 number the elements from object to image.

### E1 — Negative meniscus, aspherical

nd = 1.59201, νd = 67.02. Hoya M-PCD51 equivalent. f = −29.85 mm.

The convex object-side asphere reduces the strength of the negative lens away from the axis and helps control distortion at the wide field (¶0043, ¶0077–0078).

### E2 — Negative meniscus

nd = 1.56732, νd = 42.84. Hoya E-FL6 equivalent. f = −30.85 mm.

The second meniscus is also convex toward the object and shares the diverging power of the fixed front group.

### E3 — Positive meniscus

nd = 1.88300, νd = 40.81. Hoya TAFD30 equivalent. f = +25.89 mm.

The high-index positive meniscus offsets part of the front group's negative power before the beam reaches the stop.

### E4 and E5 — Positive focusing doublet L2F

- E4 is biconcave. nd = 1.62004, νd = 36.30. Hoya E-F2 equivalent. f = −26.95 mm.
- E5 is biconvex. nd = 1.80420, νd = 46.50. Hoya TAF3 equivalent. f = +17.79 mm.

E4 presents a shallow concave face to the iris. Its rear face is cemented to the stronger positive element E5. The pair is net positive and its share of the moving group's power balances the spherical and off-axis aberrations of the rear meniscus (¶0036–0041).

### E6 — Positive meniscus, aspherical

nd = 1.77010, νd = 49.34. Glass unmatched. f = +19.63 mm.

The meniscus forms L2R. Its convex image-side face is aspherical and weakens the positive power away from the axis, limiting the change in astigmatism as the group moves (¶0044, ¶0081–0083).

### E7 and E8 — Negative rear doublet L3

- E7 is biconcave. nd = 1.74077, νd = 27.76. Hoya E-FD13 equivalent. f = −19.24 mm.
- E8 is biconvex and aspherical. nd = 1.59201, νd = 67.02. Hoya M-PCD51 equivalent. f = +29.56 mm.

The dense-flint E7 supplies the stronger negative power. E8 contributes positive power with lower dispersion, and its aspherical exit surface gives a further correction at the rear of the fixed pair (¶0084–0085).

## Focus Mechanism

The patent focuses internally. Group L2 moves 0.5722 mm toward the object from infinity to 400 mm. L1, the stop, L3 and the image plane stay fixed. The spacing before L2 shrinks by the amount that the spacing after it grows.

| Shooting distance, object to image | d7, iris to L2 | d12, L2 to L3 | Basis                         |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------: | ------------: | ----------------------------- |
| Infinity                           |      4.9942 mm |     2.0000 mm | Printed                       |
| 800 mm                             |     4.73283 mm |    2.26137 mm | Calculated for paraxial focus |
| 400 mm                             |      4.4220 mm |     2.5722 mm | Printed                       |

At the closest printed state, the object lies 338.6359 mm ahead of the first vertex. The model gives 18.9466 mm effective focal length, against the printed 18.95 mm, and −0.0554× transverse magnification, about 1:18. The patent lists f/2.94 at this distance. The model keeps the iris fixed and gives a paraxial working f-number of 2.93. The model ends at 400 mm. Sigma specifies 200 mm at 1:7.4 for the production lens. [Sigma specifications](https://www.sigma-global.com/en/lenses/a_19_28/).

## Aspherical Surfaces

The patent's sag equation uses the factor 1 + K under the square root, with even powers of the radial height added to the conic sag (¶0109–0110). All three surfaces have a printed K of zero, so each starts from a spherical base.

| Surface | Location |   K |           A4 |          A6 |           A8 |          A10 |
| ------- | -------- | --: | -----------: | ----------: | -----------: | -----------: |
| 1A      | E1 front |   0 |  3.71314E−05 | 1.26369E−07 | −8.81328E−10 |  9.83774E−12 |
| 12A     | E6 rear  |   0 |  6.03409E−05 | 2.65895E−08 | −1.24719E−10 |  5.67838E−12 |
| 15A     | E8 rear  |   0 | −8.21604E−06 | 8.43206E−08 |  6.20932E−10 | −2.28850E−12 |

The departures on 1A and 12A reduce the outer-zone power of their respective negative and positive elements. On 15A the negative fourth-order term initially steepens the rear face relative to the sphere. The positive higher-order terms dominate toward the rim and reverse the sign of the departure. Sigma identifies all three production aspheres as glass mouldings. [Sigma's January 2013 press release](https://www.sigma-foto.de/fileadmin/content/products/lenses/19mm_F2.8_DN/PR_DE_A_19_28_DN.pdf).
