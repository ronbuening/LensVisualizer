# SIGMA 19mm f/2.8 DN | Art

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP 2013-029658 A\
**Application Number:** JP 2011-165433\
**Filed:** July 28, 2011\
**Published:** February 7, 2013\
**Inventors:** Atsushi Yamazaki\
**Assignee:** Sigma Corporation\
**Title:** 結像光学系 (Imaging optical system)\
**Example details:** 数値実施例4, PDF pp. 14–15. Figure 16 shows the lens section at infinity, and Figures 17–20 show aberrations at infinity and at a 400 mm shooting distance.

Sigma announced the 19mm F2.8 EX DN in January 2012 and the 19mm F2.8 DN | Art in January 2013. The Art retained the optics: Sigma publishes the same construction drawing and specifications for both. Both versions cover APS-C and were sold in Sony E and Micro Four Thirds mounts. [Sigma's EX DN announcement](https://press.sigmaphoto.com/corporate/01/sigma-corporation-of-america-announces-prime-lenses-for-mirrorless-interchangeable-lens-cameras-at-ces-2012/), [Sigma France's Art announcement](https://www.sigma-photo.fr/content/111-nouveautes-cp-sigma-19mm-f28-dn), [Sigma EX DN specifications and construction](https://www.sigma-global.com/en/lenses/19_28/), [Sigma DN Art specifications and construction](https://www.sigma-global.com/en/lenses/a_19_28/).

1. **Construction.** Both Sigma drawings show eight elements in six groups. Examples 3 and 4 share the same layout of three singlets before the iris, a cemented L4–L5 pair and a singlet behind it, and a cemented L7–L8 rear pair. Examples 1 and 2 have seven optical groups. Example 5 cements L2–L3 and separates the rear pair. [Sigma EX DN construction](https://www.sigma-global.com/en/lenses/19_28/), [Sigma DN Art construction](https://www.sigma-global.com/en/lenses/a_19_28/).
2. **Aspheres and focus.** Both drawings mark L1, L6 and L8 as aspherical, matching Examples 3 and 4. Sigma describes three glass-moulded aspheres and a linear motor for internal focus. The patent moves the doublet immediately behind the iris together with the following meniscus. The same two parts appear in Sigma's drawings, which give no travel dimensions. [Sigma DN Art construction](https://www.sigma-global.com/en/lenses/a_19_28/), [Sigma France's Art announcement](https://www.sigma-photo.fr/content/111-nouveautes-cp-sigma-19mm-f28-dn).
3. **Focal length, aperture and field.** Example 4 gives 18.91 mm, f/2.88 and a 75.2° full field at an image height of 14.20 mm. Sigma specifies 19 mm, f/2.8–22, a seven-blade rounded diaphragm, and 73.5° on Sony E and 59.3° on Micro Four Thirds for both versions. Example 6 gives 21.94 mm. [Sigma EX DN specifications](https://www.sigma-global.com/en/lenses/19_28/), [Sigma DN Art specifications](https://www.sigma-global.com/en/lenses/a_19_28/).

The shared construction and field strongly link the patent to the lens. Examples 3 and 4 fit Sigma's drawing about equally. L3 thickness favours Example 4, and L6 thickness favours Example 3. The production drawing places the doublet and meniscus in G2 about 1–1.5 mm farther back than either example. Example 4 supplies this prescription because its variable-gap labels agree with its surface table. The factory prescription remains unpublished. [Sigma DN Art construction](https://www.sigma-global.com/en/lenses/a_19_28/).

## Optical Architecture

The wide-angle design has a negative–positive–negative power distribution across G1–G3, which the patent labels L1–L3. Here L1–L8 number the elements from object to image. The six-group construction count treats each air-spaced singlet or cemented pair as one group.

| Group        | Elements | Focal length | Function                                                                                          |
| ------------ | -------- | -----------: | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| G1           | L1–L3    |    −38.05 mm | Fixed diverging front group, with two negative menisci and a positive meniscus ahead of the iris. |
| G2           | L4–L6    |    +15.85 mm | Moving positive group, comprising the cemented doublet G2F and the aspherical meniscus G2R.       |
| G3           | L7–L8    |    −69.78 mm | Fixed rear doublet with net negative power.                                                       |

The negative front group and the positive central group give a back focus longer than the focal length. The patent splits the central power between a +48.55 mm doublet and a +19.63 mm meniscus. This division limits the power required at either component and helps control changes in off-axis aberrations as G2 moves (¶0036–0044).

The patent prints radii, axial spacings, glass constants, aspheric coefficients, stop position and a 22.2884 mm image gap. At infinity the model gives 18.91 mm EFL, 22.289 mm paraxial back focus and a 61.36 mm total track. Clear radii are estimated from Fig. 16, and the 4.164 mm iris radius follows from the printed f/2.88. As in that figure, the concave rear faces of L1 and L2 and the concave front face of L7 end short of their elements' edges in flat lands. The 37.6° chief ray reaches the printed 14.20 mm image height. The axial beam and the APS-C corner chief ray clear the rims at both printed focus positions. Geometric pupil transmission at the infinity field edge is about 75% wide open.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below describe the individual elements in air.

### L1 — Negative meniscus, aspherical

nd = 1.59201, νd = 67.02. Hoya M-PCD51 equivalent. f = −29.85 mm.

The convex object-side asphere reduces the strength of the negative lens away from the axis and helps control distortion at the wide field (¶0043, ¶0077–0078).

### L2 — Negative meniscus

nd = 1.56732, νd = 42.84. Hoya E-FL6 equivalent. f = −30.85 mm.

The second meniscus is also convex toward the object and shares the diverging power of the fixed front group.

### L3 — Positive meniscus

nd = 1.88300, νd = 40.81. Hoya TAFD30 equivalent. f = +25.89 mm.

The high-index positive meniscus offsets part of the front group's negative power before the beam reaches the stop.

### L4 and L5 — Positive focusing doublet G2F

- L4 is biconcave. nd = 1.62004, νd = 36.30. Hoya E-F2 equivalent. f = −26.95 mm.
- L5 is biconvex. nd = 1.80420, νd = 46.50. Hoya TAF3 equivalent. f = +17.79 mm.

L4 presents a shallow concave face to the iris. Its rear face is cemented to the stronger positive element L5. The pair is net positive and its share of the moving group's power balances the spherical and off-axis aberrations of the rear meniscus (¶0036–0041).

### L6 — Positive meniscus, aspherical

nd = 1.77010, νd = 49.34. Glass unmatched. f = +19.63 mm.

The meniscus forms G2R. Its convex image-side face is aspherical and weakens the positive power away from the axis, limiting the change in astigmatism as the group moves (¶0044, ¶0081–0083).

### L7 and L8 — Negative rear doublet G3

- L7 is biconcave. nd = 1.74077, νd = 27.76. Hoya E-FD13 equivalent. f = −19.24 mm.
- L8 is biconvex and aspherical. nd = 1.59201, νd = 67.02. Hoya M-PCD51 equivalent. f = +29.56 mm.

The dense-flint L7 supplies the stronger negative power. L8 contributes positive power with lower dispersion, and its aspherical exit surface gives a further correction at the rear of the fixed pair (¶0084–0085).

## Focus Mechanism

The patent focuses internally. Group G2 moves 0.5722 mm toward the object from infinity to 400 mm. G1, the stop, G3 and the image plane stay fixed. The spacing before G2 shrinks by the amount that the spacing after it grows.

| Shooting distance, object to image | d7, iris to G2 | d12, G2 to G3 | Basis                         |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------: | ------------: | ----------------------------- |
| Infinity                           |      4.9942 mm |     2.0000 mm | Printed                       |
| 800 mm                             |     4.73283 mm |    2.26137 mm | Calculated for paraxial focus |
| 400 mm                             |      4.4220 mm |     2.5722 mm | Printed                       |

At the closest printed state, the object is 338.636 mm ahead of the first vertex. The model gives 18.95 mm EFL, matching the printed value, and −0.0554× magnification, about 1:18. The fixed iris gives a paraxial working f/2.93 versus the printed f/2.94. The model ends at 400 mm from the image plane. Sigma specifies 200 mm from the image plane at 1:7.4 for both production versions. [Sigma EX DN specifications](https://www.sigma-global.com/en/lenses/19_28/), [Sigma DN Art specifications](https://www.sigma-global.com/en/lenses/a_19_28/), [Sigma's focus-distance definition](https://www.sigma-global.com/en/shooting-with-sigma/a020-105-28-imp/).

## Aspherical Surfaces

The patent's sag equation uses the factor 1 + K under the square root, with even powers of the radial height added to the conic sag (¶0109–0110). All three surfaces have a printed K of zero, so each starts from a spherical base.

| Surface | Location |   K |           A4 |          A6 |           A8 |          A10 |
| ------- | -------- | --: | -----------: | ----------: | -----------: | -----------: |
| 1A      | L1 front |   0 |  3.71314E−05 | 1.26369E−07 | −8.81328E−10 |  9.83774E−12 |
| 12A     | L6 rear  |   0 |  6.03409E−05 | 2.65895E−08 | −1.24719E−10 |  5.67838E−12 |
| 15A     | L8 rear  |   0 | −8.21604E−06 | 8.43206E−08 |  6.20932E−10 | −2.28850E−12 |

The departures on 1A and 12A reduce the outer-zone power of their respective negative and positive elements. On 15A the negative fourth-order term initially steepens the rear face relative to the sphere. The positive higher-order terms dominate toward the rim and reverse the sign of the departure. Sigma identifies all three production aspheres as glass mouldings. [Sigma's 2012 EX DN announcement](https://press.sigmaphoto.com/corporate/01/sigma-corporation-of-america-announces-prime-lenses-for-mirrorless-interchangeable-lens-cameras-at-ces-2012/), [Sigma France's 2013 Art announcement](https://www.sigma-photo.fr/content/111-nouveautes-cp-sigma-19mm-f28-dn).
