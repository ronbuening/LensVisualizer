# SIGMA APO 135-400mm f/4.5-5.6 ASPHERICAL RF

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP H10-039215 A, _Rear-focus telephoto zoom lens_\
**Inventors:** Yutaka Kamimura (上村 豊)\
**Assignee:** Sigma Corporation\
**Filed:** 19 July 1996, application JP H08-207594\
**Published:** 13 February 1998\
**Example details:** Example 2 (数値実施例2), ¶0023–0026, Tables 3–4 and Figure 2

Sigma’s 2004 catalogue names the pre-DG lens **APO 135-400mm F4.5-5.6 ASPHERICAL RF**. Its section drawing and Example 2 agree in several details that distinguish it from other telephoto zooms. [Sigma catalogue, lens description and drawing](https://w5.fuji.com.tw/pdf/SIGMA-lens-Japanese.pdf#page=8)

- Three air-spaced front lenses precede a cemented doublet. The second cemented doublet sits in L4, immediately ahead of a positive singlet. These two shared glass interfaces account for 13 glass elements in 11 optical groups.
- Sigma marks the second and third front lenses and the first lens of L4 as special low-dispersion (SLD) glass. All three occupy the same positions in Figure 2 and have the same printed index and Abbe number. The single aspherical element in the catalogue is the first lens behind the iris, where the patent places its thin aspherical layer.
- Sigma describes a five-group zoom with rear focusing. Figure 2 shows the same five moving or stationary assemblies, and the patent identifies the three-element L5 as the focusing group. Example 1 has two aspheres, an iris ahead of L3 and a 102.65 mm short end. Example 3 has a similar layout to Example 2 but starts at 154.44 mm. Example 2 is the strongest match. Sigma does not identify a production prescription by example number. [Patent, Examples 1–3 and Figures 1–3](https://patents.google.com/patent/JPH1039215A/en)

The catalogue lists the lens in Sigma, Minolta A, Nikon AF (D type), Pentax and Canon mounts, without compatibility with Sigma’s APO teleconverters. In November 2005 Sigma released the APO 135-400mm F4.5-5.6 DG, first in Canon, Sigma and Nikon mounts. It keeps 13 elements in 11 groups with three SLD and one aspherical element, rear focusing, 2.0–2.2 m minimum focus, a 77 mm filter and 1,245 g, and adds coatings intended to reduce flare and ghosting on digital SLRs. Neither source says whether the prescription changed. [Sigma catalogue, specifications](https://w5.fuji.com.tw/pdf/SIGMA-lens-English.pdf#page=12) · [Impress Watch, DG announcement](https://dc.watch.impress.co.jp/cda/lens/2005/11/11/2684.html)

## Optical Architecture

The five groups have positive, negative, positive, positive and negative power from front to rear. Their respective focal lengths in air are +200.53, −129.08, +201.38, +99.43 and −60.32 mm. L1–L3 form a positive front assembly. As the zoom opens the gap between L1 and L2, the air space between L4 and L5 closes around the iris. The combined power of L4 and L5 changes from positive at the short end to negative at the long end, giving the lens its telephoto form (¶0006–0011).

These are unscaled patent dimensions. Sigma specifies 135–400 mm, f/4.5–5.6 and a diagonal field of 18.2°–6.2° for 35 mm film. Example 2 is 2.9% longer than the marked focal length at the short end and, as printed, 2.8% shorter at the long end; its f/4.63–5.78 is slightly slower than the marked f/4.5–5.6, and its printed half-fields give 17.8°–6.4° across the diagonal. Each modelled setting covers the 36 × 24 mm frame. The clear radii are estimated from Figure 2 and the iris radius at each setting is calculated from the design f-number. With these, geometric transmission at the corner is approximately 71%, 82% and 60% at the three printed settings. The 77 mm filter diameter limits the front clear radius to an estimated 37 mm. ## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are paraxial values for each member in air. The cemented pairs and the hybrid lens also have a combined power at their shared interfaces.

### L1 — positive front group

- **L1-1, negative meniscus.** nd = 1.83400, νd = 37.3, equivalent to Hoya NBFD10, f = −257.16 mm. This high-index front lens precedes the two lower-index positive elements. Only 0.10 mm of air separates it from L1-2 on axis.
- **L1-2, biconvex positive.** nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6, equivalent to Hoya FCD1, f = +188.44 mm. It supplies the stronger of the two positive powers and occupies the first SLD position.
- **L1-3, positive meniscus.** nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6, equivalent to Hoya FCD1, f = +280.88 mm. The second SLD element completes the front group. Its gently curved rear face borders the principal expanding zoom gap.

### L2 — stationary negative doublet

- **L2-1, biconcave negative.** nd = 1.80610, νd = 33.3, equivalent to Hoya NBFD15, f = −37.87 mm. Its concave front face supplies nearly all of the doublet’s negative power; the cemented interface at surface 8 is almost neutral.
- **L2-2, positive meniscus.** nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.8, equivalent to Hoya FDS90, f = +54.27 mm. This more dispersive positive member partly offsets the negative member’s chromatic power. Its rear face has a radius of 1000 mm.

### L3 — positive middle group

- **L3-1, negative meniscus.** nd = 1.65844, νd = 50.9, equivalent to Hoya BACED5, f = −280.29 mm. The weaker negative member faces the air space that narrows behind L2.
- **L3-2, biconvex positive.** nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.4, equivalent to Hoya FC5, f = +118.74 mm. This lower-dispersion lens gives L3 its net positive power. The space behind it increases from 1.50 to 24.47 mm toward telephoto.

### L4 — positive group ahead of the iris

- **L4-1, biconvex positive.** nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6, equivalent to Hoya FCD1, f = +75.01 mm. The third SLD element supplies the converging member of the cemented pair.
- **L4-2, negative meniscus.** nd = 1.83400, νd = 37.3, equivalent to Hoya NBFD10, f = −81.68 mm. It meets L4-1 at surface 15. The pair has a long positive focal length of +736.65 mm despite the substantial power of each member.
- **L4-3, biconvex positive.** nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.4, equivalent to Hoya FC5, f = +112.08 mm. A 0.20 mm air gap separates it from the doublet. It supplies most of L4’s net convergence.

### L5 — negative rear focusing group

- **L5-1, hybrid negative meniscus.** Its glass substrate has nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.6, equivalent to Hoya TAF1, f = −51.03 mm. The bonded resin R1 has nd = 1.52071, νd = 51.7 and f = −317.61 mm in air. Its composition is unknown. Together they form one production element with f = −43.76 mm and an aspherical front face.
- **L5-2, positive meniscus.** nd = 1.71736, νd = 29.5, equivalent to Ohara S-TIH1, f = +63.99 mm. This dispersive positive member sits between the two negative menisci.
- **L5-3, negative meniscus.** nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.6, equivalent to Hoya TAF1, f = −106.75 mm. Its negative power forms part of the patent’s condition on the power distribution within L5 (¶0013, ¶0026).

## Glass Identification and Colour Correction

The three positive SLD elements have the highest Abbe number in Example 2. In L1, two of them share the positive power opposite a more dispersive negative lens. In L4 the SLD element is cemented directly to a negative lens of the same 1.83400 / 37.3 class as the front meniscus. Sigma attributes secondary-spectrum correction to these three positions. The patent supplies d-line indices and Abbe numbers. The equivalent glasses provide dispersion curves for the model, while the original melts and resin spectrum remain unknown. ## Focus and Zoom Motion

For a closer subject, the negative L5 group moves toward the film (claim 1), which Sigma describes as rear focusing. Figures 11, 13 and 15 show aberrations at 1:40, without the corresponding spacings. The model therefore shows infinity focus only. Sigma lists a minimum focus of 2.0–2.2 m across the zoom, measured from the film plane, and a maximum magnification of 1:5.3, far closer than the patent’s 1:40 plots. The production diaphragm has nine blades and a minimum aperture of f/32. [Sigma catalogue, specifications](https://w5.fuji.com.tw/pdf/SIGMA-lens-English.pdf#page=12)

Table 3 gives five spacings at each of three infinity settings. Figure 2 shows L1, L3, L4 and L5 moving toward the object while L2 stays still. At wide and middle, the rear face of L2 lies 185.826 and 185.810 mm from paraxial focus. The model holds that face 185.818 mm from the film through the calculated 386.86 mm setting. Its 30 intermediate settings are calculated: d9, d13, d18, d19 and the f-number are interpolated, and d6 is solved to focus parallel rays. They estimate travel between the printed settings.

The telephoto row is the exception. Its printed d6 of 90.02 mm gives a focal length 1.01 mm longer than stated and places paraxial focus 0.755 mm farther behind L2. Only the last interval includes that shift. The image gap stays within 0.020 mm of paraxial focus over the full zoom. Sigma’s actual cam profile is unpublished.

## Aspherical Surface

Table 4 gives the asphere on surface 20A, the exposed face of the 0.10 mm layer bonded to L5-1. The patent’s conic factor multiplies the squared ratio of ray height to radius inside the square root. Its value of 42.9 corresponds to the usual conic constant K = 41.9. The base radius is +99.75 mm. With ray height in millimetres, the polynomial terms are A₄ = +8.697 × 10⁻⁶ mm⁻³, A₆ = +3.938 × 10⁻⁹ mm⁻⁵, A₈ = −1.993 × 10⁻¹¹ mm⁻⁷ and A₁₀ = 0. The patent uses this surface in the negative rear group to correct aberrations as focus changes (¶0013).
