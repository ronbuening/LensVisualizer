**Patent:** JP 2016-012034 A
**Application Number:** JP 2014-133470
**Filed:** 2014-06-30
**Published:** 2016-01-21

**Applicant:** Sigma Corporation
**Title:** Optical system (光学系)
**Example details:** 数値実施例1, pp. 14–15; Figure 1, p. 27; conventions ¶0077–0084.

This is an unscaled reconstruction of Numerical Example 1, a **related patent variant**,
not a uniquely identified factory prescription for the SIGMA 24mm f/1.4 DG HSM | Art A015.
The complete 34-page A publication supplies the prescription; no original grant
comparison or A/B numerical parity is claimed. Numerical Example 6 remains a viable
alternative with the same architecture and special-material positions.

The manufacturer-to-patent correlation rests on several convergent but non-unique facts:

1. Both have 15 elements in 11 air-separated components, including two doublets and one triplet.
2. The maker's optical diagram places three FLD-marked elements at L3/L5/L10 and
   four SLD-marked elements at L7/L8/L12/L13, matching the source's respective
   νd = 95.10 and 68.63 positions. These are material-family correlations, not melt identities.
3. Three aspherical surfaces occur on two elements, L2 and L15, matching the maker diagram.
4. Source infinity f = 24.65 mm, F1.47 and 83.38° full field are consistent with the
   marketed 24mm f/1.4 class, but the marketed values are not substituted into the prescription.
5. The source finite configuration is 247.5954 mm object-to-image; the caption says
   248 mm and the manufacturer specifies 250 mm. Their proximity supports a relation,
   not an exact identity or permission to alter the source distances.

The staged catalog scope is the original Sigma SA, Canon EF and Nikon F DSLR versions.
Later mirrorless mount extensions are not separately reconstructed. The DG DN lens is
not this design. Manufacturer filter diameter is not used as a lens clear aperture.

## Optical Architecture

The source describes a large-aperture retrofocus-type wide-angle system with a
front group G1 and positive rear group G2 divided into G2A and G2B (¶0031–0035).
Numerical Example 1 has group focal lengths approximately −129.91, +81.81 and
+42.03 mm, respectively. The first three elements form a negative meniscus front
section; the following positive/cemented components restore convergence.

G1 spans source surfaces S1–S11 (L1–L6) and is fixed relative to the image plane.
G2A spans S12–S16 (L7–L9). G2B includes the explicit S17 stop and S18–S27
(L10–L15). The stop is 4.1343 mm ahead of S18 and moves with G2B.
The groups move independently objectward for close focus; the model does not turn
this into unit focusing. Total first-vertex-to-image distance remains 132.5954 mm.

There are 26 refracting surfaces and one explicit plane stop. No source-listed
cover plate, resin layer, front protective filter or mechanical barrel is added.
Cemented contacts are L5/L6, L8/L9, L10/L11 and L11/L12. The model's 11 physical
components should not be confused with its three motion-group annotations.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The following powers are isolated thick-element d-line paraxial focal lengths in
air, computed from the printed radii, thickness and index. Cemented in-system power
is not the sum of these values. Glass identities are intentionally unresolved.
Element-role descriptions below are optical interpretations of position and power,
not independently measured aberration allocations or manufacturer manufacturing claims.

### L1 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.92119, νd = 23.96. Glass: Unmatched (source index/Abbe only). f = -55.30 mm.

The high-index, strongly dispersive front negative meniscus is convex toward the object. It establishes the negative front power and long-back-focus architecture. Source claim 1 constrains its index and contrast with the higher-Abbe front-group materials.

### L2 — Negative Meniscus (Asph)

nd = 1.58913, νd = 61.25. Glass: Unmatched (source index/Abbe only). f = -143.35 mm.

This second negative meniscus carries aspheric S3 on its object-side face. It participates in the front negative section; its aspheric departure provides a degree of freedom beyond spherical bending for the wide-field ray bundles.

### L3 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.43700, νd = 95.10. Glass: Unmatched (source index/Abbe only). f = -112.39 mm.

This low-index, very-high-Abbe negative meniscus completes the three-element front negative section. Its material placement matches an FLD-marked position in the manufacturer diagram; that correspondence does not identify a supplier or partial-dispersion curve.

### L4 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.90366, νd = 31.32. Glass: Unmatched (source index/Abbe only). f = +72.70 mm.

This high-index positive biconvex element introduces positive power behind the negative front section. Its glass has much lower Abbe number than the adjacent high-Abbe elements, providing contrasting dispersion rather than a demonstrated apochromatic correction.

### L5 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.43700, νd = 95.10. Glass: Unmatched (source index/Abbe only). f = -55.51 mm.

This low-index, very-high-Abbe negative member is cemented to L6 at S10. Together they form doublet D1, completing fixed G1. Its geometric contact and material contrast are source facts; its precise allocation of chromatic correction is inferred.

### L6 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 2.00100, νd = 29.13. Glass: Unmatched (source index/Abbe only). f = +45.35 mm.

The high-index positive member of D1 shares S10 with L5. The large index and dispersion contrast across the cemented interface supplies substantial refractive power in a compact component. No catalog glass name is assigned from the two published constants alone.

### L7 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.59282, νd = 68.63. Glass: Unmatched (source index/Abbe only). f = +48.32 mm.

The positive biconvex element starts moving group G2A. Its moderately low index and high Abbe number match an SLD-marked manufacturer position at the material-family level only.

### L8 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.59282, νd = 68.63. Glass: Unmatched (source index/Abbe only). f = +50.98 mm.

This positive member of doublet D2 uses the same printed index/Abbe pair as L7 and is cemented to L9 at S15. D2 is part of G2A, which has smaller objectward travel than G2B.

### L9 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.64769, νd = 33.84. Glass: Unmatched (source index/Abbe only). f = -31.69 mm.

The negative member of D2 provides opposite power and substantially lower Abbe number than L8. It terminates G2A at S16, before the variable air interval leading to the stop in G2B.

### L10 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.43700, νd = 95.10. Glass: Unmatched (source index/Abbe only). f = +73.23 mm.

This low-index, very-high-Abbe positive meniscus is the first member of triplet T1 behind the stop. It is cemented to L11 at S19. Its high-Abbe material correlates with the third FLD-marked position but does not establish anomalous partial dispersion numerically.

### L11 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.80610, νd = 33.27. Glass: Unmatched (source index/Abbe only). f = -19.04 mm.

The strongly negative central member of T1 lies between positive high-Abbe members L10 and L12. Its two cemented interfaces form a compact combination of opposite powers and contrasting dispersion.

### L12 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.59282, νd = 68.63. Glass: Unmatched (source index/Abbe only). f = +90.12 mm.

The positive rear member of T1 shares S20 with L11 and ends at S21. It uses the same printed material pair as L7/L8/L13. Its inferred clear rim is important to finite on-axis clipping and must not be mistaken for a patent-specified aperture.

### L13 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.59282, νd = 68.63. Glass: Unmatched (source index/Abbe only). f = +38.75 mm.

This positive biconvex element follows T1 in G2B and has the strongest positive isolated power among the four νd68.63 elements. Its inferred rims were increased only to accommodate the mandatory nominal infinity marginal ray, not optimized to improve MTF.

### L14 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.60342, νd = 38.01. Glass: Unmatched (source index/Abbe only). f = -87.72 mm.

This negative biconcave element lies between positive L13 and L15. It supplies opposing rear-group power and bending freedom. The source does not separately quantify its field-curvature or astigmatism contribution.

### L15 — Biconvex Positive (Asph)

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.47. Glass: Unmatched (source index/Abbe only). f = +36.84 mm.

The final positive biconvex element has aspheres on both S26 and S27. Its two independently specified departures provide rear-group correction freedom near the large image-side field bundles. The source does not establish a specific fabrication process or a resin layer.

## Focus Mechanism

The source's two moving subgroups provide floating internal focus (¶0031–0035):
G1 remains fixed, G2A moves 4.2435 mm objectward, and G2B with the stop moves
4.9011 mm objectward. The maker specifies HSM autofocus, but no factory cam or
actuator-to-displacement law is recovered from the numerical prescription.

| Source interval | Infinity (mm) | Native finite (mm) |
|---|---:|---:|
| d11, G1 to G2A | 5.2435 | 1.0000 |
| d16, G2A to stop/G2B | 5.9413 | 5.2837 |
| BF, S27 to native image plane | 38.4500 | 43.3511 |
| First vertex to image | 132.5954 | 132.5954 |
| Object to first vertex | Infinity | 115.0000 |
| Object to image | Infinity | 247.5954 |

Only the native endpoints are source-published. The finite conjugate declaration
uses 247.5954 mm referenced to the image plane, not the rounded 248 mm caption,
retail 250 mm, or the 115 mm front-vertex distance in the wrong reference frame.
The literal finite paraxial conjugate at the printed image plane is 247.633411 mm;
this small discrepancy is retained, not silently attributed to rounding.

The source does not publish intermediate travel. The candidate therefore uses an
**inferred illustrative 65-control path**: independently moving G2A and G2B share
one assumed monotone path parameter while retaining their different endpoint
travels. At each control the scalar solve preserves the linearly interpolated
native image-plane residual, rather than refocusing the source to zero residual.
This is an author inference, not a recovered factory motion ratio or calibrated
continuous object-distance law. The initial two-point linear travel was rejected:
at slider0.5 it misses the implied paraxial image plane by −0.723271 mm.

Across 1,025 samples, the inferred path's maximum absolute paraxial native-plane
residual is 0.00132912 mm. That is an image-plane result, **not an all-focus
object-distance accuracy bound**. The algebraic conjugate has a pole near
slider0.000473904, where the illustrative distance label is about 522.46 m;
below it the paraxial object conjugate is virtual. The sampled 92.9% maximum
relative label error is not a continuous maximum. Near-infinity distance labels
must not support quantitative claims. Use source/patent positions for source
comparison. Intermediate states are not finite-conjugate certified for MTF.

## Aspherical Surfaces

The printed equation uses the standard `(1+K)` conic convention plus even powers
through A12 (¶0077–0084). K = 0 on all three surfaces; it is not the alternative
κ = 1 spherical-base convention. The nonzero S3 A12 term is retained exactly.
All dimensions and coefficients remain at the source scale. No uniform scaling,
coefficient sign correction or omitted high-order term is applied.

| Surface | Element | K | A4 | A6 | A8 | A10 | A12 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| S3 (3A) | L2 front | 0 | 5.04840e−6 | 4.60040e−9 | −5.25920e−12 | 1.99610e−14 | −1.20870e−17 |
| S26 (26A) | L15 front | 0 | −4.73140e−6 | −1.83480e−9 | −2.61740e−12 | 2.04104e−14 | 0 |
| S27 (27A) | L15 rear | 0 | 4.41070e−6 | −1.73090e−9 | 9.75320e−13 | 3.31800e−14 | 0 |

S3 starts with a positive quartic departure, S26 with a negative quartic departure,
and S27 with a positive quartic departure. Higher terms affect the outer zone,
so quartic sign alone does not establish the complete peripheral shape or an
aberration correction budget. The prescription supports two bulk aspheric elements,
not a separate thin composite layer; fabrication method remains unverified.

## Aperture, Clear-Rim and Source-Plane Qualifications

The original does not list clear semi-diameters or an iris radius. Figure 1 was
measured at the native axial scale to seed outer extents. Treating every outline
as a square clear rim failed edge-thickness, rim-slope and cross-gap checks.
The candidate uses explicit **inferred clear-rim adaptations**, preserving the
large front extent while reducing unsupported curved rear and flange extents.
Only the infinity on-axis nominal marginal-ray floors were then rounded upward
by 0.01 mm where needed. No gap-sag or other validator override is used.
The accepted rims are not proven factory apertures, and the inferred finite
clipping must not be presented as measured production vignetting.

The pinned ordinary-prime contract derives a fixed physical iris from the
infinity real-ray nominal F1.47. Its inferred radius is 12.5714239023 mm.
No authored stop radius, fake zoom station or inside-element-stop workaround is
used. The finite table prints F1.60 without identifying its finite convention.
With this fixed iris, an unvignetted exact ray gives working `1/(2 NA)` =
**1.56725433** at the native finite object. The same configuration illuminated
by a collimated beam instead gives EFL/entrance-pupil F1.47675988; these are not
interchangeable quantities. F1.567254 does not round to 1.60, but that alone does
not establish a source error or prove a varying iris.

An independent alternate paraxial-infinity calibration would produce a smaller
fixed iris and near exact working F1.59877. This illustrates convention ambiguity,
not source evidence or permission to tune the pinned contract. It is not adopted.
Hard-rim ray loss is a third, distinct quantity: at the inferred near rims the
full on-axis iris bundle clips at S21; the surviving limiting marginal ray gives
about F1.61778, not an authored iris change or compliance with source F1.60.

The native source planes remain literal. Required paraxial shifts from them are
+0.00117190 mm at infinity and +0.00132912 mm at near. Diagnostic best-axial MTF
shifts are larger and are not corrections applied to BF.

## Verification Summary and Remaining Gates

The recovered source audit contains 236 independently compared fields with no
mismatches. This is a source-transcription result, not model integration approval.
Independent source-only exact Snell tracing reproduces the fixed-iris working-NA
values to better than 1e−10 in F-number. Independent matrix analysis confirms
the source-plane residuals and inferred-path conjugate pole.

The staged candidate builds under pinned runtime77ad7af3543833602509a6ab164fbf6870e12ad2.
Its 1,025-state render diagnostics retain no hidden rim trim. Static SVG-derived
images are not an actual interactive-viewer pass. Source chief rays reach height
21.63 mm: launch half angles are 41.68823° at infinity and 39.70900° at near,
matching source41.69°/39.71°. The near object-to-first-vertex angle is instead
45.85384°; it is a different geometric quantity, not a field-angle disagreement.

The bounded native audit requests 2,440 rays over both endpoints, source21.63 mm
and generic21.65 mm domains, five field fractions, nominal/F8 and 61 iris points.
It records 1,870 passes, 432 hard-rim clips and 138 unlaunched numerical iris-aim
requests. Unlaunched requests are not physical clipping; all failures remain in
the evidence. No complete pupil-coverage or all-field physical pass is claimed.

Reference-wavelength diffraction MTF at the unchanged native image planes converges
for the tested center/mid/edge fields. **Convergence is not optical quality.**
At 40 lp/mm, wide-open infinity center is about0.543; the native finite generic
edge is approximately0.264 sagittal / **0.069 tangential**. Best-axial diagnostics
retain weak edges and do not rescue the native-plane claim. F8 improves some
values but is not a substitute for native aperture evidence. Clear-rim assumptions,
partial-pupil coverage, single-wavelength support and the unresolved finite
F-number convention qualify all numerical performance results. Manufacturer
MTF charts are provenance assets, not a quantitatively calibrated match.
