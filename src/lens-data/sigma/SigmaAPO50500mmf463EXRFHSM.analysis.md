# Sigma APO 50-500mm f/4-6.3 EX RF HSM: Example 1 continuous reconstruction

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP 2001-108902 A  
**Application Number:** JP 11-289192  
**Filed:** October 12, 1999  
**Published:** April 20, 2001  
**Inventor:** Tomoki Kono (幸野 朋来; the original English PAJ cover prints KONO TOMOKI)  
**Assignee:** Sigma Corporation  
**Title:** 高変倍ズームレンズ / High Variable Power Zoom Lens  
**Example details:** Figure 1, all three published infinity stations; 37 numbered rows including stop row 20.

The fixed example has a strong architectural association with the original **APO 50-500mm F4-6.3 EX RF / EX RF HSM**, not a certified factory prescription. Sigma's dated 2004.9 catalog shows 20 elements in 16 air-separated groups and four SLD positions corresponding to L2, L3, L8 and L9. This selects Example 1 rather than Example 2 (20/15) or Example 3 (19/15). The 2004.9 date identifies this catalog edition, not the lens's introduction date.

The maker describes rear focusing and lists nine blades, a wide-end minimum aperture f/22, minimum distance 1–3 m, 1:5.2 maximum reproduction, an 86 mm filter, 95 × 218.5 mm dimensions and 1,840 g. The specification is based on Sigma mount. HSM and non-HSM mount variants are distinguished; the model's HSM association covers Sigma SA, Nikon F and Canon EF, not a silent HSM attribution to the Minolta/Pentax versions. These product specifications do not provide factory radii, clear apertures or a numerical focus law. The later DG/OS generation is outside this model.

The source's design range is 50.1620–483.7741 mm, f/4.15–6.39 and 47–5° full field. Its overview rounds focal length differently. The wide aberration diagram, Figure 4, literally prints f/6.39 despite the wide design table's f/4.15; Figures 5 and 6 print f/6.09 and f/6.39. This is a retained source discrepancy, not a repaired table cell. The Figure 4 aperture is separately evaluated rather than substituted for the wide design aperture.

## Optical Architecture and Source Conditions

The seven functional groups have signs **positive–negative–positive–positive–negative–positive–negative**. G1 contains three singlets; G2 four singlets; G3 one singlet; G4 a cemented doublet; G5 a cemented doublet; G6 two singlets plus a cemented doublet; G7 a cemented doublet plus two singlets. Shared interfaces are r18, r22, r29 and r32. The stop is source row 20, with a literal 4.4600 mm interval before r21. There are 36 refracting faces, all spherical.

The isolated functional-group EFLs are +205.149862, −39.832220, +83.450391, +171.364073, −57.119810, +39.166287 and −50.767561 mm. These are components in air, not powers that can simply be added to obtain system power. The complete source anchors calculate EFL 50.161965595, 200.340798202 and 483.774076512 mm, and Gaussian BFL 41.180532450, 67.806936169 and 80.877364156 mm. The model uses the separately printed image gaps 41.1805, 67.8069 and 80.8774 mm, leaving +0.000032450, +0.000036169 and −0.000035844 mm residuals. No source cell or printed image plane was fitted.

All five design inequalities pass at the published anchors: f1/ft = 0.424061276 lies between 0.3 and 0.5; |f7/ft| = 0.104940634 lies between 0.05 and 0.2; Lt/Lw = 1.260803632 lies between 1 and 1.5; |f4/f5| = 3.000081286 exceeds 1; and the G4 lateral magnifications 0.434735695, 0.233321134 and 0.268508381 have magnitude below 0.7. Lt and Lw are explicitly **stop-to-image distances**, not total front-to-image lengths. The literal ratio differs from the printed 1.2610 more than simple four-decimal rounding would explain; both are retained. The source gives aberration plots rather than a full tabulated Seidel decomposition; the qualitative roles below do not claim numerical reproduction of every aberration coefficient.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The source explicitly defines d-line index and Abbe number in paragraph 0015. Every numerical coordinate is retained. Thick-element focal lengths below are independently calculated **in air**, including an air boundary on the far side of each isolated cemented member. Actual cemented tracing uses the two real adjacent indices instead.

### L1 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.83400, νd = 37.3. Glass: NBFD10 (HOYA coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed). Isolated f = -280.597713 mm.

The high-index negative front meniscus precedes two lower-dispersion positive elements. It moderates the positive first-group power and contributes a contrasting dispersion term; the complete group remains positive.

### L2 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.49700, νd = 79.3. Glass: unmatched; historical melt unconfirmed. Isolated f = +188.535472 mm. Maker-marked SLD position; nearest catalog row K-PFK80 (SUMITA) has Δνd = +2.0504, exceeding the 0.1 coordinate-equivalence tolerance.

This large positive front-group element is one of the maker-marked SLD positions. Its literal νd 79.3 must not be harmonized to a modern ED catalog value merely because nd is close.

### L3 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.49700, νd = 79.3. Glass: unmatched; historical melt unconfirmed. Isolated f = +324.505748 mm. Maker-marked SLD position; nearest catalog row K-PFK80 (SUMITA) has Δνd = +2.0504, exceeding the 0.1 coordinate-equivalence tolerance.

The positive meniscus completes G1 and is the second maker-marked SLD position. Its separated geometry and positive power differ from L2 even though the source repeats the same optical coordinates.

### L4 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.71300, νd = 54.1. Glass: unmatched; historical melt unconfirmed. Isolated f = -74.670675 mm. Nearest rows J-LAK8 (HIKARI) and LAC8 (HOYA) have Δνd = −0.14 and −0.16, outside the 0.1 coordinate-equivalence tolerance.

The negative meniscus begins G2. Its front/rear inferred clear radii are 25.5/19.5 mm: clear cap estimates scaled from Figure 1, not source dimensions. The rear concave cap now ends on an exposed flat land reaching the25.5mm body. The rear rim is constrained by the following short-gap sag geometry; Figure 1 draws r8 and L5's front face meeting at the rim, and the literal prescription makes them touch near 20.6 mm.

### L5 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.69680, νd = 55.6. Glass: S-LAL14 (OHARA coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed). Isolated f = -74.653253 mm.

This biconcave member reinforces the negative variator group. Its moderate dispersion and negative power are balanced by the following positive element rather than being interpreted as an isolated aberration correction.

### L6 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.71736, νd = 29.5. Glass: S-TIH1 (OHARA coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed). Isolated f = +44.759398 mm.

The strong positive member interrupts the negative sequence in G2. Its relatively high dispersion permits a chromatic-balancing interpretation with the negative neighbors, without demonstrating exact cancellation of a Seidel or chromatic coefficient.

### L7 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.7. Glass: TAF1 (HOYA coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed). Isolated f = -51.451513 mm.

The final biconcave singlet restores net-negative G2 power. The large changing D14 separation follows this group and is part of the connected zoom geometry.

### L8 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.49700, νd = 79.3. Glass: unmatched; historical melt unconfirmed. Isolated f = +83.450391 mm. Maker-marked SLD position; nearest catalog row K-PFK80 (SUMITA) has Δνd = +2.0504, exceeding the 0.1 coordinate-equivalence tolerance.

The single positive element constitutes G3 and is the third maker-marked SLD position. Its isolated power is also the functional-group power; the following D16 gap changes with zoom.

### L9 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.49700, νd = 79.3. Glass: unmatched; historical melt unconfirmed. Isolated f = +72.575969 mm. Maker-marked SLD position; nearest catalog row K-PFK80 (SUMITA) has Δνd = +2.0504, exceeding the 0.1 coordinate-equivalence tolerance.

This SLD-marked positive member is cemented to L10. It has appreciable positive isolated power, but the real interface r18 uses the index difference to the high-dispersion negative member.

### L10 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: S-TIH6 (OHARA coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed). Isolated f = -122.553482 mm.

The negative meniscus completes G4. The combination has only modest net positive power; the source links G4 magnification and the G4/G5 power ratio to controlling the iris diameter.

### L11 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.71300, νd = 54.1. Glass: unmatched; historical melt unconfirmed. Isolated f = -22.203956 mm. Nearest rows J-LAK8 (HIKARI) and LAC8 (HOYA) have Δνd = −0.14 and −0.16, outside the 0.1 coordinate-equivalence tolerance.

The strong biconcave member starts negative G5, immediately after the source stop-to-r21 interval. It is cemented to a positive partner at r22.

### L12 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.80610, νd = 33.3. Glass: NBFD15 (HOYA coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed). Isolated f = +36.312340 mm.

The positive meniscus partly offsets L11, while the complete fifth group remains negative. Its high index and source dispersion are retained independently of any modern glass substitute.

### L13 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.2. Glass: S-FSL5 (OHARA coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed). Isolated f = +115.725354 mm.

Despite both radii being negative, the rear curvature is stronger and this isolated meniscus is positive. It starts G6 and is not mislabeled negative from its front-face sign alone.

### L14 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.58144, νd = 40.8. Glass: S-TIL25 (OHARA coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed). Isolated f = +90.027604 mm.

This biconvex singlet adds positive power inside G6. Its dispersion is markedly different from the preceding low-dispersion element; the small intervening air gaps are not replaced by invented cemented interfaces.

### L15 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.2. Glass: S-FSL5 (OHARA coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed). Isolated f = +41.959956 mm.

The stronger positive member begins the rear G6 doublet. Its low-dispersion coordinates repeat L13, but equal coordinates do not prove one historical melt or production batch.

### L16 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: S-TIH6 (OHARA coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed). Isolated f = -52.366158 mm.

The negative meniscus completes that doublet at r29 and moderates G6 power. The nearly flat rear face leads into reconstructed D30, the compensating separation in the adopted continuous model.

### L17 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.75520, νd = 27.5. Glass: S-TIH4 (OHARA coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed). Isolated f = +61.422641 mm.

This positive member starts the negative rear focusing group and is cemented to L18. The doublet's front and cemented faces are inferred at 12.5 mm from Figure 1; L18's rear face stays at 10.7 mm because the following 3.38 mm airspace closes near 11.35 mm.

### L18 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.80610, νd = 40.8. Glass: NBFD13 (HOYA coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed). Isolated f = -23.467857 mm.

The strong biconcave member gives the front G7 doublet net negative action. Its steep rear face makes the following gap and solid boundary particularly important for off-axis containment.

### L19 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.74330, νd = 49.5. Glass: N-LAF35 (SCHOTT coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed). Isolated f = -58.195597 mm.

This biconcave singlet supplies a further negative contribution in G7. The clear radius is independently inferred and the small following air gap remains literal.

### L20 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.64769, νd = 33.8. Glass: S-TIM22 (OHARA coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed). Isolated f = +46.104872 mm.

The final biconvex singlet offsets part of the rear negative power. G7 remains negative overall and moves imageward for close focusing in the source drawing; no exact finite displacement is published.

## Glass Identification

All 20 elements retain the patent's printed d-line index and Abbe number. Catalog coordinate equivalents provide dispersion for 14 elements: HOYA NBFD10 (L1), TAF1 (L7), NBFD15 (L12) and NBFD13 (L18); OHARA S-LAL14 (L5), S-TIH1 (L6), S-TIH6 (L10/L16), S-FSL5 (L13/L15), S-TIL25 (L14), S-TIH4 (L17) and S-TIM22 (L20); and SCHOTT N-LAF35 (L19). These curves support secondary-spectrum and chromatic calculations while tracing retains the printed nd/νd at d; production suppliers and melts remain unconfirmed.

L2/L3/L8/L9 (1.49700/79.3) and L4/L11 (1.71300/54.1) remain unmatched: the nearest catalog rows exceed the 0.1 Abbe-number tolerance. Their dispersion remains an Abbe-based approximation. The diagram tints L2, L3, L8 and L9 as inferred anomalous-dispersion elements: 1.49700/79.3 lies in the fluorophosphate low-dispersion region, about two Abbe units below today's FCD1/S-FPL51-class rows, and these are the positions Sigma's catalog marks SLD; the patent itself names no special glass. The four maker-marked SLD positions do not establish complete apochromatic performance from index and Abbe number alone.

## Zoom, Stop and Focus Reconstruction

The source supplies three infinity stations, not a continuous factory cam. The model uses 65 connected stations sampled from the original 513-node reconstruction, retaining all three source anchors exactly. G1–G6 positions relative to the image plane are interpolated between the source anchors; G7 was solved from its quadratic first-order imaging condition, with one positive-gap compensator root per original node. The separately interpolated printed-plane residual is retained. Samples are approximately logarithmic in EFL, with additional telephoto points. Gaps interpolate linearly between adjacent stations. Maximum absolute image-plane defocus at their midpoints is 0.044276 mm; a denser interval sweep gives 0.044280 mm, compared with 0.000758359 mm at the original cam's midpoints. EFL and the source-directed group motions remain monotone: from wide to tele G1 and G3–G7 move toward the object by 88.08, 42.15, 42.14, 26.54, 37.30 and 39.70 mm (the stop travels with G5), while G2 moves 25.74 mm toward the image, as the patent text and Figure 1's arrows state. Positions are continuous; smooth velocity and the factory trajectory are not established.

For comparison, the original reconstruction's 32, 64, 128, 256 and 512 intervals reduced maximum Gaussian-target interpolation error from 0.189363 mm through 0.049058, 0.012489 and 0.003151 to 0.000791419 mm; the corresponding printed-plane maximum was 0.000755708 mm. A separate uniform sweep gave 0.000794063 mm against the Gaussian target and 0.000758359 mm against the printed plane, with its worst near-tele point at normalized zoom 0.9990234375. These are historical dense-cam results, not the accuracy of the present 65-station interpolation. Solving G2 alone instead of G7 required a branch switch near telephoto and produced multi-millimetre interpolation errors; that alternative was rejected.

The stop's source row and following 4.4600 mm interval are literal, but its diameter is not published. Exact source-anchor iris radii derived from marginal tracing are 10.496817030, 10.511540739 and 10.498609038 mm. Their similarity does not establish a constant factory iris. The model derives the iris schedule from design f-numbers. Retained station f-numbers are unchanged and strictly increase through f/4.15, f/6.09 and f/6.39; they follow the original piecewise interpolation coordinate rather than a newly fitted focal-length law. The pupil-based marginal launch can exceed the physical stop, so its full beam may clip even when an exactly calibrated nominal beam passes.

All clear rims are inferred. Their proportions follow Figure 1 where the literal geometry allows: L4 has25.5/19.5mm front/rear clear caps with a rear flat land to25.5mm; L5 has19.5/22mm clear caps with a front land to22mm; L17/L18 retains its12.5/12.5/10.7mm profile. The three capped faces sit at the short air gaps that Figure 1 draws closing at the rim. Complete spherical caps remain literal. The two exposed annuli follow the source outline, while the remaining rim joins are constrained geometric estimates. The gap-sag allowance remains 0.9.

The patent prints only the infinity state, so the model is shown at infinity only. Figure 1 shows G7 moving imageward to focus, and the maker gives an unpaired 1–3 m minimum-distance range measured from the film surface. Neither supplies numerical focus travel. The 3 m value is product metadata, not a modeled finite conjugate or a factory limit at every zoom. The format requires two focus columns; both retain identical infinity geometry. Finite-distance MTF is unavailable.

## Aspherical Surfaces

None. All 36 selected refracting surfaces are spherical. No unprescribed filter, sensor stack, correction plate or asphere is added.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The original dense cam's geometric study, with the earlier rims, found minimum positive common-rim edge thickness 0.341993257 mm, maximum gap-sag fraction 0.891654460 within the unchanged 0.9 allowance, maximum spherical edge slope 37.042670928° and no hidden outline trimming. The later Figure 1 rims leave the capped r8, r9 and r33 faces and the 37.04° maximum edge slope unchanged, and pass the surface validator without a gap-sag override. The historical52.5° L4 taper angle is superseded by the exposed rear flat land; spherical edge slope remains a distinct quantity. All retained nodes have the same geometry; the current interpolation's paraxial defocus bound is given above.

At wide/middle/tele, pupil-based marginal launch radii are 6.066725108, 16.538690790 and 38.039228195 mm, versus exact nominal radii 6.043610313, 16.448341396 and 37.853996597 mm. Corresponding paraxial pupil f-numbers are 4.134188108, 6.056730873 and 6.358883966; these estimates do not replace the physically admitted source-calibrated f/4.15, f/6.09 and f/6.39 boundaries. Full pupil-based axial beams clip at the iris, while separately calibrated nominal beams pass. Enlarging physical rims would not resolve this distinction.

Earlier complete-solid studies of the dense cam found no wrong-material traversal along transmitted rays or examined failed prefixes. Exterior misses of the first solid or the complete next L4 solid, including its two caps and tapered join, remain misses rather than transmitted paths. The physically admitted chief rays reached the stop within 8.83 × 10⁻¹⁰ mm; clipped chiefs occurred only in interpolated source or stress fields. Chief-root uniqueness and continuous ray throughput are not established. These sampled results do not establish unchanged intermediate-field performance after thinning.

Earlier reference MTF calculations covered five zooms at nominal aperture and f/8, with the wide f/6.39 plot setting evaluated separately. Independent calculations converged through at least 40 lp/mm; optional 80 lp/mm values were not universally converged. Exact source-wide 23.5° and tele 2.5° lie just outside the normal format domain, at normalized field fractions 1.000666859 and 1.009566820; middle 5.9° lies inside at 0.990536387. Separate calculations at those exact source angles converged at nominal aperture and f/8, plus wide f/6.39, without enlarging the normal format domain. These are reference calculations, not measurements of production performance. At the published field edges, the unchanged wide/middle/tele anchor geometries have substantial vignetting. Photopic and finite-distance MTF remain unavailable because complete partial dispersion and finite conjugates are absent.

The patent's Figure 4 aperture discrepancy and stop-to-image ratio residual, unknown melts, inferred clear rims, iris and cam, and infinity-only scope remain limitations. No measured factory illumination, smooth factory cam, historical dispersion or finite-focus performance is established.
