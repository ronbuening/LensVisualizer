## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 8,451,542 B2  
**Application Number:** US 13/183,445  
**Filed:** 2011-07-15  
**Granted:** 2013-05-28  
**Priority:** 2010-07-15 (JP 2010-160378); 2011-06-02 (JP 2011-124618)  
**Inventors:** Tsuneo Uchida, Koji Hoshi  
**Assignee:** Panasonic Corporation  
**Title:** Zoom lens system, interchangeable lens apparatus and camera system  
**Example details:** Embodiment 1; Fig. 1, Tables 2–6  
**Production correlation:** PANASONIC LUMIX G VARIO 100-300mm f/4.0-5.6 MEGA O.I.S. (H-FS100300), a qualified
correlation, not confirmed by Panasonic

The patent describes four numerical examples of one five-unit telephoto zoom for an interchangeable-lens camera with
a 10.815 mm image height, the Four Thirds half-diagonal. All four cover about 103–291 mm at f/4.1–5.8. Example 1 is the
one that matches the original H-FS100300. [P1], front page; Tables 3, 8, 13 and 18.

1. **Construction.** Example 1 has 17 elements. Five pairs are cemented (L2+L3, L4+L5, L7+L8, L10+L11, L13+L14),
   leaving 12 air-separated components. Panasonic's operating instructions give "17 elements in 12 groups (1 ED lens)".
   [P1], col. 4, ll. 10–47; Table 2; [M1], p. 11.
2. **One ED element.** The only low-dispersion element in Example 1 is L3 (nd 1.49700, νd 81.6), in the front group.
   Examples 2 and 3 use the same glass for three elements, and Example 4 for four. Neither matches Panasonic's single
   ED lens. [P1], Tables 2, 7, 12 and 17.
3. **Camera embodiment.** The patent's interchangeable-lens camera system (Embodiment 5, Fig. 13) is described with
   the zoom lens of Embodiment 1 in place. [P1], col. 9, ll. 21–52.
4. **Specification and mechanism.** The printed range is 102.7209–290.3070 mm at f/4.11397–5.77082, against
   Panasonic's 100–300 mm f/4.0–5.6. The inner-focusing G2 and the decentring G4 match a lens with internal focus and
   MEGA O.I.S. [P1], col. 6, l. 49 – col. 7, l. 7; Table 3; [M1], p. 11.
5. **Timing.** The Japanese priority date, 15 July 2010, falls about seven weeks before the lens was announced in
   September 2010. The operating instructions carry August 2010 page stamps. [P1], front page; [M1]; [M3].

Together these support a correlation. Panasonic has not published the prescription, and the patent does not name a
product. The later H-FSA100300 (2016), also listed as 17 elements in 12 groups with one ED lens, is a separate
design and is not identified with this example.

## Optical Architecture

Example 1 is a positive-lead telephoto zoom with five units of power + + + − +. Every unit moves toward the object as
the lens zooms from wide to tele, and the stop moves with G3. The G1–G2 interval grows, and the G2–G3, G3–G4 and
G4–G5 intervals shrink. [P1], col. 6, ll. 22–35.

| Unit | Elements | Surfaces       | Focal length (Table 5), mm | Role                                     |
| ---- | -------- | -------------- | -------------------------: | ---------------------------------------- |
| G1   | L1–L3    | 1–5            |                   +143.138 | Front collector with the ED achromat     |
| G2   | L4+L5    | 6–8            |                    +87.201 | Cemented inner-focus doublet             |
| G3   | L6–L11   | 9–19 (stop 14) |                    +25.739 | Main zooming unit, split around the stop |
| G4   | L12–L14  | 20–24          |                    −15.702 | Negative O.I.S. unit                     |
| G5   | L15–L17  | 25–30          |                    +41.352 | Positive rear group                      |

G3 is not a simple positive stack. Its pre-stop part G31 (L6–L8) computes to −25.33 mm and its post-stop part G32
(L9–L11) to +25.24 mm. The 21.46 mm interval between them, which contains the stop, makes the combination strongly
positive. The patent places the stop next to the negative sub-unit so that the axial beam is low at the diaphragm,
which keeps the barrel diameter down. [P1], col. 7, ll. 9–22; condition (3).

Two further choices follow the patent's stated reasoning. A moving G1 lowers the beam heights in the following units,
so the inner-focus G2 stays small and light. The negative O.I.S. unit sits behind the stop, where its diameter can be
small, with at least three positive-group elements behind it to hold performance when it is decentred. [P1], col. 6,
ll. 36–48; col. 6, l. 62 – col. 7, l. 7; col. 7, ll. 23–28.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Element focal lengths are the patent's single-lens values (Table 4), each element taken alone in air. The glass names
are catalog equivalents chosen by nd/νd; see the next section.

### G1 — front collector

#### L1 — Plano-convex positive, convex toward the object

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.4. Glass: FK5 equivalent (fluor crown). f = +247.21 mm.

A weak crown collector with a flat rear face. It takes a first share of G1's convergence in a low-index,
low-dispersion glass, so the strongly curved achromat behind it works on an already narrowed beam. At 26.3 mm
semi-diameter it is the largest element; the barrel's 67 mm filter thread sits in front of it.

#### L2 — Negative meniscus, convex toward the object

nd = 1.68893, νd = 31.2. Glass: J-SF8 equivalent (dense flint). f = −218.86 mm.

The flint half of the cemented G1 achromat. Its cemented rear face (R 54.12 mm) carries the colour correction against
L3.

#### L3 — Positive meniscus, convex toward the object (ED)

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6. Glass: FCD1 equivalent (fluor-crown ED). f = +131.68 mm.

The only ED element in the lens. At the front of a 3× telephoto zoom the marginal ray is highest in G1, so axial colour
and secondary spectrum at the long end are decided mostly here. The flint–FCD1 pair L2+L3 computes to +342.0 mm, a
weak positive achromat whose glass difference (Δνd 50.4) lets the cemented interface stay moderate. FCD1's positive
partial-dispersion deviation (dPgF about +0.032 on its catalog curve) also reduces the secondary spectrum of the
front group. The patent does not discuss the glass choice.

### G2 — inner-focus doublet

#### L4 — Negative meniscus, convex toward the object

nd = 1.62004, νd = 36.3. Glass: E-F2 equivalent (flint). f = −90.79 mm.

#### L5 — Plano-convex positive, convex toward the object

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.4. Glass: FK5 equivalent (fluor crown). f = +44.05 mm.

The cemented pair is G2 (+87.20 mm), the whole focusing unit. The patent keeps it to two elements so that it is light,
and prefers a single cemented doublet so that chromatic aberration at the tele end stays corrected while it moves for
focus. Condition (2) ties its power to G1's: a stronger G2 would hurt spherical aberration and lateral colour at tele,
and a weaker one would need more focus travel and a longer lens. [P1], col. 6, ll. 49–61; col. 8, ll. 12–33.

### G3 — main zooming unit

#### L6 — Biconcave negative

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.6. Glass: S-LAH66 equivalent (lanthanum crown). f = −27.33 mm.

The first and strongest member of the negative sub-unit G31. Its high index lets it supply strong negative power with
moderate curvatures, which keeps its Petzval contribution and higher-order aberrations down.

#### L7 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.94595, νd = 18.0. Glass: FDS18 equivalent (very-high-index dense flint). f = +30.27 mm.

#### L8 — Biconcave negative

nd = 1.90366, νd = 31.3. Glass: TAFD25 equivalent (lanthanum dense flint). f = −28.01 mm.

L7 and L8 are nearly equal and opposite in power, and the cemented pair computes to −924 mm: almost afocal. Its effect
is chromatic rather than refractive. The extreme dispersion of L7 (νd 18.0, dPgF about +0.041) against the
lanthanum flint L8 lets the pair add colour correction just ahead of the stop without changing G31's power. This reading
is an interpretation; the patent describes the shapes and the cementing only. [P1], col. 4, ll. 26–34.

#### L9 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.58913, νd = 61.3. Glass: N-SK5 equivalent (dense crown). f = +51.68 mm.

The first element behind the stop and the start of the positive sub-unit G32. With the beam low at the stop, this
modest crown begins G3's convergence.

#### L10 — Negative meniscus, convex toward the object

nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.8. Glass: J-SF03 equivalent (dense flint). f = −41.56 mm.

#### L11 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.58913, νd = 61.3. Glass: N-SK5 equivalent (dense crown). f = +22.12 mm.

The cemented L10+L11 (+47.54 mm) is the achromat that closes G3. L11 is the strongest positive element in the lens, so
G32 carries most of G3's +25.7 mm power. Its flint partner corrects the colour it introduces.

### G4 — O.I.S. unit

#### L12 — Negative meniscus, convex toward the object

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.6. Glass: S-LAH66 equivalent (lanthanum crown). f = −40.30 mm.

#### L13 — Positive meniscus, concave toward the object

nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.8. Glass: J-SF03 equivalent (dense flint). f = +31.44 mm.

#### L14 — Biconcave negative

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.6. Glass: S-LAH66 equivalent (lanthanum crown). f = −14.79 mm.

G4 (−15.70 mm) is the stabilizing unit and the strongest unit in the lens. The two lanthanum negatives give it its power
with high-index curvatures. The positive flint L13, cemented to L14 (pair −27.23 mm), achromatizes the unit. A
decentring unit that is not colour-corrected on its own would add lateral colour as it shifts. This last point is an
inference from the construction; the patent states only that a negative unit behind the stop keeps the O.I.S. diameter
small. [P1], col. 4, ll. 36–41; col. 6, l. 62 – col. 7, l. 2.

### G5 — rear group

#### L15 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.51680, νd = 64.2. Glass: BSC7 equivalent (borosilicate crown). f = +22.58 mm.

#### L16 — Negative meniscus, concave toward the object

nd = 1.91082, νd = 35.2. Glass: TAFD35 equivalent (lanthanum dense flint). f = −21.26 mm.

#### L17 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.8. Glass: J-SF03 equivalent (dense flint). f = +48.19 mm.

G5 (+41.35 mm) re-converges the beam after the negative G4 and sets the exit pupil. Its positive–negative–positive
arrangement, with an air space between L15 and the high-index meniscus L16, gives the rear group freedom to balance
field curvature and distortion. The patent's reason for at least three elements here is to hold performance while G4
is decentred. Using a dense flint for the positive L17 is an unusual choice for a last element. It plausibly trims
lateral colour at the field edge; the patent does not explain it. [P1], col. 7, ll. 23–28.

The palette has eleven distinct glasses. The chromatic logic is distributed: one ED element where the marginal ray is
highest (L3), a cemented achromat in each moving unit that must stay colour-corrected on its own (G2 for focus, G4 for
stabilization, L10+L11 in G3), and the near-afocal L7+L8 pair ahead of the stop. The high-index lanthanum negatives
(S-LAH66, TAFD25, TAFD35) keep the strong negative powers of G31, G4 and G5 from bending the field. The Petzval sum
of the whole prescription is +0.00018 mm⁻¹, a Petzval radius of about 5.5 m.

## Focus Mechanism

Example 1 focuses internally with G2. The cemented L4+L5 moves toward the object to focus closer, while the other units
stay where the zoom setting puts them. Fig. 1 marks this with an arrow on G2, and claim 2 covers focusing with a unit
ahead of the stop. [P1], col. 3, ll. 58–63; col. 6, ll. 49–61; claim 2.

The patent publishes no close-focus spacings, focus travel or close-focus magnification for any example. The model
therefore carries infinity focus only, and the focus control does not move G2. Panasonic gives 1.5 m minimum focus
(from the focus-distance mark) and 0.21× maximum magnification; these are production specifications and are not
reproduced by the model. [M1], p. 11.

## Image Stabilization

G4 (L12–L14) moves perpendicular to the axis to compensate image blur. Table 1 gives the shift needed at the tele end
for a 0.3° tilt of the lens: 0.454 mm for Example 1. The lateral-aberration plots of Fig. 3 compare the centred and
shifted states at ±70 % image height and on axis, and the patent reports small decentring coma and astigmatism.
[P1], col. 10, ll. 16–52; Table 1; Fig. 3.

A negative unit behind the stop can stay small, and the positive G5 behind it keeps the shifted state well
corrected. [P1], col. 6, l. 62 – col. 7, l. 7. The model draws and traces G4 centred; the
decentred state is not modelled.

## Conditional Expressions

| Condition     | Range (preferred)     | Printed (Table 22) |                   Computed |
| ------------- | --------------------- | -----------------: | -------------------------: |
| (1) D12W / fW | 0.2–0.5 (0.3–0.4)     |               0.33 | 33.6396 / 102.7209 = 0.327 |
| (2) f1 / f2   | 1.0–2.0 (1.2–1.8)     |               1.64 |   143.138 / 87.201 = 1.641 |
| (3) D3 / fW   | 0.05–0.25 (0.08–0.23) |               0.21 | 21.4562 / 102.7209 = 0.209 |

D12W is the G1–G2 interval at wide, and D3 the interval between G31 and G32, from surface 13 to surface 15 through
the stop. Condition (1) keeps G2 small enough to focus quickly without lengthening the lens. Condition (2) balances
G1 and G2 for tele spherical aberration and lateral colour against focus travel. Condition (3) sets how far apart the
two halves of G3 sit. Too far apart lengthens the lens; too close makes each half too strong, which worsens wide-end
spherical aberration and tolerance sensitivity. [P1], col. 7, l. 45 – col. 9, l. 6; Table 22.

## Model Scope and Limitations

### Printed precision

All 30 surface rows and the three printed spacing sets are carried exactly. The computed focal lengths are 102.7170 /
174.1825 / 290.2861 mm against the printed 102.7209 / 174.1910 / 290.3070 mm. The paraxial focus falls 0.0019 /
0.0032 / 0.0060 mm ahead of the printed image plane, consistent with the table's rounding. The single-lens and unit
focal lengths agree with Tables 4 and 5 to about 0.001 mm. The description of Fig. 1 defines the middle
position as √(fW·fT) = 172.686 mm, but Table 3 prints 174.1910 mm, which the printed middle spacings reproduce. The
table is kept. [P1], col. 3; Tables 3–5.

### Intermediate zoom positions

Between the three printed stations the viewer needs a continuous zoom path, which the patent does not give.
Interpolating the printed spacings in straight lines leaves the image badly out of focus between the middle and tele
stations: up to 3.19 mm behind the image plane near 243 mm. The model therefore adds 98 derived stations.

- D19, D24 and BF are interpolated linearly between the printed stations.
- D5 and D8 carry an equal and opposite correction that moves only G2, the patent's focusing unit. It is solved
  paraxially at every station to hold focus at the printed image plane.
- The largest correction moves G2 0.60 mm toward the object, near 231 mm. The residual focus error stays within about
  0.006 mm, the size of the printed stations' own rounding residual.
- Focal lengths of the derived stations are computed from the corrected spacings; their f-numbers are interpolated
  from the printed ones.

The result is a continuous path that stays in focus, but it is not Panasonic's zoom cam. It changes slope at the
middle station, and the units' real paths need not be piecewise linear.

### Aperture and clear apertures

The stop is surface 14 as printed. Its radius is set by the printed F-numbers and comes out at about 6.80 mm at all
three stations, so the iris need not change diameter as the lens zooms; this is a calculation, not a published
diameter. The patent prints no clear apertures. The semi-diameters were measured from Fig. 1 at the wide position and
agree with the drawing within about 6 % for every unit.

G4 is drawn square-cut at about 6.6 mm, a little larger than modelled (6.3 mm). At 6.41 mm the facing L12 and L13
surfaces would meet across their 2.08 mm air gap; at 6.3 mm their sags use 97 % of it, which is as close as the model
goes. With these rims the axial beam at the printed f-numbers and the chief ray to the full image height pass at every
station. The rims vignette the corners: the viewer's geometric transmission at the printed half-field is about 65 % at
wide, 63 % at middle and 55 % at tele. It is limited by the G4 and G5 rear rims at wide and by G1 at tele.

### What is not modelled

Close focus, the decentred O.I.S. state and production glass melts are not modelled. The rims are estimates. Spectral
behaviour comes from catalog equivalents rather than measured production glass.
