# PANASONIC LUMIX G X VARIO PZ 45-175mm f/4-5.6 ASPH. POWER O.I.S.

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP 5649252 B2
**Applicant:** Panasonic Intellectual Property Management Co., Ltd.
**Inventors:** Isamu Izuhara; Kyoichi Miyazaki
**Filed:** 2012-08-10 (PCT); priority 2011-08-25
**Published:** 2015-01-07 (Japanese grant publication; registered 2014-11-21)

Fourteen elements in ten groups form six zoom units of power + − + − − + (¶0018). G1, G3 and G6 stay fixed during zoom, and G2, G4 and G5 move (¶0028). Figures 1, 3 and 5 separate Example 1 from Examples 2 and 3 by its plano-convex L1 and its meniscus L13 and L14; those examples use a biconvex L1, a biconcave L13 and a biconvex L14. Panasonic lists 14 elements in 10 groups with two aspherical and two ED elements, and its construction diagram marks ED glass at L3 and L9 and aspherical elements at L7 and L10. Example 1 is the only example with two elements of νd 81.6; Examples 2 and 3 have one. Its printed 107.53 mm total length less the 19.25 mm Micro Four Thirds flange distance is 88.28 mm, inside Panasonic's 90.0 mm barrel length, while Examples 2 and 3 would need 93.75 mm and 96.75 mm.

These matches make the correlation strong, but Panasonic has not confirmed this prescription. The model keeps the patent's values: 45.17–167.24 mm at f/4.16–5.84, against the marketed 45–175 mm f/4–5.6. No example in the patent reaches 175 mm; Examples 2 and 3 end at 169.75 mm, and US 9,158,092 B2 prints the same 167.2377 mm for Example 1.

## Optical Architecture

The model keeps all 25 numbered surfaces. The aperture stop is S13, between L7 and L8, with the printed distances on both sides. The 14 elements fall in 10 air-separated groups: L1, L2+L3, L4+L5, L6, L7, L8+L9, L10+L11, L12, L13 and L14.

The patent prints spacings at three zoom positions, and the model's 201 zoom stations keep them unchanged at stations 0, 100 and 200. The 198 stations between them are inferred and are not a recovered factory cam. Radii, glass coordinates and fixed distances are as printed. The overall length holds during zoom, as reviewers report for the lens: d5+d10 stays at 27.7518 mm and d19+d21+d23 at 27.7018–27.7019 mm, for a total length of 107.5266–107.5269 mm. The iris radius is set from the f-number at each station: 7.43 mm, 5.86 mm and 5.23 mm at the three printed positions, with f-numbers interpolated between them. The patent prints no stop diameter.

### Model assumptions

- All semi-diameters and iris radii are inferred. Rims are estimated from Figure 1, which is compressed 3.2 % in height, and checked by real-ray trace. The printed f-number sets the entrance pupil; the stop diameter is not printed.
- Between the printed positions, G2, G5 and the back focus change linearly with the zoom control, and G4 is placed to hold paraxial focus. G4 then reverses once, near 100 mm, with d19 peaking at 6.39 mm against the printed 6.23 mm at 87.71 mm. The printed spacings require that one reversal, and the patent describes a path convex toward the image (¶0028). G2 moves steadily imageward and G5 steadily objectward, as the patent states.
- Close focus, decentering, O.I.S. stroke and performance at the marketed 0.9 m are not modeled.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Plano-Convex Positive

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.4. Glass: FK5 (SUMITA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +90.826 mm (thick lens in air).

Front element of G1, with a flat rear surface (S2). The patent describes it as plano-convex; Examples 2 and 3 use a biconvex element here (¶0022, ¶0030).

### L2 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.80100, νd = 35.0. Glass: S-LAM66 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = -74.857 mm (thick lens in air).

Object-side half of the cemented G1 doublet. Its flint pairs with the low-dispersion L3 across S4 to correct the color of the first group.

### L3 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6. Glass: FCD1 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +54.773 mm (thick lens in air).

Image-side half of the G1 doublet and the strongest positive element in the group. Panasonic's diagram marks this position as ED, and the printed coordinates match FCD1-class fluorophosphate crown. Its anomalous dispersion is tagged as inferred because the patent does not describe the glass that way.

### L4 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.80450, νd = 39.6. Glass: NBFD3 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = -15.576 mm (thick lens in air).

Object-side half of the cemented G2 doublet and the strongest negative element in the group. G2 moves steadily imageward from wide to tele (¶0028), closing d10 from 25.78 mm to 0.76 mm.

### L5 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.92286, νd = 20.9. Glass: N-SF66 (SCHOTT catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +23.432 mm (thick lens in air).

Image-side half of the G2 doublet. Its very dense flint is the positive partner in a negative doublet, the usual way to correct the color of a negative group.

### L6 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.80420, νd = 46.5. Glass: N-LASF44 (SCHOTT catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = -32.993 mm (thick lens in air).

Biconcave singlet closing G2, 1.81 mm behind the doublet.

### L7 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.80755, νd = 40.9. Glass: D-ZLaF81-25 (CDGM catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +21.116 mm (thick lens in air).

First element of G3, biconvex and aspherical on both surfaces (S11, S12). It sits 2.0 mm ahead of the stop, where the axial beam is wider than anywhere else in G3.

### L8 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.90366, νd = 31.3. Glass: TAFD25 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = -15.602 mm (thick lens in air).

Object-side half of the doublet directly behind the stop, 1.7 mm from it. Its high-index flint pairs with L9 across the cemented surface S15.

### L9 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6. Glass: FCD1 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +29.521 mm (thick lens in air).

Image-side half of that doublet. Panasonic's diagram marks this position as ED, and the coordinates match the same FCD1 class as L3. Examples 2 and 3 use nd 1.48749, νd 70.4 here, which leaves them with one element of that class.

### L10 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.58250, νd = 59.4. Glass: L-BAL42 (OHARA moulding glass, coordinate equivalent). f = +18.916 mm (thick lens in air).

Object-side half of the rear G3 doublet, with an aspherical front surface (S17). The printed index sits 0.0006 below the nd 1.58313, νd 59.4 class (L-BAL42, M-BACD12), a gap of the size molded glass can show. That reading is an inference; the patent does not say how the surface is made.

### L11 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.75520, νd = 27.5. Glass: S-TIH4 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = -74.863 mm (thick lens in air).

Image-side half of that doublet and the last element of G3. Its rear surface S19 is the fixed reference from which d19 opens and closes as G4 moves.

### L12 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.72916, νd = 54.7. Glass: TAC8 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = -31.612 mm (thick lens in air).

The whole of G4, 0.5 mm thick. From wide to tele it moves imageward and then back, on a path convex toward the image (¶0028). The patent also uses it for close focus, moving imageward with L13 (¶0029).

### L13 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.72916, νd = 54.7. Glass: TAC8 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = -28.699 mm (thick lens in air).

The whole of G5, 0.7 mm thick and convex toward the image. It moves steadily objectward from wide to tele (¶0028) and imageward for close focus with L12 (¶0029). Together G4 and G5 give 0.1110 in condition (2), which the patent ties to fast, quiet focusing (¶0063).

### L14 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.8. Glass: J-SF03 (HIKARI catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +32.512 mm (thick lens in air).

The whole of G6, a fixed positive meniscus convex toward the image and the last element, 14.60 mm ahead of the image plane.

The patent names no glass; the catalog names below are coordinate equivalents that supply dispersion curves, not identified suppliers or melts. L10 prints 0.0006 below the L-BAL42 index, which suits a moulded asphere; it keeps the printed nd and takes its dispersion from that row. ## Focus Mechanism

Example 1 focuses with two groups: G4 (L12) and G5 (L13) both move imageward from infinity to close focus (¶0017, ¶0029), as the arrows in Figure 1 show. It is the only example that does; Examples 2 and 3 move G4 alone and Example 4 moves G5 alone. This agrees with Panasonic's name for the system, Multi-Actuator Floating Inner Focus, and its statement that three lens groups are controlled separately. In this design those would be the moving groups G2, G4 and G5, which the patent says suit independent actuator drive (¶0011); that mapping is an inference.

The patent prints no close-focus spacings, focus travel or object distance, so the model covers infinity focus only. The focus control changes nothing, and the 0.9 m in the catalog data is the marketed minimum focus distance, not a modeled state. No close-focus performance is available, including finite-distance MTF.

## Aspherical Surfaces

The patent's equation (¶0076) is the standard conic plus even polynomial, with κ entered directly as K in the radicand 1−(1+κ)(h/r)². All three surfaces print K = 0. S11 and S12 are the two faces of L7 and S17 is the front face of L10, the two aspherical elements on Panasonic's diagram.

## Image Stabilization

The production lens has POWER O.I.S., but the patent identifies no O.I.S. group: neither its text nor its tables mention image stabilization, a shift group or a decentering stroke. The model stays centered at every zoom position and marks no stabilizer element.

## Model Scope and Limitations

First-order values do not depend on the aspheric terms. Computed values differ from the printed ones only at the level of rounding.

| Wide | 45.1684 | 45.168287887 | 14.60331 | 14.602537211 |
| Middle | 87.7062 | 87.705038975 | 14.60288 | 14.601138169 |
| Tele | 167.2377 | 167.231958512 | 14.60312 | 14.598946525 |

Half-field at the printed 10.815 mm image height is 13.54°, 6.90° and 3.63°, matching the printed 13.5369°, 6.8993° and 3.6302°. Longitudinal spherical aberration stays within about 0.10 mm at every zoom station, the scale of the patent's Figure 2.

Semi-diameters are inferred, so vignetting reflects the model's rims and not measured data. Wide open, the corner keeps 66.5 %, 94.8 % and 71.3 % of its beam at the three printed positions, with the cut at the long end coming from G1 and the cut at the short end mainly from L12, L6 and L4. Polychromatic MTF and chromatic results depend on the catalog equivalents, with L10 on its printed values, and are not predictions of the production lens. MTF is available at infinity focus only. The patent's aberration plots have not been digitized for comparison, and no comparison with measured MTF has been made.
