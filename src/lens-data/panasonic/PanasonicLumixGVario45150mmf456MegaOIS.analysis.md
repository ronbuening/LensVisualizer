**Title:** Zoom lens system, interchangeable lens apparatus and camera system  
**Example details:** Embodiment 4; Fig. 13, Tables 13–16, ¶¶0111–0119; granted as US 9,250,423 B2 on 2016-02-02  
**Production correlation:** PANASONIC LUMIX G VARIO 45-150mm f/4.0-5.6 ASPH. MEGA O.I.S. (H-FS45150), a
representative correlation, not confirmed by Panasonic

The application describes eight numerical examples (Embodiments 1–8) of one telephoto zoom for an interchangeable-lens
camera with a 10.815 mm image height, the Four Thirds half-diagonal. All eight cover about 45.40–144.00 mm and open to
f/4.15–4.16 at the wide end. Embodiments 1 and 3–7 have six zoom units (+ − + − − +); Embodiments 2 and 8 have five,
ending in one positive unit. [P1], ¶¶0078–0084; Tables 3, 7, 11, 15, 19, 23, 27 and 31.

The production match rests on these points:

1. **Construction.** Example 4 has 12 elements. Three pairs are cemented (L1+L2, L3+L4, L7+L8), which leaves nine
   air-separated groups. Panasonic lists "12 elements in 9 groups". [P1], ¶¶0111–0116; [M1]; [M2].
2. **Special elements.** The image-side faces of L6 (surface 10) and L9 (surface 16) are aspherical, and L8 has
   nd 2.00100. Panasonic lists two aspherical lenses and one UHR (ultra-high refractive index) lens, and its
   construction diagram marks L6 and L9 as the aspherical elements and L8 as the UHR element. The diagram matches
   Example 4 element for element. [P1], ¶0113; Table 13; [M1]; [M3].
3. **Mechanism.** Focus moves the single element L10 (G4); image stabilization decentres the single element L9.
   Panasonic describes an inner focus system driven by a stepping motor, with MEGA O.I.S. [P1], ¶¶0117, 0119; [M2].
4. **Specification.** The printed range is 45.4004–144.0003 mm at f/4.16038–5.75634, with 2ω 26.88°–8.55°. Panasonic
   gives 45–150 mm, f/4.0–5.6 and a 27°–8.2° angle of view. [P1], Table 15; [M1].
5. **Timing.** The first Japanese priority, 15 June 2012, is about five weeks before Panasonic announced the lens on
   18 July 2012. [P1], front page; [M2].

The match identifies the patent family, not one example. All eight examples share the twelve-element layout, the two
aspherical surfaces, the stop between L8 and L9, the single-element focus and O.I.S. units, and the 1.94595 and 2.00100
glasses. They differ in small curvature and spacing changes and in a few glasses: Table 33 lists L2 at νd 64.2 in six
examples, 70.4 in Example 3 and 60.8 in Example 4 alone, and L9 at νd 63.5 except in Examples 6 (71.7) and 7 (81.6).
No public source distinguishes among them, and the patent's camera embodiment names Embodiment 1 (¶0222). Example 4 is
therefore a representative member of the family; Panasonic has not published the production prescription.

## Optical Architecture

Example 4 is a positive-lead telephoto zoom with six units of power + − + − − +. From wide to tele G1 moves
monotonically toward the object, G3, G4 and G5 move toward the object on paths slightly convex toward the object, and
G2 and G6 stay fixed relative to the image. The G1–G2, G4–G5 and G5–G6 intervals grow, the G2–G3 interval shrinks and
the G3–G4 interval varies. The stop sits inside G3, between L8 and L9, and moves with it. [P1], ¶¶0084, 0118.

| Unit | Elements | Surfaces       | Focal length (Table 15), mm | Role                                      |
| ---- | -------- | -------------- | --------------------------: | ----------------------------------------- |
| G1   | L1+L2    | 1–3            |                   +110.9979 | Cemented front collector                  |
| G2   | L3–L5    | 4–8            |                    −26.4972 | Fixed negative variator                   |
| G3   | L6–L9    | 9–16 (stop 14) |                    +21.5866 | Main positive unit, split around the stop |
| G4   | L10      | 17–18          |                    −40.3398 | Single-element focus unit                 |
| G5   | L11      | 19–20          |                    −83.7516 | Weak negative zooming unit                |
| G6   | L12      | 21–22          |                    +54.8147 | Fixed positive rear element               |

G3 has two positive sub-units. G3a (L6–L8) computes to +29.91 mm and G3b (L9 alone) to +34.38 mm. The patent puts the
stop between them to keep the diaphragm small, and makes G3b a single aspherical element so that the O.I.S. unit is
light and its decentring coma stays low. It also fixes G2 to the image plane, so that it is less likely to be
decentred in manufacture, and gives G3a two positive elements of nd above 1.7 for spherical aberration at the tele end.
[P1], ¶¶0161–0167.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Element focal lengths are thick-lens values for each element alone in air. The glass names are catalog equivalents
chosen by nd/νd; see the next section.

### G1 — cemented front collector

#### L1 — Negative meniscus, convex toward the object

nd = 1.80610, νd = 33.3. Glass: NBFD15 (HOYA) equivalent. f = −116.78 mm.

The flint half of the G1 achromat. Its strongly curved cemented rear face (R 35.09 mm) carries the colour correction
against L2. The patent's condition (3) holds its Abbe number between 28 and 40, here 33.3. [P1], ¶0111; condition (3).

#### L2 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.56384, νd = 60.8. Glass: N-SK11 (SCHOTT) equivalent. f = +56.53 mm.

The crown half, and the element the main claim is written around: conditions (1) and (2) require 1.47 < nd < 1.57 and
60 < νd < 75 for the positive element of G1. The patent ties the index bounds to length (a more curved, thicker element
below 1.47) and weight (a denser glass above 1.57), and the Abbe bounds to colour correction below 60 and glass cost
above 75. Its shape factor, condition (4), is 0.810. L1+L2 computes to +111.00 mm, the G1 value of Table 15.
[P1], ¶¶0170–0186; Table 33.

### G2 — fixed negative variator

#### L3 — Biconcave negative

nd = 1.80610, νd = 33.3. Glass: NBFD15 (HOYA) equivalent. f = −18.17 mm.

The strongest negative element of the lens and the main source of G2's power. It uses the same glass as L1.

#### L4 — Positive meniscus, convex toward the object

nd = 1.94595, νd = 18.0. Glass: FDS18 (HOYA) equivalent. f = +24.54 mm.

A very high-dispersion positive meniscus cemented behind L3. Giving the positive element the higher dispersion (νd 18.0
against 33.3) is the usual arrangement for an achromatic negative doublet, and keeps the colour that the variator adds
small over the zoom range. L3+L4 computes to −64.98 mm.

#### L5 — Biconcave negative

nd = 1.72825, νd = 28.3. Glass: E-FD10 (HOYA) equivalent. f = −45.56 mm.

Closes G2. Its front face (R −58.51 mm) faces L4's convex rear face (R +49.74 mm) across a 1.1474 mm air gap, which
limits how large these rims can be drawn (see Model Scope and Limitations).

### G3 — main positive unit

#### L6 — Biconvex positive (aspherical rear surface)

nd = 1.80998, νd = 40.9. Glass: K-VC89 (SUMITA) equivalent, a precision-moulding glass. f = +21.95 mm.

The first element after the long D8 gap, where the axial beam leaving the negative G2 is at its widest behind G1
(up to 7.5 mm high at surface 9). Its aspherical rear face (surface 10) is one of the lens's two aspheres. A high-index
moulding glass suits a moulded asphere; the catalog match is an nd/νd equivalence, not a confirmed melt.

#### L7 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.72916, νd = 54.7. Glass: S-LAL18 (OHARA) equivalent. f = +17.28 mm.

The second positive element of G3a with nd above 1.7, as the patent prefers for tele-end spherical aberration. It is
cemented to L8. [P1], ¶0167.

#### L8 — Biconcave negative (UHR)

nd = 2.00100, νd = 29.1. Glass: TAFD55 (HOYA) equivalent. f = −10.21 mm.

Panasonic's UHR element. Its very high index gives the strong negative power (−10.21 mm) directly ahead of the stop
with less steep surfaces than a lower-index glass would need, which keeps the cemented L7+L8 pair (−38.52 mm) compact
and, in the author's reading, limits the higher-order aberrations steeper surfaces would add. It is a high-index
lanthanum flint, not a low-dispersion glass.

#### L9 — Biconvex positive (aspherical rear surface), O.I.S. element

nd = 1.51760, νd = 63.5. Glass: D-K59 (CDGM) equivalent, a precision-moulding glass. f = +34.38 mm.

G3b on its own: the image-stabilizing element behind the stop. A single moulded crown element keeps the moving mass and
its actuator small, the asphere (surface 16) holds decentring coma down, and condition (10) asks for νd between 60 and
85 to limit colour during decentring. [P1], ¶¶0162, 0166, 0215–0218.

### G4 — focus unit

#### L10 — Negative meniscus, convex toward the object

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.6. Glass: S-LAH66 (OHARA) equivalent. f = −40.34 mm.

The whole focus group: a thin (0.60 mm) high-index meniscus that moves toward the image for close focus. A single light
element suits a stepping-motor drive. [P1], ¶¶0119, 0160.

### G5 — weak negative zooming unit

#### L11 — Negative meniscus, concave toward the object

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.4. Glass: FK5 (SUMITA) equivalent. f = −83.75 mm.

A weak crown meniscus that moves with zoom. With G6 it forms the patent's "subsequent lens unit", whose tele focal
length (+102.42 mm) sets condition (5).

### G6 — fixed rear element

#### L12 — Positive meniscus, concave toward the object

nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.8. Glass: S-TIH53 (OHARA) equivalent. f = +54.81 mm.

The fixed last element, 18.93 mm ahead of the image. This close to the image it acts mainly on the off-axis bundles; a
dense flint in this position bears on lateral colour as well as field curvature, an author interpretation.

selects that glass's dispersion curve; supplier and melt are not established.

Panasonic lists no ED element, and the prescription has none: the lowest-dispersion glass is the νd 70.4 fluor crown of

precision-moulded aspheres, but the patent does not say how they are made.

## Focus Mechanism

Inner focus: G4 (L10) alone moves toward the image, and every other unit stays where zoom puts it. The patent prints
infinity (Table 15) and close-focus (Table 16) spacings at all three stations for an object distance of 1000 mm; only
D16 and D18 change. [P1], ¶¶0119, 0232; Tables 15–16.

| Station | D16 ∞ → close (mm) | D18 ∞ → close (mm) | L10 travel (mm) | Magnification |
| ------- | -----------------: | -----------------: | --------------: | ------------: |
| Wide    |    1.5348 → 2.4002 |  17.1221 → 16.2567 |          0.8654 |       −0.0444 |
| Middle  |    4.3295 → 6.5154 |  16.7857 → 14.5998 |          2.1859 |       −0.0782 |
| Tele    |    2.3574 → 7.8103 |  20.4688 → 15.0159 |          5.4529 |       −0.1366 |

The patent does not say where the 1000 mm is measured from. Measured from the first lens surface, the printed close
gaps focus paraxially on objects 1000.15, 1000.02 and 999.99 mm away, so the model reads it that way. The close states
then lie 1.092, 1.107 and 1.130 m from the image plane. The focal length shortens at close focus, to about 44.23,
76.67 and 130.09 mm.

Panasonic's 0.9 m minimum focus and 0.17× magnification lie beyond the printed close state. The production focus
travel needed for them is not printed and is not modelled. [M1].

## Aspherical Surfaces

Two surfaces are aspherical, both on image-side faces and both with κ = 0. The patent's equation is

z = (h²/r) / [1 + √(1 − (1 + κ)(h/r)²)] + Σ Aₙhⁿ,

which is the engine's convention with K = κ. Coefficients as printed (Table 14):

| Surface      | K   | A4          | A6           | A8           | A10          |
| ------------ | --- | ----------- | ------------ | ------------ | ------------ |
| 10 (L6 rear) | 0   | 1.25359E-05 | −8.52468E-09 | −9.66856E-11 | 2.43778E-13  |
| 16 (L9 rear) | 0   | 1.30246E-05 | −2.51365E-07 | 9.13729E-09  | −1.07104E-10 |

**Surface 10.** The departure from the base sphere is +0.035 mm at 7.4 mm, the largest axial marginal height on this
face, and +0.050 mm at the 8.1 mm rim. A4 dominates. The positive departure pulls the rim of this convex-to-image face
back toward the image, weakening its positive power at the margin. That reading, an author interpretation, fits a
surface placed to counter the spherical aberration of the strongly converging G3a.

**Surface 16.** The departure is smaller: +0.006 mm at the 4.9 mm axial marginal height and +0.016 mm at the 6.2 mm rim.
The A6, A8 and A10 terms largely cancel inside the axial beam. The patent assigns this asphere to coma control while L9
is decentred for O.I.S. [P1], ¶0166.

## Image Stabilization

L9 (G3b) moves perpendicular to the axis. For Example 4 the patent gives a decentring of 0.4457 mm at the tele end,
which produces the same image shift as tilting the whole lens by the patent's reference angle. The lateral aberration
plots at ±70 % image height show small decentring coma and astigmatism. [P1], ¶¶0234–0238; Fig. 16.

Four conditions bound the O.I.S. unit. Condition (7) sets G3a's focal length against G3b's (here 0.87), so the shift
is neither too large for a small barrel nor too sensitive to manufacture. Condition (8) relates G3b's focal length to
√(fW·fT) (here 0.43), and condition (9) places G3b within G3 (here 0.86). Condition (10) asks for a G3b Abbe number of
60–85 (here 63.5). Decentring is not modelled in the viewer.

## Model Scope and Limitations

### Printed precision

The surface data, both aspheres and all thirty printed variable-gap values (five gaps at six states) are transcribed
without change. Computed focal lengths are 45.3995, 80.8546 and 143.9975 mm against the printed 45.4004, 80.8563 and 144.0003 mm. The paraxial
image lies 0.4, 0.7 and 1.3 µm short of the fixed 18.9311 mm back focus, a rounding-level residual that is kept.
Table 15's overall lengths (91.9007, 107.1967 and 129.9016 mm) exceed the sum of the printed spacings (91.8999,
107.1952 and 129.8999 mm) by up to 0.0017 mm; the spacings are kept. On-axis longitudinal spherical aberration is
within 0.04 mm at every station.

### Intermediate zoom positions

The viewer interpolates the printed spacings linearly between the three stations. This is not Panasonic's zoom cam,
and it does not hold focus: the paraxial image moves up to about 0.57 mm ahead of the image plane near 63 mm and up to
about 2.67 mm behind it near 116 mm. Close states between stations likewise focus at about 760–1130 mm from the front
vertex instead of 1000 mm. Only the six printed states are exact.

### Aperture and clear apertures

The stop is surface 14 as printed. Its radius comes from the printed F-numbers and is about 5.16–5.18 mm at all three
stations, so the iris barely changes with zoom; this is a calculation, not a published diameter. F/22 and the seven
blades are Panasonic's. [M1].

The patent prints no clear apertures. The semi-diameters are estimated from Fig. 13(a). G2's are 7.4 mm, a little
under the drawing: at the drawn 7.7 mm the facing L4 and L5 surfaces would fill 97 % of their 1.1474 mm air gap. As
the figure draws it, L8's concave rear face ends at a radius of about 5.95 mm, 1.7 mm behind its vertex, and a flat
land square to the axis runs from there to the 7 mm edge; no traced beam reaches the land. The axial beam at the
printed F-numbers and the chief ray to the full image height pass every rim at every station, at infinity and at close
focus. The rims vignette the corner bundle, most strongly at G1 and G2: wide open, about 61 %, 80 % and 55 % of the
axial light reaches the image corner at the wide, middle and tele stations at infinity.

### What is not modelled

The O.I.S. decentring of L9, the production focus travel to 0.9 m and production glass melts are not modelled. Spectral
behaviour comes from catalog equivalents.
