# Nikon AF-S Teleconverter TC-20E III

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP 2010-191211 A  
**Application Number:** JP 2009-035798  
**Filed:** 18 February 2009  
**Published:** 2 September 2010  
**Inventor:** Yoko Kimura (木村 陽子)  
**Applicant:** Nikon Corporation  
**Title:** Converter lens, optical device, method for extending focal length of master lens  
**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 2, Table 2, Figures 3–4

The prescription is the second published converter embodiment, comprising original surfaces 26–37. The model relabels these surfaces 1–12, with source surface 34 represented as 9A. The patent's master lens (f = 294.001 mm, F/2.89) is the design of the catalog's AF-S Nikkor 300mm f/2.8D IF-ED (modeled from US 5,745,306, with the same radii but a different rear filter position), one of the hosts the converter mounts on.

Nikon announced the TC-20E III on 10 December 2009. Its published construction is seven elements in five groups with one aspherical element, and its nominal multiplier is 2×. The manufacturer lists a restricted set of compatible AF-S/AF-I lenses and an unchanged minimum focusing distance relative to the primary lens. This does not establish compatibility with every Nikon F-mount lens.

The published-design correlation rests on convergent evidence:

1. Seven glass elements in five air-separated components, including a central positive/negative/positive cemented triplet.
2. One asphere on the object face of the sixth element and the terminal negative meniscus form.
3. Nearly equal-magnitude spherical radii at the rear triplet element, and a larger air gap before than after the triplet. These distinguish Example 2 from the otherwise similar Example 3.
4. A 2009 filing and Nikon's 2009 product announcement are consistent with the 2010 patent publication.

This is a strong published-design correlation, not Nikon's confirmation that the manufactured radii, glasses, clear apertures or measured performance equal this table. The nominal product multiplier, the patent's printed β = 1.97 and the computed first-order multiplier are separate quantities.

## Optical Architecture

The converter has a net negative equivalent power and works in the converging beam behind the master lens. Its two patent functional groups are G1, containing L1–L2, and G2, containing L3–L7. These two functional groups must not be confused with the five air-separated components used in the marketed construction count.

The central triplet carries positive net power despite a strongly negative middle member. The patent describes this positive/negative/positive arrangement as a means to accommodate stronger negative cemented-member power while controlling the Petzval contribution and image-surface curvature (¶0011). That source explanation is not a measured aberration allocation for each individual manufactured element.

Table 2 gives a converter first-to-last-vertex length of 44.20 mm. The converter contains no aperture stop; the master stop governs the composed system. No radius, internal thickness, air gap, refractive index or asphere coefficient is fitted to marketing values.

The geometry is specified by the master image plane. The printed master gives a calculated air back focus of 87.5101 mm behind its rear filter plate (source surface 25). Subtracting the printed 38.62 mm master-to-converter gap places the master's image 48.8901 mm behind the converter's first vertex. The converter's final gap is the printed back focus, 52.5 mm.

The separately computed focal lengths are:

- G1: −64.824424 mm
- G2: +444.675591 mm
- Central cemented triplet: +108.076038 mm
- Isolated converter equivalent focal length: −73.220944 mm

The isolated negative equivalent focal length is not the focal length of an image-taking lens used alone. The converter is used with a virtual object supplied by the converging master beam.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Biconvex positive entrance element

nd = 1.62004, νd = 36.26. Glass: 620363 — flint class (catalog-compatible S-TIM2; supplier unproven). Standalone-air f = +54.141102 mm.

L1 precedes the negative L2 in G1. Their combined power is negative, so L1’s positive standalone power does not determine the role of the whole group. The patent associates a positive-first sequence with spherical-aberration correction (¶0012).

### L2 — Biconcave negative element

nd = 1.80400, νd = 46.58. Glass: 804466 — lanthanum flint class (S-LAH65V compatible; supplier unproven). Standalone-air f = -27.264257 mm.

L2 supplies the stronger negative component of the first group. Its object surface is weakly curved compared with its image surface. The following air gap separates G1 from the positive-net-power rear group.

### L3 — Positive front member of the cemented triplet

nd = 1.62004, νd = 36.30. Glass: 620363 — flint class (E-F2 compatible; supplier unproven). Standalone-air f = +22.892495 mm.

L3 is cemented to L4, and the source surface between them carries the index of L4. Its standalone-air power is quoted only as a shape/material descriptor. The in-situ cemented interfaces use the differences between neighbouring glass indices.

### L4 — Negative cemented middle member

nd = 1.88300, νd = 40.76. Glass: 883408 — lanthanum dense-flint class (S-LAH58 / N-LASF31A compatible; supplier unproven). Standalone-air f = -13.912209 mm.

L4 lies between two positive elements. Its standalone-air focal length differs greatly from the patent condition’s fn. The latter is the inverse reduced power with the adjacent L3 and L5 media present: fn = −44.541329 mm, versus −13.912209 mm in air. Confusing these definitions would invalidate condition (2).

### L5 — Positive rear member of the cemented triplet

nd = 1.57501, νd = 41.49. Glass: 575415 — light-flint class (S-TIL27 / LF7 compatible; supplier unproven). Standalone-air f = +31.930232 mm.

L5 completes the net-positive triplet. Its two bounding spherical radii have equal magnitude in the selected table. The triplet net focal length is not a simple sum or average of its members’ standalone focal lengths.

### L6 — Positive aspherical meniscus

nd = 1.58913, νd = 61.16. Glass: S-BAL35 spectral proxy (supplier and moulding grade unconfirmed). Standalone-air f = +87.663085 mm.

The object face is source surface 34, model surface 9A. The patent places the asphere behind the cemented group and associates this arrangement with image-surface correction (¶0033–0034). The glass coordinate matches moulding-grade barium crowns, which suggests but does not prove a moulded asphere.

### L7 — Negative terminal meniscus

nd = 1.88300, νd = 40.76. Glass: 883408 — lanthanum dense-flint class (S-LAH58 / N-LASF31A compatible; supplier unproven). Standalone-air f = -55.153589 mm.

The last element is concave toward the object. It completes the rear group, after which the original table specifies the 52.5 mm image-space gap. Its role is described by the published arrangement; no isolated aberration benefit is inferred solely from its negative power.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent supplies d-line refractive indices and Abbe numbers; it does not name production suppliers or give per-element C/F/g-line measurements. Original OHARA, HOYA, SCHOTT, HIKARI, CDGM and SUMITA catalogs were searched by coordinate before labels were assigned.

The compatible palette includes S-TIM2-class flint for L1, S-LAH65V-class glass for L2, E-F2-class flint for L3, S-LAH58/N-LASF31A-class dense flint for L4 and L7, and S-TIL27/LF7-class light flint for L5. These are coordinate-compatible classes, not certificates of supplier identity.

L6 (nd = 1.58913, νd = 61.16) uses the coordinate-compatible S-BAL35 catalog curve (nd 1.58913, νd about 61.14) as a spectral proxy. This does not identify the production supplier or establish a moulding grade; L-BAL35 is not silently aliased to S-BAL35. All seven elements now use catalog dispersion.

The model does not claim apochromatic correction or a known production melt. For the other elements the matched catalog glass supplies the dispersion curve as a stand-in for the unknown production glass.

## Focus Mechanism

The converter is a fixed detachable rear assembly with no focusing movement of its own and no stop. Example 2 is an infinity-focus prescription. The master controls focus; Nikon’s statement that the minimum focusing distance remains the same does not supply a numerical master-lens motion law.

The converter has no internal focusing. The patent gives no close-focus data, so close-range performance is not modeled from it; with a host attached, focusing uses the host's own focus states.

## Aspherical Surface

Only model surface 9A, the object side of L6, is geometrically aspherical. Paragraph 0042 defines the source equation with κ inside the square root rather than the conventional 1 + K. The printed κ = 1.0000 therefore maps to K = 0, a spherical base.

The native coefficients, without dimensional scaling, are:

- A4 = 1.05550 × 10⁻⁵ mm⁻³
- A6 = 7.82650 × 10⁻⁹ mm⁻⁵
- A8 = 0 and A10 = 0

At the modeled 16 mm semi-diameter, the polynomial departure from the base sphere is +0.823 mm. This is computed at an estimated rim, not a published clear aperture.

The printed β = 1.97 appears truncated rather than rounded (581.9 / 294.001 = 1.979); Examples 1 and 3 behave the same way. The negative member fn is evaluated in its cemented neighbouring media as defined above. Surface-by-surface Petzval calculation, with each contribution φ/(n·n′), gives a converter sum of −0.000146986890 mm⁻¹. This sum is not a complete prediction of astigmatic image-surface shape.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The semi-diameters are estimates from Figure 3, since the patent publishes no clear apertures: 12 mm for L1 and L2, 14 mm across the triplet, and 16 mm for both rear menisci. They pass the axial beam of hosts down to about F/2.05, and off-axis marginal vignetting at the image corners is kept rather than removed by enlarging the rims.

The patent's master plus this converter computes to f = 582.13 mm with a paraxial back focus of 52.59 mm, against the printed 581.9 mm and 52.5 mm. The printed rows are kept as published; nothing is adjusted to the printed summaries. The master computes to F/2.88 (printed F/2.89), and with the converter the same stop gives F/5.70 (printed 5.7).

The catalog's existing AF-S 300mm f/2.8 D IF-ED host has a separate rim limit at its rear L11 surface 21: its stated f/2.88 beam traces at about f/3.45 even without a converter. The composed model therefore traces about f/6.83 instead of its stated f/5.70. The converter's own rims clear that axial path; the host's inferred rear rim needs a separate source review.

The converter accepts hosts at F/2.8 or slower, following Nikon's rating of autofocus with f/2.8-or-faster AF-S and AF-I lenses. It mounts only on lenses that Nikon lists as compatible, because the rear elements of other lenses can strike the converter's front element.
