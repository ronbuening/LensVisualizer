# NIKON ZOOM-NIKKOR ED 180-600mm f/8 — Audit Log

## Source

[US 3,743,384, Optical System for the Magnification Varying Portion of an Ultra-Telephoto Type Zoom Lens](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/80/e7/33/3dc167dab27fbf/US3743384.pdf), Soichi Nakamura, Nippon Kogaku K.K. The application (No. 208,637) was filed December 16, 1971, with Japanese priority 45-116646 of December 24, 1970. The patent was granted July 3, 1973. Example II, a zoom lens for 35 mm still cameras of f = 180–600 mm, F/8 and telephoto ratio 0.7, is printed in column 7 (PDF p. 9) and repeated in the claim 2 table (columns 8–9). Fig. 3 on sheet 2 (PDF p. 3) shows its four assemblies, and Fig. 5 (PDF p. 5) gives its aberration curves at the short, middle and long settings. The Certificate of Correction, signed March 5, 1974, is on PDF p. 12. [Patent page](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3743384A/en)

Nikon's dimensioned section of May 1976 shows the production optics. Nikon's AI-S instruction manual gives 18 elements in 11 groups, ED glass, 180–600 mm, f/8, a 13°40′–4°10′ picture angle, an f/8–f/32 aperture scale, a 2.5 m to infinity distance scale, a fully automatic diaphragm, the Nikon bayonet mount and a 95 mm filter thread. Version histories come from the MIR Nikkor resource page, Roland Vink's lens list, Marco Cavina's article and Richard de Stoutz's record of lens no. 174882. ## Transcription Check

The 29 radii, 25 fixed axial thicknesses and air spaces, the two printed values of each of D6, D13 and D19, and the 18 index and Abbe-number pairs were reread against the column 7 table, the claim tables and the certificate. Nothing disagrees once the certificate is applied. All surfaces are spherical, and all lengths are in millimetres at the original scale. The indices are for the helium d line, and the table prints no plates.

The certificate makes three changes to Example II in column 7: R17 from −251.3 to +251.3 mm, the stated compensator focal length from 133.2688 to 113.2688 mm and the relay designation from f14 to f4. The claim tables already print the corrected R17. With the printed −251.3 mm the focal length becomes −4202 mm at the short end and −1617 mm at the long end, so the corrected sign is the only workable reading. The certificate's other entries correct the abstract, the Example I table and the placement of rows in the claims.

The prescription has one cemented triplet (L2–L4) and five cemented doublets (L5–L6, L9–L10, L11–L12, L14–L15 and L17–L18). Each shared interface appears once. The 0.200 mm gap after L1 and the 0.100 mm gap between the compensator doublets are air.

The long-end shortfall of 0.95% is a property of the printed data, not a misprint: the four group focal lengths agree with the corrected patent values within 0.09%, the endpoint back focal distances differ by only 40 µm, and Example I shows the same pattern (1188.4 mm against a nominal 1200 mm). The d-line longitudinal spherical aberration of the full-aperture marginal ray is −0.115 mm at the short end and +0.082 mm at the long end, consistent with Fig. 5a.

The surfaces have positive edge thickness and stay clear of each other. The full f/8 axial bundle clears every rim at all 56 zoom settings, and the corner chief ray reaches the 35 mm frame at each of them. Outer pupil rays are vignetted at the corner, where the geometric illumination is about 61% at the short end and 60% at the long end.

**Iris and image plane.** Fig. 3 gives the long relay gap D24 without locating the diaphragm. The iris is placed 35.75 mm from either face, 228.568 mm behind R1. The paraxial stop radius for f/8 is 12.785 mm wide and 12.782 mm tele. The fixed film spacing of 140.2743 mm is the rounded mean of the two endpoint back focal distances; Nikon's drawing gives 140 mm.

**Intermediate zoom settings.** At infinity the fixed front group and relay leave 124.918 mm for the three moving air gaps. For each intermediate position of the variator, the position of the compensator is solved for paraxial focus on the fixed film plane. The 54 calculated settings are more closely spaced in variator travel toward the long end, where the compensator motion curves more sharply. On the unrounded focus curve, 55 settings give a minimum peak defocus of about 20.95 µm between settings. With 56, rounding each gap to 0.0001 mm preserves the sum and leaves at most 0.30 µm of defocus at a calculated setting. The maximum between adjacent calculated settings is 19.95 µm. At the two printed settings the paraxial image falls 20.006 µm behind the film plane and 20.062 µm in front of it, respectively. Their 40.068 µm separation sets a lower bound on the error achievable with one film plane.

**Focus.** The patent gives spacings only at infinity. `focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity; Nikon's 2.5 m minimum is recorded only as the product value.

**Glass.** The catalog equivalents apply to 13 elements and reproduce their printed indices and Abbe numbers within 0.00001 and 0.05, respectively. L2/L14, L4/L18 and L17 have three distinct pairs without a close catalog match. Nikon identifies ED glass in the lens. L2 and L14 are the probable positions from their common 1.48606 / 81.5 pair.

## Product Identification

The match to the pre-AI Zoom-Nikkor is strong. Nikon's May 1976 drawing shows the same sequence of 11 air-separated groups, including the front triplet, paired compensator doublets and the distinctive long gap within the relay. The four assemblies at the wide end fall within about 0.4 mm of their drawn positions. Nikon labels the optical stack 281 mm and the back focus 140 mm; the model gives 281.018 mm and 140.274 mm. The example states f = 180–600 mm, F/8 and 35 mm still cameras, and Nikon gives the same 18 elements in 11 groups and ED glass. The 1970 priority date precedes the 1975–76 introduction. Example I belongs to the larger 360–1200 mm f/11 design, with 20 elements in 12 groups. Cavina credits the design of both super-telephoto zooms to Soichi Nakamura, the patent's inventor. [Nikon drawing](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/zoomsMF/180600mmEDpreAIopt.gif) · [Nikon AI-S manual text](https://portablemedia.manualsonline.com/manuals/mfg/nikon/ed180600mm.html?p=10) · [Cavina](https://www.marcocavina.com/articoli_fotografici/Nikon_180-600_e_360-1200mm_zoom/00_pag.htm)

The pre-AI, AI and AI-S versions share the 18-element, 11-group optics. The pre-AI and AI lenses meter at the working aperture; the AI-S adds full-aperture metering, and its manual lists the TC-300 and TC-14 teleconverters as accessories. Nikon's 2.5 m minimum distance, which the manual describes as camera-to-subject distance, and Vink's 1:3 maximum reproduction describe the production lens; Example II gives spacings only at infinity. ## Corrections, 2026-11-25

- The working aperture was opened from f/11 at every zoom setting to the patent's f/8. The aperture scale runs f/8, 11, 16, 22 and 32, from Nikon's f/8–f/32 scale.
- The field extends from the former ±1° limit to the corner of the 36 × 24 mm frame at 21.63 mm image height, 6.82° at the short end and 2.06° at the long end, matching the f/8 plots of Fig. 5.
- The original estimated clear radii (38.0–40.8 mm in the front group, 14.6–16.0 mm in the variator, 15.1–15.5 mm in the compensator, 13.4–15.3 mm ahead of the iris space and 8.9–9.3 mm in the rear relay) held the lens to about f/9.2 at the f/8 setting and cut the axial beam at 20 surfaces. They were replaced by the Fig. 3 radii above. R22–R24 stand 0.4 mm above the central reading of the drawn edge, inside the line width, so that the f/8 axial beam passes.
- The 257 zoom settings, 255 of them calculated, were reduced to 56: the two printed settings and 54 calculated ones, which keep the paraxial image within 19.95 µm of the film plane between settings. Each gap is rounded to 0.0001 mm with the 124.918 mm sum preserved, and only the two printed settings carry labels.
- The film spacing of 140.27427 mm, the unrounded mean of the endpoint back focal distances, was rounded to 140.2743 mm. The iris stays at the middle of D24.
- The certificate's R17 correction to +251.3 mm, already applied in the first prescription, is recorded as a corrected erratum with the claim tables as evidence. The patent number takes the form US 3,743,384.
- The lens was identified as the Nikon Zoom-Nikkor ED 180-600mm f/8 for the Nikon F mount and 35 mm film, without the former "(1976 Pre-AI)" suffix because the AI and AI-S versions share the optics. The catalog records 180–600 mm, f/8, 18 elements in 11 groups, two ED elements and a 2.5 m minimum focus. The blade count is left out because the sources disagree (see Remaining Questions). The subtitle and specifications were rewritten in the maker-and-patent form. The elements are named L1–L18 with their forms and roles, the groups follow the patent's f1–f4, and the cemented members are C1–C6.
- Catalog equivalents were added for dispersion, keeping the printed nd and νd. The differences below are catalog minus patent.

## Remaining Questions

- Nikon's drawing shows slightly larger relay radii than Fig. 3, about 18.4 and 18.0 mm ahead of the iris space and 15.5 mm behind it. The production clear diameters, exact diaphragm position and film spacing are unknown.
- The patent leaves the intermediate zoom cam, the moving group or groups for close focus, and their travel to 2.5 m unspecified.
- Nikon's unshaded drawing cannot establish which elements carry ED glass. Cavina places them at L2 and L14, matching the two 1.48606 / 81.5 elements. The production glass types and individual partial dispersions of the five unmatched elements remain unknown.
- Vink's version-specific listing gives the early lens seven blades and the AI-S nine. Cavina gives nine in his account of the design, and de Stoutz counts nine on AI-S lens no. 174882. Nikon's AI-S manual describes the diaphragm as fully automatic and gives no count.
