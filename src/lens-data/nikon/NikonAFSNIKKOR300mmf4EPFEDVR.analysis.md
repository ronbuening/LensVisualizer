# NIKON AF-S NIKKOR 300mm f/4 E PF ED VR

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP 2015-102852 A\
**Application Number:** JP 2013-245879\
**Filed:** 2013-11-28\
**Published:** 2015-06-04\
**Inventor:** Makoto Fujimoto\
**Applicant:** Nikon Corporation\
**Title:** 光学系、光学機器、および光学系の製造方法 (Optical system, optical instrument, and method for manufacturing an optical system)\
**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 1 (第1実施例)

The prescription modeled here is Example 1 of JP 2015-102852 A. The patent describes a long-focus photographic optical system in which a diffractive optical element is positioned within the positive front group to combine chromatic correction with control of flare generated by oblique illumination of the diffractive structure (¶0019–0038). Example 1 is then specified as a positive first group G1 followed by a negative second group G2, with the diffractive surface on L14 and an internal-focus doublet LF in G2 (¶0041–0043).

The production correlation is treated as fixed for this analysis, but it remains an author/modeling correlation rather than a statement that Nikon has identified this patent example as the production prescription. Several independent features converge:

1. The patent example has a published focal length of 294 mm and FNO = 4.08, closely corresponding to the production lens's marketed 300 mm f/4 specification.
2. Example 1 contains 16 physical lens elements in 10 air-separated groups. Nikon specifies the production lens as 16 elements in 10 groups.
3. The example contains a contact multilayer diffractive structure on L14 and a very high-Abbe positive element L12. Nikon specifies one PF element and one ED element in the production lens. L12 is therefore treated as the probable ED element by correlation, not as a patent-stated glass identity.
4. The patent uses a single cemented internal-focus doublet LF that translates imageward for closer focus (¶0043), while Nikon identifies the production lens as an internal-focusing design.
5. The patent specifically proposes L25+L26+L27 as a lateral vibration-reduction group (¶0074); the production lens incorporates VR.
6. The patent was filed in November 2013. Nikon announced the AF-S NIKKOR 300mm f/4E PF ED VR in January 2015, before the Japanese application was published in June 2015.

No focal-length scaling is applied. The model uses **s = 1**: radii, thicknesses, image-space distances, and diffractive coefficients remain at the Example-1 scale. The marketed 300 mm and f/4 values are retained separately from the exact modeled values, whose computed effective focal length is 293.914643 mm and whose authored wide-open f-number is 4.08.

## Optical Architecture

Example 1 is a two-group telephoto system: a positive G1 followed by a negative G2 (¶0041). G1 is itself divided into G1a and G1b by the longest air space within the first group (¶0042). The computed first-surface-to-image track is 190.450 mm, and the computed EFL is 293.914643 mm, giving a track/EFL ratio of 0.647977. Under the project definition this is a telephoto design because the ratio is below unity. The computed BFD/EFL ratio is 0.183803, so it is not a retrofocus system.

The optical sequence is:

- **G1a:** L11, cemented L12+L13, and L14 with the contact multilayer DOE on its image-side structure.
- **G1b:** cemented L15+L16.
- **G2:** translating focus doublet LF = L21+L22, the aperture stop, LC1 = L23+L24, cemented L25+L26, L27, LC2 = L28+L29, and L30.

The patent therefore contains 16 physical lens elements in 10 air-separated groups. The LensVisualizer data file has **18 model element entries** because the two optically active UV-curing resin media of the contact multilayer DOE are represented separately. They are not counted as additional physical lens elements in `elementCount`.

The front part of the system concentrates most of the positive power before the long internal air space. G1a has a computed EFL of 106.691266 mm. G1b is a nearly canceling cemented pair with only weak net positive power. The negative G2 then provides the telephoto shortening and contains the focusing, stop, correction, stabilization-candidate, and relay functions.

All subsystem focal lengths quoted below are air-bounded comparison values computed from the indicated surfaces. They characterize standalone or cemented subassemblies and are not additive in-situ powers of the complete lens.

The stop is at the patent's surface-16 station, between LF and LC1. Its **position** is source-published; its physical semi-diameter is not. The model infers a stop semi-diameter of 9.888248 mm so that the entrance pupil reproduces F/4.08. The resulting modeled entrance-pupil diameter is 72.037903 mm.

No geometric aspherical surface is present in Example 1 or in the data file. The special surface at surface 8 is diffractive phase data, not an aspheric sag surface; accordingly `asph` is empty.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L11 — Positive Meniscus

**nd = 1.4875, νd = 70.3. Glass: J-FK5 / 487703 class (HIKARI spectral proxy). f = +254.390 mm.**

L11 is the first positive collector in G1a. Its weak meniscus form starts the converging front-group action without requiring the front surface to carry all of the front-group power. The relatively low index and high Abbe number make its refractive contribution comparatively benign chromatically while leaving the stronger chromatic balancing to the cemented pair and the diffractive structure behind it.

The glass name is not a patent fact. The patent publishes only the refractive coordinates. J-FK5 is used in the data file as a current HIKARI spectral proxy whose coordinates closely match the stored nd/νd pair; the authored nC, nF, ng, and dPgF values are catalog-derived proxy data.

### L12 + L13 — Cemented Front Achromat

**L12 — Biconvex Positive: nd = 1.4978, νd = 82.6. Glass: J-FKH1 / 498826 class (HIKARI spectral proxy; ED-class). f = +140.506 mm.**\
**L13 — Biconcave Negative: nd = 1.5750, νd = 41.5. Glass: J-LF7 / 575415 class (HIKARI spectral proxy). f = -262.213 mm.**

L12 and L13 form the first cemented pair in G1a. Their standalone powers are opposite in sign, while the cemented pair remains net positive; the computed air-bounded EFL of the pair is +284.604 mm. This is the expected pattern for a positive achromatizing component: a high-Abbe positive member carries positive power while the more dispersive negative member supplies compensating chromatic power.

L12 is the strongest candidate for the production lens's single ED element because its patent coordinates, nd = 1.4978 and νd = 82.6, are distinctly high-Abbe and because Nikon specifies one ED element in the production lens. That mapping is an inference from the fixed production correlation. The patent does not identify the glass by catalog name or call L12 an ED material.

The data file uses J-FKH1 as the spectral proxy for L12 and records the proxy's nC/nF/ng values and dPgF = +0.0327. Those values permit a higher-fidelity model of the chosen proxy than an Abbe-only approximation, but they do not establish the production melt and are not used to claim an apochromatic designation.

### L14 Substrate and Contact Multilayer DOE

**L14 substrate — Positive Meniscus: nd = 1.5168, νd = 63.9. Glass: Unmatched (legacy 1.5168/63.9 crown; J-BK7A spectral proxy). f = +513.396 mm.**\
**DOE resin 1 — Diffractive Resin Layer: nd = 1.5278, νd = 33.4. Glass: Unmatched UV-curing DOE resin. Standalone lens focal length is not assigned.**\
**DOE resin 2 — Diffractive Resin Layer: nd = 1.5572, νd = 50.0. Glass: Unmatched UV-curing DOE resin. Standalone lens focal length is not assigned.**

L14 is the third positive lens in G1a. The patent places the DOE on the image-side structure of this element and describes a contact multilayer construction made from two different UV-curing resins, with an approximately 20 μm first-order rotationally symmetric grating (¶0042). The data model therefore retains both resin media as active optical entries rather than collapsing them into a generic cement layer.

The substrate itself is weakly positive when considered as an isolated refractive element. The important additional action comes from the phase surface at surface 8. With the authored diffractive phase enabled, the complete system computes to 293.914643 mm EFL; disabling the phase term while retaining the refractive media changes the computed EFL to 302.797346 mm. This isolates the substantial first-order contribution of the DOE without treating it as a fictitious refractive glass power.

The L14 substrate's stored nd/νd pair does not match current J-BK7A closely enough to justify naming it as that glass. The data file therefore uses `Unmatched (...)` for identity while retaining J-BK7A line data only as a disclosed spectral proxy. The two DOE resins have no invented nC/nF/ng/dPgF data.

### L15 + L16 — G1b Cemented Pair

**L15 — Negative Meniscus: nd = 1.9108, νd = 35.2. Glass: TAFD35 / 911353 class (HOYA spectral proxy). f = -85.128 mm.**\
**L16 — Positive Meniscus: nd = 1.4875, νd = 70.3. Glass: J-FK5 / 487703 class (HIKARI spectral proxy). f = +79.940 mm.**

L15 and L16 form G1b, the cemented pair behind the long G1a-to-G1b air interval. Their standalone powers are strong and of opposite sign. For the air-bounded cemented pair those powers nearly cancel: its computed EFL is +7212.948 mm. G1b is therefore not a weak pair because its individual surfaces are weak; it is weak **net** because substantial negative and positive powers cancel.

The dense, relatively dispersive L15 material and high-Abbe L16 proxy are consistent with an achromatizing role in the front system. The analysis does not assign them a separate production special-glass designation because Nikon's production specification identifies only one ED element and does not map it to a patent surface.

### L21 + L22 — Internal-Focus Doublet LF

**L21 — Biconvex Positive: nd = 1.6200, νd = 36.4. Glass: J-F2 / 620364 class (HIKARI spectral proxy). f = +145.149 mm.**\
**L22 — Biconcave Negative: nd = 1.6968, νd = 55.5. Glass: J-LAK14 / 697555 class (HIKARI spectral proxy). f = -53.732 mm.**

L21 and L22 form the cemented focusing doublet LF. Although L21 is positive as an isolated element, the stronger negative L22 makes the cemented pair net negative; its computed air-bounded EFL is -87.007 mm. This distinction is important because focusing behavior follows the compound group, not the sign of either element in isolation.

The patent explicitly states that LF translates toward image space when focusing from infinity to a finite-distance object (¶0043). The focus motion is therefore source-constrained even though the patent does not publish a finite-focus spacing table. The close-focus endpoint in the data file is a constrained reconstruction discussed below rather than a transcription of a patent row.

### L23 + L24 — LC1 Achromat Behind the Stop

**L23 — Negative Meniscus: nd = 1.9108, νd = 35.2. Glass: TAFD35 / 911353 class (HOYA spectral proxy). f = -47.677 mm.**\
**L24 — Positive Meniscus: nd = 1.5750, νd = 41.5. Glass: J-LF7 / 575415 class (HIKARI spectral proxy). f = +45.671 mm.**

LC1 is the first cemented pair behind the aperture stop. The two standalone elements again carry substantial opposite powers. Their computed cemented EFL is +2087.867 mm, so the pair is weakly positive as a unit despite the much stronger individual element powers.

Its position immediately behind the stop makes LC1 part of the rear correction structure rather than a primary positive collector. The patent labels this pair as the first achromatic lens LC1 (¶0043), which is the source basis for treating its chromatic role as deliberate rather than inferred only from the glass coordinates.

### L25 + L26 and L27 — Negative Rear Set and Preferred VR Group

**L25 — Biconvex Positive: nd = 1.7283, νd = 28.4. Glass: J-SF10 / 728284 class (HIKARI spectral proxy). f = +45.401 mm.**\
**L26 — Biconcave Negative: nd = 1.7292, νd = 54.6. Glass: 729546 class (J-LAK18 spectral proxy). f = -28.246 mm.**\
**L27 — Biconcave Negative: nd = 1.7292, νd = 54.6. Glass: 729546 class (J-LAK18 spectral proxy). f = -55.998 mm.**

L25 and L26 are cemented, with the negative member dominating the pair; their computed air-bounded EFL is -79.612 mm. L27 is a separate negative element immediately behind the pair. The three-element L25+L26+L27 set has a computed air-bounded EFL of -32.691 mm in the nominal axial prescription.

Paragraph 0074 identifies these three elements as the preferred candidate for lateral displacement or swing to compensate image shake, with LC1 and LC2 placed on either side. This is an in-situ functional role, not a statement that the axial prescription changes during VR operation. The LensVisualizer data therefore keeps their axial spacings fixed and does not invent a VR decenter magnitude or alternate axial state.

### L28 + L29 — LC2 Rear Achromat

**L28 — Biconvex Positive: nd = 1.5481, νd = 45.5. Glass: J-LLF1 / 548455 class (HIKARI spectral proxy). f = +56.808 mm.**\
**L29 — Negative Meniscus: nd = 1.7880, νd = 47.4. Glass: J-LASF014 / 788474 class (HIKARI spectral proxy). f = -85.687 mm.**

L28 and L29 form the second patent-labeled achromatic pair LC2 (¶0043). Their computed cemented EFL is +164.152 mm. LC2 follows the preferred VR set and provides a positive rear correction component before the final relay element.

The J-LASF014 label is used as a close current spectral proxy for L29's patent coordinates, not as a claim that the historical or production element used that catalog melt. The same provenance distinction applies to the other HIKARI class labels in the file.

### L30 — Final Biconvex Positive Relay

**nd = 1.4875, νd = 70.3. Glass: J-FK5 / 487703 class (HIKARI spectral proxy). f = +71.371 mm.**

L30 is the final positive refractive element. It follows LC2 and precedes the 54.00 mm patent rear air spacing. Its symmetric biconvex form supplies substantial positive standalone power at the back of G2, helping complete the relay to the image plane after the strongly negative middle portion of the second group.

The final 54.00 mm spacing is retained as the patent's air-equivalent rear distance. No sensor cover glass, filter, inactive dummy plane, or flare-cutter plane is inserted into the sequential prescription.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent uses **internal focusing** with a single rigid cemented doublet, LF = L21+L22. Paragraph 0043 states the direction unambiguously: LF moves toward the image plane when focusing from infinity toward a nearer object. No patent table gives a close-focus spacing, intermediate object distance, focus travel, or magnification.

The data file therefore carries the focus status **CONSTRAINED_RECONSTRUCTION**. The close endpoint is solved from Nikon's production minimum focus distance of 1.4 m measured from the focal plane, while preserving the patent's one-group mechanism and a fixed image plane. Nikon's published 0.24× maximum reproduction is not used as the solving constraint; it is an independent corroborating value.

| Quantity | Infinity | Reconstructed close focus |
|---|---:|---:|
| Gap after surface 12, ahead of LF | 6.540000 mm | 20.029772 mm |
| Gap after surface 15, behind LF | 23.410000 mm | 9.920228 mm |
| Sum of the two adjacent gaps | 29.950000 mm | 29.950000 mm |

The equal and opposite gap changes correspond to **13.489772 mm imageward LF travel**. The independently solved paraxial conjugate gives |m| = 0.243580 at the reconstructed close endpoint, compared with Nikon's rounded 0.24× production specification. This agreement supports the reconstruction but does not turn it into patent-published focus data.

No additional floating group is introduced. The model does not infer focus breathing beyond what follows from the reconstructed paraxial state, and it does not use a second independent movement to force the magnification result.

## Diffractive Optical Element and Flare-Control Strategy

The defining feature of the patent is not merely the presence of a DOE, but its **axial placement**. The patent explains that oblique light striking the edge structure of a diffractive element can generate higher-order diffracted or scattered light that reaches the image as flare (¶0021–0024). Conditions on Ldoe/f place the DOE farther toward image space than in the comparison arrangement, reducing the amount of large-angle incident light that reaches the diffractive structure while retaining useful diffractive correction (¶0023–0025).

In Example 1, the DOE lies on the image-side structure of L14 in G1a. The patent uses a contact multilayer arrangement of two UV-curing resins and describes a first-order rotationally symmetric grating with approximately 20 μm height (¶0042). This contact multilayer structure is represented explicitly by the two resin media in the data model.

The source has an internal coefficient-indexing contradiction. Equation (B) prints coefficients on h², h⁴, h⁶, ... as C2, C4, C6, ...; Equation (C) then calls C1 the lowest-order coefficient; Table 1 publishes only C1 = -4.058E-05 and C2 = -3.974E-09. Independent first-order reproduction resolves the intended table mapping: the table's C1 must multiply h² and its C2 must multiply h⁴. The data file therefore uses:

- radial power 2: C1 = -4.058e-5 mm⁻¹;
- radial power 4: C2 = -3.974e-9 mm⁻³.

This is a documented source correction, not a geometric-asphere conversion. The quadratic phase term contributes +8.116e-5 mm⁻¹ paraxial diffractive power at λ = λ0. Turning that phase contribution off changes the system EFL from 293.914643 mm to 302.797346 mm.

The patent does not give a numeric λ0. The data file uses **587.6 nm** as a disclosed d-line modeling reference. At the verification condition λ = λ0, the numerical value of λ0 cancels from the first-order power check, so this inference does not alter the reproduced d-line paraxial power.

A second source inconsistency concerns Ddoe. The patent defines Ddoe as the distance from the rear focal position of G1a to the diffractive surface, but its published Ddoe = 87.77 mm matches the computed rear BFD measured from surface 9. The literal distance to diffractive surface 8 is 88.074490 mm. The data model does not alter the prescription to force either interpretation; both versions satisfy the patent's condition (3) at the printed precision.

## Chromatic Correction Strategy

The patent combines ordinary refractive achromatization with a diffractive contribution whose dispersion acts in the opposite order from ordinary refraction (¶0002–0005). In Example 1, the principal chromatic structures are the high-Abbe L12 positive element paired with L13 in the front group, the DOE on L14, the strongly canceling L15+L16 pair, and the patent-labeled rear achromats LC1 and LC2.

The production lens is specified by Nikon as containing one ED glass element and one PF element. In the fixed correlation used here, the PF relationship is direct because Example 1 explicitly contains the DOE on L14. The probable mapping of the production ED element to L12 is inferential: its νd = 82.6 is the exceptional refractive coordinate in the prescription and its J-FKH1 proxy is ED-class, but neither the patent nor Nikon's product specification identifies the corresponding surface number.

The authored spectral proxies allow LensVisualizer to distinguish the modeled C, d, F, and g-line behavior of the selected current catalog proxies rather than treating every glass as an Abbe-only material. That improvement is a modeling feature of the data file. It does not imply that the actual production lens used those precise current catalog formulations.

## Image Stabilization

Nikon's production lens incorporates Vibration Reduction. The patent provides a corresponding optical mechanism in ¶0074: at least part of the lens system behind the aperture stop may be moved with a component perpendicular to the optical axis or swung to compensate image shake, and the preferred set is L25, L26, and L27. The patent further places LC1 and LC2 on the object and image sides of that preferred set.

The data file records this role in the relevant element annotations but does not encode a VR displacement. The patent supplies no numerical lateral shift, swing angle, decentered prescription, or altered axial spacing for the stabilized state. Introducing one would therefore be an unsupported reconstruction. The nominal sequential model represents the centered VR state only.

Those checks use the current geometry rules rather than the obsolete universal sd/|R| < 0.90 or element semi-diameter ratio ≤ 1.25 rules.

Several model quantities are necessarily inferred rather than source-published. The patent gives the stop station but not its diameter; the authored 9.888248 mm stop semi-diameter is solved from F/4.08. No sensor cover, filter, inactive dummy plane, or flare-cutter plane is present in the selected Example-1 prescription, so none is removed through a compensating plate transformation. The final 54.00 mm rear spacing remains the patent's air-equivalent back-focus interval. There is no geometric asphere and therefore no aspheric coefficient scaling or conic conversion. With s = 1, no dimensional or diffractive coefficient scale transform is applied.
