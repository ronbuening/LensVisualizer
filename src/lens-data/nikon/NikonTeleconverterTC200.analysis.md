# NIKON TELECONVERTER TC-200

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 4,154,508, _Rear conversion lens_\
**Inventors:** Soichi Nakamura\
**Assignee:** Nippon Kogaku K.K.\
**Filed:** March 2, 1977, with Japanese priority of March 11, 1976\
**Published:** May 15, 1979\
**Example details:** Example 11, Figure 11 and claim 21. Dimensions are in millimetres at 1:1 scale.

Soichi Nakamura completed the TC-1 optics in November 1975. Nikon retained them in May 1977 when it introduced the TC-200 with automatic maximum-aperture indexing (AI). Its later TC-201 added AI-S coupling. On the Nikon FA, the TC-200 selects the normal exposure program and the TC-201 selects the high-speed program. [Nikon, Tale 73, §III](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/information/story/0073/) · [Nikon FA manual, programmed exposure](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/02711/02711.pdf)

Nikon's section shows four small front elements and three larger rear elements. L3/L4 and L5/L6 share cemented interfaces, with a thin biconcave singlet immediately behind the rear doublet. Figure 11 shares these features, and its outer vertices span the 38.74 mm marked on the TC-200 drawing. Together they strongly support Example 11 as the production design. Nikon gives no numerical production prescription. [Nikon, Tale 73, Fig. 1](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/information/story/0073/) · [Dimensioned TC-200 drawing](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/teleconverter/TC200.gif)

The front cemented pair helps distinguish examples of the same 2× design. Example 10 cements the middle two front elements, and Example 12 cements three. The five-element Example 2 uses a 300 mm master and matches the longer TC-300. Example 11 uses a 51.6 mm f/2 Gauss-type master. Neither the example nor Nikon's section identifies an asphere or an ED element. [US 4,154,508, Figures 2 and 9–12 and description](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4154508A/en)

## Optical Architecture

The TC-200 sits in the converging beam behind the taking lens. Its negative power pushes the image farther back and approximately doubles its size. The five air-separated components form two functional groups. The forward group (L1–L4) diverges, with a focal length of −50.49 mm. Across a 14.29 mm air space, the rearward group (L5–L7) converges, with a focal length of +208.11 mm. Together they give −76.49 mm. Nakamura uses the high-index negative glasses and the strong, low-index positive element in the rear group to control the converter's contribution to field curvature. [US 4,154,508, description of the diverging and converging groups](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4154508A/en)

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Focal lengths below are for each element in air. A cemented pair has a single refracting interface between its two glasses.

### L1 — negative entrance meniscus

nd = 1.79668, νd = 45.4, unmatched glass, f = −45.28 mm. Its shallow convex entrance and more strongly curved exit start the divergence of the forward group.

### L2 — biconvex positive singlet

nd = 1.59507, νd = 35.6, unmatched glass, f = +25.29 mm. This air-spaced positive element separates the entrance meniscus from the negative doublet behind it.

### L3 and L4 — forward cemented doublet

L3 has nd = 1.77279, νd = 49.4, unmatched glass and f = −20.18 mm. Its biconcave shape supplies most of the doublet's negative power. L4 has nd = 1.59507, νd = 35.6, unmatched glass and f = +96.19 mm. This thin positive meniscus shares its front face with L3. The pair has a focal length of −25.42 mm.

### L5 and L6 — rear cemented doublet

L5 has nd = 1.79631, νd = 40.8, unmatched glass and f = −76.02 mm. This negative meniscus is only 1.5 mm thick on axis. L6 has nd = 1.51823, νd = 59.0, Hoya E-C3 equivalent and f = +29.37 mm. Its 10 mm thickness and strongly convex rear face distinguish it in both drawings. The doublet has a focal length of +44.24 mm.

### L7 — biconcave rear singlet

nd = 1.78797, νd = 47.5, Hoya TAF4 equivalent, f = −55.13 mm. Equal and opposite 87.1 mm radii give this element a symmetric profile. A 0.1 mm air gap separates it from L6. Its negative power partly offsets the preceding doublet.

## Glass Identification and Selection

Example 11 prints a refractive index and an Abbe number for each element and leaves the reference wavelength unspecified. The glass comparison assumes the helium d line. Five elements have no sufficiently close catalog match. Hoya E-C3 agrees with L6 to the printed index and 0.04 in Abbe number. Hoya TAF4 agrees with L7 to 0.00003 in index and 0.01 in Abbe number. These catalog glasses supply dispersion curves. The suppliers and melts used by Nikon remain unknown.

## Focus Mechanism

The converter elements stay fixed. The taking lens supplies the focusing movement and the iris. Nikon specifies the same closest focusing distance and twice the reproduction ratio of the taking lens. The patent gives only the infinity arrangement. At finite focus, the model follows the taking lens's travel and adds the converter's 44 mm extension to distances measured from the image plane. For the 180 mm f/2.8 ED, this gives 1.84 m at the lens's 1.80 m setting. [Nikon catalog, TC-200 specifications](https://device.report/m/e6e67eaa9bf9b326e2a5cd5070757e862fb41e48d7bc53a4be5be7c751ace45b)

## Compatible Lenses and Metering

Nikon specifies the TC-200 for most AI lenses through 200 mm on its 35 mm F-mount cameras. Its f/2–f/32 coupling range gives effective apertures of f/4–f/64, a two-stop loss. The automatic diaphragm permits full-aperture metering on AI bodies and stop-down metering on non-AI bodies. Nikon specifies multilayer coating on the optical surfaces. [Nikon catalog, teleconverters and technical specifications](https://device.report/m/e6e67eaa9bf9b326e2a5cd5070757e862fb41e48d7bc53a4be5be7c751ace45b)

Nikon lists the 24 mm f/2, 35 mm f/2, Micro-Nikkor 55 mm f/2.8, 135 mm f/2 and f/2.8, 180 mm f/2.8 ED, 200 mm f/2 IF-ED and the 50–135 mm f/3.5 and 80–200 mm f/4 zooms. Its chart excludes the AI 80–200 mm f/4.5. It warns of possible vignetting or loss of image quality at close focus with the 135 mm f/2.8, and uneven exposure at high shutter speeds past f/11 with the 135 mm f/2, 180 mm ED and 200 mm f/2. [Nikon compatibility chart, pp. 2–4](https://service.nikonrepair.eu/article_files/46818/TC_EN.pdf)

The AF-I 300 mm f/2.8D, AF-I 400 mm f/2.8D and AF-S 80–200 mm f/2.8D require manual focusing with the TC-200. Nikon warns of possible vignetting or loss of image quality with the 300 mm, the zoom and the Reflex-Nikkor 500 mm f/8. Nikon also lists faster lenses, including the AI 50 mm f/1.2 used within the coupling range. The model limits combinations to refracting lenses with a maximum aperture of f/2 or slower. [Nikon catalog, aperture coupling](https://device.report/m/e6e67eaa9bf9b326e2a5cd5070757e862fb41e48d7bc53a4be5be7c751ace45b) · [Nikon compatibility chart, pp. 1–3](https://service.nikonrepair.eu/article_files/46818/TC_EN.pdf)

## Group Powers and Field Curvature

Nakamura defines an equivalent index for each group from its paraxial power and Petzval sum. The limits are 1.5 to 2.9 for the diverging group and 0.6 to 1.3 for the converging group. The corrected prescription gives 2.3692 and 0.7698, respectively, close to the printed 2.369 and 0.7701. The rear group's positive Petzval contribution largely offsets the front group's negative contribution. [US 4,154,508, equations (5)–(9) and Embodiment 11](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4154508A/en)

## First Radius and Mount Spacing

Example 11 and claim 21 print +37.545 mm for the first converter radius. At the printed 0.6917 mm separation from the master, that radius gives 113.02 mm at f/4.38. With +35.545 mm, the pair gives 103.29 mm at f/4.003, 2.0017× and 41.73 mm back focus. The patent specifies 103.2 mm at f/4 and 2×. Column 4, under Embodiment 1, gives a front-group focal length of −50.489 mm, a principal-plane separation of 20.258 mm and a converter focal length of −76.492 mm. Corrected Example 11 gives −50.489, 20.257 and −76.492 mm. The printed radius gives −47.30, 20.59 and −70.21 mm. The corrected Petzval sum is −0.002118 mm⁻¹ against the Example 11 value of −0.00211 mm⁻¹. The TC-200 drawing independently marks a focal-length magnitude of 76.5 mm and 147.6 mm between focal points, against 147.63 mm calculated. [Patent, cols. 4–6 and 9, claim 21](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4154508A/en) · [Dimensioned TC-200 drawing](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/teleconverter/TC200.gif)

Nikon specifies 44 mm between mounts. At that extension, the calculated original image plane lies 36.865 mm behind the first converter vertex and the new image lies 42.125 mm behind the last. With the patent master the result is 103.56 mm at f/4.014 and 2.0069×. The clear radii are estimates from Nikon's section. Surfaces 2, 9 and 10 end inside the intersections of adjacent spherical faces. Placing the master iris halfway across its 9.4 mm central air space gives a calculated f/2 radius of 9.08 mm. The axial beam clears the glass and calculated relative illumination at the 35 mm frame corner is 53.6%.
