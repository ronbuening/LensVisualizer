## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 4,398,807 A
**Title:** Zoom lens
**Inventor:** Yutaka Iizuka
**Assignee:** Nippon Kogaku K.K.
**Priority:** 26 February 1980, Japan
**Filed:** 17 February 1981
**Granted:** 16 August 1983
**Embodiment analyzed:** Unnumbered embodiment, prescription on printed page 5

This is a patent-derived model provisionally associated with the Nikon Series E 75–150mm f/3.5. The association is inferred, not a manufacturer confirmation of an unchanged factory prescription. The original source contains one unnumbered embodiment; it is not “Example 1.”

Several independent features support the association:

1. Nikon identifies Iizuka as the production lens designer and dates the Japanese release to May 1980, consistent with the patent's earlier Japanese priority.
2. The actual Nikon and patent sections share twelve glass elements in nine air-separated components, partitioned into four functional groups of 3 + 3 + 2 + 4 elements.
3. The source specifies the same constant f/3.5, approximately 75–150mm range, first-group focusing, and 1m minimum distance.
4. The second group moves imageward and the third reverses direction during zooming, as Nikon describes.
5. The unaltered table reproduces its focal-length and group-power values. A closely related twelve-element alternative, US4223981A, uses different designers and approximately 2.5× focal ranges; uniform scaling cannot make those ratios equal to this design's approximately 2× range.

Scaled to the patent's vertex track, Nikon's section also matches the printed wide-angle spacings closely (for example the 42.0mm relay air space) and draws the iris about 1.4mm ahead of L9 with an opening of about 13.7mm radius. A second, fixed mark about 20mm behind L10 has an opening of about 9.7mm radius, close to the f/3.5 axial beam at that point, and reads as a flare stop. The patent's table gives neither position nor any clear diameter. The model does not assert a serial-specific factory configuration.

The contemporary brochure lists a full field of 31°40′–17°, while the patent's infinity plots extend to 33.2°–16.0°. Exact production field-of-view agreement is therefore not established. Measurement conventions or production revisions remain possible explanations, rather than verified reconciliations.

## Optical Architecture

The four functional groups have positive, negative, positive and positive power. Their calculated focal lengths are approximately +103.016, −35.355, +88.074 and +95.281mm. “Nine groups” in the catalog counts air-separated glass components; it does not replace the patent's four moving or stationary functional groups.

The first group is a positive singlet followed by a cemented doublet. It focuses by moving as a unit. The negative second group is a cemented doublet followed by a separate negative singlet and acts as the variator. The positive third group is a cemented doublet and acts as the compensator. The fourth group contains four separated relay elements and remains stationary during the source zoom motion.

The three printed intergroup gaps are retained exactly. Their sums are 34.360, 34.360 and 34.362mm, leaving the fourth group stationary relative to the front vertex at the printed precision. Seven intermediate stations, at about 82, 90, 98, 116, 126, 135 and 143mm, are solved rather than printed: each keeps the printed gap sum and places the paraxial image on the fixed image plane. With only the three printed rows, linear interpolation would defocus the model by up to 1.46mm near 128mm; with the solved stations the largest residual is 0.09mm. The solved stations are first-order states, not a recovered production cam law. They place the third group's reversal (minimum d13) between about 106 and 116mm.

The first-to-last vertex track is approximately 119.260mm at wide and middle and 119.262mm at telephoto. Including the printed 40.165mm image gap gives a first-vertex-to-image distance of about 159.425–159.427mm. These vertex distances are distinct from the mechanical barrel length. The model is not telephoto by a strict total-track/EFL criterion, even at its longest station.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are standalone thick-element powers in air, calculated from the stored radii, thickness and index. Cemented elements do not have these standalone powers in situ. Their actual shared interfaces use the adjoining glasses' indices.

### L1 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.62041, νd = 60.3. Glass: source-only crown-type coordinate; supplier unverified. f = +133.877mm.

This positive singlet forms the front component of the focusing group. The patent constrains its power relative to the complete first group and constrains the bending of its two surfaces.

### L2 and L3 — Cemented Positive/Negative Doublet

L2: nd = 1.62041, νd = 60.3. Glass: source-only crown-type coordinate; supplier unverified. f = +102.083mm.

L3: nd = 1.75520, νd = 27.5. Glass: source-only flint-type coordinate; supplier unverified. f = −127.186mm.

L2 is biconvex and L3 biconcave in their standalone surface descriptions. Their shared fourth surface is concave toward the object. Together they form the positive rear component of Group I, whose net focal length is approximately +467.476mm. The patent specifically constrains that component's power and the refractive power of its cemented interface to improve close-distance behavior.

### L4 and L5 — Cemented Variator Doublet

L4: nd = 1.78470, νd = 26.1. Glass: source-only high-index flint-type coordinate; supplier unverified. f = +66.825mm.

L5: nd = 1.51680, νd = 64.2. Glass: source-only crown-type coordinate; supplier unverified. f = −44.324mm.

The positive meniscus L4 and biconcave L5 form the front part of the negative variator. Positive standalone power for L4 does not make the complete second group positive. The source indices, shared interface and surrounding negative elements determine its group behavior.

### L6 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.62041, νd = 60.3. Glass: source-only crown-type coordinate; supplier unverified. f = −50.502mm.

This separated negative singlet completes Group II. The air gap between L5 and L6 is the binding aperture constraint at telephoto. Its curved facing surfaces limit the modeled clear pupil even though their axial vertices remain separated.

### L7 and L8 — Cemented Compensator Doublet

L7: nd = 1.62041, νd = 60.3. Glass: source-only crown-type coordinate; supplier unverified. f = +40.945mm.

L8: nd = 1.75520, νd = 27.5. Glass: source-only flint-type coordinate; supplier unverified. f = −76.383mm.

The biconvex positive and negative meniscus form the positive third group. Its motion compensates for the image-plane displacement caused by the variator; the source gap schedule retains its reversal rather than forcing a single monotonic motion.

### L9 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.62041, νd = 60.3. Glass: source-only crown-type coordinate; supplier unverified. f = +71.594mm.

L9 begins the stationary relay. The inferred stop is placed in the preceding source gap, without altering that gap's total length.

### L10 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.75520, νd = 27.5. Glass: source-only flint-type coordinate; supplier unverified. f = −354.437mm.

This weak biconcave singlet precedes the relay's long air space. The 1.0mm air gap it shares with L9 limits how large both rims can be drawn.

### L11 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.74950, νd = 34.96. Glass: source-only flint-type coordinate; supplier unverified. f = −93.087mm.

This element begins the rear relay pair. The long preceding air space holds the fixed flare stop drawn in Nikon's production section; the model does not include it.

### L12 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.62588, νd = 35.6. Glass: source-only flint-type coordinate; supplier unverified. f = +105.054mm.

The final positive singlet completes the relay and delivers rays into the source image gap. Its numerical prescription is retained rather than adjusted to eliminate the source-rounded longitudinal residuals.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The source gives six distinct nd/νd pairs without supplier or glass trade names. Modern catalog screening finds several compatible equivalents, including N-SK16/S-BSM16 for the repeated 1.62041/60.3 coordinate, SF4-type entries for 1.75520/27.5, and BK7-family entries for 1.51680/64.2. Such equivalence is not evidence of a 1980 supplier, formulation or melt.

The data preserves every source index and Abbe number while using N-SK16 (L1/L2/L6/L7/L9), E-FD4 (L3/L8/L10), SF56A (L4), N-BK7 (L5), LAFN7 (L11) and F1 (L12) as coordinate-compatible spectral proxies. All twelve glasses use catalog dispersion. These curves do not identify the 1980 supplier, formulation or melt. No ED, anomalous partial dispersion or apochromatic performance is established by these coordinates alone.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent explicitly moves only the first positive group for focusing. It supplies close-distance aberration plots and magnifications but no numerical close-focus spacings.

The model's close-focus states are solved for a 1000mm distance from object to the fixed image plane. Only d5 increases, moving Group I objectward relative to the remaining optics. The paraxial solution with compact travel gives:

| Source station | Reconstructed Group I travel | Calculated magnification | Printed close-plot magnification |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 75.024mm | 14.688991mm | −0.103839 | −0.104 |
| 106.106mm | 14.699453mm | −0.146870 | −0.147 |
| 150.086mm | 14.703842mm | −0.207724 | −0.208 |

The compact solution reproduces the rounded plot magnifications. A second mathematical solution requires roughly 722.5mm of travel and is incompatible with the compact production system. The same solve at the seven intermediate zoom stations gives 14.688–14.689mm of travel. These spacings are reconstructed, not factory measurements, and focus settings between the endpoints interpolate spacings linearly.

## Stop, Clear Apertures and Model Limits

The patent gives no stop position. The model places the stop in d13, 1mm before surface 14, fixed relative to Group IV; splitting the gap preserves each printed total. Nikon's production section draws the iris about 1.4mm ahead of L9 with an opening of about 13.7mm radius, in agreement with this position and with the 13.69mm stop radius obtained by setting the source f/3.5 at the wide station.

Clear semi-diameters follow the element proportions of patent Figure 1 and Nikon's section, with the front singlet at 25.5mm and the following doublet at 23.6mm, matching Figure 1's taller front outline, within the edge-thickness and air-gap limits of the prescription. The L5/L6 rims are the exception: both drawings show them about as large as the rest of Group II, but the facing surfaces of the 3.8mm air gap between them nearly touch at that height. Their facing rims are 13.1mm, the smallest tenth-millimetre values that pass the stated axial beam.

The facing surfaces retain about 0.0917mm positive clearance at the 13.1mm rims. A lens-specific gapSagFrac of 0.978 admits the measured 97.59% sag intrusion, consistent with Figure 1's nearly meeting rims; the source gap is unchanged. The modeled axial beam now passes at f/3.5 throughout the zoom. This does not establish unvignetted off-axis transmission or a published physical clear aperture.

At the patent's infinity fields of 16.6°, 11.5° and 8.0°, chief rays clear every rim while peripheral rays are vignetted. Corner throughput depends on the inferred rims and is not measured production vignetting.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The model reproduces the patent's infinity focal lengths (75.024, 106.106 and 150.087mm) and all seven of its conditional expressions, with source radii, indices and printed gaps unchanged.

Calculated back focal distances are 40.165, 40.177 and 40.198mm against the single printed Bf = 40.165mm, which the model keeps as its image distance. The 0.012mm and 0.033mm residuals at the middle and telephoto stations are consistent with the table's printed precision, including the coarsely printed d17 = 42.0mm.

The seven intermediate zoom stations and all close-focus spacings are calculated states, not patent rows or factory data.
