# Nikon AF-S VR Nikkor 200mm f/2G IF-ED

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 7,411,745 B2  
**Filed:** 3 May 2005  
**Granted:** 12 August 2008  
**Priority:** 7 May 2004  
**Inventor:** Mitsuaki Wada  
**Assignee:** Nikon Corporation  
**Title:** Large-Aperture-Ratio Internal Focusing Telephoto Lens  
**Example details:** Table 3 (columns 9–10), Figure 7. Kept at the patent's scale, f = 195 mm.

This is a patent design associated with the original AF-S VR Nikkor 200mm f/2G IF-ED, not a confirmed factory prescription; Nikon does not link the patent to the lens. The example's f = 195 mm, F/1.84 and 12.62° full field stand against the marketed 200 mm, f/2 and 12°20′. The association rests on these points:

1. **Construction.** Nikon lists 13 elements in 9 groups. Examples 1, 3, 5 and 6 have that count; Example 2 has 12 in 8 and Example 4 has 12 in 9.
2. **Special glass.** Nikon lists three ED elements and one Super ED element. Example 3 is the only example with three νd 82.6 elements and one νd 91.0 element. Example 1 has four near νd 82.5, Example 5 two of each, and Example 6 three νd 91.0 and one νd 82.5.
3. **Positions.** Nikon's construction drawing marks ED on the first two positive elements and on the rear element of the second doublet, and Super ED on the meniscus. In Example 3 these are L1, L2, L6 and L4.
4. **Mechanism.** The patent focuses with G2 and reduces vibration with G3; Nikon's lens is internal-focus with VR.
5. **Close focus.** Example 3's printed close state computes to about 1.897 m, against Nikon's 1.9 m (see Focus Mechanism).
6. **Timing.** The priority date is 7 May 2004, and Nikon announced the lens on 28 May 2004.

The match is at family level. Example 1 is the only example printed at F/2.0, and Example 6's closest-distance β is nearer Nikon's 1:8.1 than Example 3's.

## Optical Architecture

Thirteen spherical elements form nine air-separated groups in a positive-negative-positive telephoto. G1 has six elements with two cemented pairs, G2 has four elements in two cemented pairs, and G3 has three singlets. The iris follows G2. G2 moves along the axis to focus, and G3 moves across the axis to reduce vibration. The patent gives G2 the job of correcting G1's spherical aberration and astigmatism and the whole system's chromatic aberration (col. 4).

The production lens also has a meniscus protective glass and a 52 mm drop-in filter. Neither is in the patent, and neither is modeled. The decentering of G3 is not modeled either.

## Focus Mechanism and Image Plane

G2 moves toward the image as one unit to focus; G1 and G3 stay fixed along the axis. Production focus is driven by Nikon's Silent Wave Motor. The patent prints both ends of the travel, and the model uses them:

| Spacing | Infinity | Closest distance |
|---|---:|---:|
| β | 0 | −0.1258 |
| D10 | 5.5000 mm | 15.0995 mm |
| D16 | 15.5239 mm | 5.9244 mm |
| BF | 59.4575 mm | 59.4386 mm |

G2 travels 9.5995 mm. The patent prints no object distance. At the printed close spacings, the paraxial object-to-image distance computes to about 1.897 m at β = −0.1257, against Nikon's 1.9 m minimum focus, which the model uses as its close limit. Positions between the two printed states are interpolated.

The printed close row belongs to the patent example, not to the production lens. Example 3's β is 1:7.95 and Nikon states 1:8.1; Example 6's printed β of −0.1235 is closer to Nikon's figure.

The printed infinity spacings put the image plane 217.3714 mm behind surface 1. The paraxial back focus computes to 59.461 mm, and the model keeps the printed 59.4575 mm.

## Stop and Clear-Aperture Assumptions

Surface 17 is the printed aperture stop, 133.5139 mm behind surface 1 and 1.20 mm ahead of surface 18. Its radius of 18.207 mm follows from F/1.84.

The patent prints no clear apertures. The semi-diameters are inferred from the entrance pupil, Figure 7 and element edge thickness, and they are model values, not production measurements. L1 is square-cut at 53.5 mm on both faces, the height Figure 7 draws, which leaves about 0.5 mm over the 52.99 mm entrance-pupil radius.

With these rims the model passes about 47% of the full pupil at the 6.3° corner at F/1.84. That figure follows from the inferred rims and is not measured production vignetting.

The patent's half-field is 6.31°, and Figure 8A gives Y = 21.6 mm. The model reaches 21.60 mm, just short of the 21.65 mm full-frame corner reference.
