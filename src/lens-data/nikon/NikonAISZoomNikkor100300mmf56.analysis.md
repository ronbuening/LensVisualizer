## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 4,641,928 A
**Application Number:** US 593,924
**Priority:** March 30, 1983 (Japan 58-54913)
**Filed:** March 27, 1984
**Granted:** February 10, 1987
**Inventor:** Yoshinari Hamanishi
**Assignee:** Nippon Kogaku K.K.
**Title:** *Four-Group Telephoto Zoom Lens*
**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 2

The prescription modeled here is the second numerical embodiment of US 4,641,928 A. The patent describes a four-group zoom in which a positive first group G1 provides focusing, a negative second group G2 acts as the variator, a positive third group G3 compensates image-plane displacement, and a positive fourth group G4 acts as the relay system. Example 2 is the f/5.6 embodiment shown in Fig. 2A and repeated numerically in Claim 12. [1, Abstract; cols. 1–6; Fig. 2A; Claim 12]

The correlation to the production NIKON ZOOM-NIKKOR 100-300mm f/5.6 is treated as the fixed project identification rather than as a manufacturer-confirmed patent attribution. The display name follows Nikon's instruction-manual title without adding an unsupported AI-S qualifier. Several independent facts converge:

1. Nikon specifies the production lens as a 100–300 mm f/5.6 lens with 14 elements in 10 groups. [2, Specifications]
2. Patent Example 2 is a 14-element, 10-group design with published zoom endpoints of 102 and 294.784 mm at f/5.6. [1, cols. 5–8; Fig. 2A; Claim 12]
3. Nikon specifies a Nikon F mount, a 135-format picture angle of 24°20′ to 8°10′, normal focusing to 2 m, and a 100 mm-only macro range to 0.71 m. [2, Specifications]
4. The patent's half-angle range of 12.2° to 4.1° corresponds to full angles of 24.4° to 8.2°, closely matching Nikon's marketed picture-angle range. [1, Example 2; 2, Specifications]
5. The patent describes ordinary focusing by G1 and a special short-focal-length close-up mode in which G1 is moved farther objectward while G2 is coupled objectward with it. [1, col. 4]

No uniform scaling is applied. The design focal lengths remain the patent-derived 101.993503 and 294.768552 mm values computed from the final data file; 100–300 mm remains the separate marketed range.

The patent prints `r19 = +29.949 mm` in the Example 2 table and again in Claim 12. That radius is inconsistent with the patent's own G41, G4, endpoint focal-length, and back-focus invariants. Solving that single radius independently against six first-order constraints gives 26.949035–26.949522 mm (mean 26.949316 mm), so the data file uses `r19 = +26.949 mm` at the patent's radius precision. All other radii, thicknesses, refractive indices, and Abbe numbers are transcribed from the printed Example 2 values.

## Optical Architecture

The optical train is an all-spherical, four-group positive-negative-positive-positive zoom: G1(+), G2(−), G3(+), G4(+). It contains 14 elements in 10 air-separated groups. G1 contains a cemented positive component L11 followed by L12; G2 contains two cemented negative components L21 and L22; G3 is the cemented positive component L3; and G4 is a five-element relay divided into the positive front subgroup G41 and the weakly negative rear subgroup G42. [1, cols. 1–6; Fig. 2A]

At the two published infinity zoom states, G1 and G4 remain stationary. From the 102 mm state to the 294.784 mm state, G2 moves 52.300 mm toward the image and G3 moves 15.898 mm toward the image. The three published zoom gaps preserve a constant sum of 61.786 mm, leaving the final glass surface and image plane fixed. Only the two endpoints are source-published spacing states. LensVisualizer linearly interpolates between them for the continuous zoom control; those intermediate spacings are visualization interpolation, not additional patent data, and no intermediate reversal is asserted.

The patent's design strategy centers on a strongly negative G2 divided into two cemented negative components rather than one highly powered variator component. The text explicitly connects the relative powers and index differences of L21 and L22 to the control of zoom-dependent coma, astigmatism, spherical aberration, distortion, chromatic variation, and Petzval balance. G3 then compensates the image-plane shift generated by the variator, while the positive G4 relay provides the final imaging power. [1, cols. 1–3]

The patent title calls the family a telephoto zoom. Under the project's strict geometric classification, the long endpoint is telephoto because total track divided by EFL is 0.804133, while the wide endpoint is not because the ratio is 2.324001. Neither endpoint is retrofocus because back focal distance is smaller than EFL in both states.

The patent does not publish an aperture-stop station or stop diameter. The data model therefore inserts exactly one neutral `STO` as an explicit modeling inference in the 49.000 mm air space between r20 and r21, 3.000 mm behind r20 and 46.000 mm ahead of r21. Its 11.387365 mm semi-diameter is solved from the final prescription, giving modeled endpoint f-numbers of 5.599962 and 5.600038. A production-lens teardown places the iris mechanism in the rear optical assembly, which supports the general region but not the exact modeled axial station. [3]

The patent likewise publishes no clear semi-diameters. The authored apertures are modeling values constrained by paraxial and off-axis ray envelopes, the Fig. 2A section, and production mechanical dimensions. They are not patent measurements. No cover glass, filter, inactive dummy plane, folded path, or aspherical surface is included in the active optical model.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L11 — Cemented Positive Front Component of G1

**L11a:** nd = 1.71736, νd = 29.48. Glass: 717295 — flint class (vendor unresolved). Standalone f = −197.811316 mm.

**L11b:** nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.24. Glass: 487702 — S-FSL5 catalog equivalent (production supplier unspecified). Standalone f = +140.761830 mm.

L11a is a negative meniscus and L11b is a biconvex positive element. Their cemented combination is nevertheless positive, with a computed net focal length of +483.224613 mm. That distinction matters: neither standalone element power describes the behavior of the cemented component.

The patent specifically uses a negative-plus-positive cemented component at the object side of G1 and relates the L11/L12 power ratio to short-distance aberration stability. It also notes that the Abbe-number separation across L11 is important to first-group chromatic correction. [1, col. 3]

### L12 — Positive Meniscus in G1

**nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.24. Glass: 487702 — S-FSL5 catalog equivalent (production supplier unspecified). Standalone f = +176.748356 mm.**

L12 is the rear positive component of the focusing group. Together with the cemented L11 component it produces a computed G1 focal length of +129.910300 mm. The patent's supplemental conditions constrain L12 relative to the complete G1 so that focusing does not drive spherical aberration and astigmatism excessively at the ends of the zoom range. [1, col. 3]

The ordinary focus mechanism moves G1 as a group rather than changing the L11–L12 internal spacing. The data file therefore treats L11 and L12 as a fixed internal assembly at the published infinity zoom states.

### L21 — First Cemented Negative Variator Component

**L21a:** nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.36. Glass: 805254 — dense flint class (vendor unresolved). Standalone f = +83.462277 mm.

**L21b:** nd = 1.71700, νd = 48.12. Glass: 717481 — LAF3 catalog equivalent (production supplier unspecified). Standalone f = −40.359567 mm.

L21a is a positive meniscus and L21b is biconcave negative. Cemented together they form the first negative component of G2, with a net focal length of −78.320794 mm. The component's negative net power is therefore substantially different from the positive standalone power of L21a.

The patent makes this cemented surface part of the variator's aberration-balancing strategy. Its index difference is bounded so that the cemented-interface power does not push coma, astigmatism, or Petzval correction too far in either direction as focal length changes. [1, col. 2]

### L22 — Second Cemented Negative Variator Component

**L22a:** nd = 1.71300, νd = 53.97. Glass: 713540 — J-LAK8 catalog equivalent (production supplier unspecified). Standalone f = −43.622977 mm.

**L22b:** nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.36. Glass: 805254 — dense flint class (vendor unresolved). Standalone f = +91.597298 mm.

L22a is biconcave negative and L22b is a positive meniscus. Their cemented net focal length is −83.526950 mm. L21 and L22 together form G2, whose computed focal length is −39.245801 mm.

The second variator doublet reverses the sign order used in L21: negative glass comes first and positive glass second. The patent explicitly couples the L21/L22 power ratios and index differences to the control of zoom-dependent aberration. In particular, it warns that excessive negative Petzval balance and chromatic spherical aberration can result when the second cemented component is driven outside the stated ranges. [1, cols. 1–3]

### L3 — Cemented Positive Compensator G3

**L3a:** nd = 1.51835, νd = 60.34. Glass: 518603 — BALK3 catalog equivalent (production supplier unspecified). Standalone f = +53.290885 mm.

**L3b:** nd = 1.71736, νd = 29.48. Glass: 717295 — flint class (vendor unresolved). Standalone f = −109.717294 mm.

L3a is biconvex positive and L3b is a negative meniscus. Their cemented combination has a computed focal length of +102.998896 mm; because this cemented component is the entire G3, the cemented-component and group powers are identical.

G3 is the compensator. During zooming it moves less than G2 and is assigned by the patent to maintain the image plane at a fixed position while the variator changes the system focal length. [1, Abstract; cols. 1–2]

### L41 — Biconvex Positive Front Element of G41

**nd = 1.50137, νd = 56.46. Glass: 501565 — K10 catalog equivalent (production supplier unspecified). Standalone f = +72.113136 mm.**

L41 begins the front relay subgroup G41. In Example 2 the patent specifies G41 as a biconvex positive lens followed by a negative meniscus and a positive meniscus. The three air-separated elements combine to a computed G41 focal length of +124.448648 mm. [1, col. 5]

L41 therefore contributes strong positive standalone power, but its in-situ relay behavior is determined jointly with L42 and L43 rather than by that standalone value alone.

### L42 — Negative Meniscus in G41

**nd = 1.71736, νd = 29.48. Glass: 717295 — flint class (vendor unresolved). Standalone f = −92.613991 mm.**

L42 supplies negative power within the otherwise positive G41 front relay subgroup. Its use between two positive relay elements gives the subgroup an additional degree of freedom relative to a simple positive relay pair.

The patent ties the G41/G4 power ratio to compactness and to the correction of spherical aberration, Petzval sum, astigmatism, and coma. That statement concerns the subgroup's combined in-situ behavior, not L42's standalone focal length. [1, col. 5]

### L43 — Positive Meniscus Completing G41

**nd = 1.51680, νd = 64.12. Glass: 517641 — BK7-class crown (vendor unresolved). Standalone f = +259.494446 mm.**

L43 is the final positive meniscus of G41. Its front surface is r19, the one corrected source value in the prescription. The data uses +26.949 mm rather than the patent's printed +29.949 mm; the stated standalone focal length and every G41/G4 result in this analysis are based on that documented correction.

With L41 and L42, L43 completes the +124.448648 mm G41 subgroup. The comparatively weak standalone power of L43 is therefore not a measure of the relay subgroup's total positive power.

### L44 — Negative Meniscus Beginning G42

**nd = 1.71300, νd = 53.97. Glass: 713540 — J-LAK8 catalog equivalent (production supplier unspecified). Standalone f = −51.301805 mm.**

L44 begins the rear relay subgroup G42 and is followed, across a 0.2 mm air gap, by L45. The patent describes L44 in the second embodiment as a negative meniscus convex toward the object side. [1, col. 5]

Although L44 has substantial standalone negative power, G42 must be interpreted as an air-spaced two-element subgroup rather than a cemented component.

### L45 — Biconvex Positive Final Element

**nd = 1.57501, νd = 41.55. Glass: 575416 — J-LF7 catalog equivalent (production supplier unspecified). Standalone f = +68.028394 mm.**

L45 is the final positive element. Together with L44 it gives G42 a computed focal length of −264.821732 mm, so the rear subgroup is weakly negative as a unit. The complete five-element relay G4 remains positive at +164.783517 mm because the positive G41 subgroup dominates the combined relay power.

The patent's condition on `f41/f4` formalizes this balance between a relatively strong positive front relay subgroup and the complete positive relay. [1, col. 5]

## Focus Mechanism

The patent uses front-group focusing for ordinary subject-distance adjustment. G1 moves toward the object for shorter focus while the internal geometry of G1 remains fixed. [1, Abstract; cols. 1, 3]

For close-up photography at the short focal-length end, the patent describes a second regime: after G1 reaches the ordinary short-distance state, G1 moves farther objectward and G2 is mechanically associated with it and also moves toward the object. This is a coupled two-group close-up mechanism rather than a simple extension of the ordinary G1-only focus motion. [1, col. 4]

Nikon's production manual specifies normal focusing from infinity to 2 m. At the 100 mm setting only, the lens enters a macro range from 2 m to 0.71 m, and zooming is disabled in that macro range. Focus and zoom share one mechanical ring: rotation focuses and axial sliding changes focal length. [2, pp. 7–9]

The patent does not publish numerical focus travels or close-focus air-space tables for Example 2. The data therefore uses `NO_INTERNAL_RECONSTRUCTION`: the three zoom-variable gaps contain identical infinity/close members at each zoom endpoint, and 0.71 m is retained only as manufacturer metadata. No internal 2 m or 0.71 m focus state is synthesized from object distance alone.

## Aberration-Correction Strategy

The patent begins from the difficulty of making a roughly 3× four-group zoom compact without allowing a strong negative variator to drive Petzval sum, spherical aberration, astigmatism, coma, chromatic aberration, and their zoom variation out of balance. It also notes that excessively strong G1 power can worsen short-distance aberration at the long end and that a compact telephoto-type relay can create zonal spherical and chromatic problems. [1, col. 1]

The design answers those constraints in three linked ways. First, G2 is split into two cemented negative components with opposite positive/negative ordering. Second, G1 divides its positive power between a weak cemented positive component and a separate positive meniscus, with explicit power-ratio constraints intended to moderate focus-induced aberration changes. Third, G4 uses a multi-element relay whose G41/G4 power ratio is bounded to retain compactness without sacrificing correction freedom. [1, cols. 1–5]

The corrected final model gives a total surface-by-surface Petzval sum of +0.000390261 mm⁻¹ using `φ/(n·n′)`. The small residual follows from strong positive and negative contributions distributed across the four groups rather than from any single nominally "flat-field" element.

## Verification Summary

The exact US 4,641,928 patent PDF was inspected at high resolution on PDF page 5, Fig. 2A. Comparing optical rims rather
than brackets, labels, or leader lines supports enlarging L12 to a 30.0 mm semi-diameter and reducing the G3 compensator
to 13.5 mm. The revised silhouette follows the source more closely while retaining the required ray and geometry margins.

The final data file was retraced independently with reduced-angle sequential rays and an ABCD matrix cross-check. The two methods agree to floating-point precision for both published zoom endpoints.

|---|---:|---:|

| Patent EFL | 102.000000 mm | 294.784000 mm |
| Computed BFD | 67.041979 mm | 67.043524 mm |
| Patent Bf | 67.047000 mm | 67.047000 mm |
| Modeled f-number | 5.599962 | 5.600038 |
| Total track | 237.033000 mm | 237.033000 mm |

The residuals are consistent with the precision of the printed radii and refractive indices. The final arrays also reproduce the patent's published group/component focal-length invariants within source-aware tolerances.

Because the aperture stop and clear apertures are inferred rather than published, their geometry was checked separately. The revised apertures pass the surface-domain, positive-edge-thickness, rim-slope, shared-gap, and image-circle checks. The full on-axis marginal fan clears every modeled surface at both zoom endpoints. At the default telephoto off-axis sample, peripheral vignetting begins at the front exterior aperture rather than at a cemented interface.

The stop and semi-diameter results establish a self-consistent visualization model; they do not convert those inferred dimensions into patent facts. No asphere departure, scaling transform, cover-glass correction, or internal close-focus reconstruction is applicable to this data set.
