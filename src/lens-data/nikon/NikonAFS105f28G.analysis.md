# Nikon AF-S VR Micro-Nikkor 105mm f/2.8G

## Patent Reference

**Patent:** US 7,218,457 B2, "Interchangeable Lens"

**Inventor:** Takayuki Sensui

**Assignee:** Nikon Corporation

**Filed:** September 29, 2005 (priority JP 2004-289051, September 30, 2004)

**Granted:** May 15, 2007

US 7,218,457 B2, Takayuki Sensui, Nikon, Example 3. Table 3 is PDF page 24 (printed columns 13–14); Figures 9A–9C are PDF page 10. The source design is 104.00 mm, f/2.88. The retail name is an association, not proof that every published dimension matches production. In particular, this prescription has fourteen elements in eleven air-separated components, including three cemented doublets. A production specification with twelve groups does not justify adding an unsupported flat element.

## Architecture

G1 comprises L1–L4, G2 comprises L5 and doublet L6/L7, G3 comprises L8 and doublet L9/L10, and G4 comprises stabilization doublet L11/L12 plus singlets L13/L14. The stop is source surface 14. Surface 15 is an air reference plane, not glass or a filter. All 27 source rows are retained; there is no cover-glass or filter block to render or convert. The prescription is spherical throughout.

## Glass

The patent gives nd/vd, without individual catalog names or partial dispersions. Catalog counterparts are explicitly qualified; supplier identity and production chemistry remain unspecified. L2 uses BACD4, whose Abbe number differs slightly from the source. The source nd/vd values are preserved. The unsupported L8 dPgF=0.028 override and inferred APD claim are removed; compatible catalog dispersion now governs without an invented patent anomaly.

## Focus and aperture

All three source focus stations are retained, including the previously omitted nominal half-magnification state:

| BF | 53.12017 | 52.48194 | 52.48113 |

Source object distances D0 are 240.735 and 151.487 mm from the first vertex. Adding the corresponding full optical tracks gives image-to-object distances 0.39909544 and 0.30984563 m. These define the reciprocal-distance slider positions 0, 0.7763697576 and 1. The aperture series begins at f/2.88.

Relative to G1, G2 moves 18.289 mm imageward and G3 moves 14.484 mm objectward. The source text describes G1, G4 and the stop as fixed, but its BF changes 0.63904 mm. Preserving the numerical table gives that common camera-relative displacement: near G1/G4 +0.63904, G2 +18.92804 and G3 −13.84496 mm. Middle-state rounded gaps add a further 0.001 mm difference between front and rear reference groups. The source inconsistency is disclosed rather than silently flattening BF.

Independent paraxial evaluation gives EFL 103.99888, 96.02557 and 75.85044 mm across the three stations. Evaluating the source object distances gives magnifications approximately −0.49221 and −0.98786, rather than exactly −0.5 and −1. Solving the stored prescription for exact conjugacy instead gives total distances 0.40213019 and 0.31077608 m. These differences remain source/model limitations; the published distances and gaps are retained.

The patent explicitly varies stop diameter with focus and gives finite-conjugate NA values 0.14 and 0.11, but no physical iris schedule. The viewer does not model that focus-dependent iris diameter. Its finite-focus aperture and related analyses therefore cannot be read as reproductions of the patent NA. Lateral stabilization motion is also not simulated; the displayed VR doublet is centered. There is no zoom control.

## Semi-diameter notes

Figures 9A–9C were inspected from the original at 600 dpi. The valid Figure 9A measurement excludes drawing leaders and uses optical rims. Measured-to-authored rim ratios are approximately 0.83–1.18, below the strong-revision threshold. Existing estimated SDs are retained; the drawing does not publish exact clear apertures. Surface and image-circle audits pass, and all three source stations plus half slider have no hidden rim trimming.
