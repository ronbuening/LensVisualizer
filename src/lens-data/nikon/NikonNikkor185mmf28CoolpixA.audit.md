# NIKON NIKKOR 18.5mm f/2.8 (Nikon COOLPIX A) — audit

## Source

[US 9,915,803 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9915803B2/en), Atsushi Suzuki, Nikon Corporation.
PCT filed February 14, 2014, with priority from JP 2013-029580 and JP 2013-029581 of February 19, 2013.
U.S. grant issued March 13, 2018. Example 1 is specified by Table 1 (PDF page 29), the sag equation on
PDF page 28, and Figures 1–2 (PDF pages 3–4).

## Transcription

The 17 numbered surface rows reproduce the printed radii and axial steps, including stop 5 and the two cemented
interfaces at surfaces 7 and 10. All nine d-line index and Abbe-number pairs agree with Table 1. The linear
dimensions use the same ×18.5 scale. The four aspheric coefficients use the inverse scale factor to the power
of their order minus one. The patent's sag formula uses $\kappa$ in the curvature term, so its $\kappa=1$ is
the spherical base $K=0$.

The filter plates are 1.84445 mm and 0.70485 mm thick, with physical air gaps of 13.35515 mm before,
1.48185 mm between, and 0.70485 mm after them. Example 1 gives a single infinity prescription without
variable spacings.

The stop semi-diameter is 3.935 mm from the paraxial entrance pupil required by f/2.88. The drawn opening
has a semi-diameter of about 3.98 mm. The surfaces retain physical clearance at their edges and show no
overlap. The rims admit the f/2.88 axial beam and the chief ray at the nominal APS-C corner (14.175 mm,
38.0°), the actual DX corner (14.145 mm, 38.0°), and the patent field (14.615 mm, 38.87°).
Marginal rays in the outer field are vignetted, mainly at surfaces 6–8. At 38.0°, about 96.8% of the
pupil area is transmitted before accounting for geometric illumination falloff.

## Product Identification

Atsushi Suzuki's role in the COOLPIX A design, the priority date, and the seven-element, five-group wide-angle
lens with one aspherical element support a probable link to the camera. Examples 1, 3, 5, 6, 7, and 8 share
that count. Examples 2 and 4 use eight elements in six groups. The published product information does not
establish which of the six matching designs was manufactured. Nikon also states that the production camera
has no optical low-pass filter. The filter plates come from the patent's optical system.

Sources include [Nikon's announcement](https://www.nikon.com/company/news/2013/0305_cpx_01/),
[Nikon's specifications](https://nij.nikon.com/products/lineup/compact/a/spec.html),
[DC Watch's designer interview](https://dc.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/news/interview/593500.html), and
[DPReview's construction description](https://www.dpreview.com/reviews/1257143006/nikon-coolpix-a/).

## Corrections

### 2026-11-24

- Table 1's dimensional prescription is reproduced at the stated scale. The image plane follows the printed
  final gap. Figure 1 supplies the rims, and the printed f/2.88 sets the widest aperture for the calculation.
- Nikon's seven-blade iris, marked f/2.8, and f/22 minimum opening describe the camera. Its 0.10 m macro
  autofocus limit is a distance from the front lens surface. The optical prescription covers infinity focus.
- The named glass equivalents preserve the printed coordinates. Relative to Table 1, J-LASF016 differs by
  0.00 in νd, J-LASF08A by +0.03, S-TIM35 by −0.002, J-LASKH2 by +0.01, and BSC7 by 0.00 at the catalog's
  stated precision. The catalog nd difference is no more than 0.000003 for those glasses.

## Remaining Questions

The production cross-section, the focusing motion and travel, the degree of iris rounding, and the composition
of the production sensor stack remain undocumented in the cited material. L1 has no matching Hikari, Ohara, or Hoya entry. Schott
N-BALF4 lies near its printed coordinates. L7 has no matching catalog entry. The exact clear apertures and
iris diameter are absent from Table 1, so the drawn rim heights remain approximate.
