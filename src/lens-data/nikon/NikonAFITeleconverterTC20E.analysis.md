## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 5,253,112 A  
**Filed:** 1992-11-12  
**Granted:** 1993-10-12  
**Priority:** 1991-11-18  
**Inventors:** Kenzaburo Suzuki; Yoshinari Hamanishi  
**Assignee:** Nikon Corporation  
**Title:** Rear Conversion Lens  
**Example details:** Example 1, Table 2 and Fig. 1.

The prescription represents the detachable rear conversion lens, with the original Nikon AF-I Teleconverter TC-20E as the selected production correlation. Three observations support that association:

1. Nikon is the patent assignee, and the selected example belongs to the nominal 2× rear-converter category.
2. The original Nikon product specification and Example 1 both have seven elements in six air-separated groups, with no aspherical or ED elements identified in the manufacturer's specification.
3. The positive–negative–positive functional-unit arrangement and central cemented pair agree with the selected original patent figure. These structural observations are compatible with the production category, but are not a unique fingerprint: the patent's other examples share its gross element/group count.

The exact production prescription has not been confirmed by Nikon. Example 1 is the selected embodiment, not a conclusion derived by tuning its numbers to a marketed specification. The original model must also be distinguished from the later TC-20E III, for which Nikon documents a different construction. [1, front page, Fig. 1 and Table 2; 2; 3]

## Optical Architecture

The all-spherical rear converter operates in the converging beam behind a master lens. Its seven elements form six air-separated groups and three functional units. The central negative cemented pair distinguishes this arrangement from a single negative attachment; weak positive power ahead of it and positive net power behind it distribute the refraction along the converter. [1, cols. 3–8, Fig. 1]

The computed converter effective focal length is -96.597469 mm and its first-to-last vertex track is 46.0 mm. The functional-unit focal lengths are G1 +765.810971 mm, G2 -38.505990 mm, and G3 +87.455379 mm. These are thick-unit paraxial powers, including their internal spacings; adding isolated element powers is not a substitute. The converter has no independent aperture stop or meaningful standalone taking-lens f-number.

G1 contains positive L1, negative L2 and positive L3; its small net positive power reflects strong internal cancellation. G2 is the negative cemented L41/L42 pair. G3 combines positive L5 with negative L6 and remains net positive. The patent describes the inter-unit gaps as part of the balance among spherical aberration, coma, astigmatism, back focus and rear-element diameter. This is the patent's group-level design rationale, not an independently measured aberration contribution assigned to a single element. [1, cols. 3–6, conditions (1)–(5)]

The modeled object includes only the detachable converter. The source master lens, both of its protective plates and its source stop are retained in analytical test fixtures. They are not an application host hidden inside the converter data. No converter plane is removed and no air-equivalent replacement or scale change is made. The converter radii, spacings, indices and Abbe numbers remain literal; the analytical master placements are distinguished below.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are computed for each thick element in air at the native d line. For the cemented pair they describe isolated elements, not the refraction of the common glass-to-glass interface in situ. Optical types and labels follow the final data.

### L1 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.60342, νd = 38.12. Glass: F5 (SCHOTT catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +88.6 mm.

L1 is the positive entrance element of G1. Its biconvex form supplies the first part of the alternating positive–negative–positive sequence. The patent constrains the first element's shape through condition (8); its role must be interpreted together with L2, L3 and the spacing to G2. F5 matches the printed nd exactly; its catalog νd is 38.03 against the printed 38.12, so the label is a catalog equivalent with the supplier unresolved. [1, cols. 5–6, condition (8), Table 2]

### L2 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.79668, νd = 45.42. Glass: Unmatched (high-index glass nd = 1.79668, νd = 45.42). f = -22.1 mm.

L2 supplies the negative member of G1. Its relatively strong isolated power is opposed by the two positive neighbors, leaving the complete unit only weakly positive. As one of the negative converter elements, it participates in the patent's minimum-Abbe-number condition. The source coordinates are preserved despite the absence of an exact identified catalog glass. No individual chromatic or coma correction is inferred from its negative sign alone. [1, conditions (3), (6), Table 2]

### L3 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.59507, νd = 35.51. Glass: Unmatched (flint nd = 1.59507, νd = 35.51). f = +31.4 mm.

L3 closes G1 with positive power. Its short positive focal length and finite spacing from L2 are essential to the unit's net power; the following gap separates G1 from the negative central unit. The relatively dispersive glass is retained as unmatched rather than replaced by a nearby catalog coordinate. The patent discusses correction at the unit level, so a separate measured aberration role is not asserted. [1, conditions (1), (3), Table 2]

### L41 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.84042, νd = 43.30. Glass: Unmatched (high-index glass nd = 1.84042, νd = 43.30). f = -17.9 mm.

L41 is the negative component of the central cemented L4 pair. It has the highest printed refractive index among the negative converter elements and sets the maximum-index quantity in condition (7). The patent links its high-index negative-glass requirement to reducing the total Petzval sum. This does not by itself establish flat-field performance or identify a supplier. [1, col. 6, conditions (6)–(7), Table 2]

### L42 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.57501, νd = 41.55. Glass: J-LF7 (HIKARI catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +35.7 mm.

L42 is the positive cemented partner of L41. Its standalone positive power does not make the pair positive: the shared interface refracts directly from L41 glass into L42 glass, and G2 remains net negative. The pair must be calculated as a cemented unit. J-LF7 matches the printed nd, with catalog νd 41.51 against 41.55; the label is a catalog equivalent, not a supplier identification. [1, Fig. 1, Table 2]

### L5 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.24. Glass: S-FSL5 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +41.8 mm.

L5 is the positive component of G3 and uses the largest printed Abbe number in this prescription. S-FSL5 is the catalog equivalent at this coordinate, with supplier and production composition unconfirmed. Neither the catalog association nor the low dispersion alone demonstrates ED branding, anomalous partial dispersion or apochromatic performance. G3's positive net power includes its interaction with L6 and the intervening air gap. [1, condition (5), Table 2; 4]

### L6 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.74810, νd = 52.28. Glass: E-LAKH1 (HIKARI catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = -74.9 mm.

L6 supplies the rear negative component of G3. The complete rear unit remains positive despite this isolated negative element. HIKARI's discontinued E-LAKH1 (nd 1.748099, νd 52.305) sits at this coordinate; the label is a catalog equivalent, not a supplier identification. A field-flattener role is not assigned solely from the negative power or rear position; the supported curvature discussion concerns the computed sum of all surface Petzval contributions. [1, conditions (5)–(7), Table 2]

The printed d-line coordinates are authoritative. The archived search covers OHARA, HOYA, HIKARI, SCHOTT, CDGM and SUMITA, including retained obsolete rows where supplied. A catalog candidate is evidence of proximity, not evidence of the original supplier. The distinct glass coordinates and their dispositions are:

For example, J-F5 has νd 38.03 against the source L1 value 38.12. The OHARA AGF S-FSL5 row gives νd 70.236252, which rounds to the source 70.24; the retained workbook instead lists 70.23. These differences remain catalog observations rather than prescription corrections. [4–9]

The runtime resolves L1 to SCHOTT F5, L42 to HIKARI J-LF7, L5 to OHARA S-FSL5 and L6 to HIKARI E-LAKH1; the three Unmatched elements, L2, L3 and L41, use an Abbe normal-line approximation. Those catalog or normal-line channels are spectral proxies. The patent supplies no measured nC, nF, ng or ΔPgF for these glasses, and the model adds none. No supplier identity, secondary-spectrum measurement or APO performance follows from this reconstruction.

The patent's chromatic constraint places a lower bound on the Abbe numbers of the negative elements; it does not replace the missing partial-dispersion information. Its separate maximum-index condition addresses Petzval balance. [1, col. 6, conditions (6)–(7)]

## Focus Mechanism

The converter is fixed. No internal focusing group, zoom group, stabilization group, variable spacing or converter focus travel is reconstructed. The host supplies focusing and the aperture stop. Nikon states that the host's minimum focusing distance is retained. The patent provides the fixed source master for analysis, not a numerical converter focus-motion law. No motor type or finite-distance internal motion is inferred. [1, Fig. 5 and Tables 1–2; 2]

## Verification Summary

### Reference planes and the two placement branches

The literal Table 1 master computes to EFL 293.385738 mm and back focal distance 78.188746 mm, measured from its last surface, the rear face of its protective plate. The patent's printed junction d0 is 30.0 mm from that face to the first converter vertex. The adopted converter field masterImageDistanceMm is 48.626 mm, a derived distance from the first converter vertex to the master beam's virtual object in air. It is not a printed master back focus. The current composer therefore produces 29.562746 mm at the junction. Both branches retain the printed final gap of 57.75 mm. [1, Tables 1–2]

### Inferred clear apertures and physical geometry

No numerical converter clear apertures are published. Every modeled semi-diameter is inferred from an original-aspect Fig. 1 optical-cap endpoint pair; mechanical steps and bevel shelves are distinguished from the optical boundary. Fig. 1 draws L5 with a flat land joining both faces, so its rear surface 11 shares the front surface's 17.5 mm rim. In the cemented L4 pair the figure shows L42 standing slightly above L41; with one shared interface rim, the model keeps L41 square at 13.8 mm and lets L42 rise to 14.2 mm at its rear face. Exact spherical-domain, rim-slope, shared-band edge-thickness and air-gap-extremum checks pass under the unchanged default geometry policy. No gap-sag, rim-angle or layout override is used. The actual renderer reports zero face trimming for both placement branches.

The analytical master also requires inferred rims. Fig. 5 and the source f/2.9 axial envelope support revising the initial R9 estimate from 30.0 mm to 30.2 mm, above its computed floor of 30.1168515 mm. R12 changes from 24.0 mm to 24.4 mm above its 24.3472292 mm floor; its square-rimmed R13 partner shares the chosen rim. These are explicit inference corrections, not changes to published radii, spacings or glasses. The earlier clipped trials are retained as failed observations.

The source stop is positioned as published, 19.2 mm ahead of R23. Its runtime physical semi-diameter is calibrated to 17.268479794 mm for the printed f/2.9 master and is preserved by composition. The patent does not independently publish that diaphragm diameter.

At f/2.9, the fifth off-axis ray in each placement is physically vignetted by the master front section; it has no launch or intersection failure. Ghost continuation does not turn it into transmitted light.

The f/2.8 rows are a separate faster-than-published master stress test. Each branch has 64 physically master-blocked axial rays. A separate analytical continuation of all 1,025 launched axial rays finds no converter clipping; those blocked rays are not counted as transmitted by the composed system. Accordingly, minHostFno = 2.8 is only a bounded analytical axial-cone qualification. It is not a full-system f/2.8 qualification, a universal-host claim, or proof that no faster host could work.

The two f/2.8 stress-viewer failures mean one fifth off-axis sample in each of the two placement branches. Each branch retains its five-ray denominator: four clear, one failed. On that single failed ray, physical clipping at master R1 and R2 precedes a noBracket intersection failure at R3, before the converter. These overlapping event flags describe the same launched ray, not additional failed rays. The exclusive count assigns it to failed rather than physical-only clipped. Both failures remain explicit observations; no failed mandatory source f/2.9 sample is silently excluded.

### Compatibility and limits of interpretation

The converter is not universal: it mounts only on catalog lenses that declare teleconverter acceptance. Of those, it attaches only to the AF-I Nikkor 300mm f/2.8D IF-ED, the catalog lens closest to the patent's 294 mm f/2.9 master; the converter lists the other accepting Nikon F lenses as excluded. That exclusion is a conservative release choice, not a claim of mechanical incompatibility, and it does not contradict Nikon's documented dedicated-lens compatibility.

The computed results establish the stated source-condition and converter-only checks within their inferred-aperture and spectral limits. They do not establish production MTF, full-field illumination, APO correction, or performance with an unspecified real host.
