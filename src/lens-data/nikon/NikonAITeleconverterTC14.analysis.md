# NIKON AI TELECONVERTER TC-14

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 4,206,971, *Rear Conversion Lens*\
**Inventors:** Yoshinari Hamanishi and Soichi Nakamura\
**Assignee:** Nippon Kogaku K.K.\
**Priority:** 29 July 1977, Japan\
**Filed:** 27 July 1978\
**Published:** 10 June 1980\
**Example details:** First Embodiment, Figure 3 and claim 4. Figures 5A–5C show aberrations with the 200 mm f/2 master lens.

Nikon introduced the AI TC-14 in fall 1978 for its 35 mm F-mount cameras. The factory section shows five singlets. Its ten axial vertices agree with Embodiment 1 to about 1.1 pixels RMS across 178 pixels at a common scale. The 0.200 mm air space between L4 and L5 distinguishes this design from Embodiment 2, which cements that pair and has nine refracting surfaces. The section and the dimensions strongly identify Embodiment 1 with the original TC-14. [Nikon dealer catalog](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00594/00594.pdf#page=107) · [Nikon construction drawing](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/teleconverter/TC14.gif) · [Converter patent](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4206971)

Nikon gives an overall length of 33.5 mm and an extension of 22 mm. The calculated flange-to-flange distance is 22.033 mm. With Nikon’s 46.5 mm flange-to-film distance, the first glass vertex lies 2.47 mm ahead of the host-side flange, or 24.50 mm ahead of the camera-side flange. MIR lists 24.5 mm of extension without identifying the forward measuring point. [Nikon dealer catalog](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00594/00594.pdf#page=107) · [Nikon flange dimensions](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00765/00765.pdf) · [MIR specifications](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/teleconverter/index3.htm)

Nikon used its NIC multicoating on the original. By 1984 the range included the AI-S TC-14A for shorter lenses, the TC-14B for longer lenses and the TC-14C supplied with the 300 mm f/2 IF-ED. [Nikon dealer catalog](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00594/00594.pdf#page=107) · [Nikon’s 1984 lens catalog](https://www.base2photo.com/pdf/Nikkor_Lenses_Sales_Brochure_1984/Nikon_Nikkor_Lenses_Sales_Brochure_1984.pdf) · [MIR’s TC-14 history](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/teleconverter/index3.htm)

## Optical Architecture

The TC-14 intercepts the converging beam from its host and moves the image farther back. The patent divides its five air-spaced elements into two groups by function. The three forward elements have weak net negative power (f = −886.02 mm). The rearward negative-positive pair supplies most of the divergence (f = −145.99 mm). Together they give the converter a focal length of −125.40 mm. Neither the prescription nor Nikon’s section identifies an asphere or ED element in the converter.

The table gives ten signed radii, five glass thicknesses and four air spaces in millimetres. The bare master forms its image 48.968 mm behind the first converter vertex. This is the converter’s virtual object. The table places the film 71.001 mm behind that same vertex. Subtracting the 30.883 mm between the first and last glass vertices gives 40.118 mm from the last surface to the film.

All 18 refracting surfaces of the patent’s 200 mm f/2 master match the AI Nikkor 200mm f/2 IF-ED prescription at infinity. Its calculated back focus of 63.597 mm leaves 14.629 mm between the host and converter. With the same iris opening, the combination gives 280.007 mm at f/2.8001 and a focal-length ratio of 1.40003×. The patent gives 280 mm at f/2.8, consistent with Nikon’s nominal 1.4× increase and one-stop loss. [Converter patent, Figures 3 and 5](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4206971) · [Master lens patent, Example 2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4176913A/en)

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are for each singlet in air. The patent prints the index and Abbe number as n and ν. Their interpretation as helium d-line values is inferred.

### L1 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.59507, νd = 35.6. Glass unmatched. f = +92.73 mm.

L1 begins the weakly divergent forward group. Its two faces have similar curvature, and it has the weaker positive power of the two elements flanking L2.

### L2 — Biconcave negative

nd = 1.79668, νd = 45.4. Glass unmatched. f = −34.11 mm.

L2 is the strongest element in the converter. Its steeper face points toward the image. Its negative power outweighs the combined effect of L1 and L3.

### L3 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.59507, νd = 35.6. Glass unmatched. f = +61.43 mm.

L3 bends the beam mainly at its front face. Its rear radius is −5196.816 mm, giving that face the nearly flat outline visible in Nikon’s section. A 4.575 mm air space follows L3 and separates the two functional groups.

### L4 — Biconcave negative

nd = 1.79668, νd = 45.4. Glass unmatched. f = −51.49 mm.

L4 gives the rearward pair its negative power. Its more strongly curved rear face borders the 0.200 mm air space ahead of L5.

### L5 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.51009, νd = 63.4. Sumita BK1 matches the printed values. f = +82.53 mm.

The thickest singlet (9 mm) closes the rearward group. Its stronger front curvature supplies most of its positive power. The patent relates the bending of this last element to distortion, astigmatism and back focus.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The two negative elements share the highest index. The patent uses this index distribution to limit field curvature and assigns the forward group most of the longitudinal color correction and the rearward group most of the lateral color correction. Only L5 has a matching catalog glass. Sumita BK1 supplies its dispersion curve. The suppliers and melts of the five production elements remain unknown. [Patent, columns 4–6](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4206971) · [Sumita glass table, BK1](https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee347/SumitaAll.PDF)

## Focus Mechanism and Aperture

All five singlets stay fixed. The converter patent gives only the infinity setting and leaves the host iris unmarked. The model places it 12 mm behind host surface 16, with a calculated radius of 19.21 mm at f/2. The TC-14 transmits automatic diaphragm control and couples from f/2 to f/32 (effective f/2.8 to f/45). AI bodies meter at full aperture. Nikon specifies stop-down metering on non-AI bodies. [Converter patent, Figure 3](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4206971) · [Nikon TC-14 specifications](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00594/00594.pdf#page=107)

Nikon gives the host’s closest focusing distance and 1.4× its reproduction ratio with the converter attached. The 200 mm f/2 IF-ED is specified to 2.5 m. Its own patent moves the negative middle group 17 mm toward the film. At that setting the modeled combination focuses at 2.35 m from the film plane and gives −0.147×. [Nikon TC-14 specifications](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00594/00594.pdf#page=107) · [Nikon’s 200 mm specifications](https://www.base2photo.com/pdf/Nikkor_Lenses_Sales_Brochure_1984/Nikon_Nikkor_Lenses_Sales_Brochure_1984.pdf) · [Master lens patent, Example 2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4176913A/en)

## Design Conditions

Embodiment 1 meets the nine conditions in column 4. The absolute focal-length ratios L1/L2, L3/L2 and L5/L4 are 2.719, 1.801 and 1.603. The first two lie between 1.5 and 3.2, and the third between 1.0 and 2.5. The patent’s shape factors for L1, L3 and L5 are −0.094, +0.986 and +0.541, within the respective ranges (−0.2, 0.1), (0.4, 2.0) and (0.1, 2.2). The remaining conditions concern glass selection. The mean index of L1 and L3 is below L2’s, L4 has a higher index than L5, and the mean Abbe number of L1 and L3 is below L2’s. [Patent, column 4, conditions 1–9](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4206971)

## Field Coverage and Compatible Lenses

In Figure 3 both faces of each singlet end at the same height. Their estimated clear radii are 15.2, 15.0, 15.7, 16.0 and 16.8 mm from front to rear. With the 200 mm master at infinity and full aperture, the axial beam clears every surface and the chief ray reaches the 21.65 mm corner at 4.39°, close to the 4.41° field on the patent plots. Some marginal rays are lost there. Calculated corner illumination is 60% with the rims of the separately modeled AI 200 mm f/2 IF-ED and 53% when the master’s rims are also read from Figure 3. Factory clear apertures and baffle dimensions would be needed to predict illumination more closely.

Nikon lists AI-coupled 300–1200 mm Nikkor primes and the 180 mm f/2.8, excluding zooms and Reflex-Nikkors. The 180 mm shown in that catalog has five elements in four groups. The later five-group 180 mm f/2.8 ED overlaps the converter by 7.71 mm in the model at infinity. The 300 mm f/4.5 ED and AI 600 mm f/5.6 IF-ED both cover the full frame. The 200 mm f/2 also covers it and matches the patent master, although the dealer sheet omits it. The later manual of the AI-S Zoom-Nikkor ED 180–600 mm f/8 nevertheless lists the TC-14 among that lens’s accessories, and with the converter the zoom covers the full frame at every setting.
