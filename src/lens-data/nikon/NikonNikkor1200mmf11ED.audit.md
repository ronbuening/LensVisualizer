# NIKON NIKKOR 1200mm f/11 ED — audit

## Source

Yoshiyuki Shimizu, _Achromatic Objective Lens_ (色消対物レンズ), JP S51-43920 A. Nippon Kogaku K.K. filed application 49-117055 on October 14, 1974. The publication of April 15, 1976 gives the sole example twice, in the claim on page 1 and in the description on page 2. Figure 1 and the aberration curves are on page 3. [Patent record](https://patents.google.com/patent/JPS5143920A/en)

## Transcription

The two tables agree on all nine radii with their signs, eight successive glass and air thicknesses, five helium d-line refractive indices and five Abbe numbers. The dimensions are in millimetres. The 659 mm air space is the separation between surfaces 6 and 7. Surface 8 is the only cemented interface. Figure 1 calls the rear doublet L4, whose front and rear members are identified here as L4a and L4b. The example has spherical surfaces throughout and gives no filter plate or finite-focus spacings.

The spherical-aberration coefficients in column I of the Seidel table agree surface by surface to within 0.06% (surface 8's two-digit value aside) when expressed at the patent's unit focal length. At f/11 the marginal d-line ray crosses the axis 0.102 mm ahead of the paraxial focus, consistent with the scale of Figure 2A.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

- **Clear radii.** The 709.0 mm vertex span in Nikon's production drawing provides the scale. Surfaces 1–6 measure 56.8 mm from axis to rim, surfaces 7–9 about 24.4 mm. Figure 1 in the patent suggests about 56.0 and 18.7 mm respectively, with a break in the long air space. The production drawing shows the larger rear opening. [Dimensioned section](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/preAI70/1200mmf11EDpreAIopt.gif)
- **Diaphragm.** The head contains a manual diaphragm because an iris in the focusing unit vignetted the corners. The estimated position divides the 659 mm air space equally. At this position a 31.93 mm paraxial stop radius gives an entrance-pupil diameter of 109.06 mm at f/11. Nikon's account establishes why the iris belongs in the head, but gives no distance from a glass vertex. The off-axis Seidel coefficients do not independently establish that distance. [Nikon Tale 50](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/information/story/0050/)
- **Glass names.** Hoya LAF2 agrees with the printed values for L2. Hikari E-BAF8 matches the L3 index and differs by +0.04 in νd. Ohara S-BSL7 at L4a differs by +0.00001 in nd and −0.06 in νd. L1 (1.50032 / 81.9) and L4b (1.66854 / 39.2) have no matching catalog glass. L1's low dispersion and Nikon's ED designation support the inferred ED position. The patent gives no partial-dispersion value for it.

With these openings the axial f/11 beam clears all nine refracting surfaces. The 21.63 mm format corner at 1.03° is reachable, with about 91% geometric transmission near 1°. At the patent's 2.5° edge the chief ray reaches 25.36 mm at surface 9 and falls outside its 24.4 mm radius. There is no measured illumination curve for comparison.

## Product Identification

The 1975 ED head and the patent share the five-element, four-group construction, the rear cemented pair, the 709.0 mm vertex span and the 240.2 mm back focus. Nikon's drawing gives 2487.7 mm from the front focal point to the first vertex, 1036.9 mm between principal planes and 3436.9 mm between focal points. The prescription gives 2487.03, 1036.68 and 3436.09 mm respectively. This agreement strongly identifies the design. The earlier Nikkor-P has five groups and the later IF-ED has eight. Of the two 1200 mm examples in Shimizu's US 3,774,991, Example IV has a shorter 623 mm vertex span and Example V has seven elements. [Nikon drawing](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/preAI70/1200mmf11EDpreAIopt.gif) · [Nikon sales manual](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/01037/01037.pdf) · [Nikon IF manual](https://cdn-10.nikon-cdn.com/pdf/manuals/archive/Nikkor%20ED%201200mm%20f-11IF.pdf) · [US 3,774,991](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3774991A/en)

Nikon's sales specification for product 1296 gives 1200 mm, a manual f/11–f/64 diaphragm and 2° coverage on the Nikon F system. Nikon's 1974–76 sales manuals, as extracted by MIR, give 4°10′ for the same head. The model's 4.81° rim-limited field covers both figures. The catalog minimum of 50 m applies with the AU-1 focusing mount. The patent describes the lens at infinity only. ## Corrections

### 2026-11-25

The model follows both printed tables, including the 240.2 mm back focus. The clear radii come from Nikon's dimensioned section. The midpoint iris adds a reference plane within the printed 659 mm air space.

## Remaining Questions

The axial position and blade count of the production iris are undocumented in the cited Nikon material. The glass suppliers, the dispersion curves for L1 and L4b, and the production illumination at the edge of the patent's 5° field remain unknown.
