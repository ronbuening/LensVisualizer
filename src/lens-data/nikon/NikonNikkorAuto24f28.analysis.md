# NIKON NIKKOR-N AUTO 24mm f/2.8

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP S45-39875 B\
**Inventors:** Zenji Wakimoto\
**Assignee:** Nippon Kogaku K.K.\
**Filed:** September 23, 1967 (Japanese application S42-60854)\
**Published:** December 15, 1970\
**Title:** Wide-angle lens with a close-range correction gap in the converging lens group (translated)\
**Example details:** Example 1 (実施例 1), pages 2–3 and Figures 1–4. The lengths are scaled by 0.24 from a focal length of 100 to 24 mm.

A thin glass element immediately ahead of the diaphragm distinguishes Wakimoto’s Example 1 from Shimizu’s design. In Nikon’s 1967 section, L5 measures about 0.68 mm on axis. Wakimoto’s example gives 0.70 mm. The earlier identification, Example I of US 3,622,227, gives 3.00 mm. With the first and last vertices aligned, the internal vertices of the Japanese example fit Nikon’s drawing to 0.6 pixels RMS, compared with 23 pixels for the US example. Nikon’s sales drawing independently gives a glass span of 54.3 mm and back focus of 37.8 mm. Example 1 gives 54.29 mm and 37.824 mm. [Nikon’s 1967 section](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/information/story/0014/img/img2.jpg) · [Nikon/Nikkormat Sales Manual, L-18](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00766/00766.pdf)

The cemented pairs and the floating gap behind L6 in Example 1 match Nikon’s drawing. Wakimoto’s other examples have eight elements covering 62° and seven covering 74°. Nikon credits Yoshiyuki Shimizu with the basic design and Zenji Wakimoto with close-range correction. Shimizu’s US patent belongs to a separate family and gives no floating-focus spacing. [Nikon, Tale 14](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/information/story/0014/index.html) · [Wakimoto’s British family member](https://patents.google.com/patent/GB1238668A/en) · [Shimizu’s US 3,622,227](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3622227A/en)

The Nikkor-N Auto went on sale in June 1967 as the first lens with close-range correction. Early single-coated lenses have “Nippon Kogaku” on the front ring and later ones read “Nikon”. The Nikkor-N·C gained multicoating in 1972, and the 1975 New Nikkor gained a rubber focusing grip. In 1977 Nikon introduced Ikuo Mori’s AI redesign with a positive front element. [MIR, Nikkor-N Auto history](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/6070nikkor/wides/24mm.htm) · [Nikon, Tale 14](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/information/story/0014/index.html) · [JAPB, Nikkor-N versions](https://japb.net/gear/gear-review-index/ds_nikkor-f-24f28/) · [Nikon, Tale 86](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/information/story/0086/index.html)

## Optical Architecture

The nine spherical elements form seven air-spaced groups. L1–L3 diverge the beam and L4–L9 bring it to focus, leaving room for the reflex mirror. At f/2.8 the calculated focal length is 23.999 mm and the paraxial back focus is 37.835 mm. The film sits at the patent’s 37.824 mm. Nikon specifies 24 mm, f/2.8 and an 84° field on 35 mm film in the Nikon F mount. The traced corner is at 42.5° half-field. The original aperture ring runs from f/2.8 to f/16 in full stops. A 1967 specimen has six iris blades. [Nikon’s lens manual, p. 10](https://cameramanuals.org/lenses/nikon_nikkor_manual_lenses.pdf) · [JAPB, aperture scale](https://japb.net/gear/gear-review-index/ds_nikkor-f-24f28/) · [1967 lens No. 242932](https://destoutz.ch/lens_24mm_f2.8_242932.html)

The diaphragm sits between L5 and L6. Figure 1 of the patent shows it within the printed air space. Nikon’s section gives the clear radii and places the iris about 0.48 mm behind surface 9. Its calculated wide-open radius is 6.27 mm. [Nikon, Tale 14, Figure 1](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/information/story/0014/img/img2.jpg)

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are for individual elements in air. Example 1 gives the refractive indices. All nine Abbe numbers are inferred. The glass names identify catalog dispersion curves.

### L1 — Negative meniscus

nd = 1.62299, νd = 58.12. Hoya BACD15. f = −57.05 mm. The wide front meniscus accepts the oblique beam. Its rear face is more strongly curved than its front face and starts the divergence of the front section. Its 15.5 mm clear rear face ends in a short flat step that reaches 16.7 mm, as Nikon’s section and the patent figure both draw it.

### L2 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.6228, νd = 57.05. Ohara S-BSM10. f = +78.83 mm. L2 partially reconverges the beam between the two negative menisci. Its rear vertex lies just 0.10 mm ahead of L3.

### L3 — Negative meniscus

nd = 1.62041, νd = 60.3. Schott N-SK16. f = −29.31 mm. Its 10.55 mm rear radius supplies the strongest negative power of the front three elements. A 9.00 mm air space separates it from the first doublet.

### L4 and L5 — Positive cemented doublet

- **L4, biconvex positive.** nd = 1.62004, νd = 36.3. Hoya E-F2. f = +17.71 mm. L4 brings the diverging front beam toward convergence.
- **L5, negative meniscus.** nd = 1.5168, νd = 64.17. Schott N-BK7. f = −83.86 mm. This thin crown shares surface 8 with L4 and reduces its positive power. The doublet has a focal length of +22.22 mm.

### L6 — Biconcave negative

nd = 1.71736, νd = 29.51. Schott SF1. f = −28.31 mm. L6 diverges the beam just behind the iris. Its rear face bounds the air space that narrows at close focus.

### L7 and L8 — Positive cemented doublet

- **L7, biconcave negative.** nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.43. Schott SF6. f = −27.83 mm. Its front face defines the other side of the variable air space. Its more strongly curved rear face is cemented to L8 at surface 13.
- **L8, biconvex positive.** nd = 1.62041, νd = 60.3. Schott N-SK16. f = +20.06 mm. L8 supplies enough convergence to give the doublet a net focal length of +57.83 mm.

### L9 — Positive meniscus

nd = 1.74443, νd = 49.4. Glass unidentified. f = +43.53 mm. Its shallow front surface faces L8 across a 0.05 mm air gap. The rear surface supplies most of the final element’s positive power.

## Glass Identification and Dispersion

Example 1 prints nine refractive indices without naming the wavelength or giving any Abbe numbers. Treating them as helium d-line indices allows three independent comparisons. The resulting chromatic aberrations are estimates. The Japanese patent gives no spectral curves or glass trade names against which to check them.

| Elements           | Basis for the inferred dispersion                                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| L1, L2, L5, L6, L7 | Hoya BACD15, Ohara S-BSM10 and Schott N-BK7, SF1 and SF6 have the corresponding d-line indices at the printed precision (four or five decimals). Their catalog rows supply the Abbe numbers. |
| L3, L8 and L4      | Shimizu’s US 3,622,227 pairs 1.62041 with 60.3 and 1.62004 with 36.3. Schott N-SK16 and Hoya E-F2 supply closely matching dispersion curves.                                                 |
| L9                 | Nippon Kogaku’s US 4,139,265 pairs 1.74443 with 49.4. The supplier and catalog curve remain unidentified.                                                                                    |

## Focus Mechanism

Wakimoto selects a gap where the axial beam is nearly parallel. In Example 1 (pp. 2–3), the distance from the front assembly’s rear focus to the far side of the gap has magnitude 277.76 in normalized units, greater than twice the rear assembly’s focal length of 102.65. Changing this gap affects astigmatism and field curvature while changing spherical aberration comparatively little.

The focusing ring moves both assemblies toward the subject. The rear three elements advance 3.10 mm from infinity to 0.114×. The air gap narrows by 1.07 mm, so the front six advance 2.03 mm relative to the film. Figures 3 and 4 compare aberrations at 0.114× with the infinity gap and with the narrower gap. Nikon attributes the improved corner definition at close focus to this correction of astigmatism. [Nikon, Tale 14](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/information/story/0014/index.html)

| Distance from subject to film               | Air gap between L6 and L7 | Last surface to film    |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Infinity                                    | 2.14008 mm, printed       | 37.824 mm, printed      |
| 298.49 mm, calculated at the printed 0.114× | 1.0704 mm, printed        | 40.92554 mm, calculated |

At 0.114× the subject lies 204.34 mm ahead of the first surface and 298.49 mm from the film, consistent with Nikon’s 0.3 m minimum measured from the film plane. Nikon’s sales sheet gives the distance alone. JAPB lists 1:9.1 (0.110×) for the original Nikkor-N. Between the two patent positions, the model narrows the gap in proportion to inverse subject-to-film distance. Three intermediate image distances keep the subject in focus on the film. The production cam profile is unknown.
