# NIKON NIKKOR-N AUTO 24mm f/2.8

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP S45-39875 B\
**Inventors:** Zenji Wakimoto\
**Assignee:** Nippon Kogaku K.K.\
**Filed:** September 23, 1967 (Japanese application S42-60854)\
**Published:** December 15, 1970\
**Example details:** Example 1 (実施例 1), pages 2–3 and Figures 1–4. The lengths are scaled by 0.24 from a focal length of 100 to 24 mm.

A thin glass element immediately ahead of the diaphragm distinguishes Wakimoto’s Example 1 from Shimizu’s design. In Nikon’s 1967 section, L5 measures about 0.68 mm on axis. Wakimoto’s example gives 0.70 mm. The earlier identification, Example I of US 3,622,227, gives 3.00 mm. With the first and last vertices aligned, the internal vertices of the Japanese example fit Nikon’s drawing to 0.6 pixels RMS, compared with 23 pixels for the US example. Nikon’s sales drawing independently gives a glass span of 54.3 mm and back focus of 37.8 mm. Example 1 gives 54.29 mm and 37.824 mm. [Nikon’s 1967 section](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/information/story/0014/img/img2.jpg) · [Nikon/Nikkormat Sales Manual, L-18](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00766/00766.pdf)

The Japanese example also has both cemented pairs in the production positions and gives the near-focus spacing between L6 and L7. The other two examples describe eight elements covering 62° and seven elements covering 74°. Nikon credits Yoshiyuki Shimizu with the basic design and Zenji Wakimoto with close-range correction. Shimizu’s US 3,622,227 is a separate filing outside Wakimoto’s patent family. Its prescription differs and it gives no floating-focus spacing. [Nikon, Tale 14](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/information/story/0014/index.html) · [GB 1,238,668, Wakimoto patent family](https://patents.google.com/patent/GB1238668A/en) · [Shimizu’s US 3,622,227](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3622227A/en)

## Optical Architecture

The nine spherical elements form seven air-spaced groups. L1–L3 act as a diverging front section and L4–L9 bring the beam to focus. This retrofocus arrangement leaves room for a reflex mirror. The calculated focal length is 23.9993 mm at f/2.8, with a paraxial back focal distance of 37.8349 mm. The film sits at the patent’s 37.824 mm. Nikon specifies a 24 mm f/2.8 lens covering 84° on 35 mm film in the Nikon F mount. The traced diagonal corner is at a half-angle of 42.5°. The aperture ring spans f/2.8 to f/16, and a documented 1967 specimen has six iris blades. [Nikon’s lens manual, p. 10](https://cameramanuals.org/lenses/nikon_nikkor_manual_lenses.pdf) · [1967 lens No. 242932](https://destoutz.ch/lens_24mm_f2.8_242932.html)

The diaphragm sits between L5 and L6. Nikon’s section supplies the clear radii of the glass and places the iris about 0.48 mm behind surface 9. Its calculated wide-open radius is 6.27 mm. The patent gives the total air space between L5 and L6 and shows the iris in Figure 1. [JP S45-39875 B](https://www.j-platpat.inpit.go.jp/c1801/PU/JP-S45-039875/12/ja)

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are those of individual elements in air. Each index comes from Example 1. All Abbe numbers and glass identifications are inferred.

### L1 — Negative meniscus

nd = 1.62299, νd = 58.12. Hoya BACD15. f = −57.05 mm. The wide front meniscus accepts the oblique beam. Its rear face is more strongly curved than its front face and starts the divergence of the front section.

### L2 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.62280, νd = 57.05. Ohara S-BSM10. f = +78.83 mm. L2 partially reconverges the beam between the two negative menisci. Its rear vertex lies just 0.10 mm ahead of L3.

### L3 — Negative meniscus

nd = 1.62041, νd = 60.3. Schott N-SK16. f = −29.31 mm. Its 10.55 mm rear radius supplies the strongest negative power of the front three elements. A 9.00 mm air space separates it from the first doublet.

### L4 and L5 — Positive cemented doublet

- **L4, biconvex positive.** nd = 1.62004, νd = 36.3. Hoya E-F2. f = +17.71 mm. L4 brings the diverging front beam toward convergence.
- **L5, negative meniscus.** nd = 1.51680, νd = 64.17. Schott N-BK7. f = −83.86 mm. This thin crown shares surface 8 with L4 and reduces its positive power. The doublet has a focal length of +22.22 mm.

### L6 — Biconcave negative

nd = 1.71736, νd = 29.51. Schott SF1. f = −28.31 mm. L6 diverges the beam just behind the iris. Its rear face bounds the air space that narrows at close focus.

### L7 and L8 — Positive cemented doublet

- **L7, biconcave negative.** nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.43. Schott SF6. f = −27.83 mm. Its front face defines the other side of the variable air space. Its more strongly curved rear face is cemented to L8 at surface 13.
- **L8, biconvex positive.** nd = 1.62041, νd = 60.3. Schott N-SK16. f = +20.06 mm. L8 supplies enough convergence to give the doublet a net focal length of +57.83 mm.

### L9 — Positive meniscus

nd = 1.74443, νd = 49.4. Glass unidentified. f = +43.53 mm. Its shallow front surface faces L8 across a 0.05 mm air gap. The rear surface supplies most of the final element’s positive power.

## Glass Identification and Dispersion

Example 1 prints nine refractive indices without naming the wavelength or giving any Abbe numbers. Treating them as helium d-line indices allows three independent comparisons. The resulting chromatic aberrations are estimates. The Japanese patent gives no spectral curves or glass trade names against which to check them.

| Elements           | Basis for the inferred dispersion                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| L1, L2, L5, L6, L7 | Hoya BACD15, Ohara S-BSM10 and Schott N-BK7, SF1 and SF6 have the corresponding d-line indices at five decimal places. Their catalog rows supply the Abbe numbers. |
| L3, L8 and L4      | Shimizu’s US 3,622,227 pairs 1.62041 with 60.3 and 1.62004 with 36.3. Schott N-SK16 and Hoya E-F2 supply closely matching dispersion curves.                       |

## Focus Mechanism

Wakimoto selects a gap where the axial beam is nearly parallel. In the patent’s normalized units, the distance from the front assembly’s rear focus to the far side of the gap has magnitude 277.76, greater than twice the rear assembly’s focal length of 102.65. Changing this gap has a strong effect on astigmatism and field curvature while changing spherical aberration comparatively little. [JP S45-39875 B, pp. 2–3](https://www.j-platpat.inpit.go.jp/c1801/PU/JP-S45-039875/12/ja)

The movement divides the lens behind L6. Both assemblies move toward the subject as the focusing ring turns. The rear three elements advance 3.10 mm from infinity to 0.114×. Their separation from the front six narrows by 1.07 mm, so the front six advance 2.03 mm relative to the film. Nikon describes this motion as a correction for the astigmatism that would blur the corners at short distances. Figures 3 and 4 of the patent compare the same magnification before and after the change in separation. [Nikon, Tale 14](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/information/story/0014/index.html) · [Wakimoto, Figures 3–4](https://www.j-platpat.inpit.go.jp/c1801/PU/JP-S45-039875/12/ja)

| Distance from subject to film               | Air gap between L6 and L7 | Last surface to film    |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Infinity                                    | 2.14008 mm, printed       | 37.824 mm, printed      |
| 298.49 mm, calculated at the printed 0.114× | 1.0704 mm, printed        | 40.92554 mm, calculated |

The close conjugate places the subject 204.34 mm ahead of the first surface. Its distance from the film rounds to the 0.3 m marking on the production lens. Nikon’s sales sheet specifies the distance and leaves the reproduction ratio unstated. Between the two patent positions, the model closes the internal gap in proportion to inverse subject-to-film distance. Three intermediate image distances are calculated for focus on the fixed film plane. The production cam profile is unknown. [Nikon/Nikkormat Sales Manual, L-18](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00766/00766.pdf)
