# NIKON TELECONVERTER TC-200 — audit

## Source

Soichi Nakamura, _Rear conversion lens_, US 4,154,508, assigned to Nippon Kogaku K.K. and published May 15, 1979. Figure 11 appears on drawing sheet 4 (PDF page 5). The standard lens and combined specifications are in column 6, the converter table is in column 9, and claim 21 repeats it on PDF pages 17–18. Dimensions are in millimetres at 1:1 scale. [US 4,154,508](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4154508A/en)

## Transcription

The twelve surfaces are spherical. The eleven internal spacings and seven refractive-index and Abbe-number pairs agree in Example 11 and claim 21. The only departure in the radii is the first surface, described under Corrections. Surfaces 5–7 form the L3/L4 doublet, and surfaces 8–10 form the L5/L6 doublet. Surface 10 is followed by 0.1 mm of air before L7. The twelve vertices span 38.74 mm.

The patent calls the glass quantities n and ν. The helium d line is assumed. L6 has a Hoya E-C3 equivalent (catalog 1.51823/58.96, printed 1.51823/59.0). L7 has a Hoya TAF4 equivalent (catalog 1.78800/47.49, printed 1.78797/47.5). The Schott N-LAF21 row also fits L7. The printed numbers govern all seven elements. L1 through L5 have no close catalog equivalent at the stated precision.

At 44 mm extension the calculated paraxial image falls within 0.0001 mm of the calculated rear gap. The surface edges clear one another and the master retains its f/2 axial beam. A chief ray reaches the 21.63 mm corner of a 36 × 24 mm frame at 11.88°. Calculated relative illumination there is 53.6% of the axial value. No numerical image height, total track or finite-focus state is printed.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

**Cell position.** The printed 0.6917 mm master-to-converter separation puts the host's original image 36.7662 mm behind the first converter vertex. For the production TC-200, Nikon gives a 44 mm mount-to-mount length and a separate 52.5 mm overall length. Infinity focus and the 44 mm extension give a distance of 36.8649 mm from the first vertex to the original image and a rear gap of 42.1249 mm. With the patent master, the resulting separation is 0.5930 mm. The 38.74 mm glass assembly plus the rear gap, less the distance to the original image, accounts for the full extension. [Nikon catalog, teleconverter specifications](https://device.report/m/e6e67eaa9bf9b326e2a5cd5070757e862fb41e48d7bc53a4be5be7c751ace45b)

**Clear radii.** Nikon's drawing of the shared TC-1 optics can be scaled from the first and last glass vertices, 38.74 mm apart. The following values refer to the curved optical faces. **Master diaphragm.** The converter has no iris. For the patent master, the diaphragm is assumed to lie halfway across its 9.4 mm central air space. Its calculated radius at f/2 is 9.08 mm. Each catalog lens supplies its own diaphragm and focusing movement.

## Product Identification

Nikon confirms that its May 1977 TC-200 added AI coupling to the TC-1 while retaining Nakamura's optics. Nikon's section and Example 11 agree on seven elements in five air-separated groups, the cemented L3/L4 and L5/L6 pairs, the wide central space and the symmetric biconcave rear singlet. Example 10 joins different front elements, Example 12 has a front triplet, and the TC-300's five-element Example 2 uses a longer master lens. The evidence strongly supports Example 11 for the TC-200. [Nikon, Tale 73](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/information/story/0073/) · [Patent Figures 2 and 10–12](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4154508A/en)

Nikon lists nominal 2× magnification, most AI lenses through 200 mm and an aperture coupling range starting at f/2. With the AI 135mm f/2.8, the combination gives 270.95 mm at f/5.620 and 22.65 mm vertex clearance. The AI 180mm f/2.8 ED gives 361.25 mm at f/5.619 and 4.40 mm clearance. Both keep the host iris and pass the axial and corner chief rays. Nikon cautions about close-range image quality with the 135mm and possible exposure unevenness at small apertures and high shutter speeds with the 180mm ED. The stated field of the 135mm f/2.8 covers 21.43 mm of the 21.63 mm corner radius. The corner chief ray clears the glass just beyond that field. [Nikon compatibility chart, manual-focus telephoto lenses](https://service.nikonrepair.eu/article_files/46818/TC_EN.pdf)

The 24mm f/2, 35mm f/2, Micro-Nikkor 55mm f/2.8, 135mm f/2, 200mm f/2 IF-ED, 50–135mm f/3.5 and 80–200mm f/4 also pass the axial and corner rays at each tabulated focal length. Nikon lists these combinations. The AI 80–200mm f/4.5 reaches the full corner even at 200 mm, but Nikon marks that model unusable with the TC-200. The AF-I 300mm f/2.8D, AF-I 400mm f/2.8D and AF-S 80–200mm f/2.8D are further combinations Nikon lists, with image-quality cautions for the 300mm and the zoom. These also pass the axial and corner rays. A shared F mount alone does not establish compatibility. [Nikon compatibility chart](https://service.nikonrepair.eu/article_files/46818/TC_EN.pdf)

## Corrections

### 2026-11-25

Example 11 and claim 21 both print +37.545 mm at surface 1. The correction to +35.545 mm brings the combined focal length to 103.29 mm against the patent's 103.2 mm, and gives f/4.003 and 2.0017× at the printed spacing. It also reproduces the printed forward-group equivalent index (2.369), brings the converter Petzval sum to −0.002118 mm⁻¹ against −0.00211 mm⁻¹, and gives a converter focal length of −76.49 mm. Column 3 of the patent prints the converter's design values as f1 = −50.48869, f2 = 208.1101, e = 20.25835 and fR = −76.492 mm, with Embodiment 1. The corrected Example 11 gives −50.4887, 208.108, 20.257 and −76.4917 mm, while the printed radius gives −47.30, 208.108, 20.59 and −70.21 mm. The dimensioned TC-200 section independently marks a focal-length magnitude of 76.5 mm and 147.6 mm between the two focal points; the corrected converter gives 147.63 mm and the printed radius 135.05 mm. All other radii, internal spacings and glass values follow the patent. ## Remaining Questions

A production optical drawing dimensioned from the mount faces would establish the exact position of the glass and its clear apertures. The 44 mm dimension constrains the cell position only through the infinity-focus calculation. The master iris position, factory glass identities and reference wavelength also remain unconfirmed.
