# NIKON TELECONVERTER TC-200 — Audit Log

## Source

Soichi Nakamura, _Rear conversion lens_, US 4,154,508, assigned to Nippon Kogaku K.K. The US application was filed March 2, 1977, with Japanese priority of March 11, 1976. The patent was published May 15, 1979. Figure 11 is on drawing sheet 4 (PDF p. 5). Column 6 gives the standard lens and combined specifications. Column 9 gives Example 11 (PDF p. 15), and claim 21 repeats the converter table (PDF pp. 17–18). The dimensions are in millimetres at 1:1 scale. [US 4,154,508](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4154508A/en)

Nikon identifies the TC-1 and TC-200 as the same optical design. Its catalog gives the TC-200 construction, nominal magnification, mount extension and metering range. The compatibility chart distinguishes individual lens versions. [Nikon, Tale 73, §§III–IV](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/information/story/0073/) · [Nikon catalog, teleconverter specifications](https://device.report/m/e6e67eaa9bf9b326e2a5cd5070757e862fb41e48d7bc53a4be5be7c751ace45b) · [Nikon F-mount compatibility chart](https://service.nikonrepair.eu/article_files/46818/TC_EN.pdf)

## Transcription Check

The Example 11 and claim 21 page images agree on all twelve signed spherical radii, eleven internal spacings and seven index/Abbe pairs. Both print +37.545 mm for the first radius. This is the one radius corrected below. Surfaces 5–7 form the L3/L4 doublet and surfaces 8–10 form the L5/L6 doublet. The 0.1 mm air gap after surface 10 separates L6 from L7. The first and last vertices are 38.74 mm apart. [Patent, Example 11 and claim 21](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4154508A/en)

The patent specifies n and ν without naming the reference wavelength. The model assumes the helium d line and retains the printed glass values throughout. L6 and L7 have catalog dispersion equivalents. Schott N-LAF21 also fits L7. L1–L5 have no close catalog equivalent at the stated precision.

The patent gives no numerical image height, total track, back focus or finite-focus position.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

**Cell position.** At the printed 0.6917 mm gap the original image lies 36.766 mm behind the first converter vertex. Nikon specifies a 44 mm distance between mounts and a 52.5 mm overall length. At infinity, the 44 mm extension places the original image 36.8649 mm behind the first vertex and the new image 42.1249 mm behind the last. The extension equals the 38.74 mm vertex span plus the rear gap less the distance to the original image. [Nikon catalog, TC-200 dimensions](https://device.report/m/e6e67eaa9bf9b326e2a5cd5070757e862fb41e48d7bc53a4be5be7c751ace45b)

**Clear radii.** On drawing sheet 4, the 38.74 mm between the first and last converter vertices spans about 459 pixels at 300 dpi, or 11.85 pixels/mm. The curvature of surface 6 gives about 11.9 pixels/mm radially, and the corrected first surface gives about 12.0. The front rims lie about 129 pixels from the axis on either side. Figure 11 thus gives about 10.8 mm where Nikon's section gives 9.75 mm. The rear outlines lie about 16.4 mm from the axis on the vertex scale, but the drawn arcs vary in curvature. Nikon's section resolves the thin edge of L6 and the slightly taller rear singlet. **Diaphragm and focus.** The patent master has a 9.4 mm central air gap. An iris at its midpoint has a calculated f/2 radius of 9.08 mm. The converter has fixed elements and uses the taking lens's iris and focus travel. The patent specifies only infinity. Nikon gives the taking lens's closest focusing distance and twice its reproduction ratio for the production combination. The modeled image-plane distance includes the 44 mm extension. ## Product Identification

Nikon released the AI-coupled TC-200 in May 1977 with Nakamura's TC-1 optics. Nikon's section and Figure 11 agree on seven elements in five air-separated groups, both cemented pairs, the wide central air gap and the symmetric biconcave rear singlet. Example 10 cements the middle two front elements, Example 12 cements three, and the TC-300's five-element Example 2 uses a 300 mm master. The drawing and dimensions strongly support Example 11 for the TC-200. The later TC-201 added AI-S coupling. [Nikon, Tale 73](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/information/story/0073/) · [Patent, Figs. 2 and 10–12](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4154508A/en) · [TC-200 section](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/teleconverter/TC200.gif) · [MIR, TC-201 and TC-301](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/teleconverter/index6.htm)

Nikon specifies nominal 2× magnification, most AI lenses through 200 mm and f/2–f/32 coupling. The 135 mm f/2.8 reaches 270.95 mm at f/5.620 with 22.65 mm between the facing vertices. The 180 mm f/2.8 ED reaches 361.25 mm at f/5.619 with 4.40 mm clearance. The axial beam and corner chief ray pass with the original iris on each lens. The 135 mm lens's stated field reaches 21.43 mm, just inside the 21.63 mm frame corner. A chief ray aimed at the actual corner also clears the glass. Nikon cautions about vignetting or degraded image quality at close range with the 135 mm f/2.8, and occasional uneven exposure with the 180 mm ED at high shutter speeds past f/11. [Nikon catalog](https://device.report/m/e6e67eaa9bf9b326e2a5cd5070757e862fb41e48d7bc53a4be5be7c751ace45b) · [Nikon compatibility chart, p. 3](https://service.nikonrepair.eu/article_files/46818/TC_EN.pdf)

The 24 mm f/2, 35 mm f/2, Micro-Nikkor 55 mm f/2.8, 135 mm f/2, 200 mm f/2 IF-ED, 50–135 mm f/3.5 and 80–200 mm f/4 also pass the axial and corner rays at each tabulated focal length. Nikon lists all seven. Its chart excludes the AI 80–200 mm f/4.5, although that lens passes a corner ray even at 200 mm. Nikon also lists the AF-I 300 mm f/2.8D, AF-I 400 mm f/2.8D and AF-S 80–200 mm f/2.8D. All three pass the axial and corner rays. Nikon warns of possible vignetting or degraded image quality with the 300 mm and the zoom. [Nikon compatibility chart, pp. 2–4](https://service.nikonrepair.eu/article_files/46818/TC_EN.pdf)

Nikon lists some faster taking lenses, including the AI 50 mm f/1.2 used within the coupling range, and the Reflex-Nikkor 500 mm f/8 with an image-quality caution. The model covers refracting lenses of f/2 or slower at maximum aperture. It does not combine the converter with six of the Nikon-listed lenses above: the 24 mm f/2, 35 mm f/2, Micro-Nikkor 55 mm f/2.8, 135 mm f/2, 50–135 mm f/3.5 and 80–200 mm f/4. Nikon's period catalog lists the 360–1200 mm f/11 ED with possible vignetting. Its later chart marks that combination unusable, and the model excludes it. [Nikon catalog, lens/teleconverter chart and aperture coupling](https://device.report/m/e6e67eaa9bf9b326e2a5cd5070757e862fb41e48d7bc53a4be5be7c751ace45b) · [Nikon compatibility chart](https://service.nikonrepair.eu/article_files/46818/TC_EN.pdf)

## Corrections, 2026-11-25

- Surface 1 uses +35.545 mm in place of the +37.545 mm printed in both tables. This radius recovers Example 11's forward-group equivalent index and brings its Petzval sum close to the printed value. It also reproduces the following design values printed under Embodiment 1 in column 4. The TC-200 drawing independently gives a focal-length magnitude of 76.5 mm and a distance of 147.6 mm between focal points. This distance calculates to 147.63 mm with the corrected radius and 135.05 mm with the printed radius. Catalog minus patent gives Δnd ≈ −0.00000007 and Δνd = −0.04 for L6, and Δnd ≈ +0.000027 and Δνd = −0.01 for L7. The five other elements retain the printed index and Abbe number alone.

At the patent spacing the corrected pair gives 103.29 mm at f/4.003 and 2.00172×, with 41.728 mm back focus. At Nikon's 44 mm extension it gives 103.56 mm at f/4.014 and 2.00691×, with 42.125 mm back focus and a 9.08 mm master iris radius.

2026-11-25 rim review:

- Surfaces 1, 3–4 and 5–7 increased from 9.75 to 10.8 mm to follow the common front edge in Figure 11. The outer radius of the flat land on surface 2 also increased from 9.75 to 10.8 mm. Its 8.8 mm clear radius keeps the air gap open. Nikon's section shows this land and a smaller front outline.
- With the 51.6 mm f/2 patent master at Nikon's 44 mm extension, corner illumination was 53.6% before and after. All six compatible catalog lenses passed the axial beam at infinity. Mounted corner coverage was 100% before and after on each lens, including all three focal lengths of the AF-S 80–200 mm. Its wide, middle and long settings retained corner illumination of 52.7%, 50.4% and 32.6%, respectively.

## Remaining Questions

- The 44 mm extension constrains the cell position at infinity. The exact glass positions relative to the mounts and Nikon's production clear radii remain undocumented.
- The patent leaves the master iris position and the glass reference wavelength unspecified. The production glass types, suppliers and melts remain unknown.
