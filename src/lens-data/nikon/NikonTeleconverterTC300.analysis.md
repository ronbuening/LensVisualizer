# NIKON TELECONVERTER TC-300

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 4,154,508, _Rear conversion lens_\
**Inventors:** Soichi Nakamura\
**Assignee:** Nippon Kogaku K.K.\
**Filed:** March 2, 1977, with Japanese priority of March 11, 1976\
**Published:** May 15, 1979\
**Example details:** Example 2, Figure 2 and claim 4; the table is headed “EXAMPLE 2” in column 7. Dimensions are in millimetres at 1:1 scale.

Nikon introduced the AI-coupled TC-300 together with the TC-200 in 1977, as revised versions of the TC-2 and TC-1. Like the TC-2, the TC-300 serves Nikkor telephotos of 300 mm and longer, whose rear elements sit deep in the barrel. In 1983 AI-S coupling turned the pair into the TC-201 and TC-301. [Nikon, Tale 73, TC-200 release](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/information/story/0073/) · [MIR, TC-200 and TC-300](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/teleconverter/index2.htm) · [MIR, Nikon teleconverters](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/teleconverter/index.htm)

Of the twelve examples, only the second uses a 300 mm f/4.5 master lens. Nakamura gives the combination as 600 mm f/9. Its ten converter surfaces also match Nikon’s dimensioned TC-300 section. Both show three small air-spaced singlets at the front, a long air space, and two larger singlets at the rear. All five are spherical, and Nikon gives five elements in five groups. Neither source identifies an ED element in the converter. [Patent, Figure 2 and columns 6–7](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4154508A/en) · [Nikon’s TC-300 section](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/teleconverter/tc300.gif) · [MIR, TC-300 specifications](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/teleconverter/index2.htm)

Nikon dimensions the optical length at 88.5 mm, the back focus at 63.1 mm and the converter’s focal length at 237.7 mm. The prescription gives 88.54, 63.11 and −236.42 mm. The front tube carrying the first three singlets projects 23.5 mm ahead of the lens flange, and the body adds 83.1 mm behind it. Example 2 moves the master’s image back by 83.14 mm, matching that extension. Example 1 has the same five-singlet arrangement, but only 0.514 mm between the two optical groups, against 66.24 mm in Example 2. Example 11, on a 51.6 mm master, is the separate seven-element TC-200 design. The group spacing and image position strongly identify Example 2 with the TC-300. [Nikon’s dimensioned drawing](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/teleconverter/tc300.gif) · [MIR, 83 mm extension](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/teleconverter/index2.htm) · [Patent, Examples 1, 2 and 11](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4154508A/en)

## Optical Architecture

The TC-300 sits in the converging beam behind the host lens. Nakamura divides the five singlets into a diverging front group (L1–L3, −96.80 mm) and a converging rear group (L4–L5, +281.13 mm). The two groups together have a focal length of −236.42 mm. The patent describes this distribution of power as a way to reduce the negative Petzval sum of a rear converter and limit the separation of the sagittal and tangential image surfaces. [Patent, columns 1–4](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4154508A/en)

The patent prints the radii, spacings, indices and Abbe numbers of both systems, the 1.5423 mm junction between them, and the combined focal length, f-number and field. The image distances are calculated from those tables. The master alone focuses 70.053 mm behind its last surface. The first converter surface lies 1.5423 mm behind that surface, so the incoming beam converges toward a point 68.511 mm inside the converter. Refraction through the five singlets places the new image 63.112 mm beyond the last surface. At infinity, the combination gives 600.01 mm at f/9.00, a focal-length ratio of 2.0000×.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are for each singlet in air. The glass coordinates are the patent’s values, with the helium d line inferred.

### L1 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.59507, νd = 35.6. Glass unmatched. Focal length +101.91 mm. L1 increases the convergence of the incoming beam before it reaches the stronger negative L2.

### L2 — Biconcave negative

nd = 1.74443, νd = 49.4. Glass unmatched. Focal length −33.44 mm. The rear surface has the steeper curvature. L2 gives the front group its net negative power despite the positive elements on either side.

### L3 — Positive meniscus

nd = 1.59507, νd = 35.6. Glass unmatched. Focal length +112.42 mm. The convex face points toward the master. L3 offsets part of L2’s divergence at the rear of the front group and shares L1’s index and dispersion.

### L4 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.50137, νd = 56.5. Schott K10 supplies the dispersion curve. Focal length +94.42 mm. After the 66.24 mm air space, L4 provides the rear group’s positive power. Its rear face curves more strongly than its front face.

### L5 — Biconcave negative

nd = 1.80454, νd = 39.5. Hoya NBFD3 supplies the dispersion curve. Focal length −140.30 mm. A 0.2 mm air space separates L5 from L4. L5 reduces the convergence imparted by L4 while the pair retains positive power.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent prints an index and an Abbe number for each glass without naming a reference wavelength or supplier. A separate optical patent, US 4,348,085, explicitly gives the L1/L3 and L2 pairs at the helium d line. L4 and L5 are also close to catalog d-line values. Together these observations support using the d line here. The front three elements still lack a close catalog match for both index and dispersion. [US 4,348,085, d-line glass tables](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4348085A/en)

Schott K10 has nd 1.50137 and νd 56.41, against 1.50137 and 56.5 for L4. Hoya lists NBFD3 under code 805-396, within 0.0005 in index and 0.1 in Abbe number of L5. Their catalog curves approximate the dispersion of L4 and L5. The glass suppliers, melt data and partial dispersion of the production TC-300 remain unknown. [Schott K10](https://us.shop.schott.com/advanced-optics/en/Optical-Glass/K10/c/glass-K10) · [Hoya optical glass index](https://www.hoyaoptics.eu/glass-cross-reference-index)

## Focus Mechanism

All five singlets have fixed spacing. The host lens supplies the focusing movement and the iris. The patent gives an infinity prescription only. Nikon gives the closest focusing distance as that of the lens in use and the depth of field as half. With the Micro-Nikkor 200 mm f/4 IF the combination focuses continuously from infinity to life size. At finite focus, the model follows the host’s travel and adds the converter’s 83.14 mm extension to distances measured from the image plane. With the 300 mm f/4.5 ED, the lens’s 4.0 m setting gives 4.08 m. [MIR, TC-300 specifications](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/teleconverter/index2.htm)

## Compatible Lenses and Metering

Nikon specifies the TC-300 for lenses of 300 mm and longer, plus the Micro-Nikkor 200 mm f/4 IF, and only for lenses with AI coupling. Its automatic diaphragm couples from f/2.8 through f/32 on the lens, equivalent to f/5.6 through f/64 with the converter, a two-stop loss. Metering is at full aperture on AI bodies and stopped down on non-AI bodies. The f/4.5 master in Figure 2 is one example within that range. [MIR, TC-300 specifications](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/teleconverter/index2.htm)

Nikon’s compatibility chart gives the TC-300 and TC-301 one column. It lists the Micro-Nikkor 200 mm f/4 IF, the AI 300 mm f/2.8 IF-ED, the 300 mm f/4.5 IF-ED, the AI-S 400 mm f/3.5 IF-ED, the 400 mm f/5.6 IF-ED, the AI-S 600 mm f/4 IF-ED, the 600 mm f/5.6 IF-ED, the 500 mm f/4P IF-ED and the Zoom-Nikkor 180–600 mm f/8 ED. It warns of occasional uneven illumination at high shutter speeds and apertures smaller than f/11 with the 400 mm f/2.8 IF-ED, the 800 mm f/5.6 IF-ED and the AI 800 mm f/8 IF. The Reflex-Nikkor 1000 mm f/11 is usable without its filter. The chart also lists the AF 300 mm f/2.8 and f/4 IF-ED and the AF-I and AF-S D-type 300–600 mm telephotos, without autofocus. [Nikon compatibility chart, pp. 2–4](https://service.nikonrepair.eu/article_files/46818/TC_EN.pdf) · [MIR, TC-301 and AF lenses](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/teleconverter/index6.htm)

The same chart marks the Nikkor 300 mm f/4.5 ED as unusable with either converter, although the patent’s master repeats the radii, spacings and indices of that lens’s US 3,774,991 design. It does the same for the 400 mm f/5.6 ED, the 1200 mm f/11 IF-ED and the Zoom-Nikkor 360–1200 mm f/11 ED, without giving a reason for each lens. At infinity the converter passes the full axial beam and reaches the 35 mm frame corner on the 300 mm f/4.5 ED, the AI 600 mm f/5.6 IF-ED, both ends of the 360–1200 mm zoom and every setting of the Zoom-Nikkor ED 180–600 mm f/8, whose AI-S manual lists the TC-300 among its accessories. The model does not represent the mechanical fit of the converter’s front tube. It offers the converter on these four manual-focus lenses and not on the autofocus telephotos. [Nikon compatibility chart, pp. 3–4](https://service.nikonrepair.eu/article_files/46818/TC_EN.pdf) · [US 3,774,991, Example I](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3774991A/en) · [Nikon 180–600 mm manual, accessories](https://portablemedia.manualsonline.com/manuals/mfg/nikon/ed180600mm.html?p=12)

## Field Coverage

On the patent master at full aperture, the chief ray reaches the 21.65 mm corner of the 35 mm frame at 2.089° off axis. The patent gives a half-field of 2°05′. Both Figure 2 and Nikon’s production section show each singlet’s two faces ending at the same height. Scaled to its 88.5 mm vertex span, Nikon’s section gives clear-radius estimates of 13.3 mm for the front three singlets and 19.8 mm for the rear two. Figure 2 draws the rear pair at about 18.7–18.8 mm. Neither source dimensions the clear radii. The chief ray clears all ten surfaces. Some outer rays of the corner beam meet the rear rims, and about 96% of the pupil reaches the corner. The patent also prints no iris position for the master. The model places the iris 1.0 mm behind the master’s seventh surface, with its radius calculated from f/4.5. The corner figure depends on that position and on the clear radii read from the drawing.
