# Nikon Zoom 700VR 38-105mm f/4-7.8

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 5,530,593  
**Filed:** 3 September 1993  
**Granted:** 25 June 1996  
**Priority:** 11 September 1992 (JP 4-242485) and 6 October 1992 (JP 4-267173)  
**Inventors:** Atsushi Shibayama; Susumu Sato  
**Assignee:** Nikon Corporation  
**Title:** Zoom Lens  
**Example details:** Table 1, Figure 3. Kept at the patent's scale, f = 39–102 mm.

This is a patent design associated with the Nikon Zoom 700VR, also sold as the Zoom-Touch 105 VR, not a confirmed factory prescription; Nikon does not link the patent to the camera. The embodiment's f = 39–102 mm and F/4.08–8.05 differ from the marketed 38–105 mm f/4–7.8, and the file keeps the patent's values. The front page spells the second inventor "Susumo Sato"; the patent's recorded assignment reads SATO, SUSUMU, the spelling used across this catalog.

1. **Construction.** Embodiment 1 has 12 elements in 10 groups, as do Embodiments 3 and 5; Embodiments 2 and 4 have 13 in 11. Nikon's history pages give no count. One hobby reference page lists twelve elements in ten groups for the camera.
2. **Range.** Embodiment 1 is the closest 12-element match, at 39–102 mm against 40–90 mm for Embodiment 3 and 36.3–77.9 mm for Embodiment 5. Embodiment 2 covers nearly the same range at F/3.92–8.14 with one more element. No embodiment reaches 38–105 mm.
3. **Mechanism.** The patent shifts a lens group across the axis to correct hand shake, and Nikon describes the camera's VR as shifting some lens elements.
4. **Format.** The patent's H = 21.6 mm and its "Leica size" conditions fit a 24 × 36 mm film camera.
5. **Timing.** The priority dates are September and October 1992, and Nikon introduced the camera in April 1994.

The match is at family level: the marketed range and tele f-number are not those of any embodiment.

## Optical Architecture

Twelve spherical elements form ten air-separated groups in a positive-positive-negative zoom. G1 (f = +62.6 mm) has three singlets. G2 (f = +31.3 mm) has a front subgroup GF of very low power (f = −1598 mm), the stop, and a rear subgroup GR (f = +35.9 mm); each subgroup is a cemented pair and a singlet, and they move together in this embodiment. G3 (f = −24.2 mm) has three singlets.

From wide to tele all three groups move toward the object, d6 opens, d17 closes and the back focus grows. The printed stop is surface 12, with 2.00 mm of air on each side. GR is the patent's stabilizing group and G3 its preferred focus group; this model keeps GR centered and holds infinity focus.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Focal lengths are in-air thick-lens values; they are not the in-assembly powers of cemented members.

### L1 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.80384, νd = 33.9. Glass: E-LAFH2 (HIKARI catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = −32.50 mm.

The negative front element of G1. Its high-dispersion glass sits ahead of the two positive crowns of the group.

### L2 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.61720, νd = 54.0. Glass: K-SSK1 (SUMITA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +32.49 mm.

The main positive element of G1. Its front radius nearly matches L1's rear radius across the 1.00 mm air gap.

### L3 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.62041, νd = 60.1. Glass: J-SK16 (HIKARI catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +80.10 mm.

Convex to the object, it closes G1 and shares its positive power with L2.

### L4 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.80411, νd = 46.5. Glass: TAF3D (HOYA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = −10.45 mm.

The front of cemented pair C1 (pair f = −32.40 mm), the negative part of GF.

### L5 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.74000, νd = 28.2. Glass: FD3 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +16.12 mm.

The flint rear of C1. It weakens L4's strong negative power.

### L6 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.51680, νd = 64.1. Glass: N-BK7 (Schott catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +38.35 mm.

Concave to the object and last before the stop. Its positive power offsets C1, which leaves GF with the low net power the patent calls for.

### L7 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.51860, νd = 69.9. Glass: J-PKH1 (HIKARI catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +34.67 mm.

The patent's LR1, first element of GR and front of cemented pair C2 (pair f = −234.75 mm). Conditions (3) and (4) set its 5.70 mm thickness and concave front radius; the patent ties both to correcting coma and astigmatism.

### L8 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.86074, νd = 23.0. Glass: J-SFH2 (HIKARI catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = −35.77 mm.

The flint rear of C2 and the only negative element in GR, which it achromatizes.

### L9 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.51860, νd = 69.9. Glass: J-PKH1 (HIKARI catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +37.37 mm.

The last element of GR. It carries most of the subgroup's positive power, since C2 is nearly afocal.

### L10 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.75520, νd = 27.6. Glass: SF4 (Schott catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +39.83 mm.

Concave to the object, the only positive element in negative group G3.

### L11 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.84042, νd = 43.3. Glass: Unmatched (840433 lanthanum flint; no public catalog row). f = −24.84 mm.

Concave to the object, the strongest negative element of G3.

### L12 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.69680, νd = 55.6. Glass: S-LAL14 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = −38.63 mm.

Concave to the object, the last and largest element. Figure 3 draws a flat land outside its front curve.

The patent makes GR achromatic so that color stays controlled across the zoom and when GR decenters. Its condition (5), νP − νN ≥ 40, gives 46.9 here. Bonding a positive and a negative element in GR is its stated route to correcting both color and coma.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent gives G3 moving toward the image as the suitable focusing method, because performance change and travel stay small (column 12, claims 17 and 39). It names G2, or GR alone, moving toward the object as alternatives. Table 1 prints no close state and no travel, so no group moves with focus in this model, and it holds infinity focus at every zoom position.

The file's 0.75 m closest distance rests on a single hobby reference page. Nikon's history pages give no figure, and camera-wiki's lens name includes "Macro" without a distance.

## Zoom States

Table 1 prints three infinity states:

| Printed f (mm) | 38.9998 | 69.9996 | 101.9998 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| d6 | 3.2347 | 10.3035 | 17.0677 |
| d17 | 15.0298 | 7.2691 | 3.1954 |
| B.f | 8.9728 | 31.0851 | 48.8923 |

These give paraxial focal lengths of 39.002, 70.005 and 102.010 mm, and paraxial back focus of 8.974, 31.088 and 48.897 mm. The printed gaps are kept.

The model adds eight inferred stations, five from wide to middle and three from middle to tele, at geometric steps in f. At each one, d6 and d17 follow monotone curves through the printed values and G3 is solved to hold the paraxial image on the image plane. All three groups keep moving toward the object, and paraxial defocus between stations stays below 0.08 mm. The inferred stations are a model of the cam, not Nikon's.

## Image Stabilization

In the patent only GR moves to correct blur, in a direction almost perpendicular to the axis; Figure 3 shows a detection unit, a control unit and a drive unit acting on it. The patent places the group inside the lens so that little external force acts on it. Its sensitivity, image shift per unit of GR shift, computes to 0.950 at wide, as printed, and 1.751 at tele. Nikon's account says only that the camera shifts some lens elements.

The model keeps GR centered, and no decentering is modeled. The patent also places a stationary flare stopper SF behind GR, which stays put when GR shifts. Table 1 gives it no data, so it is not modeled.

## Stop, Field and Clear Apertures

Table 1 prints F = 4.08–8.05 and no middle value. The printed ends imply stop radii of 4.990 and 4.988 mm, so the model uses one constant iris of about 4.99 mm, sized at F/4.08. The middle f/6.40 and the inferred stations' f-numbers are computed from it. The constant iris is an assumption, not a camera measurement, and the f/16 end of the scale is a model control, not a sourced value.

Table 1 prints 2ω = 58.20°–23.32° at H = 21.6 mm. The tele value reproduces: a chief ray at 11.66° lands at 21.60 mm. The wide value does not: the model reaches H = 21.6 mm at 28.59°, so 2ω = 57.19°, and a chief ray at the printed half-field lands at 22.07 mm. Embodiments 2–5 reproduce their printed wide values within 0.04°, so 58.20 is probably a misprint of 57.20. The model reaches the 24 × 36 mm corner, 21.65 mm, at every station.

The patent prints no clear apertures. The semi-diameters are inferred from Figure 3, whose arcs show it isotropic within about 1 %, with flat lands on S2, S20 and S22 as drawn. Two differences remain. Cemented faces take no land here, so the edges of L4 and L8 slant where the figure steps. S22 sits at 11.3 mm against about 11.1 mm drawn, to clear the image circle, so it stands level with L11's 11.3 mm rim instead of just below it; the two faces stay about 0.17 mm apart there. Wide open, corner light is about 54 % at wide, 62 % at middle and 53 % at tele; these figures describe the inferred rims, not a measured camera.
