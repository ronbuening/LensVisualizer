# NIKON NEW NIKKOR 20mm f/4: US 3,549,241 Example 2

## Patent Reference / Design Identification

**Patent:** US 3,549,241 A  
**Filed:** October 15, 1968  
**Granted:** December 22, 1970  
**Priority:** October 24, 1967, Japan  
**Inventor:** Ikuo Mori  
**Assignee:** Nippon Kogaku K.K.  
**Title:** Extremely wide-angle object lens  
**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 2 (Embodiment II)

The modeled prescription is the complete Embodiment II table on PDF page 7, printed column 4,
corroborated by claim 2 on PDF page 9. The source is normalized to a focal length of 100.
Every finite prescription length is scaled uniformly by 0.2 for a model near the marketed
20 mm focal length. Indices and Abbe numbers are unchanged. This similarity transformation
is an author modeling choice; it does not establish production dimensions. [1]

The model is titled after the 1974 New Nikkor 20mm f/4, the production lens this patent family is
correlated with. That association has the following support and limits:

1. Nikon identifies Ikuo Mori as the designer of the 20mm f/4 and describes its positive-first
   retrofocus architecture. The selected patent names Mori and the historical Nikon company. [1,2]
2. The patent's f/4 and positive-front design family are consistent with the product, and its
   priority precedes the product's 1974 introduction, explicitly dated by Nikon's Tale86. [2,7]
3. Nikon does not identify this exact numerical example. The patent specifically identifies
   Examples III–V as its 35 mm camera designs, while Example 2 has a published full field of 90°.
   Neither the selected table nor the scale transformation certifies a production image circle. [1]
4. The selected prescription has 11 elements in 9 groups. Nikon's production cross-section
   depicts 10 elements in 8 groups with an unsplit central positive component. The extra split
   in Example II is a direct architectural difference, not merely an unconfirmed count. Its
   published near compensation must not be equated with the production focus mechanism. [2, Fig.2]

Accordingly, exact production correspondence is unconfirmed. The production name identifies the
correlated lens, and the subtitle states that the model is the scaled patent example and that the
production lens differs. Nikon's Tale 86 calls the 1974 lens the "New Nikkor 20mm f/4"; it was
re-issued with the same optics as the Ai Nikkor 20mm f/4 in 1977. [2,7]

The mount and image-format fields are those of the production lens: Nikon F mount and the 135
format. They describe the correlated product, not a claim that this scaled example was designed
for that frame. The patent example publishes a full field of 90°, which at this scale is an image
height of 20.0 mm (traced chief ray at 45.5°). The 21.63 mm corner of the 135 format needs a full
field of about 94.5°. The traced chief ray still passes there (47.6°), but only about 30% of the
meridional pupil height survives and the patent publishes no aberration data beyond 45°, so the
last 1.6 mm of the frame diagonal lies outside the published design field.

## Optical Architecture

The front group comprises a positive meniscus followed by two negative menisci. The patent
calls the combined front system dispersive. The rear comprises a cemented positive component,
a positive component split into two air-separated pieces, a cemented negative component, and
two positive menisci. The patent's component labels L4 and L6 therefore each cover two physical
elements; L5a and L5b identify the split component. [1, Fig. 2(A)]

At the infinity prescription, independent first-order calculations from the implemented surface
values give EFL 20.002237 mm, rear-vertex BFD 34.107379 mm, and first-to-last-vertex
track 38.530000 mm. The calculated rear focal distance exceeds the focal length, so the
retrofocus description is numerically supported. The last authored air spacing remains the
printed scaled 34.1 mm; the small difference from Gaussian BFD reflects retained source precision.
These axial optical distances do not represent barrel length or flange focal distance.

The iris station is inferred within the air gap between R12 and R13. Its midpoint placement
preserves the source vertex separation, but is not a published mechanical dimension. No sensor
plate, accessory filter, dummy plane, asphere, mirror, stabilization group or special projection
is introduced. The two plane faces in the central split component are genuine refracting
interfaces and remain in the model.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are standalone thick-element values with air on both sides. They must
not be added as if the assembled cemented components had the same surrounding media.

### L1 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.62041, νd = 60.3. Glass: 620603 dense barium crown, SK16 class (J-SK16 HIKARI / S-BSM16 OHARA coordinate-compatible; supplier unconfirmed). f = +89.598 mm.

The positive first meniscus precedes both negative front elements. The patent relates the first two elements' curvature relationship to reducing the peripheral air-space growth and the effective front diameter. That is a source design rationale, not a newly simulated aberration decomposition. [1, printed columns 2–3]

### L2 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.69680, νd = 55.6. Glass: 697556 lanthanum crown, LaK14 class (J-LAK14 HIKARI / S-LAL14 OHARA coordinate-compatible; supplier unconfirmed). f = -37.517 mm.

This element contributes negative standalone power to the dispersive front system. Its curvature must be interpreted together with the preceding positive meniscus and the following negative meniscus; its isolated focal length does not specify its in-situ aberration contribution.

### L3 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.69680, νd = 55.6. Glass: 697556 lanthanum crown, LaK14 class (J-LAK14 HIKARI / S-LAL14 OHARA coordinate-compatible; supplier unconfirmed). f = -21.745 mm.

The final front-group element is also negative in isolation. Its clear apertures (9.0 mm front, 7.3 mm rear) follow the rims drawn in Fig. 2(A), with the steep rear surface (R = 9.138 mm) held slightly larger than drawn so the full-field chief ray passes. They are figure-based estimates, not measured production rims.

### L4a — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.67790, νd = 55.5. Glass: 678555 lanthanum crown, LaK12 class (LAC12 HOYA / K-LaK12 SUMITA coordinate-compatible; supplier unconfirmed). f = -19.302 mm.

The first physical member of L4 is a negative standalone meniscus, even though the cemented component is positive. This distinction resolves the apparent difference between the patent's component description and the power of an individual glass piece.

### L4b — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.60562, νd = 43.9. Glass: 606439 barium flint, BaF4 class (S-BAM4 OHARA / N-BAF4 SCHOTT coordinate-compatible; supplier unconfirmed). f = +17.381 mm.

The second member shares the R9 interface with L4a. The cemented component extends from R7 through R10. Its net positive power is calculated with the actual index change at the shared surface; no generic cement layer is added.

### L5a — Plano-Convex

nd = 1.60342, νd = 38.0. Glass: 603380 flint, F5 class (J-F5 HIKARI / F5 SCHOTT coordinate-compatible; supplier unconfirmed). f = +36.790 mm.

The first part of L5 has a convex entrance and a plane exit. The plane is active because it changes the medium from glass to air. The source explicitly divides this positive component to permit near-distance compensation.

### L5b — Plano-Convex

nd = 1.60342, νd = 38.0. Glass: 603380 flint, F5 class (J-F5 HIKARI / F5 SCHOTT coordinate-compatible; supplier unconfirmed). f = +31.646 mm.

The second part begins with a plane entrance and ends at R12. Its glass coordinates equal those of L5a, but the two parts remain separate physical elements. The intervening gap decreases in the published near condition. [1, Fig. 2(F), printed column 4]

### L6a — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.76182, νd = 26.5. Glass: 762265 dense flint, SF14 class (J-SF14 HIKARI / S-TIH14 OHARA coordinate-compatible; supplier unconfirmed). f = -10.054 mm.

The biconcave member is the negative-power part of cemented L6. The patent associates the overall rear arrangement with its aberration objectives, but the scalar power alone does not demonstrate a particular coma or astigmatism cancellation.

### L6b — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.76684, νd = 46.2. Glass: 767462 lanthanum dense flint (nearest J-LASFH2 HIKARI, nd identical, catalog νd 46.78, Δνd +0.58; supplier unconfirmed). f = +43.089 mm.

This meniscus is positive in isolation and shares R14 with L6a. The assembled L6 component nevertheless remains negative. No current catalog glass reproduces both coordinates. J-LASFH2 (HIKARI) has the same nd to five decimals and νd = 46.78, a +0.58 residual; its dispersion curve is used as the nearest stand-in. The stored values remain the patent's 1.76684 / 46.2, and the label does not identify the historical glass.

### L7 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.51680, νd = 64.2. Glass: 517642 borosilicate crown, BK7 class (J-BK7A HIKARI / N-BK7 SCHOTT coordinate-compatible; supplier unconfirmed). f = +32.529 mm.

The penultimate positive meniscus has a relatively thin center and a strong rear curvature. Its 5.4 mm clear aperture is the largest that leaves a workable edge (about 0.17 mm); Fig. 2(A) draws it at 4.9 mm.

### L8 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.63930, νd = 45.0. Glass: 639450 barium flint, BaF12 class (BAF12 SUMITA / S-BAM12 OHARA coordinate-compatible; supplier unconfirmed). f = +25.841 mm.

The last positive meniscus terminates the prescription. Its contribution to Gaussian power and Petzval sum is included explicitly; describing it as a proven field flattener would require evidence beyond its position and sign alone.

The actual cemented L4 component has focal length +130.674 mm, while cemented L6 has focal length -12.859 mm.
Those assembled-component results include the real shared glass-to-glass refractions and are
separate from the air-surrounded values above.

elements carry a catalog-equivalent label: a current catalog glass whose nd and νd reproduce the
patent pair, used for its dispersion curve. The eleventh, L6b, uses the nearest catalog glass with
its νd residual stated. The labels name coordinate classes and do not assert

closer (nearest M-TAF101 at 1.76802 / 49.24).

The cited OHARA table supplies direct candidate line indices for its listed types; Hikari and
SCHOTT provide additional comparisons, and HOYA supplies coordinate alternatives. OHARA S-
prefix names are retained exactly and are not merged with L-prefix molding types. Independent full primary AGF coordinate scans of OHARA, HOYA and Sumita were performed;
the Hikari catalog and selected SCHOTT and CDGM primary rows were also checked. No exhaustive
SCHOTT/CDGM catalog scan is claimed. [3–6,8–10]

Modern coordinate agreement is insufficient to establish the exact spectral behavior of the
historical glass. The catalog curves are therefore stand-ins for the chromatic displays, not
measured properties of these elements, and the stored nd/νd remain the patent values. No
apochromatic or anomalous-dispersion performance is inferred from this palette.

The surface-by-surface Petzval sum is 0.005885512 mm⁻¹, evaluated as the sum of
surface power divided by the product of its incident and transmitted indices. This is a
first-order curvature quantity, not the actual sagittal or tangential best-focus surface at
an arbitrary field. The patent's plotted aberrations are not reproduced quantitatively here.

## Focus Mechanism

The focus evidence status is PUBLISHED for the relative internal states. Figure 2(F) gives
|magnification| = 1/25 and d11′ = 1.57, compared with the table's 2.16 at infinity. After the
uniform scale, the split L5 gap changes from 0.432 to 0.314 mm. The patent explains that this
reduction compensates the near-distance changes in astigmatism and coma. It does not provide
an absolute barrel registration or continuous mechanical cam law. [1, printed column 4]

Keeping the other relative spacings fixed, a Gaussian conjugate solution for signed
magnification −0.04 yields an object plane 493.597306 mm ahead of the first vertex,
a near image gap 34.908576 mm, and object-to-image distance
0.566918 m. These are calculated conjugates of the scaled patent state,
not source-published dimensions or a reconstruction from the marketed minimum focus distance.

| Quantity | Infinity | Published-relative near state |
|---|---:|---:|
| Internal split gap, mm | 0.432 | 0.314 |
| Authored rear image gap, mm | 34.1 | 34.908576 |
| Signed paraxial magnification | 0 | −0.04 |

The difference between the two authored rear gaps includes whole-system placement needed for
this conjugate model. It is not identified as a particular production helicoid motion.
The viewer's intermediate states use linear interpolation of the two authored gaps; this is
an illustrative model convention, not evidence of the manufacturer's focus law. Geometric
checks at intermediate states do not establish intermediate aberration performance.
Nikon's production minimum distance of 0.30 m is a distinct manufacturer specification and
is not used as the model's close endpoint. [2]

## Conditional Expressions and Aperture Limits

The implemented source satisfies the published ratio 1.5 < R2/R3 < 4.0, since R2/R3 =
2.656798. The second condition d13+d14>d15 has a positive native margin
7.98 normalized units. The source actually prints the preferred range 0.8<R1/R2<1.2, which this
example does not satisfy: R1/R2 = 0.442146698. This nonbinding textual preference is
inconsistent with the selected table; neither the denominator nor the radii are silently altered. Finally |R12|<R11 holds with a native margin of
15.52 units. These checks preserve native source numbering despite
the added plane and iris labels. [1, printed columns 2–3]

The inferred stop semi-diameter is 4.013665 mm. An exact axial ray launched at
EFL/(2×4) calibrates the aperture to f/4.000000. The independently evaluated
paraxial pupil gives f/3.925996. This explicit difference matters: the exact result is a
calibration to the published f/4, not independent evidence for an unpublished diaphragm size.

The patent lists no clear apertures. Fig. 2(A) is drawn to scale in its curvatures and vertex
spacings (the scaled prescription overlays the drawn surfaces at 0.0445 mm per pixel), but its rim
heights are not usable as clear apertures behind L4a. The drawn L4b–L7 rims (4.2–4.9 mm) sit only
2–14% above the f/4 axial beam (3.9–4.3 mm); taken literally they would pass 87% of the pupil at
20°, 63% at 35° and 40% at 45°. The patent's own coma plot, Fig. 2(B), is drawn out to about 0.87
of the full aperture on both sides at 35° and 45°, which those rims cannot transmit. The drawing
also closes L1 to a knife edge at 15.0 mm where the prescription's surfaces meet at 17.3 mm.

The model therefore uses the figure for L1 through L4a (16.0, 12.8/11.0, 9.0/7.3 and 6.6 mm; L1 and
the rear of L2 kept larger than the drawn 14.8 and 9.4 mm so the 45° chief ray is not blocked) and
ray-supported rims from the L4 cement to L8: 6.3 and 6.1 mm on L4b, 6.0 mm on L5a/L5b, 5.1 mm on
L6, 5.4 mm on L7 and 6.2 mm on L8. These keep the drawn order of heights (L4 tapering toward the
stop, L5 and L6 the smallest, L7 below L8) with 25–48% margin over the axial marginal ray. All
values satisfy the rim-slope, edge-thickness and gap-clearance conditions; L7 is limited to 5.4 mm
by its edge thickness.

An exact meridional trace at f/4 and infinity gives the following share of the stop height that
passes every clear aperture: 100% through 30°, 89% at 35°, 71% at 40°, 50% at 45° and 30% at the
135-format corner (47.6°). Beyond 30° the lower rays are cut by the L4–L5 rims (joined by the front-group rims
from 40°) and the upper rays by the L6–L8 rims. The chief ray passes at every field through 47.6°
(image height 20.0 mm at 45.5°; traced distortion about −1.9% at 45°, the same sign and order as
Fig. 2(E)). These are model apertures, not a measured production vignetting curve.
