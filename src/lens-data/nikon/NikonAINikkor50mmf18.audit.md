# NIKON AI NIKKOR 50mm f/1.8 — audit

## Source

Sei Matsui, _Modified Gauss Type Photographic Lens_, US 4,139,265, assigned to Nippon Kogaku K.K. and granted February 13, 1979. Example 1 spans columns 3–4, Figure 1 labels L1–L6, and claim 1 repeats the prescription. Nikon's dealer sheet for the 1978 long-barrel AI Nikkor gives the dimensions of the production optics. [Patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4139265A/en) · [Nikon dealer sheet, printed p. 60](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00594/00594.pdf#page=59)

## Transcription

All eleven radii, ten axial spacings and six d-line index and Abbe-number pairs agree with Example 1. The ×0.516 scale converts the patent's 100 mm focal length to the 51.6 mm marked on Nikon's optical drawing. Both 0.194 mm air spaces become 0.100104 mm. Surface r8 is common to L4 and L5. The table gives only spherical surfaces and one set of spacings. The September 25, 1979 certificate corrects the typesetting of condition (5) and leaves the numerical example intact.

The image plane coincides with the paraxial focus to within 0.0001 mm. At f/1.8 the full axial beam clears the glass and the corner chief ray reaches 21.65 mm image height. At that field, a ray grazing the lower 18 mm front rim reaches the stop at −6.29 mm. The front element therefore obscures the lower edge of the 10.06 mm stop in the corner pupil. With the inferred rims, geometric pupil transmission falls from 100% on axis to 54.6% at the corner.

Figure 1 places the iris 57% of the distance from r6 to r7, giving 7.467119 mm before the stop and 5.633089 mm after it. Together they preserve the 13.100208 mm scaled air space. A 10.061 mm iris radius gives a 14.334 mm entrance-pupil radius at f/1.8. The last surface to film distance is the paraxial back focus calculated from the prescription.

Hoya TAF4 for L2 differs from the printed pair by +0.00003 in nd and −0.01 in νd. Hoya FD3 for L3 and L4 differs by −0.000001 and +0.045 respectively. These catalog equivalents supply dispersion curves. The four glass pairs are printed without manufacturer names.

## Product Identification

Nikon's dealer section shows the same six element shapes and the single cemented pair behind the diaphragm. The stack length, back focus, focal-point separation and principal-plane separation each agree within 0.03 mm. Only Example 1 combines that layout with f/1.8. Examples 2–4 specify f/2. This strongly identifies the original long-barrel AI lens. Nikon identifies the compact 1980 AI-S as Nakamura's narrower design. ## Corrections

### 2026-11-25

The scaled prescription reproduces the printed table. The calculated back focus is 0.012 mm shorter than the rounded patent ratio implies and agrees with Nikon's 37.6 mm dimension. The 0.45 m minimum focus belongs to the production lens. The patent gives no spacings at that distance.

## Remaining Questions

Neither source dimensions the glass edges or iris opening. The outlines at r4–r6 leave some uncertainty about the finished edge profiles, and the calculated corner transmission lacks a factory illumination measurement for comparison. The glasses used for L1/L6 and L5 remain unidentified. A finite-distance prescription would be needed to compare the production lens at its closest focus.
