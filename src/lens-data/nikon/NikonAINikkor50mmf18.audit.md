# NIKON AI NIKKOR 50mm f/1.8 — Audit Log

## Source

[US 4,139,265, Modified Gauss Type Photographic Lens](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4139265A/en), Sei Matsui, assigned to Nippon Kogaku K.K. (now Nikon Corporation). US Application 05/757,144 was filed January 6, 1977, with Japanese priority 51-3932 of January 16, 1976, and the patent was granted February 13, 1979. The text layer of the scanned front page gives the priority date as January 6; the printed page reads January 16. The patent has four numerical examples at f = 100 mm and 2ω = 46°. Example 1, in columns 3–4, is the only one at 1:1.8; Examples 2–4 are 1:2.0. Figure 1 on the front page draws Example 1 with lenses L1–L6, and claim 1 repeats its prescription. A Certificate of Correction dated September 25, 1979 corrects the typesetting of condition (5).

Nikon's dealer sheet for the Nikkor 50mm f/1.8 (printed p. 60) gives the production specifications and a dimensioned optical section: 6 elements in 5 groups, 46° picture angle, f/1.8–f/22, a distance scale to 0.45 m, 52 mm filters and 220 g. The drawing dimensions the focal length as 51.6 mm, the first-to-last-surface length as 34.85 mm, the back focus as 37.6 mm, the principal-plane interval as 6.55 mm and the distance between the focal points as 96.65 mm. Nikon's NIKKOR histories give the release years of the 1978 lens and the compact 1980 AI-S, and the AI-S section. [Nikon dealer sheet](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00594/00594.pdf#page=59) · [Nikon, Tale 2](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/information/story/0002/) · [Nikon, Tale 60](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/information/story/0060/) · [Compact AI-S section](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/information/story/0060/img/img1.jpg)

JAPB's data sheet records measurements of an original AI lens (1978–1982 production, 45 cm minimum focus, 1:6.8, seven straight blades, click stops f/1.8–f/22, 210° focus throw), and Richard Haw's repair notes describe the helicoid and the later versions. [JAPB data sheet](https://japb.net/gear/gear-review-index/ds_nikkor-ai-50f18/) · [Richard Haw, repair notes](https://richardhaw.com/2020/05/23/repair-nikkor-50mm-f-1-8-ai/)

## Transcription Check

The eleven radii, ten axial spacings and six d-line index and Abbe-number pairs of Example 1 were reread against the 300 dpi page images of column 4 and against claim 1. None disagree. Every length in the model equals the printed value × 0.516; for example r1 = 79.457 becomes 40.999812 mm. Both 0.194 air spaces become 0.100104 mm, and the 25.388 diaphragm space becomes 13.100208 mm, split around the stop. Surface r8 is the cemented interface between L4 and L5. The table has no aspherical surfaces and only one set of spacings. The Certificate of Correction leaves the numerical example unchanged, so no value is corrected.

The calculated back focus is 0.72877f, 0.012 mm shorter than the rounded printed ratio implies, and it agrees with Nikon's 37.6 mm. The image plane lies at that paraxial focus, within 0.0001 mm. The scaled example satisfies the patent's conditions: r6/r3 = 0.757 (1), r7/r9 = 0.799 (2), |r3/r9| = 1.037 (3), |r6/r7| = 0.982 (4) and (r6 + |r7|)/2 = 0.317f (5); the mean index of condition (6) is 1.753, and the index and Abbe-number bounds of conditions (7) and (8) hold.

At f/1.8 the full axial beam clears the glass, and the 23.0° chief ray reaches the 21.65 mm corner of the 35 mm frame. At that field, a ray grazing the lower edge of the 18 mm front rim reaches the stop at −6.29 mm, so the front element obscures the lower edge of the 10.06 mm stop in the corner pupil. Geometric pupil transmission falls from 100% on axis to 55.1% at the corner.

Figure 1 places the iris 57% of the distance from r6 to r7, giving 7.467119 mm before the stop and 5.633089 mm after it; together they keep the 13.100208 mm scaled air space. The 10.061 mm iris radius is calculated from f/1.8 and gives a 14.334 mm entrance-pupil radius. The last-surface-to-film distance is the paraxial back focus calculated from the prescription.

The patent prints 2ω = 46° but no image height; the model's field reaches the 21.65 mm corner of the 35 mm frame at 23.0°.

The patent prints no finite focus state. `focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. Nikon's 0.45 m minimum is recorded as the marketed value only. If the whole scaled lens moves forward, an object 0.45 m from the film plane needs 7.70 mm of extension and gives 0.149× (1:6.7); the same distance from the front of the lens would give 0.121× (1:8.3). JAPB's measured 1:6.8 fits the film-plane reading.

## Product Identification

Nikon's dealer section shows the same six element shapes and the single cemented pair behind the diaphragm. The model's stack length, back focus, focal-point separation and principal-plane separation each agree with Nikon's dimensions within 0.03 mm. Only Example 1 combines that layout with f/1.8; Examples 2–4 are f/2. The patent's January 1976 priority precedes the lens's 1978 release, and the assignee is Nikon's. This strongly identifies the original long-barrel AI lens. [Nikon dealer sheet](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00594/00594.pdf#page=59) · [Nikon, Tale 2](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/information/story/0002/)

The compact 1980 AI-S is a different design: Nikon credits it to Souichi Nakamura, its section has a narrower diaphragm space and a flat rear face on the first element, and Nikon states that the Series E 50mm f/1.8 shares its basic optics. Richard Haw reports that a short-lived long-nose AI-S version kept the 1978 optical design in a different barrel. Nikon names no designer for the 1978 lens. [Nikon, Tale 60](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/information/story/0060/) · [Compact AI-S section](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/information/story/0060/img/img1.jpg) · [Richard Haw, repair notes](https://richardhaw.com/2020/05/23/repair-nikkor-50mm-f-1-8-ai/)

## Corrections, 2026-11-25

- The ×0.516 scale of the first-received model stays: it takes the patent's f = 100 mm table to the 51.6 mm focal length on Nikon's drawing, and the scaled stack and back focus then match Nikon's dimensions. The table has no erratum to correct.
- The stop moved from the middle of the r6–r7 air space (6.550104 mm on each side) to the Figure 1 position at 57% of the gap (7.467119 / 5.633089 mm). The aperture stays at the printed f/1.8, now with a 10.061 mm iris radius.
- The field is no longer limited by a calculated 22.75° half-field bound taken from the 135 diagonal, and the off-axis ray fan is no longer narrowed to 22% of the field. The corner chief ray reaches the 21.65 mm frame corner at 23.0°, consistent with the printed 2ω = 46°.
- The estimated rims were replaced by the Figure 1 readings in the table above: r1/r2 16.5 → 18.0 mm, r6 11.8 → 10.5 mm, r7 11.8 → 10.4 mm, r8/r9 13.5 → 12.4 mm and r10/r11 14.0 → 13.5 mm; r3 (14.0 mm) and r4/r5 (12.2 mm) kept their first values. Two rims depart from the figure: r4/r5 stay inside the drawn 14 mm because the faces would meet at about 12.95 mm, and r6 sits 0.1–0.4 mm outside the drawn edge to pass the full f/1.8 axial beam. Corner transmission at f/1.8 rises from 51.2% to 54.6%. Because the rims combine figure readings with these two limits, `source.sdSource` is not set.
- Focus stays at infinity, the only state the patent prints; no finite position is calculated.
- The lens was identified as Nikon's original 1978 long-barrel AI Nikkor 50mm f/1.8 for the Nikon F mount and the 35 mm full frame, with the patent's inventor and assignee and its 1979 grant year. The catalog records 50 mm and f/1.8 marketed, 51.6 mm and f/1.8 design, 0.45 m minimum focus, six elements in five groups, marked stops f/1.8, 2.8, 4, 5.6, 8, 11, 16 and 22 with f/22 as the smallest aperture, and seven blades. Blade shape is left unset because Nikon does not state it. [Nikon dealer sheet](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00594/00594.pdf#page=59) · [JAPB data sheet](https://japb.net/gear/gear-review-index/ds_nikkor-ai-50f18/)
- The elements carry shape types, labels L1–L6, optical roles and in-air focal lengths. L4 and L5 form the cemented doublet C1, and L2–L3 are bracketed as the air-spaced pair ahead of the diaphragm.
- The subtitle, specification chips, focus text and doublet label were rewritten in plain form.

Hoya catalog equivalents supply dispersion for three elements, keeping the printed nd and νd. Differences are catalog minus patent. L1/L6 (nd 1.79631, νd 40.8) and L5 (nd 1.74443, νd 49.4) have no catalog row within the printed precision and stay unmatched.

At infinity the finished model gives 51.601 mm EFL, f/1.800, 37.605 mm back focus, a 34.851 mm first-to-last-surface length, a 10.061 mm iris radius and a 23.0° corner half-field.

2026-11-25 rim review:

- r4 12.2 → 14.0 mm. Figure 1 draws L2 square-cut, with its flat rim 320 px above and below the axis (14.0 mm), and Nikon's section draws it the same way. At 14 mm the edge of L2 is 2.6 mm thick, as drawn. The front of L3 stays at 12.2 mm, so the two faces still do not meet.
- r6: a flat land from the 10.5 mm clear radius out to 12.2 mm. Figure 1 ends the concave rear face of L3 at 10.1–10.4 mm with a stroke square to the axis out to 12.6–12.7 mm, and Nikon's section shows the same step. The land stops at the 12.2 mm front rim of L3.
- r7: a flat land from the 10.4 mm clear radius out to 12.4 mm. Figure 1 ends the concave front face of L4 at 10.2–10.7 mm with a stroke square to the axis out to the 12.4 mm edge of the doublet, and Nikon's section agrees.
- The figure's scale was checked on its first-to-last vertex span and on the drawn r6 and r7 arcs, whose sags fall within 4–7% of the prescription. The lands do not change any ray path. With r4 at 14.0 mm, corner transmission at f/1.8 rises from 54.6% to 55.1%, and no field loses light. The traced field and f/1.800 are unchanged.

## Remaining Questions

- Neither the patent nor Nikon dimensions the glass edges or the iris opening. The front of L3 stays at 12.2 mm, inside the 14 mm both drawings show, because its face would meet the rear of L2 at about 12.95 mm; the outer step of the rear edge of L3 beyond 12.2 mm is not modelled. No factory measurement of corner illumination is published to compare with the calculated 55.1%.
- The glasses of L1/L6 and L5 remain unidentified.
- The patent prints no finite-distance spacings, and Nikon does not state whether the whole optical unit moves in focusing. The film-plane reading of the 0.45 m minimum rests on the measured 1:6.8 under that assumption.
