# NIKON ZOOM-NIKKOR ED 180-600mm f/8

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 3,743,384, _Optical System for the Magnification Varying Portion of an Ultra-Telephoto Type Zoom Lens_\
**Inventors:** Soichi Nakamura\
**Assignee:** Nippon Kogaku K.K.\
**Filed:** December 16, 1971, with Japanese priority of December 24, 1970\
**Published:** July 3, 1973\
**Example details:** Example II, Fig. 3 and claims 2 and 6, with the Certificate of Correction of March 5, 1974.

Nikon's dimensioned May 1976 drawing strongly identifies Example II with the pre-AI Zoom-Nikkor. At the short end, the example places each of the four optical assemblies within about 0.4 mm of its position on Nikon's drawing. The distance from the first to the last glass vertex is 281.018 mm, against 281 mm on the drawing. The drawing also gives 140 mm from the last glass to the film. The front singlet and triplet, the two moving assemblies and the widely separated sections of the rear relay match in shape and order. The patent's only other example, Example I, has 20 elements in 12 groups and a nominal range of 360–1200 mm at f/11. [Nikon drawing, May 1976](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/zoomsMF/180600mmEDpreAIopt.gif)

The pre-AI, AI and AI-S versions share the 180–600 mm f/8, 18-element, 11-group specification. Cavina dates the start of production to September 1975 at serial 174041, the AI mount to 1977 and the AI-S to March 1982, though his summary gives March 1983. Vink's serial list dates the first lenses from February 1976 and the AI-S from March 1983. The pre-AI and AI versions meter at the working aperture only. The AI-S has a meter-coupling ridge for full-aperture metering, and its manual lists the TC-300 and TC-14 teleconverters among the accessories. Nikon's AI-S specifications give a 13°40′ to 4°10′ picture angle, an f/8 to f/32 aperture scale, a distance scale from 2.5 m to infinity, a 95 mm filter thread and the Nikon bayonet mount for 35 mm film. Vink lists seven diaphragm blades for the early lens and nine for the AI-S. Cavina gives nine, and Nikon's specifications describe the diaphragm only as fully automatic. [Nikon AI-S specifications](https://portablemedia.manualsonline.com/manuals/mfg/nikon/ed180600mm.html?p=12) · [Version specifications and metering](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/zoomsMF/180600mmED.htm) · [Cavina's production history](https://www.marcocavina.com/articoli_fotografici/Nikon_180-600_e_360-1200mm_zoom/00_pag.htm) · [Vink's serial list](https://johnmeister.com/tech/NIKON/www.photosynthesis.co.nz/nikon/lenses.html)

## Optical Architecture

The 11 air-separated groups form four mechanical assemblies. The fixed positive front group f1, negative variator f2 and positive compensator f3 form a nearly afocal zoom system. The fixed positive group f4 forms the image. Its front and rear sections are separated by 71.5 mm of air. This telephoto arrangement puts the film 421.29 mm behind the front vertex at both ends of the zoom. All 29 refracting surfaces are spherical. [Patent, Figs. 1 and 3 and Example II](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3743384A/en)

The calculated focal lengths are 180.11 and 594.28 mm, respectively 0.06% above and 0.95% below the nominal endpoints. The four calculated group focal lengths agree with the corrected patent figures within 0.09%. The long-end focal length follows from those group powers and the printed separations. Fig. 5 gives curves at f/8 and half-field angles of 6.85°, 3.75° and 2.06°. At f/8 the entrance-pupil radius grows from 11.26 to 37.14 mm across the zoom. The patent prints no clear apertures, so the model's clear radii are measured from Fig. 3. The edge of the 36 × 24 mm frame receives a partially vignetted bundle at both ends.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent gives helium d-line refractive indices and Abbe numbers for all 18 elements. Eight catalog glasses reproduce the printed pairs of 13 elements within 0.00001 in nd and 0.05 in νd. These matches supply dispersion curves. The patent leaves the production glass types and suppliers unidentified.

Nikon's manual states that the ED glass reduces the secondary spectrum by about 90% between 400 and 1000 nm and removes the need to refocus for infrared photography. The patent likewise says that Example II needs essentially no infrared focus correction. De Stoutz records two ED elements on an AI-S lens, and Cavina places the two ED glasses, with νd 81.5, at L2 and L14. In Example II, only L2 and L14 share the exceptionally low dispersion of 1.48606 / 81.5. Their positions in the front triplet and relay doublet make them the likely ED elements. Nikon's May 1976 drawing leaves the glass types unmarked. Their individual partial dispersions are unknown, as are the glass types of L4, L17 and L18. The patent's infrared correction curves therefore cannot be reproduced from the printed glass data alone. ## Focus Mechanism and Zoom Motion

Moving from short to long focal length at infinity, f2 travels 115.837 mm toward the film. Group f3 moves in the same direction by 80.735 mm, compensating for the shift of the image as f2 changes magnification. The front group, relay and film stay fixed. The patent prints the following air gaps in millimetres.

| Infinity setting | D6, front group to variator | D13, variator to compensator | D19, compensator to relay |
| ---------------- | --------------------------: | ---------------------------: | ------------------------: |
| 180 mm nominal   |                       2.474 |                       38.532 |                    83.912 |
| 600 mm nominal   |                     118.311 |                        3.430 |                     3.177 |

The three gaps always total 124.918 mm. Their intermediate values are calculated from the variator position by moving f3 until the paraxial image falls on a fixed film plane. The plane is 140.2743 mm behind R29, halfway between the endpoint back focal distances to the stated precision. The printed settings focus 20.01 µm behind that plane at the short end and 20.06 µm in front at the long end. Between the calculated settings the largest departure is 19.95 µm. The iris position is also inferred, halfway across D24. A radius of about 12.78 mm there gives f/8.

Nikon's manual describes a mechanically compensated zoom worked by one control ring. Sliding the ring zooms, turning it focuses, and the focus setting holds while zooming. The distance scale runs from 2.5 m to infinity at every focal length. The manual calls the setting a camera-to-subject distance, consistent with measurement from the film plane. Vink lists a maximum reproduction ratio of 1:3. The patent gives neither a finite conjugate nor group displacements for close focus, and Nikon does not say which group moves to focus. The model is therefore focused at infinity throughout the zoom and does not reach the 2.5 m minimum.
