# NIKON ZOOM-NIKKOR ED 180-600mm f/8

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 3,743,384, _Optical System for the Magnification Varying Portion of an Ultra-Telephoto Type Zoom Lens_\
**Inventors:** Soichi Nakamura\
**Assignee:** Nippon Kogaku K.K.\
**Filed:** December 16, 1971, with Japanese priority of December 24, 1970\
**Published:** July 3, 1973\
**Example details:** Example II, Fig. 3 and claims 2 and 6, with the Certificate of Correction of March 5, 1974.

Nikon's dimensioned May 1976 drawing strongly identifies Example II with the pre-AI Zoom-Nikkor. At the short end, the example places each of the four optical assemblies within about 0.4 mm of its position on Nikon's drawing. The distance from the first to the last glass vertex is 281.018 mm, against 281 mm on the drawing. The drawing also gives 140 mm from the last glass to the film. The front singlet and triplet, the two moving assemblies and the widely separated sections of the rear relay match in shape and order. The patent's only other example, Example I, has 20 elements in 12 groups and a nominal range of 360–1200 mm at f/11. [Nikon drawing, May 1976](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/zoomsMF/180600mmEDpreAIopt.gif)

The pre-AI, AI and AI-S versions share the 180–600 mm f/8, 18-element, 11-group specification. Nikon lists a 13°40′ to 4°10′ diagonal field, an f/8 to f/32 aperture scale and a 2.5 m minimum focusing distance. The mount is Nikon F for 35 mm film. [Nikon specifications](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/01037/01037.pdf) · [Version specifications and optical sections](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/zoomsMF/180600mmED.htm)

## Optical Architecture

The 11 air-separated groups form four mechanical assemblies. The fixed positive front group f1, negative variator f2 and positive compensator f3 form a nearly afocal zoom system. The fixed positive group f4 forms the image. Its front and rear sections are separated by 71.5 mm of air. This telephoto arrangement puts the film 421.29 mm behind the front vertex at both ends of the zoom. All 29 refracting surfaces are spherical. [Patent, Figs. 1 and 3 and Example II](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3743384A/en)

The calculated focal lengths are 180.11 and 594.28 mm, respectively 0.06% above and 0.95% below the nominal endpoints. The four calculated group focal lengths agree with the corrected patent figures within 0.09%. The long-end focal length follows from those group powers and the printed separations. Fig. 5 gives curves at f/8 and half-field angles of 6.85°, 3.75° and 2.06°. At f/8 the entrance-pupil radius grows from 11.26 to 37.14 mm across the zoom. The edge of the 36 × 24 mm frame receives a partially vignetted bundle at both ends.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent gives helium d-line refractive indices and Abbe numbers for all 18 elements. Eight catalog glasses reproduce the printed pairs of 13 elements within 0.00001 in nd and 0.05 in νd. These matches supply dispersion curves. The patent leaves the production glass types and suppliers unidentified.

Nikon describes ED glass in the finished lens, and two ED elements are documented for the AI-S version. In Example II, only L2 and L14 share the exceptionally low dispersion of 1.48606 / 81.5. Their positions in the front triplet and relay doublet make them the likely ED elements. Nikon's May 1976 drawing leaves the glass types unmarked. Their individual partial dispersions are unknown, as are the glass types of L4, L17 and L18. The patent's infrared correction curves therefore cannot be reproduced from the printed glass data alone. [Nikon instruction manual](https://cdn-10.nikon-cdn.com/pdf/manuals/archive/Zoom-Nikkor%20ED%20180-600mm%20f-8.pdf) · [AI-S specimen](https://www.destoutz.ch/lens_180-600mm_f8_174882.html) · [Cavina's analysis of the two ED zooms](https://www.marcocavina.com/articoli_fotografici/Nikon_180-600_e_360-1200mm_zoom/00_pag.htm)

## Focus Mechanism and Zoom Motion

Moving from short to long focal length at infinity, f2 travels 115.837 mm toward the film. Group f3 moves in the same direction by 80.735 mm, compensating for the shift of the image as f2 changes magnification. The front group, relay and film stay fixed. The patent prints the following air gaps in millimetres.

| Infinity setting | D6, front group to variator | D13, variator to compensator | D19, compensator to relay |
| ---------------- | --------------------------: | ---------------------------: | ------------------------: |
| 180 mm nominal   |                       2.474 |                       38.532 |                    83.912 |
| 600 mm nominal   |                     118.311 |                        3.430 |                     3.177 |

The three gaps always total 124.918 mm. Their intermediate values are calculated from the variator position by moving f3 until the paraxial image falls on a fixed film plane. The plane is 140.2743 mm behind R29, halfway between the endpoint back focal distances to the stated precision. The printed settings focus 20.01 µm behind that plane at the short end and 20.06 µm in front at the long end. Between the calculated settings the largest departure is 19.95 µm. The iris position is also inferred, halfway across D24. A radius of about 12.78 mm there gives f/8.

On the production lens, turning the control ring focuses and sliding it zooms. Nikon gives a minimum distance of 2.5 m, with a listed maximum reproduction ratio of 1:3. The patent gives neither a finite conjugate nor group displacements for close focus. The optical model therefore describes infinity focus throughout the zoom.
