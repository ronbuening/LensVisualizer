# NIKON NIKKOR 1200mm f/11 ED

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP S51-43920 A, _Achromatic Objective Lens_ (色消対物レンズ)\
**Inventors:** Yoshiyuki Shimizu\
**Assignee:** Nippon Kogaku K.K.\
**Filed:** October 14, 1974 (Japanese application 49-117055)\
**Published:** April 15, 1976\
**Example details:** The claim and description give the same prescription on pages 1 and 2. Figure 1 shows the lens section.

The sole example is a strong match for the 1975 pre-AI 1200mm f/11 ED lens head. The most distinctive evidence is dimensional. Nikon's production drawing places the first and last glass vertices 709.0 mm apart and the film a further 240.2 mm behind the last vertex. Both distances agree with the patent to the printed tenth of a millimetre. The drawing also gives 1036.9 mm between the principal planes, compared with 1036.68 mm calculated from the prescription. Both sections show three air-spaced singlets followed by a small cemented doublet. [Nikon's dimensioned section](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/preAI70/1200mmf11EDpreAIopt.gif) · [Japanese patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/JPS5143920A/en)

Nikon released the ED head in 1975 in a four-lens ED telephoto series with the 300mm f/4.5, 600mm f/5.6 and 800mm f/8, and credits Shimizu with the optical design of the 1964 focusing-unit family that included the Nikkor-P 1200mm f/11. Nikon's specifications, as transcribed by MIR, give five elements in four groups for the ED head and five in five for the Nikkor-P. The ED glass is the head's main change from the earlier lens: Nikon states that its colour correction holds at every aperture and that infrared photography needs no refocusing. The later internal-focusing 1200mm f/11 IF-ED uses nine elements in eight groups. Shimizu's earlier US 3,774,991 also contains two 1200mm f/11 examples. Example IV has five elements in four groups, but its 575 mm air space gives a glass-to-glass length of only 623 mm. Example V has seven elements. The dimensions distinguish the Japanese example even among lenses of the same focal length and speed. ## Optical Architecture

The front section has positive, negative and positive elements in sequence. Across a 659 mm air space, the cemented rear doublet intercepts the converging beam. Its net negative power lengthens the effective focal length to 1199.70 mm while the distance from the first vertex to the film is only 949.2 mm. All nine refracting surfaces are spherical. The patent gives the radii, spacings and back focus in millimetres at the stated 1200 mm scale. The calculated back focal distance is 240.06 mm, placing the printed film plane 0.14 mm behind paraxial focus.

The patent prints f/11 and a 5° full design field. Nikon specifies f/11 for the head. MIR's transcription of Nikon's 1974–76 sales manuals gives the ED head a 4°10′ angle of view, about twice the 2.07° that the 35 mm frame diagonal subtends at 1200 mm. Nikon's sales specification for the ED head (product 1296) gives 2°, the frame diagonal, as MIR does for the Nikkor-P of the same focal length. The model's 4.81° rim-limited field covers both figures. At the 21.63 mm frame corner the chief ray passes through every opening. At the patent's wider 2.5° half-field it reaches 25.36 mm from the axis at the last surface, outside the 24.4 mm rear clear radius measured from Nikon's drawing. The model therefore covers the Nikon frame and vignettes the outer patent field. ## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are for the individual elements in air. Figure 1 labels the cemented rear component L4. The letters a and b distinguish its two pieces of glass.

### L1 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.50032, νd = 81.9. Glass unmatched, inferred ED. f = +425.23 mm.

L1 supplies positive power where the incoming beam is widest. It has by far the highest Abbe number in the prescription, making it the likely location of the ED glass identified by Nikon. The production drawing does not mark individual glass types.

### L2 — Biconcave negative

nd = 1.744, νd = 44.9. Catalog equivalent LAF2 (Hoya). f = −283.24 mm.

The biconcave singlet between the two positive elements diverges the beam. Its stronger dispersion offsets part of the positive elements' longitudinal colour.

### L3 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.62374, νd = 47.0. Catalog equivalent E-BAF8 (Hikari). f = +438.76 mm.

L3 shares the front section's converging power with L1. Its lower Abbe number gives these two positive elements different contributions to colour correction.

### L4a and L4b — Cemented negative doublet

- **L4a, biconcave negative.** nd = 1.51632, νd = 64.2. Catalog equivalent S-BSL7 (Ohara). f = −261.27 mm. This member supplies most of the doublet's diverging power.
- **L4b, biconvex positive.** nd = 1.66854, νd = 39.2. Glass unmatched. f = +446.75 mm. This higher-dispersion glass adds positive power at the back of the lens.

The members share the spherical interface at surface 8. Together they have a focal length of −637.05 mm.

## Glass Identification and Chromatic Correction

Shimizu describes an objective designed to reduce secondary spectrum without fluorite crystals. Figure 2A plots longitudinal aberration at several wavelengths. At f/11, the calculated marginal d-line ray crosses the axis about 0.10 mm ahead of paraxial focus. The example gives helium d-line indices and Abbe numbers. It supplies neither partial-dispersion values nor a trade name for the front ED glass, so the original lens's secondary spectrum cannot be recovered from the table alone. [Japanese patent, pp. 1–3](https://patents.google.com/patent/JPS5143920A/en)

Three catalog equivalents supply dispersion curves. Hoya LAF2 matches L2 at the printed precision. For L3, Hikari E-BAF8 has the printed index and an Abbe number of 47.04. For L4a, Ohara S-BSL7 differs by +0.00001 in index and −0.06 in Abbe number. These agreements identify optical equivalents. The production glass suppliers are undocumented. ## Focus Mechanism and Diaphragm

The head is focused by a Nikon focusing unit. It screws into the front of the unit by an 88 mm thread, and the unit advances the entire head toward the subject, preserving the 659 mm internal separation. The unit's distance scale for this lens starts at 50 m, where Nikon's depth-of-field table lists a 1:38.8 reproduction ratio. Calculated from the prescription, 50 m measured from the film plane gives 1:38.8 and 50 m from the front vertex 1:39.6, so Nikon's distance is from the film; reaching it takes about 31 mm of extension. The model shows infinity only, because the patent gives no finite spacing or reproduction ratio. MIR's page for the Nikkor-P gives 43 m on the earlier focusing unit and 50 m on the AU-1. Nikon's description of 24× magnification compares the distant image with that from a 50 mm lens. [MIR, ED head](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/preAI70/1200mmf11ed.htm) · [Nikon depth-of-field table](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/preAI70/1200mmf11EDpreAIDOF.gif) · [MIR, Nikkor-P](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/6070nikkor/telephoto/1200mm.htm)

Nikon explains that the focusing unit's diaphragm vignetted all four corners with the 1200 mm lens. The head therefore carries a manual iris, marked from f/11 to f/64, and the unit's own diaphragm stays set at f/4.5 while shooting. The iris's precise axial location is unknown. Placing it halfway between the front and rear sections leaves 329.5 mm on either side and requires a 31.93 mm stop radius for f/11. The lens rims follow Nikon's dimensioned section, with clear radii of 56.8 mm across the three front elements and 24.4 mm across the rear doublet.
