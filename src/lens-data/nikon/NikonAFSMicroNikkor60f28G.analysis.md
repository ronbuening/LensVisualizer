# Nikon AF-S Micro-NIKKOR 60mm f/2.8G ED

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 7,898,744 B2 — *Close-Up Lens, Imaging Apparatus, and Method for Focusing Close-Up Lens*

**Inventor:** Mitsuaki Wada

**Assignee:** Nikon Corporation

**Filed:** March 13, 2008

**Granted:** March 1, 2011

**Priority:** March 14, 2007 (JP 2007-065400); February 15, 2008 (JP 2008-034053)

**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 2, Table 2 and Figure 3.

The viewer reproduces the unscaled 58.0 mm, f/2.88 patent example. The catalog associates this 12-element, nine-component, internally focusing macro design with the 60 mm product. The patent alone does not establish production identity, a uniform rescaling, glass suppliers, coating locations or manufacturing processes. Marketing focal length and aperture remain separate from the numerical prescription.

## Optical Architecture

Four optical groups have positive–negative–positive–negative power. G1 (L1–L3), G4 (L10–L12) and the stop remain fixed. G2 (L4–L6) moves imageward and G3 (L7–L9) moves objectward during focusing. Nine air-separated components comprise six singlets and three cemented doublets; this count differs from the four motion groups.

The paired moving groups change the internal separations to reach life-size magnification. Element-level aberration roles below are qualitative optical interpretations, not measured production performance. Listed element focal lengths are isolated thick-lens calculations in air and do not equal their powers inside cemented assemblies.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Neg. Meniscus (1× Asph)

nd = 1.804400, νd = 39.57. Glass: S-LAH63Q (OHARA, inferred coordinate counterpart). Isolated-in-air focal length ≈ -50.0 mm.

Front negative meniscus; aspherical rear surface S2A (standard K = −6.3148) corrects spherical aberration and coma at full aperture. Manufacturing process is not established by the prescription.

### L2 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.638540, νd = 55.48. Glass: K-SK18 (Sumita, inferred coordinate counterpart). Isolated-in-air focal length ≈ +85.0 mm.

Weak positive meniscus; contributes to Petzval sum correction with minimal added spherical aberration.

### L3 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.834807, νd = 42.71. Glass: TAFD5 (HOYA, inferred coordinate counterpart). Isolated-in-air focal length ≈ +57.6 mm.

Strongest positive element in G1; high-index LaF glass keeps curvatures moderate despite strong power.

### L4 — Neg. Meniscus (1× Asph)

nd = 1.516120, νd = 64.03. Glass: S-BSL7 (OHARA, approximate coordinate counterpart). Isolated-in-air focal length ≈ -51.5 mm.

Second asphere in moving G2; aspherical S8A (standard K = +1.1218) provides focus-dependent SA correction. Catalog counterpart is approximate; manufacturing process is unestablished.

### L5 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.620040, νd = 36.30. Glass: S-TIM2 (OHARA, inferred coordinate counterpart). Isolated-in-air focal length ≈ -26.3 mm.

Negative component of G2 cemented doublet; balances monochromatic aberration that varies during G2 travel.

### L6 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.883000, νd = 40.77. Glass: S-LAH58 (OHARA, inferred coordinate counterpart). Isolated-in-air focal length ≈ +21.9 mm.

Positive component of G2 cemented doublet; extremely high index (nd = 1.883) enables strong junction curvature for higher-order aberration control.

### L7 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.497820, νd = 82.56. Glass: J-FKH1 (Hikari, inferred coordinate counterpart). Isolated-in-air focal length ≈ +79.4 mm.

Low-dispersion element in moving G3; very low dispersion (vd = 82.56) provides secondary spectrum correction. Positioned in focusing group to compensate chromatic shift during focus travel.

### L8 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.603000, νd = 65.47. Glass: S-PHM53 (OHARA, inferred coordinate counterpart). Isolated-in-air focal length ≈ +40.6 mm.

Positive crown of G3 achromatic doublet (Δvd = 41.69); provides primary LoCA correction.

### L9 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.846660, νd = 23.78. Glass: S-TIH53 (OHARA, inferred coordinate counterpart). Isolated-in-air focal length ≈ -66.7 mm.

Negative flint of G3 achromatic doublet; dense flint provides large Abbe number contrast for chromatic correction.

### L10 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.805180, νd = 25.43. Glass: S-TIH6 (OHARA, inferred coordinate counterpart). Isolated-in-air focal length ≈ -45.3 mm.

Strong negative meniscus in fixed G4; high-dispersion flint provides chromatic balance and contributes to Petzval field flattening.

### L11 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.801000, νd = 34.96. Glass: J-LAF016 (Hikari, inferred coordinate counterpart). Isolated-in-air focal length ≈ -30.2 mm.

Negative component of G4 cemented doublet; high-index medium contributing to the group power.

### L12 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.846660, νd = 23.78. Glass: S-TIH53 (OHARA, inferred coordinate counterpart). Isolated-in-air focal length ≈ +24.0 mm.

Positive component of G4 cemented doublet; same glass as L9, strong convergence balances L11 for net field-flattening contribution.

## Glass Identification and Selection

Table 2 publishes refractive indices and Abbe numbers, not supplier identities. All named glasses above are inferred catalog counterparts. L4's S-BSL7 counterpart is approximate: the source index is 1.516120, not the catalog 1.516800. The source coordinates remain authoritative for the prescription.

L7 has the lowest dispersion (νd 82.56). This supports a low-dispersion description but does not establish Nikon's proprietary ED identity, glass chemistry or a patent-measured anomalous-dispersion value. The previous approximate ΔPgF=0.033 override was removed; wavelength behavior based on a compatible catalog curve remains a modeling inference. The L8/L9 doublet combines νd 65.47 and 23.78, providing dispersion contrast for chromatic balancing.

## Focus Mechanism

Table 2 specifies three stations; intermediate movement is interpolated.

| Air gap (mm) | Infinity | β = −0.5 | β = −1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| D6, G1–G2 | 2.62569 | 6.63506 | 13.83349 |
| D11, G2–stop | 12.29619 | 8.28682 | 1.08839 |
| D12, stop–G3 | 23.27238 | 12.33659 | 1.87196 |
| D17, G3–G4 | 4.49094 | 15.42810 | 25.89136 |

G2 travels 11.20780 mm imageward; G3 travels 21.40042 mm objectward. The source middle station has a 0.00137 mm discrepancy in D12+D17 relative to the endpoints. Its rows are preserved, so the model has that small rear-group drift rather than a silently repaired gap.

A reduced-angle paraxial matrix independently reproduces 58.01453 mm EFL at infinity, and magnifications −0.499955 and −0.999979 at the two finite stations. Derived object-to-image distances are approximately 0.229449 m and 0.177202 m. These are inferred conjugates of the patent prescription, not a production minimum-focus specification. The half-size keyframe occupies 0.77229235 of the inverse-distance slider. D17 is an internal gap; it is not BF. Rear BF remains 37.45 mm throughout.

## Diagram Dimensions and Modeling Limits

Semi-diameters are inferred from Figure 3, checked at 600 dpi, rather than published clear apertures. Front radii are about 14.2 mm for L1, 13.4 mm for L2 and 13.0 mm for L3. The G2 doublet is approximately 13.3/12.8 mm, and G3 about 12.5/12.4 mm.

Figure-sized candidates for L4 and G4 violate the model's cross-gap clearance constraints at S8A–S9 and S19–S20. Their existing conservative radii remain: L4 10.5/10.0 mm and G4 11.5 mm (S19 11.4 mm). These are unresolved figure-versus-clear-aperture differences; they are not claimed to reproduce the full drawn rims. No source radii or air gaps were altered to force a silhouette match.

This example contains no cover-glass or filter surfaces. The source rear air gap of 37.45 mm is retained unchanged. Aperture controls start at the patent f/2.88; the modeled iris and intermediate focus states do not establish a production diaphragm schedule.
