# NIKON IX-NIKKOR 20-60mm f/3.5-5.6 — audit

## Source

JP H08-334694 A, _Zoom lens_, by Haruo Sato for Nikon Corporation. Filed 5 June 1995 and published 17 December 1996. Example 1 is described in ¶¶0049–0053. Table 1 gives the prescription. Figure 1 shows the three zoom positions, and Figures 2–4 identify the apertures and half-fields for the aberration plots. The US family member is US 5,793,536 A. [Japanese publication](https://patents.google.com/patent/JPH08334694A/en)

## Transcription

The wide focal length is normalized to 1. At 20 mm per patent unit, all 19 radii or planes, axial separations and ten d-line index/Abbe pairs agree with Table 1. The 0.030 mm resin layer ends at aspherical surface 3A. Its printed κ = 0.1343 gives K = −0.8657. C₂ is zero. The coefficients of orders 4, 6, 8 and 10 are divided by the length scale to the power of their order minus one.

Surface 8 is the aperture stop. Its 0.486 mm spacing to surface 9 stays fixed. Surface 19 is a flat air-to-air flare stop with independent axial motion. At wide angle its plane passes through the vertex of surface 18. Table 1 contains no cover glass or finite-focus row.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

- **Clear radii.** Neither Figure 1 nor Nikon’s drawing dimensions the apertures. The radii are read from Nikon’s published cross-section (Nikon Tale 75), scaled to the prescription at about 15.5 pixels per millimetre: 264, 204, 200, 139, 128 and 105 pixels for surfaces 1, 5, 6–7, 9–12, 13–15 and 17–18. Surfaces 2/3A, 4 and 16 are reduced from those readings so that adjacent surfaces stay apart; the full drawn openings would give negative resin thickness at 13.35 mm (−0.203 mm) and excessive intrusion across the air gaps at 3A/4 and 15/16. For comparison, patent Figure 1 reads 17.75, 13.35, 9.1, 8.7, 8.15 and 7.0 mm at those surfaces. Line widths introduce about 0.2–0.4 mm of radial uncertainty.
- **Iris and flare stop.** The derived iris radii are 7.003, 7.309 and 7.832 mm at the printed apertures. The flare stop opening spans about 58–59 pixels in the same drawing, giving a clear radius of approximately 7.35 mm.
- **Zoom between the printed positions.** At 23 intermediate focal lengths the G1–G2 spacing is calculated from the power of the two rigid groups. D18 and the f-number vary linearly with focal length between the neighboring printed positions. D19 places the paraxial image at the film. Calculated spacings carry four decimal places, leaving at most 0.00005 mm of defocus at those positions. Between calculated positions the largest defocus is 0.0188 mm. The 0.02 mm limit is exceeded only near the printed middle and telephoto settings (approximately 33.982–34.018 mm and above 56.450 mm). The greatest offset is 0.0500 mm at telephoto.
- **Materials.** Hikari J-LAF3 (L12), J-LAFH3 (L13), J-BK7A (L23a) and J-LASF010 (L23b), and Ohara S-PHM53 (L21/L22) and S-NSL3 (L24b), provide catalog dispersion curves. Each differs from its printed pair by at most 0.000001 in nd and 0.133 in νd. The L11 substrate, its resin and L24a retain only their published nd and νd.

The axial bundle and the chief ray to the APS corner clear the rims throughout the tabulated zoom range. At 20–23.34 mm the lower marginal ray at that corner could not be located. The pupil calculation gives approximately 40.5%, 60.8% and 63.9% relative illumination at the three printed positions. Production corner illumination and the physical rim diameters remain unmeasured.

## Product Identification

Nikon’s cross-section and Example 1 agree on the nine lenses in seven components, the front hybrid asphere, both rear cemented pairs and the locations of the iris and flare stop. Sato identifies G1 as the close-focusing group and describes the flare stop’s independent movement. For thirteen axial landmarks on the maker’s drawing, Example 1 differs by 0.24 pixel RMS and Example 2 by 1.71 pixels. Example 3 has a different iris position inside G2. These details support Example 1 as the closest published design. [Designer’s account and production cross-section](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/information/story/0075/) · [Patent Figures 1, 5 and 9](https://patents.google.com/patent/JPH08334694A/en)

Nikon gives the Pronea IX lens a 20–60 mm range, f/3.5–5.6 maximum aperture, 0.35 m minimum focus, f/22 minimum aperture and APS film coverage. The original and revised barrels share the optical design. [Nikon brochure](https://www.submin.com/aps/manuals/nikon/pdf/pronea_s.pdf) · [Nikon Tale 75](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/information/story/0075/)

## Corrections

### 2026-11-25

The published spacings set the film position even where the paraxial back focus differs. The patent gives 56.54 mm at telephoto on the wide-end scale and Figure 3 gives f/4.45 at the middle setting. Nikon’s 60 mm marking and the 0.35 m production minimum describe the retail lens. All refracting surfaces and the original three zoom positions follow Table 1.

## Remaining Questions

Nikon’s drawing shows the flare-stop opening at about 6.5 mm radius, smaller than the 7.35 mm read from patent Figure 1 and used in the model. The production rim and flare-stop diameters, intermediate cam travel, factory glass identities, near-focus spacings and maximum reproduction ratio are unconfirmed. The source of the small telephoto focal-length and image-plane differences in Table 1 is unresolved.
