The front hybrid spans surfaces 1–3A. Its resin layer is 0.030 mm thick on axis. The aspherical rear face has κ = 0.1343, giving K = −0.8657, and C₂ = 0. The four coefficients of orders 4 through 10 each scale by 20 raised to one minus the order of the term. The two glass doublets are L23 (13–15, net negative) and L24 (16–18, net positive).

Surface 8 is the aperture stop. It travels with G2, 0.486 mm ahead of surface 9. Surface 19 is a flat flare stop in air. At the wide end it lies at the vertex of surface 18, then moves independently during zooming. Table 1 gives the film spacing after this stop and lists neither a cover plate nor a finite-focus position.

At each of the 23 intermediate zoom positions, the powers of the two rigid groups determine D7. D18 and the f-number vary linearly with focal length between the adjacent printed positions. D19 puts paraxial infinity focus on the film. Rounding the gaps to four decimal places leaves at most 0.00005 mm of defocus at these positions. Interpolation between calculated positions gives at most 0.0188 mm. The film lies more than 0.02 mm beyond paraxial focus only near the printed middle and telephoto settings, at about 33.982–34.018 mm and above 56.450 mm. The greatest offset is 0.0500 mm at telephoto. `focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity.

## Product Identification

The front hybrid asphere, both rear doublets, the iris just in front of G2 and the independent flare stop all appear in both Nikon’s drawing and Example 1. Across thirteen axial landmarks, Example 1 differs from Nikon’s drawing by 0.24 pixel RMS, compared with 1.71 pixels for Example 2. Example 3 places the iris inside G2. The evidence strongly favors Example 1. Sato identifies the 1995 filing but gives no example number or factory dimensions. [Nikon’s design account and cross-section](https://imaging.nikon.com/imaging/information/story/0075/) · [Patent, Figures 1, 5 and 9](https://patents.google.com/patent/JPH08334694A/en)

Sato designed the black 20–60 mm f/3.5–5.6 released for the Pronea 600i in September 1996. The silver 1998 Pronea S version used the same optics with an improved coating and revised barrel. Both have seven iris blades. The marked zoom range is 3×, against 2.827× in Table 1. Nikon gives a diagonal field of 82° to 32°10′ and a minimum aperture of f/22. G1 moves toward the subject for close focus at all focal lengths. Nikon specifies 0.35 m from the film plane and gives no separate macro setting or maximum reproduction ratio in the brochure. ## Corrections, 2026-11-25

- The original f/8, ±3° view covered only the central field. The full model reaches the APS corner at the apertures in Figures 2–4. Figure 3 gives f/4.45 at 34 mm, replacing the estimated f/4.63.
- The rear air space is split at surface 19. D18 and D19 locate the moving flare stop and preserve the printed film plane. Its 7.35 mm opening radius allows it to intercept off-axis light independently of the iris.
- The first rim estimates followed the production drawing, with openings of 12.5 mm at surface 4 and 6.7 mm at surface 16 to preserve air clearance. At the 13.35 mm radius read from the patent, the resin thickness would be −0.203 mm. The 13 mm resin radius leaves a positive edge thickness.
- The 20 mm scale preserves the printed 2.827× range and gives a nominal 56.54 mm at telephoto. The three printed infinity positions keep their exact gaps, including the small focal-length and film-plane discrepancies. The former 62 intermediate estimates are replaced by 23 calculated positions that keep the film close to paraxial focus. The patent supplies no near-focus spacings from which to calculate G1 travel.
- The model identifies the Pronea IX-mount lens for APS film, with nine lenses in seven groups, one hybrid asphere and seven blades. The catalog gives Nikon’s 20–60 mm, f/3.5–5.6 and 0.35 m specifications. The aperture scale uses f/3.5, 4, 5.6, 8, 11, 16 and 22, from the published wide-end maximum to the stated minimum.   - Zoom runs from 20 mm at the left to 56.54 mm at the right. Across all 26 positions, D7 falls from 31.545 to 2.2964 mm, while D18 rises from 0 to 11.9856 mm and D19 from 31.7724 to 53.967 mm, each without reversal. It then moves out to 103.985 mm at telephoto, as the arrows of Figure 1 show. Nikon’s account confirms the focus description: G1 moves toward the subject to reach 0.35 m.

## Remaining Questions

- The reason for the small difference between the printed and calculated telephoto focal lengths and for the film-plane offsets at the three printed positions is unknown.
- The two drawings give different flare-stop openings. The production clear diameters, including that of the flare stop, and the corner illumination remain unmeasured.
- Both drawings show a flat shoulder behind the front hybrid. Its square outline remains approximate. Extending the resin alone would leave a gap between it and the glass substrate beyond their 13 mm bonded rim.
- Production zoom-cam travel, near-focus spacings and maximum reproduction ratio remain undocumented in the cited sources.
- The original glass melts, suppliers, resin formulation and blade curvature remain unconfirmed.
- The glasses behind the L11 substrate (nd 1.796681, νd 45.37) and L24a (nd 1.772789, νd 49.45) are unidentified. Each pair recurs in another example of the patent, but neither matches a catalog glass exactly.
