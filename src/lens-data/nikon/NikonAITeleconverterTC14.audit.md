# NIKON AI TELECONVERTER TC-14 — Audit Log

## Source

US 4,206,971, *Rear Conversion Lens*, names Yoshinari Hamanishi and Soichi Nakamura as inventors and Nippon Kogaku K.K. as assignee. Japanese priority was 29 July 1977. The US application was filed on 27 July 1978 and granted on 10 June 1980. The First Embodiment table spans columns 6–7 (PDF pp. 7–8) and appears again in claim 4 (p. 9). Figure 3 shows the converter on its 200 mm f/2 master lens. Figures 5A–5C give the combined aberrations. [US 4,206,971](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4206971) · [Master lens patent, Example 2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4176913A/en)

Nikon’s dealer catalog gives the introduction date, dimensions, construction, compatible lenses and aperture coupling of the original AI TC-14. Its optical section is reproduced by MIR. Nikon’s 1984 catalog lists the later TC-14A, TC-14B and TC-14C separately. [Nikon dealer catalog](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00594/00594.pdf#page=107) · [Nikon optical section](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/teleconverter/TC14.gif) · [1984 Nikon catalog](https://www.base2photo.com/pdf/Nikkor_Lenses_Sales_Brochure_1984/Nikon_Nikkor_Lenses_Sales_Brochure_1984.pdf)

## Transcription Check

All ten signed radii, five center thicknesses, four internal air spaces and five index/Abbe pairs agree with the patent page images and claim 4 at the printed millimetre scale. All five elements are spherical singlets. The nearly flat rear face of L3 has a finite radius of −5196.816 mm. The 4.575 mm space after surface 6 separates the two functional groups. The 0.200 mm space after surface 8 separates the final two singlets. The converter has no iris or rear plate. All 18 surfaces of the separately tabulated master agree with the infinity prescription of the AI Nikkor 200 mm f/2 IF-ED. [Converter patent](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4206971) · [Master lens patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4176913A/en)

- **Master spacing and image plane.** The printed d₀ of −48.968 mm places the bare master’s image 48.968 mm behind the first converter vertex. The master’s 63.5967 mm paraxial back focus therefore leaves a 14.6287 mm gap between the lenses. The printed distance of 71.001 mm from the first converter vertex to the film, less the 30.883 mm track through the converter, gives 40.118 mm of air behind surface 10.
- **Iris and focus.** The iris is unmarked in Figure 3. The model places it 12 mm behind master surface 16 in a 22 mm air space, with a calculated f/2 radius of 19.2104 mm. All converter spacings are fixed. Its patent gives only the infinity setting. Example 2 of the master patent supplies the near setting, with its negative middle group 17 mm farther toward the film. The combination then focuses at 2.35 m from the image plane with −0.147× magnification. Nikon gives 2.5 m for the production host and specifies that its closest distance is retained with the TC-14. Hikari J-LASF017 (1.79500 / 45.31) lies within the site's spectral-proxy tolerance of the second pair. No catalog or vendor glass lies within it of the first. Sumita lists 1.51009 / 63.4 for BK1, the pair printed for L5. [Sumita glass table, BK1](https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee347/SumitaAll.PDF)

## Product Identification

Nikon introduced the AI TC-14 in fall 1978. Its five-element, five-group optical section fits the ten vertex positions of Embodiment 1 to about 1.1 pixels RMS across 178 pixels at a common scale. The model reproduces the air gap in the rear pair. Embodiment 2 cements those elements. The construction and dimensions strongly identify Embodiment 1 with the production converter. [Converter patent, Figures 3–4](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4206971) · [Nikon optical section](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/teleconverter/TC14.gif) · [Nikon dealer catalog](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00594/00594.pdf#page=107)

Nikon specifies a diameter of 64.5 mm, an overall length of 33.5 mm and an extension of 22 mm. The patent dimensions give 22.033 mm between mounting flanges. At the Nikon F register of 46.5 mm the first vertex projects 2.47 mm ahead of the host-side flange, putting it 24.50 mm from the camera-side flange. MIR gives 24.5 mm as the extension without specifying its forward measuring point. [Nikon dealer catalog](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00594/00594.pdf#page=107) · [Nikon flange dimensions](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00765/00765.pdf) · [MIR specifications](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/teleconverter/index3.htm)

The dealer sheet lists AI-coupled Nikkor primes from 300 to 1200 mm and the 180 mm f/2.8. It excludes zooms and reflex lenses. The original 180 mm has five elements in four groups. The later five-group ED prescription overlaps the converter by 7.71 mm at infinity. The dealer list omits the 200 mm f/2, whose optical prescription appears in the patent. The following combinations retain the host iris radius and reach the 21.65 mm corner at infinity. ## Corrections, 2026-11-25

- The full-aperture model uses the 200 mm master’s f/2 iris, giving the f/2.8 combination in Figure 5. The field extends to the 24 × 36 mm frame corner, where the chief ray reaches 21.65 mm at 4.39°.
- The former clear radii of 12.7 mm on L1–L3 and 14.0 mm on L4–L5 gave the converter uniform rims within each functional group. The individual glass edges in Figure 3 give the five radii above. With the same host rims and iris, this raises calculated corner illumination from 39.4% to 59.6%.
- The prescription keeps the printed scale and film position, including the 0.63 µm difference from paraxial focus. The host provides both focus positions. No converter motion or intermediate spacing is required.
- Nikon’s drawing identifies the original 1.4× AI converter for Nikon F and 35 mm film. Its five spherical, air-spaced elements account for the five construction groups. The forward three and rear two are the power groups named in the patent. Nikon specifies f/2–32 aperture coupling. [Nikon dealer catalog](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00594/00594.pdf#page=107) · [Nikon optical section](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/teleconverter/TC14.gif)
- L5 uses Sumita BK1 for dispersion. Its tabulated index and Abbe number match to the printed precision. Evaluating the catalog dispersion curve gives catalog-minus-patent differences of +0.00000023 in nd and −0.05 in νd. L1–L4 retain their printed values without a glass designation. [Sumita glass table](https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee347/SumitaAll.PDF)

At infinity the 200 mm master and converter give 280.0071 mm at f/2.8001, 1.40003× magnification, 40.1186 mm paraxial back focus and a 19.2104 mm host iris radius.

## Remaining Questions

- Nikon gives no dimensions for the production clear apertures or internal baffles, and the patent leaves the master’s iris position unspecified. With the same five converter rims, corner illumination at infinity and f/2.8 is 59.6% with the catalog master’s rims and 52.6% with master rims measured from Figure 3.
- The patent leaves the reference wavelength unspecified. The suppliers and melts of all five production elements remain unknown. Figures 5A–5C show no color curves, so nothing printed tests the dispersion modeled for L1–L4.
- MIR and Nikon give different figures for extension. The first-vertex distance accounts numerically for MIR’s 24.5 mm, but its measurement endpoints remain undocumented.
