# NIKON AI TELECONVERTER TC-14 — audit

## Source

US 4,206,971, *Rear Conversion Lens*, by Yoshinari Hamanishi and Soichi Nakamura for Nippon Kogaku K.K. Japanese priority was 29 July 1977, the US filing was 27 July 1978, and the patent was granted on 10 June 1980. The prescription table spans columns 6–7 and is headed “First Embodiment (FIG. 3).” Claim 4 repeats its values. Figures 5A–5C give the aberrations with the 200 mm f/2 master. [Patent scan](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4206971)

## Transcription

All ten radii, five center thicknesses, four internal air spaces and five index/Abbe pairs agree with the printed table, in millimetres at its original scale. The 4.575 mm space after surface 6 separates the forward and rearward groups. The 0.200 mm space after surface 8 separates the final pair. Surface 6 has the finite radius −5196.816 mm. All five elements are spherical singlets. The patent gives the 18-surface master separately. Its infinity prescription matches the AI Nikkor 200mm f/2 IF-ED.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

- **Clear radii.** In Figure 3 the first and last converter vertices span 224 pixels at 300 dpi, corresponding to the tabulated 30.883 mm optical track. Measuring the visible glass edges from the axis gives 15.2, 15.0, 15.7, 16.0 and 16.8 mm for L1–L5. Both faces of each singlet use the same reading. The line width gives an uncertainty of about 0.4 mm. Nikon’s separate drawing gives approximately 15 mm at the front and 16.4 mm at the rear.
- **Master spacing and image plane.** The printed d₀ = −48.968 mm puts the bare master’s image 48.968 mm behind the first converter vertex. Subtraction from the master’s 63.5967 mm paraxial back focus gives a physical separation of 14.6287 mm. Subtracting the converter track from the printed 71.001 mm gives the 40.118 mm air gap behind surface 10.
- **Iris and focus.** Figure 3 leaves the iris unmarked. The calculation uses the 200 mm host’s estimated iris position, 12 mm behind its surface 16 in a 22 mm air space. Its modeled f/2 stop radius is 19.2104 mm. The converter has fixed spacings and relies on the host for all focusing. The converter patent gives no finite conjugate.
- **Glass.** The patent supplies n and ν without a wavelength or a glass name. Helium d is assumed. The two pairs 1.59507 / 35.6 (L1, L3) and 1.79668 / 45.4 (L2, L4) have no exact catalog match. L5 has the same listed index and Abbe number as Sumita BK1, 1.51009 / 63.4. Its catalog curve represents the dispersion of L5. The production glass supplier is unknown. [Sumita optical glass catalogs](https://www.sumita-opt.co.jp/en/download/)

## Product Identification

Nikon dates the original AI TC-14 to fall 1978 and specifies 1.4× and five elements in five groups. Its specifications, as reproduced by MIR, give 64.5 mm diameter, 33.5 mm overall length and 24.5 mm extension from the lens flange. The patent geometry lengthens the host-to-film distance by 22.033 mm, the converter’s flange-to-flange length, and places the first vertex 2.47 mm ahead of the front flange. The two add to 24.50 mm. Its ten surface vertices match the positions in Nikon’s optical section to about 1.1 pixels RMS over a span of about 178 pixels. The air space at L4–L5 distinguishes Embodiment 1 from the cemented rear pair in Embodiment 2. This identifies the five-element 1978 design with high confidence. [Nikon dealer catalog](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00594/00594.pdf#page=107) · [Nikon construction drawing](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/teleconverter/TC14.gif) · [TC-14 specifications reproduced by MIR](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/nikon/nikkoresources/teleconverter/index3.htm)

Nikon specifies AI coupling from f/2 to f/32 and lists 300–1200 mm Nikkor primes and the 180mm f/2.8. The patent master provides separate evidence for the 200mm f/2. The following combinations retain their host’s iris radius and reach the 21.65 mm full-frame corner at infinity. [Nikon TC-14 specifications](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00594/00594.pdf#page=107)

| Host | Focal length with TC-14 | Effective aperture | Minimum axial separation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| AI 200mm f/2 IF-ED | 280.007 mm | f/2.80 | 14.63 mm |
| 300mm f/4.5 ED with AI coupling | 420.009 mm | f/6.30 | 21.09 mm |

The 135mm f/2.8 lies outside the TC-14 sheet’s stated range. Nikon also excludes zooms, including the 360–1200mm, even though that combination reaches the corners in the model. The 400mm f/8 Dodotto and the 800mm f/8 ED lose corner coverage with these rims, reaching 77.6% and 63.5% of the corner height, respectively (100% and 70.0% without the converter). The ED 180mm f/2.8 prescription overlaps the front of the converter by 7.71 mm at infinity.

## Corrections

### 2026-11-25

The prescription table is reproduced as printed. The final air gap follows from the printed film position, and the small difference from paraxial focus is retained. The optical rims are measurements from Figure 3.

## Remaining Questions

The production clear apertures, internal baffles and exact iris position of the 200 mm master are undocumented here. The modeled corner illumination at f/2.8 is 59.6% with the catalog master’s rims, or 52.6% with the master’s rims measured from Figure 3. Nikon’s drawing cannot establish which value is closer to the manufactured lens. The glass suppliers and the reference wavelength used in the patent remain unconfirmed.
