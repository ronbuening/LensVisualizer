## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2018/0196240 A1\
**Application Number:** 15/916,186\
**Filed:** March 8, 2018\
**Published:** July 12, 2018\
**Earliest family priority:** August 2, 2013\
**Inventors:** Makoto Fujimoto; Takeshi Umeda\
**Applicant:** Nikon Corporation\
**Title:** *Zoom Lens, Optical Apparatus, and Method for Manufacturing the Zoom Lens*\
**Embodiment analyzed:** Eleventh Example / Example 11 of the third embodiment, Fig. 30A/B and Table 11

The implemented prescription transcribes Example 11 of US 2018/0196240 A1. The patent describes this example as a
four-group negative-positive-negative-positive zoom, with the first three groups moving during zooming and the fourth
held fixed. Focusing is performed by translating the third group toward the image side. These points are stated directly
in ¶¶0386-0395 and are reflected in Table 11's three zoom positions and its corresponding infinity and close-focus
spacing rows. [1]

The identification with the production NIKON 1 NIKKOR VR 10-30mm f/3.5-5.6 PD-ZOOM is a strong research correlation,
not a manufacturer-confirmed patent attribution. The correlation rests on several convergent points:

1. The patent example has nine elements in seven air-separated groups; Nikon specifies the production lens as nine
   elements in seven groups. [1][2]
2. Example 11 has four glass-molded aspherical elements, while Nikon specifies four aspherical elements. The patent also
   contains one 1.498/82.6 very-low-dispersion coordinate, which is the natural candidate for the production lens's one
   marketed ED element, although the patent never labels that element "ED" and does not identify its supplier. [1][2]
3. The source design states 10.2 mm and 29.4 mm endpoint focal lengths. Nikon markets the production lens as 10-30 mm.
   The final paraxial model computes 10.1916 mm and 29.3665 mm at those endpoints. [1]
4. Table 11 gives 76.2° and 30.4° full fields at the endpoints, close to Nikon's published 77° to 29°40′ field range for
   the CX-format product. [1][2]
5. Table 11 publishes close-focus states at D = 200 mm at wide, middle, and tele positions. Nikon specifies a 0.2 m
   minimum focus distance throughout the zoom range and explicitly defines that product distance from the imaging plane.
   [1][3]
6. Fig. 30A/B and Nikon's official construction drawing show the same principal sequence: a three-element front group,
   two compact cemented components arranged around the stop, a single negative focusing element, and a final positive
   meniscus. [1][4]
7. The family priority begins before Nikon's March 13, 2014 product announcement; Nikon planned April 2014 availability.
   [1][3]

One material mismatch remains and is not normalized away. Table 11 gives FNO = 3.6 at the wide end and 6.4 at the tele
end, whereas Nikon markets the lens as f/3.5-5.6. The LensVisualizer model therefore keeps the production aperture range
as marketing information and uses the modeled design values generated by the fixed inferred stop: approximately f/3.596,
f/5.046, and f/6.407 at the three Table 11 zoom positions. This mismatch is one reason the correlation should not be
stated as proof that Example 11 is the exact production prescription. [1][2]

## Optical Architecture

Example 11 is a four-functional-group zoom with the power sequence G1− / G2+ / G3− / G4+. The final model contains nine
physical glass elements and seven air-separated groups. G1 contains L11-L13; G2 contains the cemented L21+L22 component,
the aperture stop, and the cemented L23+L24 component; G3 is L31; and G4 is L41. The verified first-order group focal
lengths are -14.4310 mm, +13.5588 mm, -23.4786 mm, and +34.9841 mm respectively, reproducing the Table 11 values of
-14.43, +13.57, -23.49, and +35.00 mm within the precision expected from the patent's rounded radii and indices. [1]

The zoom motion is not a simple monotonic translation of every moving group. With the image plane used as the reference,
G2 moves 15.59 mm objectward from wide to tele and G3 moves 5.95 mm objectward, while G4 remains fixed. G1 first moves
3.82 mm imageward between the wide and middle states, then reverses and moves 3.04 mm objectward between the middle and
tele states. The intermediate 20.0 mm control point is therefore structurally necessary; an endpoint-only interpolation
would erase the front-group reversal.

The 5.95 mm G3 wide-to-tele zoom travel reproduces the patent's `m3` value. The design deliberately reuses this mobile third group
for focusing: after G3 has moved toward the object during zooming, it moves back toward the image side for close focus.
The third-embodiment text explicitly connects this shared zoom/focus stroke to conditional expression (3-3). [1]

The second group is internally compact. Its front cemented component L21+L22 has a verified net focal length of
+20.8732 mm, and its rear cemented component L23+L24 has a verified net focal length of +25.9810 mm. These are cemented
component powers, distinct from the standalone powers of the individual elements and from the +13.5588 mm first-order
focal length of the complete isolated G2 functional group. That group focal length is not an in-situ power allocation.

The algebraic Petzval sum from the final prescription is +0.00223043 mm⁻¹, calculated surface by surface as
`phi/(n*nPrime)`. Its reciprocal is approximately 448.34 mm. This is a first-order Petzval curvature quantity, not a
traced best-focus field surface and not a claim about production sensor-plane curvature.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L11 — Negative meniscus

**nd = 1.618, νd = 63.3. Glass: 618633/618634 phosphate-crown class (supplier unresolved). f = -17.338 mm.**

L11 is the first element of G1 and is a negative meniscus with its convex surface facing the object, matching the patent's
¶0388 description. Its standalone first-order power is negative. In the implemented model its two surfaces are spherical.
The patent does not assign a unique aberration-correction task to L11, so its role is best stated structurally: it is one
of the two negative components that establish the negative front-group power.

### L12 — Negative meniscus, two aspherical surfaces

**nd = 1.623, νd = 58.2. Glass: 623582/623583 molded dense-crown class (supplier unresolved). f = -24.882 mm.**

L12 is the second negative meniscus in G1. Both surfaces, 3A and 4A, are glass-molded aspheres according to ¶0388. [1]
The element is negative in standalone first-order power. Its inclusion between L11 and the high-index positive L13 allows
the front group to remain net negative while distributing the refractive work over three elements rather than a single
strong component. The patent does not isolate how much spherical aberration, coma, or field curvature is corrected by
L12 alone; those effects cannot be assigned from the power sign and asphere presence by themselves.

### L13 — Positive meniscus

**nd = 2.001, νd = 25.5. Glass: 001255 high-index flint/lanthanum-flint class (supplier unresolved). f = +29.540 mm.**

L13 is the positive rear element of G1. Its high refractive index and positive standalone power counter the negative
powers of L11 and L12, leaving the full front group at approximately -14.431 mm. Its low Abbe number is part of the
published source coordinate, but the patent does not provide line indices or a named melt, so the analysis does not infer
a specific secondary-spectrum function from the coordinate alone.

### L21 — Biconvex positive, object-side asphere

**nd = 1.619, νd = 63.9. Glass: 619639 molded phosphate-crown class (supplier unresolved). f = +10.909 mm.**

L21 begins G2 and is cemented to L22. The patent calls it a double-convex positive lens and specifies the object-side
surface as glass-molded aspherical. [1] Its standalone focal length is +10.909 mm, but the physically meaningful power of
the cemented L21+L22 component is weaker: the verified component focal length is +20.873 mm. That distinction prevents the
positive power of L21 from being mistaken for the power of the cemented unit as a whole.

### L22 — Negative meniscus in cemented component D1

**nd = 1.603, νd = 38.0. Glass: 603380 F5/S-TIM5-class flint (supplier unresolved). f = -21.243 mm.**

L22 is cemented directly to L21 at source surface 8. The implemented cemented interface correctly carries the downstream
L22 medium and element identity. Its standalone power is negative, while the L21+L22 pair remains net positive. The
combination of unlike powers and dispersions is consistent with the usual logic of a cemented correction pair, but the
patent does not publish a decomposition of the chromatic residual by element, so no element-specific correction claim is
made here.

### L23 — Negative meniscus in cemented component D2

**nd = 1.583, νd = 46.5. Glass: 583464–583466 barium-flint class (supplier unresolved). f = -19.502 mm.**

L23 lies immediately behind the aperture stop and is cemented to L24. It is a negative meniscus with its convex surface
facing the object, as stated in ¶0389. [1] Its standalone focal length is -19.502 mm. As with D1, the standalone value is
not the component value: the complete L23+L24 cemented pair has a verified focal length of +25.981 mm.

### L24 — Biconvex positive, very-low-dispersion coordinate

**nd = 1.498, νd = 82.6. Glass: 498826 very-low-dispersion / ED-class coordinate (supplier unresolved). f = +11.078 mm.**

L24 is the strong positive member of the rear G2 cemented pair. Its 1.498/82.6 d-line coordinate is the only very-high-Abbe
coordinate in Example 11. The glass audit found a very close authoritative catalog candidate in HIKARI J-FKH1, and Nikon
markets the production lens as containing one ED element. These are convergent clues that L24 corresponds to the product's
ED-class element, but they do not establish that Nikon used HIKARI glass or that J-FKH1 is the exact melt. [2][5]

The data file therefore retains a class-level label and does not copy candidate-catalog `nC`, `nF`, `ng`, or `dPgF` values
onto L24. Without a defensible exact glass identity, those spectral quantities would imply more certainty than the patent
supports.

### L31 — Negative focusing meniscus, two aspherical surfaces

**nd = 1.619, νd = 63.9. Glass: 619639 molded phosphate-crown class (supplier unresolved). f = -23.479 mm.**

L31 is the complete G3 group, so its standalone focal length and G3 focal length are the same to calculation precision.
Both surfaces, 14A and 15A, are glass-molded aspheres according to ¶0390. [1] G3 has two distinct mechanical functions in
the selected example: it moves toward the object during zooming and toward the image during close focusing. The verified
wide-to-tele zoom travel is 5.95 mm, and the published tele close-focus travel is 3.19 mm.

This element is the clearest place where first-order power and mechanism can be linked directly to patent text. The
third-embodiment conditions explicitly define both G3 power and the ratio of its focus stroke to its zoom stroke. They do
not, however, assign a measured aberration contribution to L31 by itself.

### L41 — Positive rear meniscus, two aspherical surfaces

**nd = 1.517, νd = 63.9. Glass: BK7-family crown class near 517641/517642 (supplier unresolved). f = +34.984 mm.**

L41 is the fixed G4 group and the final glass element. It is a positive meniscus with its convex surface facing the image,
matching ¶0391. [1] Both surfaces, 16A and 17A, are glass-molded aspheres. The third-embodiment discussion specifically
states that a positive rear meniscus of this orientation is used in connection with field-curvature correction and image-
plane flatness, and that giving G4 an aspherical surface further supports flatness. Those are patent design claims, not an
independent allocation of measured aberration terms to L41. [1, ¶¶0173, 0188-0190]

The glass palette spans low- and high-dispersion materials, but the source does not provide the spectral line data needed
to quantify anomalous partial dispersion for the implemented elements. No element in the final data carries `nC`, `nF`,
`ng`, or `dPgF`. L24 now carries **inferred APD** from its compatible J-FKH1 curve (catalog deviation approximately +0.0337 from the engine normal line); the other elements remain unflagged. The lens therefore should not be described as apochromatic or as using

The L24 coordinate deserves separate treatment because it is unusually low in dispersion and aligns with Nikon's marketed
single ED element. That correlation supports the ED-class interpretation at the product level, but it remains a material-

## Focus Mechanism

Focus status is **PUBLISHED**. Table 11 directly gives both infinity and D = 200 close-focus spacings at the 10.2, 20.0,
and 29.4 mm zoom positions, and ¶0394 states that G3 moves toward the image side for close focus. No internal focus motion
has been reconstructed beyond those published states. [1]

| Zoom state | D13 infinity (mm) | D13 close (mm) | D15 infinity (mm) | D15 close (mm) | G3 imageward travel (mm) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| W, 10.2 mm | 1.57 | 1.98 | 5.36 | 4.95 | 0.41 |
| M, 20.0 mm | 6.56 | 8.15 | 8.74 | 7.16 | 1.59 |
| T, 29.4 mm | 11.21 | 14.40 | 11.31 | 8.12 | 3.19 |

At wide and tele, the increase in D13 and decrease in D15 are exactly equal to the precision printed in Table 11. At the
middle position the pair differs by 0.01 mm, consistent with table rounding. BF remains 13.30 mm in every published state,
so the close-focus state is realized by internal G3 motion rather than a rear image-plane shift.

The patent does not explicitly define the reference plane for `D`. The finite-conjugate verification therefore uses the
image-plane interpretation supported by Nikon's production specification that 0.2 m minimum focus is measured from the
imaging plane. Solving from the rounded prescription gives total object-to-image distances of approximately 211.8 mm,
199.1 mm, and 197.8 mm at W/M/T. All remain inside the source-precision sensitivity envelope used for the numerical comparison; the
wide residual is large enough that `D = 200` should not be treated as an exactly reproduced metric from the rounded table.

The computed close-focus lateral magnification reaches approximately -0.206 at the tele endpoint. Its magnitude is
consistent with Nikon's marketed approximately 0.2× maximum reproduction, but the agreement is corroborative rather than
proof of exact production geometry. [2]

## Aspherical Surfaces

Example 11 has seven aspherical surfaces on four elements: 3A and 4A on L12, 7A on L21, 14A and 15A on L31, and 16A and
17A on L41. The patent identifies all four of those elements as glass-molded aspherical lenses. [1, ¶¶0388-0391]

The patent writes the sag equation as

`x = (h^2/r) / [1 + sqrt(1 - K_patent*(h/r)^2)] + A4*h^4 + A6*h^6 + A8*h^8 + A10*h^10`.

LensVisualizer uses the conventional denominator with `1 + K_model` inside the square root. The exact mapping is therefore
`K_model = K_patent - 1`. Table 11 gives `K_patent = 1` on all seven aspheres, so every implemented surface has
`K_model = 0`. No linear scaling is applied, so the published A4, A6, A8, and A10 coefficients are copied without a scale
transformation. A12 and A14 are zero in the data because the patent publishes no nonzero terms beyond A10.

| 4A | L12 | 0 | -5.554e-4 | 8.416e-6 | -3.144e-8 | -7.595e-10 |
| 7A | L21 | 0 | -6.517e-5 | -1.259e-6 | 3.629e-8 | 8.838e-11 |

## Chromatic Correction Strategy

The source prescription combines several moderate-dispersion crowns and flints with one very-low-dispersion L24
coordinate. The two cemented pairs in G2 put unlike powers and dispersions in direct contact, and the production lens is
marketed with one ED element. Those facts are consistent with a compact zoom in which chromatic correction is distributed
through the positive second group rather than delegated to a separate rear ED group. [2]

That interpretation must remain limited. Example 11 publishes only d-line indices and Abbe numbers, not per-element
C/F/g-line indices, partial-dispersion deviation, or named glasses. The current model can therefore verify first-order
power and the stored Abbe coordinates, but it cannot independently quantify secondary spectrum or establish apochromatic
behavior from authoritative spectral data.

## Conditional Expressions

The third embodiment defines four conditions that constrain G3 travel, G4 shape, the relationship between focus and zoom
stroke, and G3 power. Recalculation from the implemented prescription satisfies all four ranges and agrees with the
Table 11 values to their published precision. [1, ¶¶0166-0194]

| Condition | Patent range | Implemented value | Table 11 rounded value | Meaning in the patent |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| (3-1) `m3/fw` | 0.50 < value < 0.80 | 0.583333 | 0.58 | G3 zoom travel relative to wide focal length |
| (3-2) `(r42+r41)/(r42-r41)` | -5.00 < value < -1.30 | -2.681266 | -2.68 | Rear-meniscus shape factor |
| (3-3) `fst/m3` | 0.45 < value < 1.00 | 0.536134 | 0.54 | Tele focus stroke relative to G3 zoom travel |
| (3-4) `(-f3)/fw` | 1.50 < value < 4.00 | 2.301821 | 2.30 | Magnitude of G3 power relative to wide focal length |

For (3-4), the implemented G3 focal length of -23.47857 mm gives 2.301821. Using the independently printed Table 11
group value `f3 = -23.49 mm` instead gives 2.302941; both round to the patent's 2.30. This distinction keeps the
implemented calculation separate from the rounded source-table control. The patent associates the four ranges with
compactness and control of several aberration families. Those statements are patent design rationale; satisfying the
conditions does not independently isolate the aberration contribution of any one condition.

## Image Stabilization

Nikon specifies the production lens as using VCM lens-shift VR. [2] The broader patent family also concerns vibration-
reduction architectures, but the selected Example 11 text at ¶¶0386-0395 publishes only the axial zoom and G3 focusing
motion. Fig. 30 labels G3 as the focusing group and does not provide a quantitative decentered stabilization state for the
selected example. [1]

Accordingly, the data file contains no invented lateral VR displacement, no stabilization `var`, and no quantitative VR
performance claim. The presence of production VR is part of the product-correlation evidence, while the implemented
prescription remains the centered Example 11 optical system.

## Verification Summary

The implemented `.data.ts` prescription was recomputed directly from its parsed values rather than from a separate
intended copy. Two independently coded paraxial implementations agree to numerical precision. The resulting infinity-state first-order
values are:

| State | Computed EFL (mm) | Table 11 f (mm) | Computed BFD (mm) | Table 11 BF (mm) | Modeled f-number |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| W | 10.1916 | 10.20 | 13.2689 | 13.30 | 3.5963 |
| M | 19.9810 | 20.00 | 13.3601 | 13.30 | 5.0463 |
| T | 29.3665 | 29.40 | 13.2044 | 13.30 | 6.4065 |

The corresponding first-surface-to-image-plane track divided by EFL is 5.887, 2.811, and 2.016 at W/M/T, so none of
the three states meets the project telephoto criterion `TL/EFL < 1`. BFD/EFL is 1.302 at W but 0.669 and 0.450 at M/T;
therefore the `BFD > EFL` retrofocus criterion is met only at the wide state, not as a zoom-wide architectural label.

The f-numbers above are not an independent measurement of the physical diaphragm. Table 11 does not publish a stop
radius. The modeled stop semi-diameter of 3.25533 mm was calibrated from the wide and tele source f-numbers; the two
endpoint calibrations differed by only 0.00663 mm, permitting one fixed modeled stop to be used consistently across the
three zoom positions.

The patent likewise publishes no surface semi-diameters. The modeled apertures pass the portable edge-thickness,
actual-rim-slope, conic-domain, shared-band gap-intrusion, and exact meridional containment checks. The tightest modeled
shared-gap margin against the current 0.90×gap criterion is 0.1064 mm, and the largest modeled rim-slope angle is 43.454°.
These are checks of the modeled geometry, not production barrel-clearance measurements. Real LensVisualizer render-trim
diagnostics remain an integration-stage check.

Two source discrepancies remain visible:

- Table 11 prints W `TL = 63.0 mm`, while the actual wide-state surface spacings sum to 59.995 mm. The 59.995 mm model
  reproduces the source focal length and back focus within source-precision-aware tolerances; inserting the missing
  3.005 mm into the wide spacing does not. The final data therefore retains the surface table and records the 63.0 mm
  value as an apparent source-table error rather than silently altering a gap. The tele spacing sum, 59.215 mm, agrees
  with the printed 59.2 mm control value. [1, Table 11]
- Fig. 31, nominally the Example 11 aberration figure, carries labels FNO = 3.61 / Y = 7.59 at wide and FNO = 5.77 /
  Y = 8.32 at tele. Those labels duplicate Fig. 33 for Example 12 and conflict with Table 11. The prescription therefore
  uses Table 11 as the Example 11 system-value authority; Fig. 31 is retained as a documented source inconsistency rather
  than used to substitute another example. [1, Figs. 31 and 33; Tables 11 and 12]

No uniform scale is applied. No sensor cover glass, filter, dummy plane, or flare-cutter plane was removed from Example 11,
because none is present in its active Table 11 sequential prescription. The final rear spacing remains the published
13.30 mm BF value rather than an air-equivalent conversion of an omitted plate.
