# Tokina AT-X 70-200mm f/4 PRO FX VCM-S

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP 5857306 B2  
**Application Number:** JP 2012-018033  
**Filed:** 2012-01-31  
**Published:** 2016-02-10, B2 grant publication; underlying JP 2013-156502 A published 2013-08-15  
**Registered:** 2015-12-25  
**Inventors:** Katsuya Hirano (平野 勝也), Eiichi Yasuda (安田 詠一), in the source’s order  
**Applicant:** Kenko Tokina Co., Ltd.  
**Title:** ズームレンズ (Zoom lens)  
**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 1, Figure 2; Table 1 on PDF/printed page 9 and Tables 2–3 on page 10.

The match to the production lens is a strong construction-based correlation, not manufacturer confirmation of an identical factory prescription. It rests on:

1. Nineteen glass elements in fourteen air-separated components, arranged 4+5+2+8 across four functional groups. Example 1 alone has the two-element G3; Examples 2–4 have twenty elements in fifteen components.
2. The official construction diagram’s front singlets, two G2 doublets, G3 doublet and G4 stabilization doublet agree with Example 1.
3. The SD (FK01) positions highlighted in that diagram correspond to L3, L4 and L12. The patent prints νd = 81.5 at L3/L4 and 81.6 at L12; these values do not identify a supplier.
4. The printed 72/105/194 mm stations and the patent’s approximately f/4.1 design are close to the marketed 70–200 mm f/4 product. Neither range is substituted for the other. The official release was 2014-05-30, following a 2012 application and 2013 first publication.
5. A chief ray traced to the 21.63 mm corner of the 24×36 mm frame gives a full field angle of 34.45° at the 72 mm station and 12.42° at the 194 mm station, equal to Tokina’s published angle of view of 34.45°–12.42°.
6. As a calculation only (no close-focus state is modelled), advancing G1Y by 13.53 mm within the 15.25 mm air space ahead of it focuses the 194 mm station at a 1.0 m object-to-image distance with a paraxial magnification of 1:3.57, the marketed maximum reproduction ratio.

The official product is a Nikon F full-frame lens with 19/14 construction, internal focusing, VCM stabilization and nine diaphragm blades. Its 1 m minimum focus, 1:3.57 maximum reproduction ratio, 67 mm filter and 167.5 mm external length are manufacturer specifications, not outputs of the infinity-only model. No teleconverter compatibility is asserted.

## Optical Architecture

This is a positive–negative–positive–positive four-group zoom. G1 includes the fixed L1–L2 subgroup G1X and the positive L3–L4 focus subgroup G1Y. G2 is the negative variator, G3 the positive compensator, and G4 a positive rear assembly containing the negative stabilization doublet. Five cemented doublets account for the difference between nineteen elements and fourteen components. All surfaces are spherical.

Every radius, thickness, index and zoom gap is used as printed. Table 1 prints no image-side spacing after s34. The paraxial back focus of the printed prescription is 54.656 / 54.658 / 54.678 mm at the three zoom stations (0.022 mm spread), so the model uses one fixed 54.66 mm from s34 to the image plane; Figure 2, which is to scale, draws about 54.7 mm. The 54.66 mm is derived, not a published image distance. No scaling is applied and no cover plate or cement layer is added.

Paraxial values at the d-line, calculated from the printed prescription, are:

The group focal lengths are G1 +95.332859 mm, G2 -27.851178 mm, G3 +85.530001 mm, G4 +89.963751 mm. The paraxial Petzval sum is +0.001653134891 mm⁻¹; it is not a prediction of sagittal or tangential field curvature. Neither BFD > EFL nor total optical track / EFL < 1 holds at these stations, so “telephoto” describes only the focal range, not the optical construction.

During wide-to-tele zoom, G2 moves imageward. G3 moves imageward to the middle station and partly returns toward the object at the tele station, consistent with the curved compensator trajectory in ¶0021. The printed gap sums are 42.32/42.32/42.31 mm; the 0.01 mm tele difference is kept as printed rather than forced to constant length.

Only the three printed stations are modelled, and the zoom slider steps between them. The patent does not print the cam curve, and linear interpolation of the gaps between stations does not hold focus: 58% of the way from the 105 mm to the 194 mm station the EFL is 157.568648 mm and the paraxial back focus is 60.959650 mm, putting the fixed image plane 6.299650 mm ahead of focus.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

All focal lengths below are thick-lens values in air from each element’s printed index, radii and center thickness; they are not cemented-component powers. Glass names are the catalog glasses whose nd and νd match the printed values (“catalog equivalent”); no supplier or melt identity is established.

### L1 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: S-TIH6 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = -199.099692 mm.

L1 is the negative member of the fixed front subgroup G1X. Its rear curvature, together with L2’s front curvature, supplies the patent’s Cs2/Cs3 condition. The patent links this configuration and a high L1 index to limiting negative spherical aberration during focusing (¶0016–0020).

### L2 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.60300, νd = 65.4. Glass: S-PHM53 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +257.655626 mm.

L2 completes the fixed G1X subgroup. The 2.01 mm air space from L1 is as printed, not a missing cement interface. Its positive power nearly balances L1: G1X as a whole is weakly negative, f = -872.34 mm.

### L3 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.5. Glass: S-FPL51 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +141.082142 mm.

L3 is the first positive member of G1Y, the two-element internal-focus subgroup identified in ¶0016. The official product diagram highlights this position as SD glass (FK01); the patent prints only nd and νd, which do not identify a supplier.

### L4 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.5. Glass: S-FPL51 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +224.570401 mm.

L4 is the second positive member of G1Y and the second SD (FK01) position in the maker’s diagram. A 0.25 mm air gap separates it from L3, so the pair is not cemented. Both elements move together for focusing; Example 1 prints no focus travel.

### L5 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.8. Glass: S-TIH53 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +88.325245 mm.

L5 begins G2 and is cemented to L6 at s10. Its positive power sits inside a negative component: the cemented L5–L6 doublet has f = -145.93 mm.

### L6 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Glass: L-BSL7 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = -55.074034 mm.

L6 is the low-index negative partner of the L5–L6 doublet. The printed 1.51633/64.1 pair matches the L-BSL7 class; the label keeps the L- prefix of that catalog entry rather than S-BSL7.

### L7 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.80400, νd = 46.6. Glass: S-LAH65V (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = -53.181297 mm.

L7 starts G2’s second cemented doublet and is cemented to positive L8 at s13. Paragraph 0022 identifies two cemented interfaces and positive members L5/L8 as a preferred means of limiting wide-end field curvature.

### L8 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.8. Glass: S-TIH53 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +65.887337 mm.

L8 completes that second doublet. The rear s14 surface borders the narrow air space before L9. The modelled clear radius there is set by the tele axial beam and by the need to keep air between the two faces; it is an inferred aperture, not a published dimension.

### L9 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.83481, νd = 42.7. Glass: S-LAH55 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = -42.489423 mm.

L9 is the final negative singlet of G2. Together with the preceding doublets it gives the variator its negative net power. The s14–s15 air gap is the tightest in the model; no curvature or center spacing is altered to enlarge its clearance.

### L10 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.65160, νd = 58.6. Glass: S-LAL7 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +35.336906 mm.

L10 is the positive member of G3, cemented to L11 at s18. This two-element G3 is a distinguishing feature of Example 1; the other examples add a separate positive singlet. G3 supplies the compensating zoom motion described in ¶0021.

### L11 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.80100, νd = 35.0. Glass: S-LAM66 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = -60.450359 mm.

L11 is the negative partner of the positive G3 component. The cemented L10–L11 doublet, which is the whole of G3, has f = +85.53 mm. No air space or cement layer is inserted at s18.

### L12 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6. Glass: FCD1 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +100.853470 mm.

L12 is the positive front singlet of G4X, immediately behind the stop. It is the third high-Abbe element, printed with νd = 81.6 rather than L3/L4’s 81.5; the difference is kept as printed even though the manufacturer diagram labels all three highlighted positions FK01.

### L13 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.83400, νd = 37.2. Glass: S-LAH60V (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = -70.551649 mm.

L13 is the negative first member of the G4X doublet, joined to positive L14 at s24. Its high-index medium and the low-index crown behind it create a strong cemented refracting interface. Its secondary-spectrum contribution is not evaluated here.

### L14 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.2. Glass: S-FSL5 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +46.270541 mm.

L14 completes the G4X doublet. Its printed νd = 70.2 matches the S-FSL5 class. The catalog dispersion curve stands in for the unpublished one and is not evidence that the production lens uses that glass.

### L15 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.8. Glass: S-TIH53 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +37.670839 mm.

L15 is the positive first member of the G4Y stabilization doublet. The patent identifies a positive-negative cemented pair that shifts transversely (¶0024–0028). It stays centred in the model.

### L16 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.76200, νd = 40.1. Glass: S-LAM55 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = -21.176292 mm.

L16 gives the G4Y doublet its negative net power (f = -48.91 mm). That focal length enters the patent’s stabilization-group ratio, whose printed value differs slightly from the calculated one (see Conditional Expressions).

### L17 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.65844, νd = 50.9. Glass: S-BSM25 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +60.205493 mm.

L17 is the first positive singlet of the final G4Z relay. The patent describes G4Z only as a positive subgroup (¶0024) and assigns no specific correction to this element.

### L18 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.8. Glass: S-TIH53 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = -60.840345 mm.

L18 is G4Z’s negative singlet. A 0.20 mm air gap separates it from L19.

### L19 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.70154, νd = 41.2. Glass: S-BAH27 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified). f = +91.487900 mm.

L19 is the final positive singlet. Its rear vertex s34 is the reference for the derived 54.66 mm back focus. The patent prints no image distance, and the manufacturer’s barrel length is not used to supply one.

## Focus Mechanism and Scope

Focus is not modelled. The patent identifies G1Y as the two-positive-element internal-focus group (¶0016–0020), and the maker describes internal focusing to 1 m. Example 1 includes aberration plots at a 1 m object-to-image distance (Figures 6–8) but prints no focus travel or close-focus spacings, so both focus positions of the model repeat the infinity spacings and the marketed 1.0 m minimum focus is a specification only. The model predicts no focus breathing or reproduction ratio; the 1:3.57 above is a separate paraxial calculation.

## Spherical Surfaces and Aperture Model

Example 1 contains no aspherical surface. The stop is s20, 1.50 mm before s21. Its diameter and the clear apertures are not printed. The approximately f/4.1 of ¶0007/¶0015 is taken as the design aperture, and the iris radius is found by tracing the f/4.1 axial ray to the stop at each station: 13.320111690/13.320639345/13.330779339 mm. Agreement with f/4.1 is by construction, not evidence of the diaphragm size.

Inferred clear radii start from the proportions of Figure 2 and are constrained by positive edge thickness, rim slope, clearance across the air gaps and traced rays. Front radii scaled directly from the drawing would make L1 and L2 overlap under the printed gap, so smaller values are used.

The tightest air gap is s14–s15 (2.36 mm). At the 194 mm station the f/4.1 axial ray reaches 12.147862871/12.134446840 mm on these faces, and the inferred radii of 12.16/12.15 mm are the smallest 0.01 mm steps that leave at least 0.01 mm beyond it. At 12.15 mm the two facing surfaces take up 95.4827314473% of the gap and 0.106607538 mm of air remains at the rim, so the model lets facing surfaces use up to 95.49% of an air gap where its default is 90%. No printed curvature, spacing or aperture is adjusted to make the beam pass.

The full f/4.1 axial beam passes at all three stations, and chief rays reach the 21.633307653 mm half-diagonal of the 24×36 mm frame. Off-axis vignetting from the inferred rims remains: at the 194 mm station a ray at three quarters of the pupil radius is cut by the rims of G1 at 50–60% of the field. The model does not predict production MTF, measured illumination or behavior between the printed zoom stations.

## Conditional Expressions and Source Discrepancy

The stricter B2 claims on pages 1–2 require 1.25 ≤ Cs2/Cs3 ≤ 1.60, 5 ≤ |FG1X/FG1Y| ≤ 10.0, ndL1 ≥ 1.75 and 0.46 ≤ |FG4Y/FG4| ≤ 0.6. The description on pages 7–8 separately gives a 0.4 lower bound for the last ratio. The printed prescription satisfies both sets.

Calculated Cs2/Cs3 = 1.529528967, |FG1X/FG1Y| = 9.919329462 and ndL1 = 1.80518 round to Table 3’s 1.530, 9.919 and 1.805. However, |FG4Y/FG4| = 0.543707242 does not round to the printed 0.540, and the cause is unresolved; satisfying the inequality does not reproduce the printed value. No radius, index, group assignment or gap is altered to force agreement.

## Image Stabilization

Paragraphs 0024–0028 identify G4Y, L15–L16, as the negative cemented stabilization doublet. The patent shows transverse shifts of 0.23/0.34/0.62 mm at the three zoom stations (¶0036, Figures 9–11). These are the conditions of the patent’s aberration plots; the model keeps the doublet centred and predicts no decentred performance. The maker’s construction diagram labels the same doublet as the VCM module.
