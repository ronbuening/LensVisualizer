# KONICA HEXANON AR 28mm f/3.5

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 4,170,403, _Retro Focus Type Wide Angle Lens_\
**Inventors:** Toshiko Shimokura\
**Assignee:** Konishiroku Photo Industry Co., Ltd.\
**Filed:** November 2, 1977, with Japanese priority of November 9, 1976\
**Published:** October 9, 1979\
**Example details:** Example 3, column 5. Figure 5 shows the section and Figure 6 gives aberrations at infinity.

Example 3 strongly matches the late five-element, five-group Hexanon AR 28mm f/3.5 that closes to f/22. Konica's 1980 dealer sheet identifies it as catalog no. 703-191, with a 75° field, Color Dynamic Coating, a 55 mm filter thread and a weight of 175 g. Only Example 3 combines 75° with f/3.5. Example 1 is the 64°, f/2.8 design associated with the 35 mm Hexanon. Example 2 covers 75° at f/2.8. [Konica dealer sheet, p. 6](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/02831/02831.pdf#page=6) · [Patent, Examples 1–3](https://patents.justia.com/patent/4170403)

The dealer drawing also distinguishes the two 75° prescriptions. Measured as fractions of the first-to-last glass surface distance, the front air space and the thickness of the biconvex lens are 42.8% and 18.5% in the drawing. Example 3 gives 42.4% and 18.2%. In Example 2 the biconvex lens occupies 23.5%. The patent does not name the retail lens. [Konica section, p. 6](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/02831/02831.pdf#page=6) · [Patent, Figures 3 and 5](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/04/e4/41/0cfb6ccdc6aeed/US4170403.pdf)

The 1965 preset Hexanon had seven elements in six groups and stopped to f/22. Its automatic seven-element successor had seven groups and stopped to f/16. Andreas Buhl dates the rubber-grip, five-element f/22 version with a round AE lock button to about 1978 and the rectangular button to about 1980. He also documents a third-party f/16 Hexar with a similar five-element section and suggests that a five-element f/16 Hexanon may have been a relabeled Hexar. The patent attribution is specific to the late f/22 Hexanon. [Hexanon versions and sections](https://www.buhla.de/Foto/Konica/Objektive/e28_35.html) · [Hexar section](https://www.buhla.de/Foto/Konica/Objektive/e28_35Hexar.html)

## Optical Architecture

All five groups are air-spaced singlets. The dealer drawing and Figure 5 show the same sequence of shapes, including the two rear positive menisci with their concave faces toward the object. There are no cemented pairs or aspherical surfaces. Neither source identifies an extra-low-dispersion element or a separately moving focus group.

A scale factor of 28 converts the patent's lengths at unit focal length to millimetres. The prescription gives a 28.01 mm effective focal length and a 34.765 mm paraxial back focus. The film lies at the printed 34.748 mm, about 17 µm ahead of paraxial focus. The patent places the stop 2.436 mm behind surface 4. Its 4.850 mm radius follows from f/3.5. The glass radii of curvature are tabulated, while the clear apertures come from Figure 5. The facing surfaces of L3 and L4 meet if continued to the drawn outline, so each has a 5.0 mm clear radius. Flat lands outside the clear apertures complete the drawn edges. The rear face of L1 ends at 10.45 mm and steps out to the 13.3 mm edge. Figure 5 and the dealer drawing both give L3 and L4 one shared edge about 6 mm from the axis, modelled at 6.05 mm. The lands lie outside every traced beam and do not change the vignetting.

Figure 6 plots aberrations through an image height of 0.7551 at unit focal length. At 28 mm this is 21.1428 mm, or 42.2856 mm across, slightly smaller than the full-frame diagonal. At infinity a chief ray reaches the plotted height at a 37.7° half-angle and reaches the 35 mm frame corner at 38.3°. At the corner the glass rims transmit part of the wide-open pupil.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Each focal length is for the individual element in air. The glass names are Ohara catalog equivalents for the patent's d-line index and Abbe number.

### L1 — Negative meniscus, convex to the object

$n_d = 1.5891$, νd = 61.1, S-BAL35 equivalent, $f = -36.17$ mm. The strongly curved rear face diverges the incoming beam. Its separation from L2 gives the lens a back focal distance longer than its focal length.

### L2 — Biconvex positive

$n_d = 1.7200$, νd = 43.7, S-LAM52 equivalent, $f = +20.99$ mm. The strongest positive element gathers the beam ahead of the stop. Shimokura uses its center thickness to help correct coma and distortion while keeping the air space around the stop short.

### L3 — Biconcave negative

$n_d = 1.7847$, νd = 26.2, PBH23 equivalent, $f = -14.09$ mm. The most dispersive glass carries negative power behind the stop and spreads off-axis rays across the rear pair.

### L4 — Positive meniscus, concave to the object

$n_d = 1.8061$, νd = 40.9, S-LAH53 equivalent, $f = +28.37$ mm. Its concave front face helps offset barrel distortion from L1. The more strongly curved rear face gives L4 positive power.

### L5 — Positive meniscus, concave to the object

$n_d = 1.6230$, νd = 58.2, S-BSM15 equivalent, $f = +37.59$ mm. The last meniscus shares the distortion correction and bends the rays toward the film.

## Glass Identification and Selection

L3 has the lowest Abbe number of the five. Its negative power balances some of the chromatic power of the positive lenses. Claim 1 constrains the mean index of L4 and L5 to between 1.69 and 1.72. Example 3 gives 1.71455, allowing the designer to distribute positive power across the two rear menisci while retaining their object-facing concavities. All five catalog equivalents agree with the printed index within 0.00003 and with the Abbe number within 0.04. Their dispersion curves can describe the model even though the supplier and melts of the production glass are unknown. [Ohara optical glass catalogs](https://oharacorp.com/glass-catalog/)

## Focus Mechanism

Konica specifies a minimum subject distance of 0.30 m from the film plane. Pekka Buttler records 170° of manual focus-ring travel and 1:8.6 maximum magnification. His specimen has six straight diaphragm blades and ring settings of f/3.5, 5.6, 8, 11, 16 and 22, also visible in Konica's photograph. [Konica dealer sheet, p. 6](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/02831/02831.pdf#page=6) · [Buttler's five-element lens data sheet](https://japb.net/gear/gear-review-index/ds_konica-ar-28f35-5/)

The patent tabulates only infinity and gives no finite magnification. To reach the product's 0.30 m limit, the model advances all five elements and the stop together by 3.469 mm. This preserves their internal separations and the infinity film-plane offset. The calculated rear gap is 38.2168 mm and the paraxial reproduction ratio is about 1:8.1. The production lens's internal travel is undocumented.
