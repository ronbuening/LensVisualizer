## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP S54-55424 A (JPS5455424A)  
**Application Number:** S52-121372  
**Filed:** 1977-10-12  
**Published:** 1979-05-02  
**Inventor:** Toshiko Shimokura (下倉敏子)  
**Applicant:** Konishiroku Photo Industry Co., Ltd.  
**Title:** 望遠写真レンズ  
**Example details:** 第1実施例 (Example 1), printed pages 149–150, with its Seidel coefficient table and Fig. 2 aberration curves. Normalized to f = 1, F4, 2ω = 12°, T = 0.809, fB = 0.261; all lengths scaled by 200.

The prescription is Example 1 of JP S54-55424 A, scaled uniformly by 200 with no renormalization of the rounded radii. The patent names no production lens. The association with the KONICA HEXANON AR 200mm f/4 is an inference from converging evidence, and the model is a patent design associated with that lens, not a confirmed factory prescription.

1. **Specification.** The Konica/Berkey 1980 product sheet (cat. no. 703-193) lists 200 mm, f/4 to f/22, five elements in five groups and a 12° angle of view. Example 1 is F4, 2ω = 12°, five singlets in five groups. Fig. 2 plots the field to Y = 0.1082, which at this scale is 21.64 mm, the half diagonal of the 35 mm frame.
2. **Section.** The sheet's section agrees shape by shape: biconvex L1 with the stronger curve forward, biconcave L2, positive meniscus L3, the long air space, negative meniscus L4 concave to the object, and L5 with a nearly flat front face. Scaled to the same vertex length, its axial spacings fall within 0.5 mm of the prescription.
3. **Length.** The sheet's 121 mm length plus the 40.5 mm AR flange distance is 161.5 mm, close to the model's 161.7 mm from front vertex to image plane.
4. **Timing.** The application was filed in October 1977; a collector listing dates the lens from 1978 to 1981.
5. **Alternatives.** The other Konica 200 mm lenses differ in construction: the automatic Hexanon AR f/3.5 has five elements in four groups with a 67 mm filter, and the Hexar f/4 has four elements in four groups.

Examples 2 and 3 of the same patent share the layout and nearly all spacings (T = 0.804 and 0.797), and they fit the sheet's section equally well; the drawing cannot separate them. Example 1 is used because only it has the Seidel table and aberration curves. It is the best-documented pick, not a proven one.

The printed radii carry four decimals, and that rounding accounts for every first-order residual. At this scale they trace to EFL 200.178 mm and paraxial back focus 52.304 mm, against the printed f = 1 and fB = 0.261 (52.2 mm), and their Petzval sum is 0.06266 against the printed ΣP = 0.0637. The Seidel table gives five coefficients per surface to four decimals, enough to recover the unrounded radii: a fit of the ten radii to its 50 entries keeps every radius within about ±0.00005 of print and matches the table to its own rounding. The fitted radii return f = 1.0000, fB = 0.2611, T = 0.809 and ΣP = 0.0637. R7 (−0.14113 against the printed −0.1411) carries about two thirds of the Petzval difference. No single digit, sign or index change comes close to that fit. The model keeps the printed radii.

## Optical Architecture

Five separate spherical singlets form a positive-negative-positive front section and a negative-positive rear section (the patent's 前群 and 後群), and the long air space between them gives a telephoto ratio of 0.809. The patent's aim is an F4, 12° lens with a telephoto ratio near 0.8 in ordinary optical glass rather than fluorite. It keeps the Petzval sum large and positive by using low-index crown in the positive L1 and L3 and high-index glass in the negative L2 and L4. There are no cemented interfaces, aspherical surfaces or cover plates. Element roles below follow the patent's conditions where it states them; the rest is interpretation of the prescription.

The caption to the Seidel table says the coefficients are for an infinite object distance with the stop placed 0.15 behind lens surface 6. With f = 1 that is 30 mm at this scale, so the printed 67 mm space between surfaces 6 and 7 is split 30 + 37 mm. The table confirms the position: the rms difference between its entries and coefficients computed from the prescription has a sharp minimum at 0.150. Neither Fig. 1 nor the product sheet's section draws a stop. The stop radius is not printed; 13.562 mm is the opening that gives a paraxial entrance-pupil diameter of EFL/4. The real marginal ray through that opening has a working f-number of f/4.12.

Semi-diameters are not printed, so all of them are inferred. L1 is set to 27 mm, the 25 mm entrance-pupil radius plus 8%, and the other elements follow outline ratios measured from Fig. 1 (1, 0.845, 0.819, 0.503, 0.535). That gives 27, 22.815, 22.113, 13.581 and 14.445 mm, one value for both faces of each element. Fig. 1 draws the same ratios at a smaller size. Scaled from its vertices and arcs, its rims read about 25.2, 21.2, 20.5, 12.5 and 13.3 mm, about 8% under the model, and the three front rims sit on the F4 axial beam at those surfaces (25.0, 21.1 and 20.3 mm). Fig. 2 agrees: the coma curves end at about 54% of the pupil at Y = 0.1082 and 78% at Y = 0.0541, and both ends trace to a front rim near 25.2 mm, where the model's L1 passes 62% and 85%. The model keeps the larger rims, since the drawn set would take geometric transmission at 6° from 77% to 61.5%. The product sheet's section, scaled to the same vertex length, gives about 27.0, 23.1, 22.1, 12.9 and 14.0 mm, within 0.7 mm of the model. Geometric transmission falls to 95% at 3° and 77% at 6°, and the 6° half-field reaches 21.19 mm image height, 98% of the 35 mm half diagonal.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Glass: S-BSL7 (Ohara) catalog equivalent; production supplier not established. f = +116.0 mm.

The patent makes L1 strong (0.52 f < f1 < 0.62 f; Example 1 prints 0.580) and follows it with a wide air space, shortening the telephoto ratio at the very front of the lens. Below the lower limit L2 must grow too strong as well, and the higher-order aberrations rule out F4. The model's rims are anchored to this element's clear radius.

### L2 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.7552, νd = 27.5. Glass: S-TIH4 (Ohara) catalog equivalent; production supplier not established. f = −135.2 mm.

The strong concave front face (0.64 f < |R3| < 0.74 f) holds down the negative spherical aberration, positive coma and distortion that grow as the front section's positive power is raised. The Seidel table shows the balance: surface 3 contributes −9.77 of spherical aberration against +10.57 from L1. Its dispersion pairs with the two crowns for front-section color correction; the patent requires ν1 − ν2 and ν3 − ν2 above 30, and both are 36.6 here.

### L3 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Glass: S-BSL7 (Ohara) catalog equivalent, as L1. f = +170.8 mm.

The strong front radius (0.21 f < R5 < 0.26 f) helps shorten the telephoto ratio, and R6 strengthens with it to help correct the positive coma from the front of the lens. If R5 grows too strong, the patent says negative spherical aberration can no longer be corrected. L3 closes the front section before the long air space that holds the stop.

### L4 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.7725, νd = 49.6. Glass: S-LAH66 (Ohara) catalog equivalent; production supplier not established. f = −56.09 mm.

L4 opens the compact rear section. Its strongly curved front radius of −28.22 mm carries the largest single Petzval term, −3.09, and the patent's high-index requirement (n4 > 1.73) serves the large Petzval sum. That index limits how high νd can go, so the condition 17 < ν4 − ν5 < 27 balances rear-section color against it; the value here is 24.2.

### L5 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: S-TIH6 (Ohara) catalog equivalent; production supplier not established. f = +104.7 mm.

Condition f5 < f1 gives L5 more power than L1 (0.524 against 0.580 as printed) to hold down positive distortion; the patent says practical distortion correction fails without it. R9 is the printed 2.3145, 462.9 mm at this scale, so the front face is nearly flat. L5 follows L4 across a 1 mm air gap.

## Glass Identification and Selection

All five elements sit on exact catalog coordinates, and the data file's labels name Ohara equivalents: S-BSL7 (516641) for L1 and L3, S-TIH4 (755275) for L2, S-LAH66 (773496) for L4 and S-TIH6 (805254) for L5. These equivalents supply the dispersion curves; the patent prints only nd and νd, and Konica's actual glass supplier is not established.

The patent's point is the avoidance of fluorite, which it calls expensive and hard to work. Ordinary low-index crown at about nd = 1.51 in the positives and high index in the negatives (n2, n4 > 1.73) keep the Petzval sum large instead.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent prints the infinity state only and says nothing about focusing. The product sheet gives a 2.5 m minimum focus from the film plane, which the model carries as catalog data. Its geometry is static: no element moves, and no close-focus state is computed.

The image plane is the printed back focus: 0.261 at this scale is 52.2 mm, for 161.7 mm from front vertex to image plane. That plane lies 0.104 mm ahead of the rounded radii's paraxial focus at 52.304 mm. About 0.08 mm of the difference comes from radius rounding and about 0.03 mm from printing fB to three decimals, since the fitted radii focus at 52.23 mm. The plane stays at the printed value, and the MTF tab reports the axial best-focus shift as a diagnostic.
