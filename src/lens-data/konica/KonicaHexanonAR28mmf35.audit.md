# KONICA HEXANON AR 28mm f/3.5 — Audit Log

## Source

Toshiko Shimokura, _Retro Focus Type Wide Angle Lens_, US 4,170,403, assigned to Konishiroku Photo Industry Co., Ltd. The US application was filed November 2, 1977, with Japanese priority of November 9, 1976. The patent was granted October 9, 1979. Example 3 and its stop-position footnote are in column 5 (PDF p. 8). Figure 5 gives the section and Figure 6 the aberrations at infinity. [Patent](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/04/e4/41/0cfb6ccdc6aeed/US4170403.pdf)

Konica's 1980 dealer sheet gives a photograph, section and specifications for catalog no. 703-191. Andreas Buhl documents the different 28 mm barrels and optical designs. Pekka Buttler records aperture markings, blade count and magnification for a five-element f/22 specimen. [Dealer sheet, p. 6](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/02831/02831.pdf#page=6) · [Buhl's version history](https://www.buhla.de/Foto/Konica/Objektive/e28_35.html) · [Buttler's specimen](https://japb.net/gear/gear-review-index/ds_konica-ar-28f35-5/)

## Transcription Check

All ten signed radii, nine axial thicknesses and air spaces, and five d-line index and Abbe-number pairs agree with the printed page. Multiplying each length by 28 converts the unit-focal-length table to millimetres. The diaphragm is 0.087 focal lengths behind surface 4, dividing the 4.690 mm L2–L3 space into 2.436 mm before the stop and 2.254 mm after it. All five lenses are spherical singlets. Even L4 and L5 have a 0.098 mm axial air gap.

Example 3 meets the four limits of Claim 1. The mean index of L4 and L5 is 1.71455. In units of focal length, the L3 thickness is 0.0698, the L2–L3 air space is 0.1675 and the L2 thickness is 0.2094.

## Product Identification

Example 3 strongly matches the late five-element f/22 Hexanon AR 28 mm f/3.5. It is the only example with Konica's 75° field and f/3.5 aperture. Example 1 covers 64° at f/2.8. Example 2 covers 75° at f/2.8, but its biconvex second lens occupies 23.5% of the axial length between the outer vertices. Konica's dealer section gives 18.5%, close to Example 3's 18.2%. The front air space occupies 42.8% in the dealer section and 42.4% in Example 3. The lens shapes agree throughout. Relative to the front lens diameter, the second and fifth lens diameters are 0.59 and 0.53 in the dealer section, and 0.57 and 0.54 in Figure 5. [Dealer section, p. 6](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/02831/02831.pdf#page=6) · [Patent](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/04/e4/41/0cfb6ccdc6aeed/US4170403.pdf)

The 1965 preset f/22 Hexanon had seven elements in six groups. The later automatic seven-element model stopped to f/16. Buhl dates the five-element f/22 Hexanon to about 1978. The third-party five-element f/16 Hexar has a similar section, and Buhl suggests the five-element f/16 Hexanon may have been a relabeled Hexar. The patent names no retail model. [Hexanon versions](https://www.buhla.de/Foto/Konica/Objektive/e28_35.html) · [Hexar section](https://www.buhla.de/Foto/Konica/Objektive/e28_35Hexar.html)

## Corrections, 2026-11-25

- The working aperture was opened from f/8 to the printed f/3.5, and the field was extended from ±5° to the full plotted field. Figure 6 gives a maximum image height of 0.7551 at unit focal length. At the 28 mm scale this gives a 42.2856 mm diameter, recorded as 42.29 mm, inside the 35 mm frame diagonal.
- The clear radii follow Figure 5 in place of the narrower estimates. The two facing rims at surfaces 6 and 7 end at 5.00 mm so the spherical faces stay apart and pass the full axial beam.
- The 28× scale applies to the stop position and printed back focus as well as the lens surfaces. The infinity image plane retains the printed back focus despite the 0.0172 mm paraxial difference. No prescription entry required correction.
- The 0.30 m endpoint extends the infinity prescription to Konica's minimum distance from the film plane. It assumes unit focusing because the patent supplies no finite spacings. [Dealer sheet, p. 6](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/02831/02831.pdf#page=6)
- The product metadata identify the late 703-191 version, for the Konica AR mount and 35 mm full frame. Its 28 mm focal length, f/3.5 aperture and five separate elements agree with Konica's sheet. The six-blade iris has ring settings of f/3.5, 5.6, 8, 11, 16 and 22. Buttler describes the blades on his specimen as straight. [Dealer sheet and ring photograph](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/02831/02831.pdf#page=6) · [Buttler's specimen](https://japb.net/gear/gear-review-index/ds_konica-ar-28f35-5/)

2026-11-25 rim review:

- Surface 2 gains a flat land from its 10.45 mm clear radius to a 13.3 mm edge. Figure 5 ends the rear face of L1 at a step square to the axis, level with the front rim. The dealer drawing shows the same step as a bevel. The figure governs.
- Surfaces 5, 6 and 7 gain flat lands to 6.05 mm, the height of the rear rim of L4. Figure 5 draws L3 and L4 with one outer edge about 6.0 mm from the axis, reached through a chamfer at the front of L3. The dealer drawing reproduces the vertex spacing of Example 3 to within 0.2 mm and shows the same shared edge at about 6.2 mm. The clear radii stay at 5.25, 5.00 and 5.00 mm.
- No clear radius changed, and the lands lie outside every traced beam. Corner transmission wide open stays 65.7% at infinity and 70.5% at 0.30 m. The f-number and traced field are unchanged.

2026-11-26 diagram review:

- The page was compared with Figure 5 and the dealer drawing, and nothing changed. Rendered at 600 dpi, Figure 5 spans 1528 pixels from surface 1 to surface 10 for 32.2448 mm, and the drawn sags of surfaces 1, 2 and 5 give the same scale across the axis. Averaged over both halves, the figure gives 13.35 mm for the L1 edge, 10.41 mm where the rear face of L1 meets its step, 7.59 mm for L2, 5.29 mm where the front face of L3 meets its chamfer, 6.06 mm for the shared L3–L4 edge and 7.27 mm for L5, each within 1% of the model.
- The flat lands on surfaces 2, 5, 6 and 7 cost no light at any field, at infinity or at 0.30 m, and Figure 5 draws all four edges. They stay.
- Names L1–L5, element types, focal lengths, the captions G1–G5 for the patent's first to fifth groups and the stop 2.436 mm behind surface 4 agree with the patent. Neither the patent nor the dealer drawing marks an aspherical or special-dispersion element, and none is tagged. Focusing toward 0.30 m moves all five elements and the stop together toward the subject; the distance reads 1.20 m, 0.60 m and 0.40 m on the way and the rear gap grows smoothly from 34.75 mm to 38.22 mm.
- Figure 6 plots a second, dashed spherical-aberration curve that starts 0.058 mm short of the d-line focus on the axis and ends about 0.22 mm beyond it at f/3.5. With the present Ohara dispersion curves the g-line focus is −0.053 mm on the axis, −0.104 mm at half aperture, −0.092 mm at 0.7 of the aperture and +0.239 mm at full aperture, following the dashed curve within about 0.02 mm. The d-line curve agrees within 0.03 mm. Ohara LAH53 and Hoya BACD5 and BACD15, which also fit the printed values, would move the g-line focus by 0.001 mm, so the labels stay.

## Remaining Questions

- The production working diameters at the L3–L4 gap and the original glass suppliers and melts are undocumented.
- Figure 6 does not label its two spherical-aberration curves. The dashed curve is taken as the g line only because the calculated g-line focus follows it; the patent text does not say.
- Neither the patent nor the dealer sheet gives a close-focus section, mechanical extension or closest magnification. The model gives about 1:8.1 at 0.30 m. Buttler lists 1:8.6 for the production lens. [Five-element specimen](https://japb.net/gear/gear-review-index/ds_konica-ar-28f35-5/)
