## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [JP 2023-130072 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP2023130072A/en)
**Filed:** 7 March 2022
**Published:** 20 September 2023
**Inventors:** Yuki Shibata
**Applicant:** Cosina Co., Ltd.
**Example details:** Tables 1 to 3 (pp. 6–8) and Fig. 1 (p. 14); f = 36.08 mm, F/2.01, half field 21.28°, modeled at patent scale.

Example 1 is the closest numerical match to the MACRO APO-ULTRON 35mm F2 X, released in August 2022. Cosina's section agrees in its nine elements in six groups, arranged L1, L2, L3f/L3r, L4f/L4r, L5f/L5r, L6, and Cosina's page states that the front four groups, six elements, move for focus, as G1 does here. Example 2 has ten elements with a cemented triplet behind the stop, and Example 3 has a biconvex/biconcave rear pair and a 42.03 mm focal length. Fig. 3 plots to an image height of 14.20 mm, so the example covers APS-C, not full frame. The design values of 36.08 mm, F/2.01 and full field 42.56° stay separate from the retail 35 mm, F2 and 42.8°. The exact production identity is unproved, since Cosina publishes no prescription and its Z-mount D35mm F2 page lists the same 9/6 construction and focusing motion.

## Optical Architecture

Nine spherical elements form six groups with three cemented doublets. The moving group G1 (f1 = 33.99 mm) holds G1A, made of L1, L2 and cemented lens L3, ahead of the printed stop and G1B, cemented lens L4, behind it. The fixed group G2 (f2 = −214.63 mm) holds cemented lens L5 and negative meniscus L6. The element names are the patent's own: L3, L4 and L5 are its cemented lenses, and f and r mark their front and rear elements. The printed h2/h1 = 0.36 describes on-axis ray heights at the first and last surfaces, not clear apertures; real rays in the model give 0.362.

The patent does not print clear apertures, iris mechanics or partial-dispersion data.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.7292, νd = 54.67. Glass: TAC8 (HOYA) catalog equivalent. f = +53.919 mm in air.

The biconvex front singlet begins G1A and the moving positive group G1.

### L2 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.7292, νd = 54.67. Glass: TAC8 (HOYA) catalog equivalent. f = +39.356 mm in air.

The air-separated positive meniscus uses the same printed pair as L1, which does not prove the same production glass.

### L3f — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.7408, νd = 27.74. Glass: E-FD13 (HOYA) catalog equivalent. f = −14.832 mm in air.

The biconcave element begins cemented lens L3 and supplies its negative power.

### L3r — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.9229, νd = 20.88. Glass: E-FDS1 (HOYA) catalog equivalent. f = +42.573 mm in air.

The positive meniscus completes cemented lens L3 and G1A, with its rear surface concave toward the stop. Cosina's section marks this element as anomalous partial dispersion.

### L4f — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.6990, νd = 30.13. Glass: S-TIM35 (OHARA) catalog equivalent. f = −36.644 mm in air.

The negative meniscus begins cemented lens L4 and G1B, 3.94 mm behind the stop. Both of its radii are positive.

### L4r — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.7639, νd = 48.49. Glass: S-LAH96 (OHARA) catalog equivalent. f = +17.041 mm in air.

The biconvex partner completes G1B and the moving group G1. Its rear face is followed by ZD11, 1.62 mm at infinity, the gap that opens during focus.

### L5f — Positive Meniscus

nd = 2.0010, νd = 29.13. Glass: TAFD55 (HOYA) catalog equivalent. f = +13.874 mm in air.

The positive meniscus begins the fixed cemented lens L5. Both of its radii are negative.

### L5r — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.7380, νd = 32.33. Glass: S-NBH53V (OHARA) catalog equivalent. f = −19.779 mm in air.

The negative meniscus completes L5. Cosina's section marks this element as anomalous partial dispersion, although the catalog curve sits near the normal line.

### L6 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.8081, νd = 22.76. Glass: S-NPH1 (OHARA) catalog equivalent. f = −40.198 mm in air.

The last negative meniscus is the widest element in Fig. 1. Cosina's section marks it as anomalous partial dispersion. The 12.49 mm gap behind it is the same in both printed states.

The focal lengths above are thick-lens values in air from each element's printed radii, thickness and nd. They are not its net power inside a cemented system.

All values are d-line (587.56 nm, ¶0033). The labels are catalog equivalents that match the printed nd/νd pairs, not production identifications; the production supplier is unspecified. L3f's printed νd of 27.74 sits 0.02 below E-FD13's listed 27.76. Dispersion away from the d-line comes from these catalog curves, so the model makes no apochromatic or secondary-spectrum claim. APO is Cosina's product name; the printed pairs do not establish it.

Cosina's page counts three anomalous partial dispersion elements and its section marks the fourth, eighth and ninth, L3r, L5r and L6, so those tags are inferred positions. The catalog curves support L3r (E-FDS1, ΔPgF +0.0303) and L6 (S-NPH1, ΔPgF +0.0252). L5r's pair is near normal: S-NBH53V has ΔPgF +0.0006, about 0.0085 below ordinary flints of similar νd, so its mark does not follow from the printed pair alone.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent uses front focus (¶0005, ¶0015): G1, with L1 to L4r and the stop, moves toward the object while G2, L5f to L6, stays fixed. Table 3 prints two states. At infinity ZD11 is 1.62 mm. At magnification 0.50 the object sits 95.58 mm from the first surface and ZD11 is 17.62 mm, so G1 travels 16.00 mm, below the printed fM of 18.03 mm (condition 3). ZD16 is 12.49 mm in both states. Adding the 53.87 mm near track gives 161.94 mm object to image, derived from the printed intervals. A paraxial trace at the printed object distance gives magnification 0.4997 and a last gap of 12.506 mm, about the same 0.015 mm residual as at infinity.

The model publishes both printed states, with the close endpoint at 0.16194 m. It is not forced to the production specification of 0.163 m at 1:2. Positions between the two states are interpolated.

## Stop and Clear-Aperture Assumptions

The stop plane is printed 13.28 mm behind surface 1, with 2.57 mm before it and 3.94 mm after it. The patent prints no iris diameter, so the engine sizes the opening from F/2.01, a semi-diameter of 6.37 mm. Clear semi-diameters are also unprinted; they are estimated from Fig. 1 and from the rays each surface has to pass.

Inferred clear semi-diameters in millimetres are 1: 9.7, 2: 9.7, 3: 8.9, 4: 8.02, 5: 7.8, 6: 7.8, 7: 6.6, 9: 7.2, 10: 7.2, 11: 7.2, 12: 8.87, 13: 8.87, 14: 8.87, 15: 8.87, 16: 10.2. Surfaces 4, 7 and 15 end there, and flat lands carry them out to the element rims of 8.9, 7.8 and 10.2 mm.

Fig. 1 is drawn to true scale: its curves fit the printed radii in both directions. Its elements read 9.00 mm for L1, 8.26 mm for L2, 7.52 mm for L3, 6.46 mm for L4, 8.01 mm for L5 and 9.21 mm for L6, and it draws the three lands, with the curves ending at 7.8 mm on surface 4, 6.47 mm on surface 7 and 8.0 mm on surface 15. Fig. 2 repeats those outlines at the same size, with a ray 8.15 mm from the axis at surface 1 rather than the 8.98 mm of F/2.01. Cosina's section shows the same size order and the lands on L3r and L6.

The model's rims stand 4 to 12 % above Fig. 1 because the rays need the room. A ray entering at the full F/2.01 height of 8.976 mm reaches 8.44 mm at surface 3, 7.72 mm at surface 5, 6.59 mm at surface 7 and 6.54 mm at the stop. Pupil aberration separates that from the paraxial iris: the real axial beam through a 6.37 mm opening enters at 8.76 mm and reaches 8.25 mm at surface 3 and 7.52 mm at surface 5, the rims Fig. 1 draws there, so the figure's front rims follow that beam. At magnification 0.50 the full-height ray reaches 6.79 mm at surface 10, and the chief ray to the 14.20 mm image height of Fig. 3(b) crosses surface 14 at 8.44 mm, surface 15 at 8.48 mm and surface 16 at 9.64 mm, past the rims Fig. 1 draws for L5 and L6. The remaining margin keeps the off-axis bundles. Off-axis clipping is retained. Clearance of the iris opening does not prove blade or mount fit.

## First-Order Properties and Aberrations

The printed gaps sum to 50.36 mm while Table 1 prints TL 50.37 mm. The 0.01 mm difference is rounding: the table cells read cleanly, no single-cell correction explains it, and Example 3 misses by the same amount in the other direction. The model keeps the printed 12.49 mm last gap, which places the image plane 50.36 mm behind surface 1, with a 37.87 mm vertex track. The numbers come from this application, not from the later grant.

A paraxial trace gives EFL 36.085 mm and BFD 12.505 mm. The printed f is reproduced, and the authored last gap falls 0.015 mm short of the computed BFD. That residual is within the rounding of the printed table and stays in place. The groups trace to 33.99 mm and −214.67 mm against the printed 33.99 mm and −214.63 mm. Image height 14.20 mm in Fig. 3 corresponds to 21.28° in the model, matching the printed half field.

The model's d-line spherical aberration agrees with Fig. 3(a): −0.061 mm at the rim and +0.032 mm near 0.7 of the pupil, against about −0.05 mm and +0.03 mm. Sagittal field curvature is −0.099 mm at 10.65 mm and tangential −0.056 mm at 14.20 mm, against about −0.10 mm and −0.06 mm. Distortion at 14.20 mm is +1.04 % against about +1.0 %. At the near state the model gives −0.173 mm near 0.8 of the pupil and −0.111 mm at the rim, against about −0.17 mm and −0.13 mm in Fig. 3(b). With a finite object at the printed distance, the near field curves reach −0.180 mm sagittal and −0.366 mm tangential at 14.20 mm, and distortion +1.97 %, against about −0.18 mm, −0.37 mm and +1.95 %.

Fig. 3 plots the C, d and g lines; the patent does not plot F (¶0036). With the catalog curves the model's C and g lines follow the plots within the reading error of about 0.01 mm in both states. At 0.75 of the pupil at infinity C is +0.048 mm and g +0.094 mm against about +0.047 mm and +0.092 mm; on axis at the near state C is +0.066 mm and g +0.029 mm against about +0.066 mm and +0.035 mm. Replacing E-FDS1 at L3r with a normal-line glass of the same νd alone moves the g line about +0.04 mm at infinity and +0.07 mm at the near state, and E-FD13 at L3f moves it about −0.02 mm and −0.06 mm, so those two labels carry most of the fit. TAFD55 at L5f and S-NBH53V at L5r sit too close to the normal line for Fig. 3 to test them. The model's F line, with no printed plot to compare, stands at −0.015 mm on axis and +0.040 mm at 0.75 of the pupil at infinity.

## Modeled Diffraction and Coverage

Wide open at infinity, the model passes 100 % of the axial beam, about 88 % at 10.6° and about 40 % at 21.3°. At magnification 0.50 a finite object at the printed distance keeps about 51 % at the 14.20 mm image height. The Vignetting tab launches parallel beams at every focus position, which reads about 74 % at 14.2°. These fractions depend on the inferred rims and are model pupils, not measured illumination.
