## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** DE 444150 C<br>
**Filed / patented from:** 18 February 1925<br>
**Published:** 24 May 1927<br>
**Inventor:** Hans Deser<br>
**Applicant:** Voigtländer & Sohn Aktiengesellschaft<br>
**Title:** Photographisches Fernobjektiv<br>
**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 1

The selected prescription is the five-element example printed in the left column of
PDF page 2, with its corresponding Abb. 1. The patent identifies the applicant and
inventor on page 1. Its numerical example is nominally 100 mm at f/6.3. [1]

The VOIGTLÄNDER TELE-DYNAR 100mm f/6.3 designation is a patent-normalized identification;
an actual marketed 100 mm variant and the exact production correspondence are
not manufacturer-confirmed. The available evidence supports a family-level correlation:

1. The patent was assigned to Voigtländer and concerns a compact photographic tele-objective.
2. The manufacturer documents a Tele-Dynar family at f/6.3 for its folding cameras.
3. The Avus instructions describe two separable cells fitting the camera shutter,
   consistent with an assembly separated at the diaphragm. [2]

The manufacturer price list preserved by CNAM, dated approximately 1930, lists Tele-Dynar
variants of 14, 20, 25.5, 29 and 32 cm, rather than 10 cm. Its product descriptions support
the historical family but do not establish a particular glass prescription. [3]
A separate manufacturer catalogue, Nr. 1709.7.25, lists five sizes from 5½ to 12⅝ inches,
also without a 100 mm production variant. [6]
No specific lens mount, image format, release date or minimum focusing distance is assigned
to this normalized example. Patent publication date is not treated as a product launch date.

## Optical Architecture

Five glass elements form three air-separated groups: a positive front cemented doublet,
a negative middle singlet and a positive rear cemented doublet. All refracting surfaces
are spherical or plane; no dimensional scaling is applied. The front and rear group
focal lengths in air are +40.192 mm and
+57.309 mm. The middle singlet is
-24.299 mm. These group values retain the actual
cemented interfaces rather than adding independently calculated element powers. [1]

At the native source index, the computed EFL is 100.210 mm. Infinity BFD is
63.461 mm from the last vertex. The first-to-last-vertex span is
23.36 mm; adding BFD gives a first-vertex-to-image track of
86.821 mm. Its track/EFL ratio is 0.866391, establishing
telephoto geometry with the image-plane reference included. The rear principal plane
lies 13.389 mm before the first vertex, as the patent describes.
These are paraxial infinity quantities, not mechanical barrel dimensions.

The middle and rear groups together have net focal length
-48.896 mm. This explains why the final
positive doublet does not contradict a net negative rear functional section.
The separation from the front doublet to the middle singlet is 11.03 mm,
whereas the middle-to-rear air separation is 2.63 mm.
The diaphragm divides the larger interval into source dimensions 7.35 and 3.68 mm. [1]

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The source writes nD for sodium D, approximately 589.3 nm, and prints an unsubscripted ν.
The historical nd/vd application slots retain those source coordinates. They are not
silently converted to the helium d line at 587.6 nm. Consequently, the first lines below
use nD and ν(source), rather than falsely labeling the coordinates nd and νd.
All element focal lengths are thick-lens values in air, not individual in-situ contributions.

### L1 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.61430, ν(source) = 56.4. Glass: Unmatched (vintage crown; native sodium D coordinates). f = +21.769 mm.

The front biconvex crown is the strongly positive member of the front doublet.
Its negative-radius rear boundary is shared with L2. The combined doublet remains
positive, but its net power is much lower than the standalone L1 power because of L2.
A power sign alone does not establish a unique spherical-aberration or coma role.

### L2 — Plano-Concave

nD = 1.64620, ν(source) = 33.9. Glass: Unmatched (vintage flint; native sodium D coordinates). f = -44.692 mm.

The front flint is plano-concave as an isolated element. Its plane rear face is
source r3; infinity in that radius entry denotes a plane, not a missing value.
The cemented boundary directly changes the medium from L1 to L2, without a synthetic
cement layer. Its lower Abbe coordinate gives a dispersive contrast to the crown.

### L3 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.58350, ν(source) = 41.9. Glass: Unmatched (vintage light flint; native sodium D coordinates). f = -24.299 mm.

The central biconcave element is the air-spaced negative singlet targeted by the
patent's low-index condition. The text associates this choice with flatter surfaces
and lower manufacturing cost compared with the cited earlier arrangement. That is
the patent's explanation, not a measured cost or aberration comparison. [1, p.1–2]

### L4 — Negative Meniscus

nD = 1.61430, ν(source) = 56.4. Glass: Unmatched (vintage crown; native sodium D coordinates). f = -119.470 mm.

Both radii are positive, but the rear radius is smaller in magnitude. The element
is therefore a negative meniscus in air. Describing every element in the rear positive
group as positive would be incorrect. L4's contribution is coupled through the actual
cemented boundary to L5; its standalone focal length is not an in-situ group assignment.

### L5 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.64620, ν(source) = 33.9. Glass: Unmatched (vintage flint; native sodium D coordinates). f = +38.846 mm.

The final flint is biconvex and positive in isolation. Together with L4 it forms
the positive final cemented group. The crown/flint ordering is reversed relative to
the front doublet. The net negative middle-plus-rear section remains the useful
functional description for interpreting the system's shortened optical track.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The three distinct source pairs are retained without assigning a supplier or historical
melt identity. Modern HOYA BACD6 is a close coordinate candidate for the crown pair;
modern SCHOTT SF2 and HOYA E-FD2 are candidates for the more dispersive flint pair.
HOYA FL3, SUMITA LF3 and SCHOTT LF5 lie near the middle glass in the screened catalogs. These
are candidate classes, not material substitutions or manufacturer identifications. [4,5]

Fresh primary HOYA coefficients and SCHOTT data sheets support the comparison.
Additional primary OHARA BSM6, HIKARI J-SF2 and CDGM H-ZF1 rows were rechecked;
SUMITA LF3 remains a project-catalog transcription rather than a newly verified vendor row. [7–9]
The historical D reference and modern d reference differ. Even coordinate-compatible
modern glass cannot establish the dispersion curve of the original melt. The authored
glass fields are explicitly Unmatched, and no nC, nF, ng or ΔPgF values are borrowed.
No apochromatic, anomalous-dispersion or secondary-spectrum performance claim is made.

The source prose presents the middle glass as having lower dispersion than the rear
glass or glasses. Taken literally for both constituents of Example 1's rear doublet,
that statement is inconsistent with the numerical table. Under the conventional Abbe
interpretation, (nD−1)/ν is approximately 0.013926 for the middle glass, compared with
0.010892 for the rear crown and 0.019062 for the rear flint. Lower dispersion is therefore
supported relative to the rear flint, but not the rear crown. The tabulated prescription
is preserved; no replacement glass is introduced to force the prose into agreement. [1]

## Focus Mechanism

Only a static infinity-reference prescription is modeled. Neither numerical example
supplies finite-conjugate spacing states or a production MFD. The two-cell shutter
arrangement documented in the Avus manual does not specify a focus law. [1,2]

The focus disposition is NO_INTERNAL_RECONSTRUCTION. There are no variable gaps and no
invented internal motion. The required closeFocusM value of 1e9 m is a schema sentinel,
not a physical MFD. It does not establish finite-distance focus performance or justify
moving any group. No focus travel or close-focus magnification is claimed.

## Aperture, Field and Model Geometry

The stop location is supported by the b1/b2 dimensions in Abb. 1; its diameter is not
published. The inferred stop radius is 5.924122 mm, calibrated with
an exact on-axis ray to a geometric entrance-pupil diameter of
15.906344 mm at f/6.3. This agreement is a calibration,
not independent recovery of the historical physical diaphragm. A paraxial interpretation
of that same finite stop gives f/6.212026; the difference
records finite-aperture pupil aberration rather than a second published f-number.

All lens semi-diameters are modeled. The front doublet uses 9.3 mm, the middle singlet
and rear doublet both use 8.0 mm, following their nearly equal-height optical rims in Abb. 1. They retain positive edge material and satisfy the
actual spherical rim-slope and shared-band air-gap intrusion checks. These dimensions
were selected for the limited ray model, not measured from a production drawing.

Exact spherical intersections and vector Snell refraction were sampled for 75 rays:
fields 0°, 2.5° and 5°, pupil center plus half/full-radius rings, and twelve azimuths
on each ring. All sampled rays clear the authored apertures, with intended edge contact
at the diaphragm. The 5° half-field is a test scope, not a published coverage limit or
an assigned photographic format. Sampling does not prove clearance at every intermediate
field and pupil coordinate, nor does it replace the application's rendering diagnostics.

An independent Newton-intersection implementation subsequently checked 7,931 rays over
0–5° in 0.5° steps, eleven pupil radii and 72 azimuths per nonzero ring. The minimum
non-stop aperture margin was 0.232382 mm; the smallest modeled element thickness was
0.230706 mm. These remain finite-sampling results. Targeted execution of the real
pinned application constructor, validator and element-render diagnostics found no
validation errors and zero hidden material trim. This does not establish production
coverage or historical optical performance.

## Patent Conditions and First-Order Limits

The central glass index 1.5835 is below the stated 1.59 limit. The central negative
focal-length magnitude divided by system EFL is 0.242483, above
0.2; the rear positive group ratio is 0.571888, above 0.3. The index,
focal-ratio, spacing-order and principal-plane conditions are reproduced. The separate
literal dispersion wording discrepancy remains as described above. [1]

The surface-by-surface Petzval sum is 0.000376812 mm⁻¹, computed as
Σ[(n′−n)/(R n n′)] using the actual cemented media. A small Petzval sum alone does not
establish a flat usable image field: astigmatism, finite-field behavior and the missing
historical dispersion curve are separate questions. No MTF, distortion percentage or
production aberration specification is inferred from these first-order quantities.
