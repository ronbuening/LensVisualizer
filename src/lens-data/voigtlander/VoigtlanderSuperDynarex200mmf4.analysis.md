# VOIGTLÄNDER SUPER-DYNAREX 200mm f/4 (Bessamatic)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 3,388,956 A\
**Filed:** April 9, 1964 (Ser. No. 358,426)\
**Priority:** Germany, April 10, 1963 (V 23,924)\
**Granted:** June 18, 1968\
**Inventors:** Joachim Eggert, Ernst Tronnier, Fritz Ueberhagen\
**Assignee:** Voigtländer A.G., Braunschweig\
**Title:** *Photographic Telephoto Lenses of High Telephoto Power*\
**Example details:** Figure 2

The prescription is Example 1 of US 3,388,956. The patent table is normalized to `f = 1.00` at a relative aperture of
1:4. The data file scales it uniformly by 200. The patent gives five worked examples, all f/4 telephotos with a
"telephoto effect" of about 4.9. Examples 1 and 4 have five elements. Examples 2, 3 and 5 split the rear positive
element IV into two lenses and have six.

The production correlation is the Voigtländer Super-Dynarex 200mm f/4 for the Bessamatic and Ultramatic reflex cameras.
Voigtländer never published Example 1 as the factory prescription, so the identification rests on converging evidence:

1. Voigtländer's Bessamatic/Ultramatic brochure W 3539 lists the Super-Dynarex f/4 200mm as a five-element telephoto
   with a 12.5° angle of view. Collector references give 5 elements in 4 groups. Example 1 has the same count and
   grouping, and scaling it ×200 gives a 12.4° paraxial diagonal field on 24×36 mm.
2. The patent is assigned to Voigtländer. Its stated purpose (col. 2 lines 1–10, col. 3 lines 25–40) is a long telephoto
   that can still be fitted to cameras whose main shutter sits close to the film plane. That describes the
   Bessamatic, whose Synchro-Compur shutter is behind the lens mount. The patent says lenses of this type "can be built
   with focal lengths of 180 mm. to over 200 mm." The earlier f/4.5–4.7 type it improves on was limited to about
   150–165 mm.
3. The scaled model is 141.1 mm from first to last vertex, with a front clear diameter of about 70 mm. That fits the
   collector-reported 14 cm barrel length and 77 mm filter thread.
4. Figure 2 is labelled as Example 1. Example 1 is also the only one of the five examples that prints the diaphragm
   position (a3V/a3H).

Some evidence points the other way, or is missing. Collector references date the lens to 1962 or 1963, which is just
before the April 1963 German priority. Example 4 is an equally count-compatible alternative. The production lens may
therefore differ in detail from Example 1. The later Icarex-mount Super-Dynarex 200mm is not
treated as the same optical design.

## Optical Architecture

The design is a classic two-group telephoto with a long air space between the groups. The patent calls the front
group the tele-positive (A) and the rear group the tele-negative (B). The diagram labels the elements with the patent's
Roman numerals I–V and brackets groups A and B; this text calls the same elements L1–L5 in the same order:

- **Group A (L1–L3):** a cemented doublet of converging lens I (L1) and diverging lens II (L2), followed by a thin
  diverging air lens a2 and the second diverging meniscus III (L3). Computed focal length +174.1 mm.
- **Large air space a3:** 0.52203 f (104.4 mm), with the iris 80.58 mm behind L3 and 23.83 mm ahead of L4.
- **Group B (L4–L5):** positive meniscus IV (L4), concave to the image, and biconcave V (L5). Computed focal
  length −171.6 mm.

The patent defines "telephoto effect" as the ratio of focal length to the back intercept, f/s′ = 1/0.20324 = 4.9. The
total track from the first vertex to the image is 181.8 mm, or 0.91 f. The long air space is what lets this
modest overall shortening coexist with a short back focus and an exit pupil close to the rear of the lens. That layout
suits a shutter just in front of the film. The patent is explicit (col. 3 lines 35–50) that the camera, not the
optics, fixes the diaphragm and exit-pupil positions in such cameras. It treats the resulting distortion and
vignetting difficulty as the problem to be solved.

All surfaces are spherical. The data file has no filter, cover glass or other plate, because the patent lists none.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Biconvex Positive (front component of D1)

nd = 1.64250, νd = 58.09, LAK6-class lanthanum crown, f = +90.6 mm in air.

L1 carries most of the positive power: the patent gives φI = +2.2524Φ, almost the top of claim 1's 2.022–2.253Φ
range. Its strongly curved front face (R1 = +0.42156 f) supplies most of that power. The patent's summary condition
puts the front converging lens at 1.5–3 times the system power.

### L2 — Negative Meniscus (rear component of D1)

nd = 1.72830, νd = 28.66, FDS2 (Hoya) coordinate-equivalent dense flint, f = −254.9 mm in air.

L2 is diverging lens II, cemented to L1 at R1′ = R2 = −0.88214 f. Its rear face is nearly flat (R2′ = −18.00073 f).
The high-index, low-νd flint against L1's crown makes the doublet an achromat. The cemented interface also corrects
spherical aberration inside the strong front group.

### L3 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.65446, νd = 33.79, SF9 dense flint, f = −567.1 mm in air.

L3 is diverging lens III, the last element of group A. It is separated from L2 by a2 = 0.01183 f. The air lens between
R2′ and R3 is diverging. From the patent's surface powers, −φa2 = −(φ2′ + φ3) = 0.436Φ, the lower limit of the
0.436–1.0Φ range the patent gives for this air lens (col. 3 lines 19–24). Claim 3 narrows the range to 0.436–0.571Φ. According to the patent, this added
diverging air lens is what corrects intermediate-zone coma in the lateral field beyond the earlier Swiss design. L3's
rear face R3′ = −5.30649 f is one wall of the large air space.

### L4 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.75787, νd = 31.56, unmatched dense flint, f = +125.9 mm in air.

L4 is converging element IV, the front of group B. Its object-side surface R4 = +0.34202 f (2.2159Φ) faces back across
the large air space toward L3's rear face. The patent's central conditions concern this pair:

- the converging air lens between them has φ3′ + φ4 = 2.339Φ, inside the 3/2–10/2Φ range of the description and
  equal to the lower limit of claim 1;
- φIII + φIV = 1.2175Φ;
- |φIV/φIII| = 4.4451.

The patent credits this balance with letting the telephoto power rise to about 5 without losing lateral image
correction (col. 2 lines 56–68).

### L5 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.65016, νd = 39.31, BAFD10 (BaSF10-type) barium dense flint, f = −66.0 mm in air.

L5 is diverging element V, the strongest surface pair in the lens (φV = −3.0243Φ). It produces most of the telephoto
effect and pulls the Petzval sum negative. The patent notes that the rear elements, being near the diaphragm, can be
kept small, while the front element is more than twice as large (col. 4 lines 70–75). Patent Figure 2 shows the same
proportion.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent gives `nd` (helium d line) and Abbe numbers only, with no glass names or supplier.

| Element | nd | νd | Data-file annotation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| L1 | 1.64250 | 58.09 | LAK6 (Sumita), coordinate-equivalent |
| L2 | 1.72830 | 28.66 | FDS2 (Hoya), coordinate-equivalent |
| L3 | 1.65446 | 33.79 | SF9 (Hikari), coordinate-equivalent |
| L4 | 1.75787 | 31.56 | Unmatched — 758/316 dense flint |
| L5 | 1.65016 | 39.31 | BAFD10 (Hoya), coordinate-equivalent |

L1, L2, L3 and L5 fall within catalog-equivalence tolerance of catalog glasses (Δnd ≤ 4·10⁻⁶, Δνd ≤ 0.07). L2 has
the index of SF10 but an Abbe number 0.25 higher; the discontinued HOYA FDS2 (1.72830 / 28.67) matches it, so the
engine traces L2 on FDS2's vendor dispersion formula. No catalog glass lies within 10⁻⁴ of L4's index, so L4 keeps an
explicit unmatched annotation and the engine models its dispersion from `nd`/`νd`. None of the labels claims the 1963
melt supplier.

The chromatic layout is an achromat in each group. In group A, the crown L1 (νd 58.1) is balanced by the two
negative flints. In group B, the positive element L4 uses the lower-νd flint (31.6) and the negative L5 the
higher-νd one (39.3). That is the arrangement a negative achromatic group needs. The patent publishes no
partial-dispersion data, and the design makes no apochromatic claim.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent gives only the infinity prescription. It has no finite-object spacing table and no moving group. Collector
references give the production lens a minimum focus of 8.5 m. The data file keeps 8.5 m as `closeFocusM` metadata only.
No variable gaps are authored, so the visualization does not move any group. With no interior air space described as
variable, the production lens was presumably focused by moving the whole optical unit. That is an inference, not a
patent statement.

## Aberration-Correction Strategy

The patent's correction strategy rests on how power is distributed around the two lenses that bound the large air
space, not on special glass. Example 1 sits at one end of nearly every range in claim 1:

| Quantity (Example 1) | Patent value | Claim 1 range |
|---|---:|---:|
| φI | 2.2524Φ | 2.022–2.253Φ |
| a3 | 0.52203 f | 0.485–0.523 f |
| φIII + φIV | 1.2175Φ | 0.931–1.218Φ |
| φ3′ + φ4 | 2.339Φ | 2.339–3.738Φ |
| \|φIV / φIII\| | 4.4451 | 3.044–4.446 |

The claim ranges are built from the worked examples, and Example 1 is the strongest-front-group, longest-air-space
extreme. Its L4 front surface is also the flattest of the five (R4 = 0.342 f against 0.20–0.24 f in Examples 2–5).

Computed from the scaled prescription:

- **Spherical aberration** is a small under-correction: −0.51 mm at the f/4 rim and −0.17 mm at half aperture.
- **Petzval sum** is −0.00118 mm⁻¹ (f·P = −0.24). The negative sign is the usual telephoto result of a strong negative
  rear element.
- **Astigmatism:** the patent claims a longitudinal astigmatic difference of 56.8 µm at f = 100 mm, i.e. under 0.6‰
  of the focal length (col. 3 lines 55–68), against 3‰ to 3% for earlier high-power telephotos.

## Semi-Diameters and Vignetting

The patent publishes no clear apertures. The rims follow the Figure 2 silhouette. The figure's axial scale comes from
the first-to-last vertex length, because the figure draws the thin rear elements thicker than their true scale. That
gives about 35.0 mm for the cemented doublet, 32.1 mm for L3, 11.5 mm for L4 and 10.5 mm for L5. These rims pass the
full f/4 axial bundle and the full-field chief ray to the 21.6 mm image corner (ω = 6.07°).

The iris radius (10.86 mm) is derived from f/4 and is not drawn from the figure. The small rear group, which the patent
describes and draws, vignettes the off-axis bundle. Geometric transmission falls to roughly 86% at 4° and 44% at the
6.1° corner. These figures describe the data model, not measured production lenses.

## Modeling Notes and Source Discrepancies

- **Scale:** every radius and axial length is ×200. The computed Gaussian EFL is 199.868 mm, not exactly 200 mm,
  because the patent's normalized table is rounded. `focalLengthMm.design` records 199.87.
- **Back focus:** the printed intercept s′ = 0.20324 (40.648 mm) is kept as the image distance. The paraxial back focus
  of the scaled table is 40.619 mm, a +0.029 mm residual that is left in place.
- **Stop:** the stop position is printed in the patent (a3V = 0.40290, a3H = 0.11913), not inferred.
- **Claim 5:** claim 5 repeats Example 1, but prints R3′ = −5.39649 where the description table gives −5.30649. The
  description's own surface-power table settles it: φ3′ = +0.1233Φ = 0.65446/5.30649. The data file uses −5.30649.
- **Example 4 not used:** Example 4's printed φ3′ (+0.0035Φ) does not follow from its printed R3′ = −18.83797, which
  gives +0.0347Φ. That example is not used.
- **Rear clearance:** the 40.6 mm back focus places the last vertex a few millimetres behind the DKL mount flange. The
  shutter and mount geometry are not modeled.
