## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP 2001-124985 A
**Application Number:** JP 11-307371
**Filed:** 28 October 1999
**Published:** 11 May 2001
**Inventor:** Yoshihisa Yomogida
**Applicant:** Cosina Co., Ltd.
**Title:** Wide-angle lens
**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 1

This is the ten-element Example 1 of JP2001124985A, uniformly scaled by 12 from its normalized source prescription.
The association with the VOIGTLÄNDER ULTRA WIDE-HELIAR 12mm f/5.6 Aspherical L39 is a supported correlation,
not a manufacturer-confirmed identification of the exact numerical design.

The principal convergent evidence is:

1. Both the patent section and Cosina's L39 archive show ten elements in eight air-separated groups.
2. Two convex-to-object negative menisci lead the system; two cemented pairs lie behind the diaphragm.
3. The image-side face of the final positive element is aspheric in both optical sections.
4. The source aperture is f/5.66 and its field exceeds 120°, close to the product's marketed f/5.6 and 121°.
5. The October 1999 filing precedes the official September 2000 product launch.

The original L39 model has manual scale focusing and no rangefinder coupling. Later VM II, VM III and E-mount
products are not substituted for the selected historical lens. The manufacturer confirms its rectilinear intent;
this is not modeled as a fisheye projection. Product topology and timing support the correlation, but do not prove
that the manufactured radii, glasses, or apertures equal the patent values.

## Optical Architecture

The patent defines four functional groups, G1–G4, with negative–positive–negative–positive net power (¶0010–0012).
Those functional groups contain eight air-separated groups and ten glass elements. The two meanings of “group”
are kept separate: the application metadata counts air-separated groups, while the diagram brackets retain G1–G4.

The patent identifies its parts by reference numeral rather than by letter. Figure 1 and ¶0010–0012 call the
four lens groups 10, 20, 30 and 40, the lenses 11, 12, 13 / 21, 22, 23 / 31, 32 / 41, 42, the stop 50 and the
aspherical surface 42b. The elements here are named L11 … L42 after those numerals, so the first digit is the
group and the second the position inside it; the diagram prints the bare numerals as Figure 1 does. G1–G4
stand for the first to fourth lens groups 10–40, and the brackets carry the power signs the claim assigns them.

The front functional group contains the two negative menisci and a positive singlet.
G2 contains a negative meniscus followed by two positive singlets. G3 is a net-negative cemented meniscus pair;
G4 is the rear cemented pair containing the final asphere. The explicitly named stop is source plane 13,
between G2 and G3, and is labeled STO. All source glass/air planes are preserved, with no rear plate invented.

The final-file paraxial result is EFL = 11.977257 mm and BFD = 13.571879 mm from the last refracting vertex.
The first-to-last-vertex track is 46.560000 mm, and the first vertex to the source image plane is
60.120000 mm. The source's final spacing becomes 13.56 mm after scaling and is not changed
to the computed BFD. Its small residual reflects the coarsely rounded normalized prescription.
The first principal plane is 21.382811 mm behind the first vertex;
the rear principal plane is 1.594622 mm behind the last vertex.

The computed BFD exceeds EFL, supporting a retrofocus classification at the published infinity-design state.
The rear principal plane is correspondingly imageward of the final glass vertex. The patent expressly links
its back-focus condition to space behind the lens for a camera's metering arrangement (¶0009), rather than to
an invented digital sensor-cover stack.

The functional-group focal lengths are G1 = -21.529686 mm,
G2 = 12.740280 mm, G3 = -58.243771 mm,
and G4 = 123.339261 mm. These are standalone composites in air, not element
focal lengths or isolated in-situ aberration contributions. The complete surface Petzval sum is
0.009180713 mm⁻¹; it is a paraxial curvature invariant, not a substitute for the patent's sagittal
and tangential aberration plots.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L11 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.6. Glass: 773496 — coordinate class (supplier unconfirmed). f = -37.934897 mm.

The first convex-to-object negative meniscus begins G1. Together with L12 it forms the negative pair used in the patent's |f12|/f condition. The source section shows stepped/beveled outer boundaries; the modeled front and rear clear apertures are consequently distinguished from a single blank diameter.

### L12 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.6. Glass: 773496 — coordinate class (supplier unconfirmed). f = -16.892186 mm.

The second convex-to-object negative meniscus shares the same native glass coordinates as L11. It completes the negative pair before the positive L13. Its standalone focal length is distinct from the compound focal length of the pair, which includes the intervening air space.

### L13 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.5. Glass: 805255 — coordinate class (supplier unconfirmed). f = +34.288050 mm.

The positive biconvex singlet completes G1 without reversing that functional group's net negative power. It is air-separated from L12 and from the next negative meniscus. Its dispersion differs substantially from those of the first two elements; the patent does not provide its individual spectral or aberration budget.

### L21 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.8. Glass: 847238 — coordinate class (supplier unconfirmed). f = -37.181467 mm.

This negative meniscus begins G2. Its convex surface faces the object, followed by the more strongly curved concave rear surface. The next two elements provide positive power, making the three-element functional group net positive.

### L22 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.51680, νd = 64.2. Glass: 517642 — coordinate class (supplier unconfirmed). f = +14.530051 mm.

This biconvex crown-coordinate singlet is the first positive component of G2. Its lower dispersion relative to L21 supplies a material contrast within the group, but an Abbe-number contrast alone does not establish apochromatic correction or identify an exact melt.

### L23 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.4. Glass: 487704 — coordinate class (supplier unconfirmed). f = +23.430937 mm.

The second positive singlet completes G2 ahead of the stop. Its native Abbe number is the highest in this prescription. Neither the source nor the adopted class label identifies it as anomalous-partial-dispersion glass, so no such label is assigned.

### L31 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.83400, νd = 37.3. Glass: 834373 — coordinate class (supplier unconfirmed). f = +17.811963 mm.

The first component of D1/G3 is a positive meniscus with its concave face toward the object. It is cemented to L32 at source surface 15. Its standalone sign does not determine the sign of the cemented pair, which is negative.

### L32 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.71736, νd = 29.5. Glass: 717295 — coordinate class (supplier unconfirmed). f = -14.366253 mm.

The negative meniscus completes D1/G3. Surface 15 carries its outgoing medium in the data model. The pair retains the real shared glass interface rather than an invented cement layer or two coincident air surfaces.

### L41 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.62004, νd = 36.3. Glass: 620363 — coordinate class (supplier unconfirmed). f = -14.169427 mm.

The biconcave component begins D2/G4. Its strongly concave front face is followed by a weakly curved positive-radius cemented interface. The next positive element leaves the two-element functional group with weak net positive power.

### L42 — Biconvex Positive (1× Asph)

nd = 1.58913, νd = 61.3. Glass: 589613 — coordinate class (supplier unconfirmed). f = +15.797921 mm.

The final biconvex positive element completes D2/G4. Its rear face, which the patent marks 42b, is the aspheric source surface 19, labeled 19A. The weak finite front radius remains in the prescription; neither the cemented interface nor the aspheric rear face is silently replaced by a plane.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent supplies native refractive indices and Abbe numbers, without supplier names, melt identification,
C/F/g-line indices or partial-dispersion measurements. The authored six-digit labels therefore identify
coordinate classes only. The d-line interpretation is inferred from Figure 2’s d/g reference curves and
ordinary catalog coordinate compatibility; the table itself uses generic N/ν headings. No wavelength
conversion is applied. The labels do not certify a catalog material or a production supplier.

Primary OHARA, HOYA, Schott, Hikari, Sumita and CDGM catalog rows were checked, including legacy entries
where the vendor provides them. Competing near-coordinate candidates remain in the evidence with native
residuals and exact source records; a nearest candidate is not selected solely because the maker is Japanese.

## Focus Mechanism

The focus status is NO_INTERNAL_RECONSTRUCTION. The patent table supplies a single infinity-design prescription
and no finite-conjugate spacing schedule. There is no constrained optical/mechanical solution from which to
infer a unit-focus travel or internal group trajectory. All spacings remain fixed, var is empty, and the
established closeFocusM = 1e15 sentinel is used. The UI explicitly says that close focus is not modeled.

Cosina specifies manual, non-rangefinder-coupled scale focus down to 0.3 m for the L39 product. That production
minimum distance remains contextual information; it does not establish the unknown optical motion law.
No close-focus magnification, travel, or breathing curve is synthesized from that distance alone.

## Aspherical Surfaces

Only source surface 19, the image-side face of L42 (the patent's surface 42b), is aspheric in Example 1.
The source equation uses sqrt(1 − (1 + K)c²h²) followed by A h⁴ + B h⁶ + C h⁸ + D h¹⁰ (¶0017).
The model therefore retains K = 0 with no conic offset. All lengths are scaled uniformly by s = 12,
while each Aₚ is transformed as Aₚ,scaled = Aₚ,source / 12⁽ᵖ⁻¹⁾. Indices and Abbe numbers are unchanged.
This scaling preserves the printed normalized source; it does not force the recomputed EFL to exactly 12 mm.

| Surface 19A term | Native normalized coefficient | Implemented coefficient |
|---|---:|---:|
| A4 | +3.065e-01 | +1.773726851852e-04 |
| A6 | +1.315e+00 | +5.284690072016e-06 |
| A8 | -1.100e+00 | -3.069898119570e-08 |
| A10 | +1.638e+00 | +3.174553737283e-10 |

Aₚ has units mm⁽¹⁻ᵖ⁾ in the chosen dimensional scale. A12 and A14 are zero because the source uses no such terms.
The positive leading fourth-order departure moves the rear profile imageward relative to its negative-radius
spherical base. Higher orders modify the peripheral shape; a coefficient sign alone does not isolate an
aberration contribution. These primary sources do not establish the particular manufacturing process.

At the modeled semi-diameter 8.200000 mm, read from the patent's Figure 1 where the drawn aspheric flank meets
the element rim, the departure from the same-radius sphere is 2.217327288 mm, and the total sag is
-2.642342956 mm. The profile slope keeps one sign across this aperture: its magnitude peaks near 5.9 mm and
eases to a local minimum near 8.1 mm, so the surface has no turnover inside the modeled rim. The full-field
bundle reaches only 7.32 mm on this surface; the outer zone is drawn glass rather than used aperture. This is
a model-aperture calculation, not a published manufacturing-rim measurement.

## Conditional Expressions

The three source conditions are preserved separately from the rounded published example ratios (¶0011–0014).
Using the actual final scaled prescription gives:

| Condition | Computed ratio | Source approximate ratio |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0.7 < f2/f < 2.5 | 1.063706031 | 1.06 |
| 0.7 < abs(f12)/f < 1.5 | 0.872603543 | 0.87 |
| fB/f > 1.0 | 1.133137492 | 1.13 |

All three strict inequalities hold. Here f12 is the composite focal length of L11 and L12 only; it is not the
focal length of the entire three-element G1. f2 denotes the whole three-element G2, while fB is the last-vertex
paraxial back focus. Uniform scaling leaves these ratios invariant.
The patent discusses balancing wide-angle correction, peripheral illumination and lens size around these
conditions (¶0007–0009); the present calculations verify the ratios, not a separately measured trade-off curve.

## Verification and Modeling Limits

The stop position is explicit in the source, but its physical diameter is not. Its authored paraxial semi-diameter
1.801254309 mm calibrates the source f/5.66 using the final prescription's pupil mapping.
The entrance-pupil radius is 1.058061550 mm. This is dependent calibration,
not independent verification of an unpublished physical diaphragm. Current buildLens maps the nominal
entrance-pupil edge through the exact front-group trace and uses runtime stop radius
1.808290261 mm. This runtime calibration is separate from the stored paraxial radius;
neither is a source-measured iris dimension.

Clear semi-diameters are estimated from the patent's Figure 1, which is drawn to the prescription's axial scale,
taking the height where each drawn curve ends, and are floor-checked by exact ray geometry; none is a published
value. Surface 2 is held at its rim-slope limit and surface 4 at its air-gap limit. The front of L12 is the 12.6 mm
blank the figure draws; it closes 92% of the L11–L12 air gap without touching L11, so this lens sets its air-gap
limit to 92% in place of the default 90%. L23 carries the 5.1 mm rim the figure draws, which stands above the
L31–L32 doublet behind the stop. The figure ends L41's front concave at 4.8 mm and continues that face as a flat
annulus out to the 8.25 mm rim, so L41 is drawn as a flat-fronted block. The cross-section joins the front and
rear rims of an element with one straight edge and cannot draw that annulus; the concave is therefore carried
to 5.2 mm, 0.4 mm past its drawn end and the largest height at which L41's front rim stays behind L32's rear
rim, which leaves L41 as a truncated cone that is as full as it can be without cupping the doublet.
Positive edge thickness, real rim angle, conic domain and shared-radial-band air-gap intrusion were checked.
The minimum sampled element edge thickness is 0.960000000 mm.
Real state-native project render diagnostics show zero hidden trim; no geometry exception is introduced.

Independent sphere/profile-intersection and vector-Snell tracing, and the real current project tracer, both
pass the actual six-ray axial fan at fractions −0.83, −0.50, −0.17, +0.17, +0.50, +0.83 of the entrance-pupil radius.
They also pass the five-ray off-axis fan at fractions −0.75, −0.375, 0, +0.375, +0.75 at 60% of the declared
60.5° half-field, and the same five fractions in the additional 60.5° full-field diagnostic.

The declared rectilinear field is Cosina's 121° production value; the source aberration graph marks a maximum
half-field of 60.65°. Neither value is inferred from a paraxial aperture estimate. Passing these finite meridional
samples does not establish continuous-field, full-pupil, spectral, MTF or production-tolerance performance.
All five authored f-stops and static focus-slider endpoints/midpoint were also checked. The actual
format audit reaches image semi-height 20.693145 mm at the declared 60.5° field,
95.580% of the 21.65 mm format corner. This is a declared angular bound, not a
clear-aperture block; the exact corner chief ray at 61.581394° clears the modeled rims but lies beyond
the declared production field. No full-format-at-60.5° or no-vignetting claim is made.
The patent's normalized distances are coarsely rounded, so the retained image plane has a small first-order
residual rather than being silently moved to optimize the model.
