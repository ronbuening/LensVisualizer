## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP 2000-321490 A
**Application Number:** JP 11-132177
**Filed:** 13 May 1999
**Published:** 24 November 2000
**Inventor:** Koji Shiokawa
**Applicant:** Cosina Co., Ltd.
**Title:** Photographic lens
**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 1

The modeled prescription is the eight-element, six-group Example 1 of JP2000321490A.
Its association with the VOIGTLÄNDER ULTRON 35mm f/1.7 Aspherical L39 is a supported production correlation,
not a manufacturer-confirmed attribution of the exact numerical prescription.

The following evidence converges:

1. Cosina specifies eight elements in six groups for the original L39 product.
2. The official optical section agrees with the patent's front biconcave element, two central cemented doublets,
   intervening diaphragm, positive rear singlet, and final negative element with an object-side asphere.
3. The patent's focal length of 36.05 mm lies near the marketed 35 mm, while the source f/1.64 differs from
   the marketed f/1.7. Both differences remain explicit; no rescaling or aperture relabeling conceals them.
4. The May 1999 filing precedes Cosina's August 1999 product launch. The official record identifies the L39 mount
   and a rangefinder-coupled minimum focusing distance of 0.9 m.

Later VM products with similar names are outside this identification. Cosina's product record calls this a
symmetric wide-angle design; the patent describes its changes to a Gaussian-type large-aperture arrangement
(¶0002–0011). The numbers below belong to the patent model, not a measured production specimen.

## Optical Architecture

The six air-separated groups have a negative–positive–positive–negative–positive–negative power sequence.
The third and fourth groups are cemented doublets, so six groups contain eight physical elements.
The diaphragm is the explicitly listed plane 8, between the two doublets; it is labeled STO in the model.
No optical plane is removed, and no rear sensor plate or filter is present in the source table.

Patent Fig. 1 numbers the six groups 11 to 16 and the diaphragm 17, and names the cemented components 13a, 13b,
14a and 14b (¶0009–0010). The diagram carries those numerals under the elements and G1 to G6, with the sign of
each group's power, for the groups. In this analysis they are L1 (11), L2 (12), L3 (13a), L4 (13b), L5 (14a),
L6 (14b), L7 (15) and L8 (16); D1 and D2 are the cemented third and fourth groups, 13 and 14. Numeral 16a in the
figure is the aspherical object-side surface of the sixth group, surface 14A here, not an element.

Tracing the final prescription gives EFL = 36.050652 mm and BFD = 19.000655 mm from the last lens vertex.
The source image-plane spacing is 19.0000 mm. The first-to-last-vertex track is 45.000000 mm,
and the first vertex to the tabulated image plane is 64.000000 mm.
The first principal plane lies 11.902443 mm behind the first vertex;
the second lies -17.049997 mm from the last vertex, with a negative value toward the object.
These are air-to-air d-line paraxial calculations. BFD is shorter than EFL, so this prescription does not support
a retrofocus classification under the BFD > EFL criterion.

The cemented groups have net focal lengths of 25.395557 mm and
-46.977274 mm, respectively. These are composite group values;
they must not be replaced by the focal length of either component or interpreted as a separate in-situ
aberration contribution. The surface-by-surface Petzval sum is 0.002785787 mm⁻¹.
That paraxial curvature invariant alone does not predict the real sagittal and tangential image surfaces.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.54814, νd = 45.8. Glass: 548458 — coordinate class (supplier unconfirmed). f = -31.235029 mm.

The first element is a biconcave negative lens, as required by claim 1. It precedes the positive collector and differentiates the design from a conventional large-aperture normal lens. The patent links this front negative element and the final asphere to the intended balance of spherical aberration and field curvature (¶0007); that statement is a design rationale, not an independently isolated aberration budget.

### L2 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.6. Glass: 773496 — coordinate class (supplier unconfirmed). f = +84.321982 mm.

The second element is an air-spaced positive biconvex lens. It changes the divergence introduced by the front negative element before the first cemented group. Its power and position are directly determined by the prescription; the patent does not provide an element-isolated aberration attribution.

### L3 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.80420, νd = 46.5. Glass: 804465 — coordinate class (supplier unconfirmed). f = +18.549963 mm.

This positive biconvex component forms the front part of D1. Its rear surface is the shared glass-to-glass interface 6, whose outgoing medium belongs to L4. Its standalone focal length is computed with air on both sides and therefore differs from its behavior while cemented.

### L4 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.64769, νd = 33.8. Glass: 648338 — coordinate class (supplier unconfirmed). f = -59.823177 mm.

The negative meniscus completes D1. Its lower Abbe number than L3 provides a dispersive contrast within the net-positive group. The patent attributes chromatic and astigmatic balancing to the central cemented constructions together (¶0008); no apochromatic or partial-dispersion claim follows from the two Abbe numbers.

### L5 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.69895, νd = 30.1. Glass: 699301 — coordinate class (supplier unconfirmed). f = -14.357707 mm.

The biconcave component begins the net-negative D2 behind the diaphragm. Its concave object-side boundary faces the central air space. The shared interface 10 enters L6, so assigning that interface the L5 glass would change the prescribed optical system.

### L6 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.80420, νd = 46.5. Glass: 804465 — coordinate class (supplier unconfirmed). f = +22.060754 mm.

The biconvex component completes D2. The positive component does not reverse the negative net sign of the cemented group. Its standalone power is given separately from the group power; neither value alone establishes a particular aberration contribution.

### L7 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.80610, νd = 40.7. Glass: 806407 — coordinate class (supplier unconfirmed). f = +28.443300 mm.

The air-spaced rear positive singlet is biconvex. It is followed by the wider air space leading to the final negative element. The source table and section establish its shape, position and power, but do not specify an independent focus motion for it.

### L8 — Negative Meniscus (1× Asph)

nd = 1.69350, νd = 53.3. Glass: 694533 — coordinate class (supplier unconfirmed). f = -55.496613 mm.

The final element is a negative meniscus with its concave aspheric face toward the object. The weakly curved rear face retains the finite source radius rather than being silently made plane. Claim 1 and ¶0007 identify the front asphere as a characteristic feature of this wide-angle arrangement.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent supplies refractive indices and Abbe numbers, but no supplier names, melt certificates, line indices,
or anomalous partial-dispersion values. The six-digit labels summarize the native d-line coordinates.
The table headings are generic N and ν; the d-line interpretation is inferred from the 587.6 nm reference
in Figure 2 and matching ordinary catalog coordinates, rather than an explicit table subscript. No wavelength
conversion was applied. They identify comparison classes only; they do not assert that a catalog glass was used in production.

The author checked primary OHARA, HOYA, Schott, Hikari, Sumita and CDGM catalogs, including obsolete rows where
provided. Several candidates coexist at each coordinate; numerical closeness cannot identify a supplier.
The OHARA S- and L-prefix families remain distinct in the evidence rather than being collapsed into one label.

The catalog candidates are equivalent-coordinate examples, not accepted identity assignments.
Their native residuals and source rows are preserved in the evidence. No candidate's C/F/g indices are copied
onto an element as though measured for the patented melt. The runtime may resolve a six-digit code to a coordinate-compatible catalog proxy; that also does not establish supplier identity. Consequently, simulated chromatic behavior remains
approximate and does not support an APO designation or a claim about secondary spectrum.

## Focus Mechanism

The implemented status is NO_INTERNAL_RECONSTRUCTION. The published table is an infinity-design prescription;
no finite-conjugate spacing schedule or mechanically constrained focus solution is available.
The model keeps every internal gap and the tabulated image spacing fixed. Its variable-gap object is empty,
and the established closeFocusM = 1e15 sentinel disables a meaningful close-focus endpoint.
The viewer description explicitly says that close focus is not modeled.

Cosina's L39 product is manually focused and rangefinder-coupled to 0.9 m. That production specification does
not determine whether this exact patent prescription moved as a unit or used another optical motion.
No travel, finite-focus magnification or breathing curve is invented from the advertised minimum distance.

## Aspherical Surfaces

Surface 14A, the object-side face of L8, is the only aspherical surface in Example 1.
The patent writes the conic base with sqrt(1 − (1 + K)c²h²), followed by A h⁴ + B h⁶ + C h⁸ + D h¹⁰;
therefore its K is already the model's K. The radius, spacings and coefficients are used without scaling.
The radial coefficient Aₚ has units mm⁽¹⁻ᵖ⁾, and h is a nonnegative radial height.

| Surface | K | A4 | A6 | A8 | A10 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 14A | 0 | −5.18095e−5 | +2.69712e−8 | −1.06630e−9 | +4.28036e−12 |

A12 and A14 are zero because those orders are not present in the source equation/table.
The negative leading fourth-order term increases objectward departure from the spherical base on this concave
front face; the higher orders modify that departure away from the axis. This description concerns the actual
polynomial shape and does not assign a separately measured aberration correction to each coefficient.
The selected primary sources do not establish a manufacturing process for this particular element.

At the modeled semi-diameter 12.400000 mm, the departure from the same-radius spherical base is
-1.354977963 mm, and the total sag is -3.449954303 mm. This is a calculation at an inferred
model aperture, not a published manufacturing rim. The actual aspheric rim slope is included in geometry checks.
Patent Fig. 1 ends the curved face near 10.2 mm and continues with a flat annulus to the 12.4 mm outer rim, so
the outer 2 mm of the modeled face extends the polynomial past the drawn curved zone.

## Verification and Modeling Limits

The physical stop position is published, but its clear radius is not. The authored paraxial radius
10.973970880 mm is calibrated from the source f/1.64 and the final-file paraxial pupil mapping.
The resulting entrance-pupil radius is 10.991052396 mm. Agreement with f/1.64 is therefore
a dependent calibration, not independent evidence of the original iris diameter. The current project
then maps the nominal entrance-pupil edge through the exact front-group ray trace and uses a runtime
stop radius of 11.460117898 mm. The stored paraxial radius and this runtime radius are separate
model conventions; neither is a published iris measurement.

Lens clear semi-diameters are estimated from the outer rims drawn in patent Fig. 1 and floor-checked by exact
ray tracing; the manufacturer section is a secondary check on proportions.
All sampled edge thicknesses remain positive, including a minimum of
0.185094548 mm at the L3 rim. Real rim slopes, conic domain and shared-band
air-gap intrusion satisfy the current geometry rules. The real project renderer reports zero hidden trim.

Separately implemented exact profile/Snell tracing and the current project tracer both pass the actual default
on-axis fractions ±0.83, ±0.50 and ±0.17. At the default off-axis field, 60% of the modeled vignetting-limited
half-field (21.24°), the fractions −0.75, −0.375, 0 and 0.375 pass and the 0.75 ray is stopped at the L1 front
rim (13.46 mm against a 13.1 mm rim); patent Fig. 1 likewise draws its off-axis bundles limited at the first
surface. The raw vignetting-limited half-field is 35.400705°;
the separate format-aware analysis field reaches the 21.65 mm format corner at 31.616638° without chief-ray clipping. At the optional 100% raw field,
some rays vignette at outer lens rims; the earlier authored-stop diagnostic also clipped one sampled f/4 ray
at the iris that clears the larger runtime-calibrated stop. Those losses remain in the results; the model does not claim complete
full-field or full-pupil clearance. These finite samples also do not certify production MTF, spectral performance,
mechanical tolerances, or manufactured surface accuracy.
