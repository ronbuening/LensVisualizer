## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,746,351 A\
**Filed:** March 3, 1953\
**Published:** May 22, 1956\
**Inventor:** Albrecht Wilhelm Tronnier\
**Applicant:** Voigtländer AG\
**Title:** Photographic objective of the modified triplet type and a meniscus shaped negative member axially separated therefrom\
**Priority:** Germany, March 5, 1952 (German counterpart DE 1 024 730, published February 20, 1958)\
**Example details:** Table 2, Fig. 4; normalized to f = 1.000 and scaled ×35. Table 2 states 1:3.4.

Voigtländer's Prominent lens literature names the Skoparon 35 mm f/3.5 as a five-element wide-angle covering about 63° and shows a large negative front meniscus ahead of a compact core. The section drawing in Voigtländer's Prominent lens booklet matches the model element for element: a meniscus convex to the object, plano-convex L2, biconcave L3, the diaphragm, and a cemented pair with a nearly flat front face, with the rear elements drawn at about 0.4 of the front meniscus diameter. Table 2 is the patent's only five-element, four-group example: Table 1 (Fig. 2) has four single elements at f/4.0, and Figs. 1 and 3 (six and seven elements) have no numerical tables. The match rests on construction, maker, designer and date; the patent does not name the product. Table 2 and the text give 1:3.4, an effective aperture of 0.294 for unit focal length, while Fig. 4 and the production lens are f/3.5; the model's iris opens to the patent's f/3.4. The patent prints no field angle, so the 31.5° half-field is Voigtländer's 63° figure. The German counterpart DE 1 024 730 carries the same Table 2.

All lengths are scaled ×35 from the normalized table. The patent's overall length G = 0.69367 (24.28 mm at this scale) runs from R1 to R9 and excludes the back focus, which the patent does not print; the model's back focus is computed from the prescription. Fig. 4's element proportions guided the inferred semi-diameters but are not dimensioned clear apertures. The later Bessamatic Skoparex 35 mm f/3.4 (US 2,927,506, Determann) describes itself as a further development of this patent but is a different six-element design.

## Optical Architecture

Five elements in four groups form a compact wide-angle retrofocus arrangement: a broad negative front meniscus (the patent's member I), a large air space A, then the converging principal system II of plano-convex L2, biconcave L3, the diaphragm and the cemented L4/L5 pair. Air space A has the shape of a negative meniscus; the patent's ratio of its bounding radii, R2/R3 = 2.07525, exceeds its 1.333 condition. Surface 4 is plane; the other interfaces are spherical. The patent's component focal lengths (at F = 100: FL1 −535, FL2 +45.7, FL3 −33.2, FL4/5 +60.7 mm) are thin-lens values; the thick-lens figures below differ from them most for L1.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.46522, νd = 65.2. Glass: no catalog row within 0.25 in νd; traced at the printed pair. f = −203.6 mm (thick lens in air, at the 35 mm scale).

A broad low-index negative meniscus forms the front member and is separated from the compact positive principal section by air space A. The patent calls its material a fluorine-crown glass of very low refractive power; it is not crystalline fluorite. The patent credits this glass with letting the front member be a single lens.

### L2 — Plano-Convex Positive

nd = 1.65770, νd = 51.2. Glass: no catalog row within 0.25 in νd; traced at the printed pair. f = +16.0 mm.

The high-index plano-convex element starts the principal section after the large front air space; its convex face R3 is the image-side boundary of air space A.

### L3 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.60266, νd = 38.4. Glass: no catalog row within 0.25 in νd; traced at the printed pair. f = −11.5 mm.

The compact negative member opposes L2 and lies immediately before the diaphragm space.

### L4 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.58360, νd = 46.2. Glass: S-BAM3 (OHARA), nearest catalog row. f = −17.3 mm.

The negative member of the rear cemented pair precedes the strongly converging shared interface.

### L5 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.69347, νd = 53.5. Glass: LAC13 (HOYA), catalog equivalent. f = +9.9 mm.

The positive rear component completes the cemented principal member; the cemented pair together is +21.3 mm. Its curvatures and thickness bound the inferred 5.4 mm semi-diameter of the doublet.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent gives mean refractive indices at the yellow helium d line and Abbe numbers, but no glass names. For dispersion, the data file uses a modern catalog glass only where one lies within about 0.25 of the printed νd: S-BAM3 is the nearest row to L4 (0.001 lower in nd), and LAC13 matches L5 as a catalog equivalent. L1, L2 and L3 have no row that close, so they trace at the printed nd/νd. None of these identifies the glass used in production. The patent pairs the very low-index L1 with a high-index L2 across air space A, and says the large index difference keeps part II simple at high aperture.

## Focus Mechanism

The model is static at infinity, with the image plane 56.56 mm behind the first vertex. The patent prints no close-focus spacing. Voigtländer's Prominent lens booklet states that the Skoparon couples automatically with the camera's built-in rangefinder once attached. The measured distance is transferred to a green metre scale on the lens to read depth of field, and that scale's yellow figures continue below 1 m to 0.7 m. The booklet does not say which part moves to focus, so no focus movement is drawn.

## Aperture and Stop

The patent places the diaphragm B in the 1.988 mm air space a3 between L3 and L4 but gives neither its position nor its diameter. The model puts the stop 65% of the way through a3 (1.292 mm after R6, 0.696 mm before R7), close to where Fig. 4 draws it. At that position the f/3.4 iris (radius 4.57 mm, from a 5.147 mm entrance-pupil radius) clears L3's concave rear face by 0.19 mm and L4's front face by 0.57 mm. Positions at 30% or 45% of the gap would cut into L3.

The semi-diameters are inferred. The Fig. 4 proportions of about 1 : 0.43 : 0.40 : 0.38 for L1, L2, L3 and the doublet, anchored on the entrance pupil, give 14.2, 6.1, 5.6 and 5.4 mm. The L3 rear face is cut to 4.85 mm, matching the bevel Fig. 4 draws there, so that it stays in front of the stop plane; the full off-axis bundle needs 4.72 mm at that face. Measured against the drawing's axial scale, Fig. 4 draws the rims about 7–10% smaller (about 13.3, 5.6, 5.4 and 4.9 mm). The wider model rims keep about 77% of the axial illumination at the 31.5° field edge, against about 57% with the drawn sizes.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The scaled prescription gives an EFL of 35.001 mm, against the patent's f = 1.000 (35.000 mm). The paraxial back focus is 32.280 mm. The patent prints no back focus, so this value is computed, and the image plane sits at that paraxial focus, 56.56 mm behind the first vertex. No radius, thickness or index was adjusted.

The 31.5° half-field is Voigtländer's 63° coverage, not a patent value. At that angle the chief ray lands 21.19 mm from the axis, just inside the 21.63 mm corner of the 24 × 36 mm frame, with about −1.2% distortion. At f/3.4 the geometric transmission is 100% to about 14°, 94% at 23° and 77% at 31.5°; these figures depend on the inferred rims. On-axis real-ray spherical aberration is +0.87 mm at the full f/3.4 pupil and −0.27 mm at the 0.7 zone. The patent prints no aberration curves to compare. Indices are the patent's d-line values; dispersion comes from the catalog glasses above, so chromatic results are approximate.
