The prescription is a direct, unscaled transcription of Example 1. The production correlation is fixed to the
VOIGTLÄNDER COLOR-SKOPAR 28mm f/2.8 Aspherical SL IIs; it is a technical correlation, not a statement that Cosina has

Several independent features converge on that identification:

1. Example 1 publishes a 29.4 mm focal length, F/2.88, and a 21.63 mm image height. The production lens is marketed as a
   28 mm f/2.8 full-frame lens. The independently traced effective focal length is 29.430296 mm, so the data keeps the
   marketed 28 mm designation separate from the exact modeled design value.
2. Both the patent and Cosina's specification table describe seven elements in six groups, with both surfaces of the
   final element aspherical. Cosina's English feature paragraph currently says “5 elements in 6 groups,” but the
   English
   and Japanese specification tables and the published optical section give seven elements in six groups; the data
   follows the internally consistent tables and diagram.
3. The patent requires a negative front group, a positive rear group, a stop between the rear subgroups, and a long back
   focus. That architecture is consistent with the production lens's Nikon Ai-S SLR mount and Cosina's published optical
   section.
4. The patent specifies whole-lens translation for focus. Cosina specifies a 0.15 m minimum focus and 1:2.3 maximum
   reproduction. Applying the patent's rigid unit-focus mechanism to the final prescription predicts 1:2.266 at 0.15 m.
5. The application was filed on 27 August 2021, shortly before the product's 20 October 2021 release, while the
   publication followed in 2023.

No uniform scale was applied: `s = 1.0`. Consequently, every radius, thickness, image-plane coordinate, and aspherical
coefficient is stored at the published scale, and no coefficient transformation was required.

## Optical Architecture

Example 1 is a compact retrofocus wide-angle design. Its optical power sequence is negative-positive: the front group
`Gf` is negative, while the rear group `Gs` is positive. The rear group is divided into a positive cemented subgroup
`Gsa` and a positive three-element subgroup `Gsb`, with the aperture stop between them (¶0023, ¶0027–¶0028).

The physical construction is seven elements in six air-spaced groups:

- `Gf`: the positive meniscus `Lp1` followed by the negative meniscus `L1`;
- `Gsa`: the cemented doublet `J1`, comprising biconcave `Ln1` and biconvex `Lp2`;
- `Gsb`: the negative meniscus `L2`, positive meniscus `L3`, and double-sided aspherical positive meniscus `Le`.

The front positive meniscus is not the principal source of front-group power. It precedes the stronger negative meniscus
and is described by the patent as a correction element for distortion (¶0024). The complete `Gf` group has a computed
equivalent focal length of -86.646 mm. The cemented `J1` doublet is weakly positive as a complete unit, with a computed
equivalent focal length of +176.415 mm, even though its front member is a strong negative element. The rear `Gsb`
subgroup is the dominant positive section, with a computed equivalent focal length of +29.017 mm; the entire `Gs` group
is +26.830 mm. These are assembled-group equivalents computed from each group's own paraxial matrix; they must not be
confused with the standalone element focal lengths listed below or with surface-by-surface in-situ ray-angle
contributions.

The first-to-last vertex track is 30.640 mm, while the modeled paraxial back focal distance is 38.532 mm. Because the
back focal distance exceeds the 29.430 mm effective focal length, the design meets the project's retrofocus criterion.
Its track-to-focal-length ratio is 1.041, so it is not telephoto under the separate `TL/EFL < 1` definition.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### Lp1 — Positive Meniscus, Convex to the Object

`nd = 1.77250`, `νd = 49.63`. Glass: N-LAF34 optical equivalent; patent vendor unspecified. Standalone `f = +65.23 mm`.

`Lp1` is the front correction lens of `Gf`. Its weakly curved rear surface and positive power precondition the oblique
bundles before they encounter the negative meniscus behind it. The patent expressly permits this added positive lens to
reduce distortion generated by the negative front element (¶0024). Its role is therefore corrective and distributive;
it does not change the patent's classification of the complete front group as negative.

### L1 — Negative Meniscus, Convex to the Object

`nd = 1.49700`, `νd = 81.61`. Glass: H-FK61 optical equivalent; patent vendor unspecified. Standalone `f = -35.49 mm`.

`L1` supplies the principal negative power of `Gf`. Its convex object-side face and strongly curved image-side face
produce the beam expansion needed for a wide field while allowing the succeeding positive system to retain a long rear
clearance. The patent requires this first negative lens to have `νd1 > 52`; the stored value, 81.61, satisfies the
condition (¶0025–¶0026).

The high Abbe number identifies a low-dispersion optical class, but the data contains no line indices or partial-
dispersion term. It therefore supports an Abbe-level chromatic model only and does not establish anomalous dispersion.

### J1 / Ln1 — Cemented Biconcave Negative Member

`nd = 1.613311465921`; `νd` is not published. Glass: Unmatched (nd inferred from published FL; νd omitted by patent).
Standalone `f = -15.32 mm`.

The patent's Table 1 leaves the material cells at surface 5 blank even though the figure and prose identify a real
biconcave lens at that position and the same row gives its standalone focal length. Treating the blank as air would
remove the front member of the cemented doublet and contradict the prescribed topology. The data therefore uses only the
d-line index solved from the published radii, 1.00 mm center thickness, and `f = -15.32 mm`. The result is
`nd = 1.613311465921`; `νd`, the line indices, and the melt identity remain unresolved.

`Ln1` is the negative front member of `J1`. It opposes the strong positive power of `Lp2`, so the cemented pair is only
weakly positive as a complete subgroup. The standalone focal length is retained to describe this member in isolation,
not the power of the cemented assembly.

### J1 / Lp2 — Cemented Biconvex Positive Member

`nd = 1.87070`, `νd = 40.73`. Glass: TAFD32 optical equivalent; patent vendor unspecified. Standalone `f = +14.47 mm`.

`Lp2` is the strong positive rear member of `J1`. The cemented interface is surface 6 and, following the data model's
junction rule, is assigned to downstream element `Lp2`. The combination converts the strongly negative front member and
strongly positive rear member into a weak positive subgroup, while the cemented boundary removes an air-glass pair and
provides chromatic balancing freedom (¶0027).

The class code is supported by the stored `nd` and `νd`, but several vendors publish close or exact equivalents. No
specific supplier is asserted.

### L2 — Negative Meniscus, Concave to the Object

`nd = 1.84666`, `νd = 23.78`. Glass: H-ZF52 optical equivalent; patent vendor unspecified. Standalone `f = -27.05 mm`.

`L2` is the first element behind the stop and the negative member of `Gsb`. Its high index permits substantial negative
power without requiring still stronger curvature. The patent makes this relationship explicit through `nd2 > 1.84` and
`νd2 < 31`, conditions intended to limit geometric and lateral-colour penalties in this position (¶0028–¶0029).

This element's low Abbe number makes it the principal dispersive negative member in the rear section. That statement
follows from the d-line/Abbe data; it does not imply a particular partial-dispersion behavior.

### L3 — Positive Meniscus, Convex to the Image

`nd = 1.59349`, `νd = 67.00`. Glass: J-PSKH4 optical equivalent; patent vendor unspecified. Standalone `f = +25.44 mm`.

`L3` restores positive power after `L2`. Its object-side surface has a radius of -2886.42 mm and is optically close to
plane, while the more strongly curved image-side surface carries most of the element's positive power. Positioned in the
expanding rear subgroup, it contributes to the system's convergence without placing a high-curvature positive surface
immediately at the stop.

### Le — Positive Meniscus with Two Aspherical Surfaces

`nd = 1.61035`, `νd = 57.90`. Glass: P-SK60 optical equivalent; patent vendor unspecified. Standalone `f = +43.43 mm`.

`Le` is the final positive corrector. Both surfaces, `13A` and `14A`, are aspherical. The patent places the asphere at
the
rear of the system so that the final ray angles and field-dependent residuals can be adjusted after the principal power
has already been established by `J1`, `L2`, and `L3` (¶0030).

Its standalone positive power is moderate. In situ, its importance is not limited to paraxial power: the two aspherical
profiles reshape the marginal zones while preserving the long rear clearance.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent specifies rigid whole-lens translation along the optical axis (¶0033). All internal element spacings are
therefore fixed, and only the distance from surface `14A` to the image plane changes. This is unit focusing, not an
inner-, rear-, or floating-focus mechanism.

The patent publishes an infinity rear spacing of 38.498 mm and one finite row with `ZD0 = 180.000 mm` and
`ZD14 = 43.330 mm`. That finite row corresponds to approximately 0.254 m from object plane to image plane; it is not the
production lens's 0.15 m minimum-focus endpoint. A direct paraxial solution of the final prescription gives 43.375340 mm
for the patent's 180 mm object-space row, a 0.045340 mm residual attributable to the precision of the published table.

The published 180 mm row is stored as an exact focus keyframe. The production endpoint in the data is a `CONSTRAINED_RECONSTRUCTION`. It retains the patent's rigid
unit-focus mechanism and fixes the object-to-image distance at Cosina's published 0.15 m. The finite-conjugate equation
has two positive reciprocal roots at that fixed total distance:

| State | Rear spacing after `14A` | Unit travel from infinity | Paraxial magnification | Treatment |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Infinity | 38.498000 mm | 0 | — | Published base state |
| 0.15 m branch | 51.521596 mm | 13.023596 mm | -0.441358 | Retained |
| 0.15 m reciprocal root | 105.213561 mm | 66.715561 mm | -2.265735 | Rejected |

The retained branch is continuous from infinity, has `|m| < 1`, and predicts approximately 1:2.266, close to Cosina's
rounded 1:2.3 maximum-reproduction specification. The reciprocal high-extension root is mathematically valid but is not
the production operating branch. This selection is a mechanism- and product-constrained inference, not a published
patent focus row. No internal floating motion has been introduced, and interpolation between keyframes is not claimed as a mechanical trajectory.

## Aspherical Surfaces

Surfaces `13A` and `14A`, the two faces of `Le`, use the patent's standard conic-constant equation:

$$
Z(h)=\frac{c h^2}{1+\sqrt{1-(1+K)c^2h^2}}+A_4h^4+A_6h^6+A_8h^8+A_{10}h^{10},\qquad c=1/R.
$$

Thus `K = 0` denotes a spherical conic base and `K = -1` a paraboloidal base. No shifted-κ conversion is required.
Because `s = 1.0`, the coefficients are copied without dimensional rescaling.

| Surface | K | A4 | A6 | A8 | A10 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 13A | -1.3663 | -7.2117E-06 | 2.3332E-08 | 2.6242E-09 | 5.8120E-13 |
| 14A | -1.2708 | 1.1229E-06 | 7.1873E-08 | 1.4566E-09 | 9.7907E-12 |

The patent does not publish semi-diameters. The authored 10.6 mm semi-diameter on both surfaces is a validated modeling
value derived from the production optical section and ray/geometry constraints. At that modeled rim, `13A` is
0.389973 mm shallower than its same-radius spherical base, while `14A` is 0.961489 mm shallower. The actual rim-slope
angles are 5.312° and 9.805°, respectively. These departures are quoted only at the verified authored semi-diameter.

Cosina identifies the production component as a double-sided aspherical element. Neither the patent nor the product
source used here specifies whether it is molded, polished, or hybrid, so no manufacturing process is assigned.

## Modeling Boundaries and Source Corrections

The aperture-stop position is published between `Gsa` and `Gsb`; its diameter is not. The physical stop semi-diameter
of 5.424771 mm is inferred from the exact modeled F/2.88 and the paraxial entrance-pupil geometry. The patent also
publishes no clear-aperture semi-diameters. All authored surface semi-diameters were inferred from Cosina's optical
section and then checked for edge thickness, actual rim slope, conic validity, cross-gap intrusion, off-axis
containment, and render trim.
They are modeling values rather than patent dimensions.

The optional wavelength-cut filter or dust-protection plate mentioned in the patent prose is absent from Example 1's
prescription table. It is omitted, as are sensor cover glass, filters, inactive dummy planes, and mechanical parts. No
air-equivalent correction is required because no omitted plate is included in the published numerical example.

The surface-5 index correction is limited to the d-line index required to preserve the published element and standalone
focal length. Its `νd` and glass identity remain unresolved. A second source discrepancy is retained rather than
silently repaired: the displayed 30.640 mm lens track plus 38.498 mm rear spacing gives 69.138 mm from the first surface
to the image plane, while the patent's condition table reports `LA = 69.15 mm`.

## Verification Summary

Independent sequential height/reduced-angle tracing and an ABCD calculation agree exactly at stored precision. The
matrix determinant is 1.0000000000000002, and the computed effective focal length is 29.430296 mm. The same trace gives
a paraxial best-focus BFD of 38.532304 mm, 0.034304 mm longer than the authored 38.498 mm patent row; this residual is
consistent with the rounded radii, indices, and the inferred surface-5 index.

The stop model reproduces F/2.880000 from a 5.424771 mm physical stop radius and a 5.109426 mm entrance-pupil radius.
The surface-by-surface Petzval sum, evaluated as `Σφ/(n·n′)`, is +0.003974842 mm⁻¹. Standalone element focal
lengths
reproduce the authored values within 0.015 mm; the `Ln1` match is necessarily tautological because its d-line index was
solved from
its published focal length.

The inferred apertures also pass the geometry checks used for the model. The minimum element edge thickness is
0.821009 mm, the maximum actual rim angle is 55.190°, and the tightest default traced-ray clearance is 0.112075 mm.
These checks establish internal model validity; they do not convert the inferred clear apertures into patent facts.
