## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JPH11326756A<br>
**Application Number:** JP1998-137807<br>
**Filed:** 20 May 1998<br>
**Published:** 26 November 1999<br>
**Inventor:** Yoshihisa Yomogida (蓬田 祥寿)<br>
**Applicant:** Cosina Co., Ltd. (株式会社コシナ)<br>
**Title:** 広角レンズ — Wide-angle lens<br>
**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 3, Table 5 and Table 6; Fig. 5

The prescription is the patent's third numerical example, retained at its native millimetre scale.
Its association with the Voigtländer Super Wide-Heliar 15mm f/4.5 Aspherical SL is a design correlation,
not a manufacturer-confirmed identification of the production prescription.
The principal convergences are:

1. The patent and Cosina's SL specification both describe eight elements in six groups.
2. A negative rear meniscus carries an aspherical face; the manufacturer's section places an asphere in the rear element.
3. The patent states 15.30 mm, f/4.57 and a 110.1° full field, close to the marketed 15 mm, f/4.5 and 110°.
4. Cosina is the applicant, and the 1999 publication predates the March 2003 SL release.

The official SL archive records Nikon F as the native mount and requires mirror-up operation on the specified compatible
35 mm bodies. Nikon S, Contax rangefinder, Leica screw and M compatibility uses adapters; those are not additional native
mounts of this SL model. The 135-full-frame metadata follows the listed 35 mm camera application, without certifying
unvignetted illumination across a particular sensor. The SL production run ended in April 2007.
The manufacturer's 12.8 mm mechanical length is mount-referenced and must not be compared directly with optical track.

## Optical Architecture

The system has two negative front menisci, a positive third element, two positive-net-power cemented doublets surrounding
the stop, and a negative aspheric rear meniscus. The fourteen refracting interfaces and the neutral stop make fifteen
prescription rows. Cementing L4 to L5 and L6 to L7 reduces eight elements to six air-separated groups.

Paragraphs 0005–0010 connect the front negative pair to the wide field and discuss balancing back focus, surface curvature,
and glass choices. Paragraphs 0011–0012 describe the element sequence and stop between the cemented groups.
The front pair's composite focal length is −12.750921 mm. The first and second cemented doublets have composite focal
lengths of +24.691384 mm and +24.152212 mm, respectively, calculated in air with their actual shared interfaces.
Those group powers are distinct from the isolated powers of their component elements.

The calculated d-line EFL is 15.301896 mm. Back focal distance is 14.501621 mm from the final lens vertex, while the
first-to-last-vertex distance is 35.21 mm. The printed image plane lies at a total first-vertex track of 49.71 mm.
Thus BFD/EFL is 0.947701: a negative front pair alone does not justify describing this example as a retrofocus system
under the BFD > EFL criterion. Its total track also exceeds the EFL, so it is not a telephoto configuration under TL/EFL < 1.
Cosina's “symmetric type” designation is qualitative; the numerical prescription is not exactly mirror-symmetric.

The stop is explicitly surface 10. The adjacent source gaps are 1.30 mm before the stop and 2.13 mm after it.
No cover plate, rear filter or dummy plane is listed, and none has been invented or folded into a spacing.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below describe each element isolated in air at its published centre thickness and vertex curvatures.
They are neither a decomposition of the full-system EFL nor a quantitative attribution of aberration correction.
Catalog names designate coordinate-compatible classes; the patent does not identify the supplier or melt.

### L1 — Front Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.62. Glass: TAF1 class (HOYA; supplier unconfirmed). f = −29.927234 mm.

The first meniscus is convex toward the object and contributes negative power to the front pair.
The patent places this pair ahead of the positive elements as part of its compact wide-field arrangement (¶0006).
The candidate TAF1 catalog Abbe number differs by +0.01; the patent value is preserved.

### L2 — Second Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.62. Glass: TAF1 class (HOYA; supplier unconfirmed). f = −25.338154 mm.

The second front meniscus has the same published glass coordinates as L1 but greater isolated negative power.
Their finite air separation matters: the pair's composite focal length is calculated from its full matrix,
rather than by adding isolated focal lengths. The ratio of their rear-surface radii is constrained by the patent.

### L3 — Positive Third Element

nd = 1.83400, νd = 37.34. Glass: NBFD10 class (HOYA; supplier unconfirmed). f = +34.184624 mm.

The actual numerical curvatures make this element biconvex, with a much weaker rear curvature than front curvature.
It follows the divergent front pair and precedes the first cemented group. Its positive power agrees with the patent's
functional description. No individual coma or spherical-aberration contribution is inferred merely from its power sign.

### L4 — Positive Component of D1

nd = 1.71300, νd = 53.94. Glass: LAC8 class (HOYA; supplier unconfirmed). f = +10.459919 mm.

L4 is the biconvex component of the first cemented doublet. Its rear face is the interface with L5,
not an air boundary in the assembled design. The patent explicitly requires this element's index to exceed 1.7.
The isolated-air focal length above therefore differs from its behavior at the cemented interface.

### L5 — Negative Component of D1

nd = 1.54814, νd = 45.82. Glass: E-FEL1 class (HOYA; supplier unconfirmed). f = −16.681878 mm.

The biconcave fifth element completes D1. The doublet retains positive net paraxial power despite this component's
negative isolated power. Its lower index and lower Abbe number distinguish it from L4, but those two coordinates alone
do not establish a measured secondary spectrum or an apochromatic correction condition.

### L6 — Positive Component of D2

nd = 1.80420, νd = 46.50. Glass: TAF3 class (HOYA; supplier unconfirmed). f = +6.979370 mm.

L6 is the strongly positive biconvex component immediately behind the stop. It begins the second cemented doublet.
The patent separately constrains its index and the Abbe-number difference between it and L7 (¶0008–0009).
These are source-described design conditions, not evidence that any one modern catalog recipe was used.

### L7 — Negative Component of D2

nd = 1.69895, νd = 30.05. Glass: E-FD15 class (HOYA; supplier unconfirmed). f = −8.865894 mm.

The negative seventh element is biconcave in the numerical example. Its rear surface is weakly curved but has a finite
positive radius; it is not silently replaced with a plane. The assembled D2 remains positive.
The difference νd(L6) − νd(L7) is 16.45, exceeding the patent's chromatic-design threshold of 12.

### L8 — Rear Aspheric Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.69350, νd = 53.34. Glass: LAC13 class (HOYA; supplier unconfirmed). f = −59.383963 mm.

The final negative meniscus is convex toward the image. Its object-side face is source surface 14, labeled 14A in the model.
Paragraph 0006 relates the rear asphere to overall aberration correction. The polynomial changes the finite-height surface
shape but does not alter the vertex-curvature first-order power, because the additional terms start at fourth order.
The inspected sources do not establish whether the production asphere was polished, molded, or made by another process.

Alternative recipes can share the same nominal coordinates: for example, TAF3 and TAF3D both match L6's pair.

The runtime resolves coordinate-compatible catalog dispersion curves from the glass annotations; catalog-derived
nC, nF and ng are not stored as if they were patent measurements. These curves serve as spectral proxies. Native patent nd and νd remain unchanged. Such proxy data permit a conditional chromatic model,
but they do not establish the actual lens's partial dispersion, secondary spectrum, or APO performance.
No anomalous-dispersion designation is assigned to the production design.

## Focus Mechanism

The optical model has status **NO_INTERNAL_RECONSTRUCTION** and contains no variable gaps.
The patent supplies the single distant-object prescription; it does not provide finite-focus spacing rows.
Cosina documents manual focusing, a 0.3 m minimum focusing distance and focus clicks at 0.5 m and 1 m,
but the inspected primary sources do not identify the internal motion law or mechanical travel.

The 0.3 m value is production metadata, not evidence that this static model reproduces that conjugate.
No internal group motion, unit-focus extension, finite-conjugate magnification or focus-breathing prediction is asserted.
The printed 14.50 mm image gap remains unchanged rather than being adjusted to remove rounding-related paraxial defocus.

## Aspherical Surface

Equation ¶0020 uses the conventional conic form with **1 + K** under the square root:

z(h) = (h²/R) / [1 + √(1 − (1 + K)(h/R)²)] + A4 h⁴ + A6 h⁶ + A8 h⁸ + A10 h¹⁰.

The model therefore retains K directly. No K − 1 conversion and no focal-length scaling are applied.
For surface 14A, Table 6 gives:

| Parameter | Value | Units |
|---|---:|---|
| K | 3.993265 | dimensionless |
| A4 | −0.195704E−03 | mm⁻³ |
| A6 | +0.178495E−05 | mm⁻⁵ |
| A8 | −0.112758E−06 | mm⁻⁷ |
| A10 | +0.900778E−09 | mm⁻⁹ |

Unused A12 and A14 are zero-valued schema padding, not extra published terms.
At the **modeled**, not published, semi-diameter of 6.3 mm, the asphere's sag is −2.284907 mm.
The vertex-matched sphere has sag −1.367365 mm, giving a departure of −0.917542 mm and an actual rim-slope angle of 54.550117°.
The mathematical conic domain ends at radial height 6.800887 mm; the modeled rim also satisfies the separate construction margin.
These finite-height quantities use the full polynomial, not only its leading coefficient.

## Numerical Model and Aperture Limits

The parsed prescription reproduces the printed EFL, BFD and front-pair focal length within their 0.01 mm source precision.
Two independently implemented first-order methods agree: sequential reduced-angle rays and matrix propagation.
The front principal plane is +16.705151 mm from the first vertex; the rear principal plane is −0.800275 mm from the final
vertex, with positive directions imageward. These reference planes are distinct from the mount reference.

The stop position is published, but all clear-aperture radii are modeled.
A radius of 2.514216 mm at the stop is calibrated from the patent f/4.57; agreement with that f-number is therefore
not independent confirmation of a physical diaphragm diameter. The calculated entrance-pupil radius is 1.674168 mm.

The modeled apertures pass sampled edge-thickness, actual-rim-slope, conic-domain, aperture-ratio and shared-gap checks.
Exact meridional Snell tracing checks the static state at 0°, ±16.515°, ±33.03°, ±44.04° and ±55.05°.
Each field uses stop fractions −1, −0.75, −0.375, 0, +0.375, +0.75 and +1.
All sampled bundles through ±44.04° and all sampled chief rays through ±55.05° are contained.
At the outermost fields, a peripheral three-quarter-radius ray clips at front-group surfaces 4/5,
and the corresponding full-radius target has no solution found on the traced branch.
Those edge limitations are retained; the published 110.1° field does not imply a wholly unvignetted pupil.

A separate audit trace sampled eleven nonnegative field angles from 0° to 55.05° in 5.505° increments,
at f/4.57, f/8 and f/22 with the same seven stop fractions. Reflection symmetry supplies the opposite meridian.
Of these 231 targets, 228 were contained. At f/4.57, the −1 stop-fraction ray at 49.545° clips at surface 7;
the −0.75 target at 55.05° clips at surfaces 4/5; the −1 target at 55.05° has no solution found on the searched branch.
All sampled rays at f/8 and f/22 were contained. No sampled aperture clipping began at a cemented junction.
These are modeled-aperture sampling results, not measurements of production illumination.

Finite meridional and radial sampling does not prove three-dimensional skew-ray clearance or every point in a continuum.
The portable silhouette and numerical checks are separate from the project's production renderer and its trim diagnostics.
No measured MTF, illumination, distortion, or finite-focus performance is inferred from these checks.
