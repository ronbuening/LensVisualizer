## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,730,017 A\
**Filed:** October 6, 1954\
**Published:** January 10, 1956\
**Inventor:** Albrecht Wilhelm Tronnier\
**Applicant:** Voigtländer AG\
**Title:** Photographic tele-objective having three lens composite front and rear members\
**Priority:** Germany, November 23, 1953\
**Example details:** Example 1 table (column 3, PDF page 3) and Fig. 1 (drawing sheet, PDF page 1); table printed at f = 100 mm and used without rescaling.

Voigtländer's German instruction booklet for the Prominent interchangeable lenses (Skoparon 35 mm, Dynaron 100 mm, Super-Dynaron 150 mm) describes the Dynaron 1:4.5/100 mm as a six-lens true tele-anastigmat with a field of about 25° and prints its section on page 3. That section and Example 1 agree on three independent points:

1. Six elements in four groups, with two cemented pairs.
2. The same layout: cemented L1/L2 and a separate negative meniscus L3 in front, a long diaphragm space with the stop slightly behind its middle, then a biconvex L4 and a cemented L5/L6 whose steep concave front faces L4.
3. Focal length and aperture: the table is printed for f = 100 mm at 1:4.5.

Example 2 of the same patent (1:4.7, six elements in five groups with three separate front lenses) fits neither the booklet section nor the marketed aperture. The patent does not state that Example 1 is the factory prescription, so production glass and rims may differ from the model.

The patent's figures are drawn for f = 300 mm while its tables refer to f = 100 mm, and the figures are schematic in diameter: Fig. 1 draws L1 smaller than the f/4.5 entrance pupil. R5 is printed as +75.934 in the table and +75.9 in claim 11. The Swiss counterpart CH 327354 corroborates the design; the later five-element US 3,215,037, which some patent databases group with this patent, is a different design and is not used.

## Optical Architecture

Six elements in four groups form a telephoto: cemented L1/L2 and separate negative L3 form the front member, followed by the broad diaphragm space, positive L4 and cemented L5/L6 as the rear member. The front member has a focal length of +70.39 mm and the rear member −85.21 mm; separated by the 25.819 mm diaphragm space (25.8% of f), they give a front-vertex-to-image length of 83.08 mm, 0.83 of the focal length. The patent's "telephoto effect", the ratio of focal length to back focus, is 2.69. All ten physical interfaces are spherical.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.58987, νd = 61.2. Glass: SK5 (Sumita), nearest catalog row. f = +27.91 mm.

The strong positive front collector provides the front member's main convergence. The patent prints its focal length as f0I = 27.910 mm, 27.9% of the system focal length, inside the claimed 22.5–47.5% range. Its interface with L2 is cemented.

### L2 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.72381, νd = 38.0. Glass: BASF8 (Sumita), nearest catalog row. f = −54.26 mm.

This high-index, lower-Abbe negative member shares the first doublet and opposes the strong front convergence; its chromatic balancing role is consistent with the paired dispersions.

### L3 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.72381, νd = 38.0. Glass: BASF8 (Sumita), nearest catalog row. f = −183.90 mm.

A separate negative meniscus, convex to the object, completes the three-element front member and bounds the large diaphragm space. With L2 it encloses the meniscus-shaped air space, convex toward the object, that the patent describes for Example 1.

### L4 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.62355, νd = 47.0. Glass: E-BAF8 (Hikari), catalog equivalent. f = +83.09 mm.

This positive biconvex element starts the rear member after the wide air gap.

### L5 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.58192, νd = 40.8. Glass: LF5 (Schott), nearest catalog row. f = −20.48 mm.

The negative component of the rear cemented pair opposes L4 and participates in the telephoto power distribution.

### L6 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.75512, νd = 27.5. Glass: SF4 (Schott), catalog equivalent. f = +44.47 mm.

This high-index rear positive element shares the final cemented interface and completes the composite rear member.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent defines its indices at the yellow helium d line and gives Abbe numbers; the data file keeps those printed values. Each element is labeled with the nearest modern catalog glass (SK5, BASF8, LF5) or a catalog equivalent (E-BAF8, SF4). These are catalog equivalents chosen from the printed nd and νd, which they match to within 0.001 in nd and 0.1 in νd; they do not identify the production melts. The front member pairs a dense crown with a dense barium flint, and the rear member pairs a barium-flint positive with a cemented light-flint / dense-flint pair. No anomalous partial dispersion is claimed.

## Focus Mechanism

The model is static. The patent tabulates only the infinity state and does not describe focusing, and the Dynaron's internal focusing motion is not published, so no focus travel is modeled; the image plane sits at the printed back focus, 83.075 mm behind the first vertex. According to the booklet, the Dynaron couples automatically to the Prominent's rangefinder once mounted (page 3), and the distance read under the camera's rangefinder knob is transferred to the lens's green meter scale to read depth of field (page 6). That scale begins at 0.9 m, the close-focus value used in the data file. The booklet also recommends the lens for portraits at close range down to about 2 m.

## Stop Position and Clear Apertures

The patent prints no clear apertures or stop position. Fig. 1 is drawn to a single scale along and across the axis, since its R1 and R8 arcs match the printed radii, but every diameter in it is about 0.69 of what f/4.5 requires, so it fixes the shapes of the elements rather than their sizes. The semi-diameters are inferred. The front rim, 12.1 mm, is 8.9% above the 11.11 mm f/4.5 entrance-pupil radius. In the front member, both drawings give L1 and L2 one square edge, and Fig. 1 chamfers the rear corners of L2 and L3. L2's rear face is clear to 11.1 mm, with a flat land out to the 12.1 mm edge; L3's rear face is clear to 10.3 mm, just outside the 10.08 mm f/4.5 marginal ray. Fig. 1 and the booklet section both end L5's concave front face at about 0.84 of the doublet's edge, so that face is clear to 8.7 mm and a flat land carries it out to the 10.4 mm rim. L4 and the rear doublet keep the booklet section's proportions, about 0.79 and 0.86 of the front rim; Fig. 1 draws both at about 0.70, which the doublet cannot match without a large loss of corner illumination. The stop is placed at 0.55 of the 25.819 mm diaphragm space measured from R5, 22.444 mm behind the first vertex, close to where Fig. 1 and the booklet section both draw it; at f/4.5 its radius is 7.52 mm.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The computed focal length is 99.993 mm and the paraxial back focus 37.198 mm, against the printed 100 mm and 37.203 mm; the 0.005 mm difference is consistent with the rounding of the three-decimal table and is not applied. Radii, thicknesses and indices are as printed.

The patent prints no field angle. The 25° full field is the booklet's figure; a 12.0° chief ray already reaches the 21.65 mm image-circle radius used for the 36 × 24 mm format. At f/4.5 the inferred rims pass the full axial beam, and geometric relative illumination falls to about 92% at 6° and 73% at 12° off axis. Those off-axis figures depend on the inferred rims and stop position, not on factory data.
