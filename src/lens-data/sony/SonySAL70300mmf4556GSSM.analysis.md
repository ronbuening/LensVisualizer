## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP 2009-168934 A  
**Filed:** 11 January 2008  
**Published:** 30 July 2009  
**Inventors:** Hisayuki Yamanaka; Dayong Li  
**Applicant:** Tamron Co., Ltd.  
**Embodiment analyzed:** Third embodiment, Tables 3 and 3-1, Figure 3.

This example is probably the design of the original Sony SAL70300G; Sony has not confirmed the prescription. Its 16 elements in 11 groups, the order of its cemented pairs and triplet, the single ED element in the front group and the rear-group focusing agree with Sony's construction diagram. The later SAL70300G2 and E-mount SEL70300G are not identified by this model. Patent applicant identity does not prove a supply relationship.

The three native focal lengths are 71.7730, 140.0942 and 291.9632 mm, with patent apertures f/4.59, f/5.60 and f/5.79. The marketed 70–300 mm f/4.5–5.6 appears only in the catalog fields. The patent labels the image plane 34 although its table ends at surface 28; the 34 matches Figure 3's own count, which numbers both faces of every element (r1–r32) and then the stop, so no surfaces are missing and none are added. Surface labels here follow the table rows. Groups and elements carry Figure 3's reference signs: groups L1–L5, and elements L11–L53, whose tens digit is the group (L11–L13 in L1, L21–L23 in L2, and so on).

## Optical Architecture

Five optical units have positive, negative, positive, positive and negative first-order powers. L1 has three elements; L2 three; L3 two; L4 five; L5 three. Four cemented assemblies include the distinctive negative-positive-negative triplet L42–L44 in L4. The stop is row 11, 2 mm before L3's first glass surface, and moves with L3. No rear cover plates are listed or added. The element focal lengths below are thick-lens values in air; for cemented elements they are not the power inside the cemented group.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The source gives d-line index and Abbe number only. The named Hoya glasses are catalog equivalents with matching nd and νd, used to model dispersion; they are not identified production glasses, and other makers list equivalents. The patent's nd and νd are used as printed. L13's ED status comes from Sony's construction diagram, not from its Abbe number alone, and no apochromatic claim is made. The source contains no aspherical surfaces for this example.

## Zoom and Focus

All three printed infinity states and all three 1.6 m states are used as printed. The 1.6 m object distances are 1424.07, 1393.16 and 1364.12 mm from the first vertex. They are not Sony's 1.2 m minimum focus. Only these three near states come from the patent.

Straight interpolation of the three zoom gap sets creates approximately 13.3 mm of intermediate paraxial defocus. This model therefore adds calculated intermediate cam stations. L1, and with it the total track, moves linearly with focal length between printed stations; L2 and L3 follow smooth one-way paths between their printed positions, L2 arriving at the middle station without overshoot; L4 and L5 are then solved so that each station has its stated focal length and a focused infinity image. This is a calculated cam, not documented factory motion. The three printed stations are unchanged.

The patent describes the zoom of its first four examples as L1, L3, L4 and L5 moving toward the object from wide to tele, with the L1–L2 gap growing and the L2–L3, L3–L4 and L4–L5 gaps shrinking; the stop travels with L3. The printed stations give, from wide to tele, L1 59.95 mm, L3 21.14 mm, L4 33.67 mm and L5 45.81 mm toward the object. L2 is not monotonic: it moves 2.15 mm toward the object from wide to middle and then 6.29 mm toward the image, ending 4.14 mm nearer the image than at wide. The calculated cam keeps those directions throughout: within each printed segment every group, and every one of the four variable gaps, moves one way only.

Fifth-group focus positions between infinity and 1.6 m are calculated with d23 + BF held constant, apart from the rounding of the printed endpoints. L5 moves toward the image by 0.73, 1.62 and 4.82 mm at wide, middle and tele, the direction of Figure 3's focus arrow, and the travel grows with focal length as the patent states. The model ends at 1.6 m. The intermediate cam and focus points reduce interpolation error; they are not production mechanics. The image plane is the patent's, not refocused for best MTF.

## Aperture and Clear Apertures

Stop radii are calculated from the patent f-numbers at each zoom station. Clear semi-diameters are measured from the wide-end panel of Figure 3 and checked against the traced axial beam: front singlet L11 25.9 mm, front doublet L12–L13 25.0 mm, L2 12 mm, L3 11.7 mm, all of L4 12.4 mm and L5 11.5 mm. Two rims stop short of the drawn outline because the curved surface meets a flat mounting flange there: the rear of L21 at 12 mm, and the strongly curved rear of L51 at 10.6 mm. L3 is drawn about 12.3 mm high, where L31 and L32 meet at the rim; the model stops at 11.7 mm, just above the axial beam, so that the two faces stay apart. These are modeling estimates rather than published dimensions.

The diagram draws its off-axis ray fan at 0.3 of the computed half-field, a central-field view; it does not imply unvignetted full-frame illumination. Radii, axial thicknesses and gaps are as printed.
