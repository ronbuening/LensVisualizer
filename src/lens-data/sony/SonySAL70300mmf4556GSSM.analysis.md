## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP 2009-168934 A  
**Filed:** 11 January 2008  
**Published:** 30 July 2009  
**Inventors:** Hisayuki Yamanaka; Dayong Li  
**Applicant:** Tamron Co., Ltd.  
**Embodiment analyzed:** Third embodiment, Tables 3 and 3-1, Figure 3.

This example is probably the design of the original Sony SAL70300G; Sony has not confirmed the prescription. Its 16 elements in 11 groups, the order of its cemented pairs and triplet, the single ED element in the front group and the rear-group focusing agree with Sony's construction diagram. The later SAL70300G2 and E-mount SEL70300G are not identified by this model. Patent applicant identity does not prove a supply relationship.

The three native focal lengths are 71.7730, 140.0942 and 291.9632 mm, with patent apertures f/4.59, f/5.60 and f/5.79. The marketed 70–300 mm f/4.5–5.6 appears only in the catalog fields. The patent labels the image plane 34 although its table ends at surface 28; no missing surfaces are added.

## Optical Architecture

Five optical units have positive, negative, positive, positive and negative first-order powers. G1 has three elements; G2 three; G3 two; G4 five; G5 three. Four cemented assemblies include the distinctive negative-positive-negative triplet in G4. The stop is row 11, 2 mm before G3's first glass surface. No rear cover plates are listed or added. The element focal lengths below are thick-lens values in air; for cemented elements they are not the power inside the cemented group.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The source gives d-line index and Abbe number only. The named Hoya glasses are catalog equivalents with matching nd and νd, used to model dispersion; they are not identified production glasses, and other makers list equivalents. The patent's nd and νd are used as printed. L3's ED status comes from Sony's construction diagram, not from its Abbe number alone, and no apochromatic claim is made. The source contains no aspherical surfaces for this example.

## Zoom and Focus

All three printed infinity states and all three 1.6 m states are used as printed. The 1.6 m object distances are 1424.07, 1393.16 and 1364.12 mm from the first vertex. They are not Sony's 1.2 m minimum focus. Only these three near states come from the patent.

Straight interpolation of the three zoom gap sets creates approximately 13.3 mm of intermediate paraxial defocus. This model therefore adds calculated intermediate cam stations. G2 and G4 positions are solved jointly to reproduce the interpolated focal length and image plane, while G1, G3, G5 and total track follow their source-station interpolation. The maximum G2 correction is approximately 3.208 mm. This is a calculated cam, not documented factory motion. The three printed stations are unchanged.

Fifth-group focus positions between infinity and 1.6 m are calculated with d23 + BF held constant, apart from the rounding of the printed endpoints. G5 moves toward the image by 0.73, 1.62 and 4.82 mm at wide, middle and tele. The model ends at 1.6 m. The intermediate cam and focus points reduce interpolation error; they are not production mechanics. The image plane is the patent's, not refocused for best MTF.

## Aperture and Clear Apertures

Stop radii are calculated from the patent f-numbers at each zoom station. Clear semi-diameters are measured from the wide-end panel of Figure 3 and checked against the traced axial beam: front singlet 25.9 mm, front doublet 25.0 mm, G2 12 mm, G3 11.6 mm, all of G4 12.4 mm and G5 11.5 mm. Two rims stop short of the drawn outline because the curved surface meets a flat mounting flange there: the rear of the first G2 element at 12 mm, and the strongly curved rear of the first G5 element at 10.6 mm. These are modeling estimates rather than published dimensions.

The diagram draws its off-axis ray fan at 0.3 of the computed half-field, a central-field view; it does not imply unvignetted full-frame illumination. Radii, axial thicknesses and gaps are as printed.
