## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** RU 2726264 C1
**Application Number:** 2019143982
**Filed:** 26 December 2019
**Published:** 10 July 2020 (Bulletin No. 19)
**Inventors:** Vladimir Aleksandrovich Bogdankov, Ilya Anatolevich Levin
**Assignee:** PJSC Krasnogorsky Zavod named after S. A. Zverev (printed as PAO "Krasnogorskij zavod im. S.A. Zvereva", PAO KMZ)
**Title:** Сверхсветосильный фотографический объектив (ultra-fast photographic lens)
**Example details:** Table 1, printed pp. 7–8; lens section Fig. 1 and polychromatic MTF Fig. 2, p. 10. The table is printed at f′ = 52 mm and is used without scaling.

The patent describes one worked example: a lens for mirrorless cameras with f′ = 52 mm, relative aperture 1:0.95 and image field 2y′ = 43 mm. KMZ sold the Zenitar 50mm f/0.95 for Sony E-mount full-frame cameras from 20 March 2019. The association with that lens is probable rather than certain:

1. The patent's nine elements in eight groups match the maker's published construction. The example is all spherical, with one cemented doublet.
2. The patent states 1:0.95 and the lens is sold as f/0.95. The patent's 52 mm is within the usual rounding of a lens marketed as 50 mm.
3. A 43 mm image field matches the 43.3 mm diagonal of the 135 full frame, and the patent names mirrorless cameras as the application. The production lens is made only in the Sony E mount for full-frame bodies.
4. The patent holder is KMZ, the maker of Zenit lenses, and Sony Alpha Rumors' announcement report credits the optical scheme to V. A. Bogdankov, the first-named inventor.
5. The application was filed on 26 December 2019, nine months after the lens went on sale. The example may therefore record a revision of the production design rather than the first production prescription. No other KMZ patent or example for this lens has been found, and no maker optical section is available for comparison.

The maker and the announcement reports also give f/0.95–16, a 14-blade iris, a minimum focus distance of 0.7 m and a 72 mm filter thread.

## Optical Architecture

The patent divides the lens into three positive components, all fixed: component I (L1–L3) ahead of the stop, and components II (L4–L6) and III (L7–L9) behind it. Their in-air focal lengths are +113.1 mm, +114.9 mm and +229.0 mm. Nine elements in eight air-separated groups form the system; L4 and L5 are the only cemented pair.

| Component | Elements | Construction | f (mm) |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| I | L1–L3 | plano-concave, biconvex, positive meniscus | +113.1 |
| (stop) | | aperture diaphragm, 14.9 mm behind L3 | |
| II | L4–L6 | cemented negative meniscus doublet, plano-convex singlet | +114.9 |
| III | L7–L9 | plano-convex, positive meniscus, plano-concave | +229.0 |

The patent defines the design against CN107219609, a ten-element f/0.95, 50 mm lens with three cemented groups. That design opens with a Gauss-type front of a positive lens and positive and negative menisci convex to the object. RU 2726264 moves the negative lens to the very front as a plano-concave element, keeps a negative cemented meniscus concave to the stop behind the diaphragm, and replaces the two cemented pairs of the earlier rear component with three singlets. The patent's stated aim is manufacturability: the number of lenses in cemented groups falls from six to two, and four elements (L1, L6, L7 and L9) have one plane face each, at the same aperture and image field.

The first vertex is 104.3 mm ahead of the last surface and 128.0 mm ahead of the image plane. The printed back focus is 23.7 mm. The largest clear diameter, 59.0 mm, is behind the stop at L5's rear face and L6, wider than the 53.2–54.3 mm front group.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Focal lengths below are computed in air from each element's printed radii, centre thickness and index. They are not in-situ powers inside the cemented pair.

### Element 1 — Plano-Concave

nd = 1.5750, νd = 41.29. Glass: QF3 (CDGM) coordinate equivalent, a light flint; the pair is the d-line value of the Russian light flint LF5 (inferred). f = −137.83 mm.

The front element is plane toward the object, with a concave rear face of R = 79.25 mm. The patent states that moving the negative lens ahead of the positive pair made it possible to correct the aberrations of the wide beams, and that the negative distortion this leaves is spread evenly over the lenses of components II and III.

### Element 2 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.7441, νd = 50.42. Glass: Unmatched; the pair is the d-line value of the Russian extra-dense crown STK19 (inferred), a lanthanum glass the glass catalog does not carry. f = +113.45 mm.

The first collector of component I. Its rear face (R = −288.4 mm) is weak, and it is separated from L3 by a 0.3 mm air space. L6 and L7 use the same glass; claim 2 sets n2 = n6 = n7 and ν2 = ν6 = ν7.

### Element 3 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.6595, νd = 57.35. Glass: STK3 (LZOS); the pair is the d-line value of the Russian extra-dense crown STK3 (inferred). f = +148.12 mm.

The positive meniscus, convex to the object, closes component I. Its rear face is 14.9 mm ahead of the stop. L8 repeats this glass (claim 2: n3 = n8).

### Element 4 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.7552, νd = 27.52. Glass: TF5 (LZOS), an SF4-class dense flint; the pair is the d-line value of the Russian dense flint TF5 (inferred), traced on the LZOS catalogue curve. f = −47.13 mm.

The first element behind the stop, 11.3 mm from it, presents a strongly curved concave face (R = −32.21 mm) to the diaphragm. That face carries the second-largest negative Petzval term in the lens, −0.0134 mm⁻¹ in the model.

### Element 5 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.6131, νd = 60.61. Glass: TK14 (LZOS); the pair is the d-line value of the Russian dense crown TK14 (inferred). f = +81.88 mm.

At 19.5 mm, L5 is the thickest element. It is cemented to L4 on a nearly flat junction (R = −361.4 mm), and the pair has a net in-air focal length of −213.15 mm, the negative two-element meniscus of the claims. The pair joins a dense flint to a crown about 33 Abbe units apart. The patent states that the axial colour introduced by the plano-concave lens of component III is corrected by this single cemented group near the stop.

### Element 6 — Plano-Convex

nd = 1.7441, νd = 50.42. Glass: Unmatched (as L2). f = +102.42 mm.

The plane front faces the object; the convex rear (R = −76.21 mm) closes component II. With L5's rear face it carries the 59.0 mm maximum clear diameter.

### Element 7 — Plano-Convex

nd = 1.7441, νd = 50.42. Glass: Unmatched (as L2). f = +95.14 mm.

Convex toward the object (R = 70.79 mm) with a plane rear face, L7 opens component III. The claims describe it as moved ahead of the positive meniscus.

### Element 8 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.6595, νd = 57.35. Glass: STK3 (LZOS), as L3. f = +72.43 mm.

The meniscus, convex to the object, is the strongest positive element in the lens. The patent attributes the correction of the aberrations of narrow oblique beams, that is astigmatism and field curvature, to replacing the earlier design's two cemented groups with the three singlets L7–L9.

### Element 9 — Plano-Concave

nd = 1.6895, νd = 31.13. Glass: PBM28 (OHARA), which lends its dispersion curve; the pair is the d-line value of the Russian dense flint TF8 (inferred). f = −36.85 mm.

The last element is plane toward the object, with a concave rear face of R = 25.41 mm that is 23.7 mm from the image. That face carries the largest negative Petzval term in the model, −0.0161 mm⁻¹, and acts as the field flattener; this role is inferred from the prescription, not stated by the patent. The patent names L9 as the source of the axial colour that the L4–L5 doublet corrects.

Table 1 heads its columns n and ν without naming a spectral line, and the patent names no glass types or supplier. Every printed pair is the d-line value of a Russian GOST optical glass as GOST 13659-78 tabulates it: each nd agrees to the printed four decimals and each νd within 0.03. At the e line those glasses read 0.0024–0.0065 higher, and no pair matches any catalog glass there, so the values are traced as nd and νd. The identification rests on that agreement alone and says nothing about the production melts.

L3, L4, L5 and L8 trace on the glass catalog's LZOS STK3, TF5 and TK14 rows, fits to the LZOS catalogue's own index tables. QF3 equals LF5 at the d line. PBM28 (0.0006 lower in nd, 0.05 lower in νd than TF8) lends its dispersion curve while the trace keeps the printed index. STK19 is traced with Abbe-number dispersion. The STK3 crown also appears in the Zenitar 35mm f/1 patent. Every element satisfies the index and dispersion ranges of claim 2.

Only six glasses serve nine elements. The three positive elements L2, L6 and L7 share one lanthanum glass, and L3 and L8 share another. The two dense flints sit where the negative power is concentrated: L4 next to the stop and L9 in front of the image.

## Aperture and Stop

Table 1 prints the aperture diaphragm as its own row, АД, 14.9 mm behind R6 and 11.3 mm ahead of R7, with a diameter of 46.2 mm. The stop takes a row in the model, so its surface labels 8–18 carry the patent's R7–R17.

At the paraxial level the printed 46.2 mm diameter gives f/0.952. The model derives the stop from the stated f/0.95 instead, a 46.29 mm diameter, and the paraxial entrance pupil is then 54.77 mm wide and 32.70 mm behind the first vertex. Pupil aberration is large at this aperture. A real ray entering at the paraxial pupil edge would cross the stop plane 23.87 mm from the axis, outside the 23.15 mm stop radius, so the stop sets the wide-open beam.

The printed clear diameters are real-ray apertures. A real axial ray that just fills the stop meets surfaces 1–9 within 0.05 mm of half the printed diameter, and surfaces 10–15 within 0.2 mm. The three rear surfaces are larger than the axial beam needs, consistent with sizing for the oblique beam. The model's semi-diameters are the printed diameters halved. At f/0.95 surface 8 trims about 0.05 mm from the edge of the axial beam; no other rim touches it.

Fig. 1 draws L4, L7, L8 and L9 as square-edged blocks wider than these clear apertures: L4's front face ends in a flat land out to a 53.0 mm edge, and L9's rear face in a land out to 36.5 mm. The diagram draws these edges, and they clip no rays. The figure also carries L5's front face out to the full 59.0 mm; the diagram, which gives a cemented face one diameter, stops it at the 53.0 mm junction with L4.

Longitudinal spherical aberration in the model is small: −0.023, −0.034, −0.037 and −0.035 mm at 0.5, 0.7, 0.85 and 1.0 of the paraxial pupil radius, relative to the paraxial focus.

## Field and Image

The patent's 2y′ = 43 mm field puts the image corner at 21.5 mm. In the model the chief ray reaching that height leaves the object side at 21.88°, a 43.8° full field, which fits the maker's 44° angle of view.

The final spacing is the printed d18 = 23.7 mm. The paraxial back focus is 23.722 mm, but the patent places the image in the plane of best setting, and real rays at pupil zones 0.5–0.85 cross the axis 23.685–23.699 mm behind the last surface. The printed plane is kept.

The Petzval sum is +0.0033 mm⁻¹, a Petzval radius of about 306 mm or 5.9 f′. The concave faces of L9 and L4 supply most of the negative contribution that keeps it this low.

Traced chief rays give a small barrel distortion of about −0.1 % at 5–10°, crossing zero near 14°, then pincushion rising to about +1.0 % at 19° and +2.9 % at the 21.5 mm corner. These are model figures at infinity, not measurements. The patent says the negative distortion left by component I is suppressed by the lenses of components II and III, and it prints no distortion figures.

At f/0.95 the corner beam is cut mainly by the front rims and the stop, as in other lenses of this speed; the model keeps that mechanical vignetting. The paraxial exit pupil is about 86 mm in front of the image.

Fig. 2 of the patent gives the polychromatic MTF at 1:0.95: at 30 mm⁻¹, contrast exceeds 48 % at the centre and 20 % over the field from y′ = 0 to 19 mm.

## Focus

The production lens is focused manually to 0.7 m. The patent prints only the infinity prescription and names no moving group, so the model is fixed at infinity. The focus slider has no optical effect, and the maker's 0.7 m is recorded as the marketed minimum focus distance.

## Model Scope and Limitations

- The radii, spacings, indices, Abbe numbers, stop position and clear diameters are those of Table 1 at f′ = 52 mm. No value is rescaled to the marketed 50 mm.
- The stop radius is derived from f/0.95, about 0.05 mm larger in radius than the printed 46.2 mm diameter implies.
- Close focus is not modeled, and infinity results do not describe the lens at 0.7 m.
- Five elements borrow the dispersion curves of catalog rows that stand in for their Russian glasses; L2, L5, L6 and L7 are traced with Abbe-number dispersion. Neither represents the production melts, so colour results are estimates.
- The example was filed after the lens went on sale. It is the only published prescription linked to the lens, but it may differ from early production.
