# COOKE 100mm f/2 (US 2,319,171 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,319,171 A, _Optical objective_](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2319171A/en), by Arthur Warmisham and Charles Gorrie Wynne, assigned to Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited, Leicester, England. British priority is 2 December 1940; the US application was filed 15 December 1941 and published 11 May 1943. The patent contains seven numerical examples.

Example II is shown in Figure 2 on PDF page 1. Its numerical table and material descriptions appear on PDF page 4, printed page 2; the table is repeated in claim 16 on PDF page 7, printed page 5. Claim 12 also identifies potassium bromide in the front divergent element. No product source documents this exact prescription.

## Transcription Check

The page images agree with all ten signed radii, six element thicknesses, three air separations, six nD/Abbe pairs and six relative partial-dispersion values in the Example II table and claim 16. Dimensions retain the printed normalized focal length of 1.000, multiplied by 100 for the nominal 100 mm design. R4 and R7 are planar cemented interfaces. There are no aspherical surfaces or variable spacings.

Element 3 is potassium bromide crystal, element 4 dense flint, and elements 1, 2, 5 and 6 dense barium flint. All indices retain the printed D-line reference, taken as sodium at 589.3 nm. The printed relative partial dispersions, in element order, are 1.02, 1.02, 1.00, 1.07, 1.02 and 1.02.

Figure 2 labels S2 as .1691, whereas the Example II table and claim 16 both print .1591. At the 100 mm scale, the table gives EFL 100.00 mm, compared with about 100.47 mm for the drawing's S2 value. Figure 2 also labels S3 as .0060, whereas both tables print .0050. The model follows the two numerical tables, retaining 15.91 and 0.50 mm respectively. No numerical-table value is corrected, so no source erratum is recorded.

## First-Order Check

The printed normalized focal length 1.000 corresponds to nominal 100 mm. Calculated EFL is 99.998 mm and the paraxial aperture is f/2.00, matching the printed f/2.0. Back focal distance is 73.905 mm; the patent prints no back focus. The image plane is at that paraxial back focus behind R10, 134.845 mm behind the first vertex. The first-to-last surface distance is 60.94 mm.

The inferred iris radius is 18.063 mm. Exact axial tracing gives an entrance-pupil equivalent near f/1.99. The iris sets the axial aperture, and the surfaces stay clear of each other. The narrowest material edge thickness is about 2.33 mm; the largest shared-radius gap sag is 80.8% of the axial separation.

The full axial opening and the 10.0° chief ray clear the rims. Off-axis losses at the front singlet and front cemented doublet remain physical vignetting; the largest one-sided clipping estimate at 10.0° is about 14% at R1. This is not an area-weighted transmission value. Geometric D-line MTF describes only the inferred infinity arrangement; measured and broadband performance remain unestablished. No image format or sourced field is available to establish image-circle coverage.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The stop position and bore, all clear semi-diameters, back focus and illustrative ±10.0° field are inferred or computed. The stop divides the printed 15.91 mm central air space into 8.80 and 7.11 mm. Its plane lies 31.46 mm behind the first vertex, between the R5 and R6 rims at about 29.80 and 32.66 mm.

`focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. The patent supplies no finite-focus distances, motion or production focusing mechanism. The f/2 to f/22 aperture range is a model choice; only the maximum relative aperture is printed.

The relative partial-dispersion convention is (n_e − n_g) / (n_C − n_F). Those ratios do not determine complete spectral curves or C-, F- and g-line indices. Approximate evaluation of the patent's two secondary-spectrum conditions gives sums of about 0.004 against individual terms of about 0.03; these are calculated values, not printed sums. Abbe-number estimates do not establish that secondary-spectrum balance. No supplier, historical melt, catalogue dispersion curve or anomalous-dispersion designation is assigned.

## Product Identification

No production lens is documented for this prescription. The Cooke attribution follows the assignee, Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited, maker of Cooke lenses; the patent identifies no retail product. It does not establish that this exact objective was manufactured.

No marketed focal length, marketed aperture, marketed minimum focus distance, lens mount, image format, diaphragm blade count or blade shape is assigned. The six-element, four-group construction and design f/2 come from Example II; the 100 mm design scale is chosen from its normalized focal length. No image circle is assigned because the patent prints no field or format.
