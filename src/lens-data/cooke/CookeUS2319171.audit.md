# COOKE 100mm f/2 (US 2,319,171 Design) — Audit

## Source

US 2,319,171 A, Example II and Figure 2, by Arthur Warmisham and Charles Gorrie Wynne, assigned to Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited. British priority is 2 December 1940; the US application was filed 15 December 1941 and published 11 May 1943. The patent contains seven numerical examples.

## Transcription

Two independent readings of the page images agree with all ten signed radii, six element thicknesses, three air separations, six nD/Abbe pairs and six relative partial-dispersion values in the Example II table and claim 16. R4 and R7 are planar cemented interfaces. Element 3 is potassium bromide crystal, element 4 dense flint, and elements 1, 2, 5 and 6 dense barium flint. All indices retain the printed D-line reference.

## First-Order Results

The printed normalized focal length 1.000 corresponds to nominal 100 mm in this model. Computed EFL is 99.997844068 mm, paraxial aperture f/2.000, back focal distance 73.905063648 mm and first-to-last surface distance 60.94 mm. The stored image gap is that paraxial back focus; total first-vertex-to-image distance is 134.845063648 mm. The inferred iris radius is 18.063391716 mm; exact axial tracing gives an entrance-pupil equivalent near f/1.989.

Geometry checks found positive material and air clearances with the inferred rims. The narrowest material closure was about 0.416 mm; the largest shared-radius gap-sag fraction was about 0.808, below the model's 0.9 setting. Off-axis losses at the front surface and the two planar cemented interfaces remain physical vignetting. Geometric D-line MTF describes only the inferred infinity arrangement; measured and broadband performance remain unestablished.

## Values Not Printed

The stop position and bore, all clear semi-diameters, back focus and modelled ±10° field are inferred or computed. The stop divides the unchanged 15.91 mm central air space into 8.80 and 7.11 mm. The patent supplies no image format, lens mount, finite-focus state or production focusing mechanism. Its relative partial dispersions are documented rather than converted into unsupported spectral coefficients.

## Product Association

No production lens is documented for this prescription. The Cooke maker attribution follows the patent's Taylor, Taylor & Hobson assignee; no retail model is assigned.

## Corrections to Drawing Labels

Figure 2 labels S2 as .1691, whereas the Example II table and claim 16 both print .1591. The table gives normalized EFL 0.99998, compared with about 1.0047 for the drawing value. Figure 2 also labels S3 as .0060, whereas both tables print .0050. The model follows both numerical tables, retaining 15.91 and 0.50 mm respectively. Neither spacing changed in this pass.

No table value is corrected, so no source erratum is recorded.

## Remaining Questions

Open: Figure 2's drawn semi-diameters, read at about ±1 mm precision, differ from the inferred rims. Element 1 is about 26 mm in the drawing versus 29 mm in the model; the doublets are roughly square at 23.4 mm, compared with R3 = 26, R4 = 23, R7 = 20 and R8 = 22 mm. R5/R6 are about 19.5 mm versus 18.6/18.4 mm; element 6 is about 26 mm versus 23 mm. The delivered rims remain unchanged because the axial opening and modelled chief rays clear them. Their relationship to the drawing remains unresolved.

Open: The printed relative partial dispersions do not supply a complete spectral curve for potassium bromide or identify the historical glass melts. No product, physical format, stated field or close-focus behavior is documented.
