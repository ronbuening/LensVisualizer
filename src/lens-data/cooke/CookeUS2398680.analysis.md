# COOKE 100mm f/1.5 (US 2,398,680 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,398,680 A, “Optical Objective”

**Inventors:** Arthur Warmisham

**Assignee:** Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited

**Filed:** 21 September 1944

**Published:** 16 April 1946

**Priority:** Great Britain, 6 July 1943

**Example details:** Example I, illustrated in Figure 1; prescription on PDF page 4 and repeated in claim 15 on PDF page 6.

The patent gives five numerical examples, I–V, for photographic, projection or similar uses. Example I uses glass throughout; several later examples use magnesium oxide crystal. No commercial product is documented for this prescription. The Cooke name follows Taylor, Taylor & Hobson's manufacture of Cooke lenses. [Taylor Hobson history](https://www.taylor-hobson.com/aboutus/history).

All dimensional prescription values are multiplied by 100 from the patent's focal-length normalization of 1.000, giving a nominal 100 mm objective in millimetres. The signed radii, glass thicknesses, airspaces, sodium-D indices and Abbe numbers follow Example I without adjustment. [Original patent](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/2398680).

## Optical Architecture

Seven elements form five groups: a positive front singlet, two cemented doublets with their concave air surfaces facing one another, and two separate positive rear singlets. Elements 2 and 3 share R4 in C1; elements 4 and 5 share R7 in C2. The three narrow airspaces are each 0.2 mm.

The front cemented pair has an in-air focal length of −278.93 mm. The rear cemented pair is nearly neutral, with an in-air focal length of about −94,600 mm; its individual elements have substantial opposing powers. The patent emphasizes the high index of element 5 and the distribution of positive power between the final two singlets. Both rear singlets have their shallower surfaces facing forward in Example I.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below describe each element by itself in air.

### Element 1 — Positive Meniscus

Sodium-D index 1.6135; Abbe number 59.3; in-air focal length +150.49 mm. The front positive collector has a strongly convex entrance surface and a much shallower rear surface. Its axial thickness is 10.86 mm, with estimated front/rear semi-diameters of 37/37 mm.

### Element 2 — Positive Meniscus

Sodium-D index 1.6431; Abbe number 48.3; in-air focal length +66.70 mm. This is the positive front member of C1, cemented to element 3 at R4. Its axial thickness is 16.93 mm and both estimated semi-diameters are 30.4 mm.

### Element 3 — Negative Meniscus

Sodium-D index 1.67605; Abbe number 32.3; in-air focal length −43.24 mm. The more dispersive member of C1 has its strongly concave rear surface facing the central stop space. Its axial thickness is 3.13 mm and its estimated semi-diameters are 30.4/21.1 mm.

### Element 4 — Biconcave Negative

Sodium-D index 1.621; Abbe number 36.2; glass S-TIM2 (Ohara), spectral proxy; in-air focal length −44.37 mm. This dispersive front member of C2 faces the concave surface of element 3 across the central airspace. Its axial thickness is 4.14 mm and its estimated semi-diameters are 20.6/24.3 mm.

### Element 5 — Biconvex Positive

Sodium-D index 1.7445; Abbe number 45.8; in-air focal length +52.23 mm. The high-index positive member of C2 offsets element 4's negative power. The patent specifies an index between 1.70 and 1.80 for this position and states that Example I uses optical glass here. Its axial thickness is 14.09 mm and both estimated semi-diameters are 24.3 mm.

### Element 6 — Biconvex Positive

Sodium-D index 1.613; Abbe number 59.3; in-air focal length +196.87 mm. This is the first of the two positive rear collectors. Its axial thickness is 5.76 mm and both estimated semi-diameters are 24.3 mm.

### Element 7 — Biconvex Positive

Sodium-D index 1.6135; Abbe number 59.3; in-air focal length +237.57 mm. The patent identifies this final collector as dense barium crown in Examples I and II (PDF page 5). Its axial thickness is 4.95 mm and both estimated semi-diameters are 24.3 mm. Its index differs from element 6's printed 1.613 value.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent explicitly specifies indices for the sodium D line, about 589.3 nm, and gives an Abbe number for each element. These are distinct from helium-d indices at about 587.6 nm.

Element 4 uses S-TIM2 (Ohara) as a spectral proxy, with Δn = −0.00111 and Δν = +0.05 (catalog minus printed, at sodium D). The model uses the printed index and Abbe number and takes the dispersion shape from the catalog curve. Elements 1–3 and 5–7 have no catalog row within the site's spectral-proxy tolerance of 0.002 in index and 0.25 in Abbe number at sodium D. The patent gives the glass family for element 7. The supplier and melt remain unknown for every element.

No individual C-, F- or g-line indices or partial-dispersion values are supplied. Colour calculations use Abbe-number estimates for the six unmatched elements and a catalog curve for element 4; they do not establish measured broadband performance or apochromatic correction.

## Focus Mechanism

Only infinity focus is modeled. The patent gives no close-focus data or mechanical focusing law.

The iris is neither drawn nor dimensioned. Its inferred station is 42.22 mm from the first vertex: 11.10 mm behind R5 and 8.96 mm ahead of R6, preserving the printed 20.06 mm airspace. The paraxial f/1.5 stop radius is 20.724 mm, or diameter 41.448 mm. An exact axial ray entering at the nominal marginal height reaches a slightly smaller radius, 20.651 mm, at that station. The inferred opening depends on the aperture definition.

Increasing the estimated iris radius by 2.0% introduces axial rim clipping. A small change in the assumed stop position also changes off-axis illumination without changing the prescription.

## Aspherical Surfaces

None. All twelve optical boundaries are spherical.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The 10.0° half-field is illustrative; the patent states neither a numerical field nor a format. The chief ray reaches about 17.62 mm image height at 10.0°, but that height does not establish an image circle. Chief rays reach the image throughout this field, while marginal rays are partly vignetted, particularly at R7 and the rear surfaces. Off-axis illumination depends on the inferred rims and stop.

The clear semi-diameters are estimates informed by Figure 1, whose vertex spacings and spherical arcs show it is drawn approximately to scale both along and across the axis. The front singlet has a 37 mm rim, C1 a 30.4 mm outer rim, and C2 and both rear singlets 24.3 mm outer rims. The cemented pairs taper towards the iris to 21.1 mm at R5 and 20.6 mm at R6. Straight edge joins approximate the drawn chamfers and their shoulders. The front rim exceeds the drawing's approximately 34.2 mm to preserve edge light. R5 exceeds its approximately 20.4 mm drawn endpoint because the f/1.5 axial ray reaches 20.90 mm there. The outer rear rims lie close to the drawing's approximately 23.6–23.7 mm while allowing 87.1% geometric transmission at the illustrative 10.0° field edge. The blanks have positive edge thicknesses and air clearances, and the iris sets the axial aperture at f/1.5. Actual manufacturing dimensions and iris geometry are unknown.
