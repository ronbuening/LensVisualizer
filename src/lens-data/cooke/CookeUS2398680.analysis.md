# COOKE 100mm f/1.5 (US 2,398,680 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,398,680 A, “Optical Objective”

**Inventors:** Arthur Warmisham

**Assignee:** Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited

**Filed:** 21 September 1944

**Published:** 16 April 1946

**Priority:** Great Britain, 6 July 1943

**Example details:** Example I, illustrated in Figure 1; prescription on PDF page 4 and repeated in claim 15 on PDF page 6.

The patent gives five numerical examples, I–V, for photographic, projection or similar uses. Example I uses glass throughout; several later examples use magnesium oxide crystal. No commercial product is documented for this prescription. The Cooke name identifies the maker associated with Taylor, Taylor & Hobson, rather than a demonstrated production model.

All dimensional prescription values are multiplied by 100 from the patent's focal-length normalization of 1.000, giving a nominal 100 mm objective in millimetres. The signed radii, glass thicknesses, airspaces, sodium-D indices and Abbe numbers follow Example I without adjustment. [Original patent](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/2398680).

## Optical Architecture

Seven elements form five groups: a positive front singlet, two cemented doublets with their concave air surfaces facing one another, and two separate positive rear singlets. Elements 2 and 3 share R4 in C1; elements 4 and 5 share R7 in C2. The three narrow airspaces are each 0.2 mm.

The front cemented pair has an in-air focal length of −278.93 mm. The rear cemented pair is nearly neutral, with an in-air focal length of about −94,558 mm; its individual elements have substantial opposing powers. The patent emphasizes the high index of element 5 and the distribution of positive power between the final two singlets. Both rear singlets have their shallower surfaces facing forward in Example I.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below describe each element by itself in air, rather than its separate contribution inside a cemented assembly. Every glass remains unmatched to a catalog identity.

### Element 1 — Positive Meniscus

Sodium-D index 1.6135; Abbe number 59.3; in-air focal length +150.49 mm. The front positive collector has a strongly convex entrance surface and a much shallower rear surface. Its axial thickness is 10.86 mm, with estimated front/rear semi-diameters of 37/37 mm.

### Element 2 — Positive Meniscus

Sodium-D index 1.6431; Abbe number 48.3; in-air focal length +66.70 mm. This is the positive front member of C1, cemented to element 3 at R4. Its axial thickness is 16.93 mm and its estimated semi-diameters are 33/32 mm.

### Element 3 — Negative Meniscus

Sodium-D index 1.67605; Abbe number 32.3; in-air focal length −43.24 mm. The more dispersive member of C1 has its strongly concave rear surface facing the central stop space. Its axial thickness is 3.13 mm and its estimated semi-diameters are 32/21.1 mm.

### Element 4 — Biconcave Negative

Sodium-D index 1.621; Abbe number 36.2; in-air focal length −44.37 mm. This dispersive front member of C2 faces the concave surface of element 3 across the central airspace. Its axial thickness is 4.14 mm and its estimated semi-diameters are 22/23 mm.

### Element 5 — Biconvex Positive

Sodium-D index 1.7445; Abbe number 45.8; in-air focal length +52.23 mm. The high-index positive member of C2 offsets element 4's negative power. The patent specifies an index between 1.70 and 1.80 for this position and states that Example I uses optical glass here. Its axial thickness is 14.09 mm and its estimated semi-diameters are 23/29 mm.

### Element 6 — Biconvex Positive

Sodium-D index 1.613; Abbe number 59.3; in-air focal length +196.87 mm. This is the first of the two positive rear collectors. Its axial thickness is 5.76 mm and its estimated semi-diameters are 28/26 mm.

### Element 7 — Biconvex Positive

Sodium-D index 1.6135; Abbe number 59.3; in-air focal length +237.57 mm. The final collector supplies the rear-component power discussed in the patent. Its axial thickness is 4.95 mm and both estimated semi-diameters are 26 mm. Its index differs from element 6's printed 1.613 value.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent explicitly specifies indices for the sodium D line, about 589.3 nm, and gives an Abbe number for each element. These are distinct from helium-d indices at about 587.6 nm. A catalog comparison at the printed line found no row meeting both the index tolerance of 0.0001 and the Abbe tolerance of 0.15. The glass labels therefore retain the printed pairs as “Unmatched”; historical suppliers and melts remain unknown.

No individual C-, F- or g-line indices or partial-dispersion values are supplied. Chromatic calculations based only on the Abbe numbers are estimates and do not establish measured broadband performance or apochromatic correction.

## Focus Mechanism

Only infinity focus is modeled. The patent gives no close-focus data or mechanical focusing law.

The iris is neither drawn nor dimensioned. Its inferred station is 42.22 mm from the first vertex: 11.10 mm behind R5 and 8.96 mm ahead of R6, preserving the printed 20.06 mm airspace. Setting the paraxial aperture to f/1.5 gives a stop radius of 20.723960 mm, or diameter 41.447919 mm. An exact axial ray entering at the nominal marginal height reaches a slightly smaller radius, 20.651008 mm, at that station. These are different ways of defining an inferred opening, rather than measured iris dimensions.

Increasing the estimated iris radius by 2% introduces axial rim clipping. A small change in the assumed stop position also changes off-axis illumination without changing the prescription.

## Aspherical Surfaces

None. All twelve optical boundaries are spherical.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The 10° half-field is illustrative; the patent states neither a numerical field nor a format. The chief ray reaches about 17.6172 mm image height at 10°, but that height does not establish an image circle. Chief rays reach the image throughout the selected field, while marginal rays are partly vignetted, particularly at R7 and the rear surfaces. Off-axis illumination depends on the inferred rims and stop.

All clear semi-diameters remain estimates. Several exceed the rims drawn in Figure 1, and narrowing them to the drawing reduces off-axis illumination. The current geometry has positive glass edge thicknesses and air clearances, with no axial rim limit at the modeled aperture. These are properties of the estimated blanks, rather than manufacturing dimensions.
