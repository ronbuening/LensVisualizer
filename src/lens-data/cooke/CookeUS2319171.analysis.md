# COOKE 100mm f/2 (US 2,319,171 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,319,171 A

**Inventors:** Arthur Warmisham and Charles Gorrie Wynne

**Assignee:** Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited

**Published:** 11 May 1943

**Filed:** 15 December 1941; British priority 2 December 1940

**Example details:** Example II, Figure 2; numerical table on PDF page 4, repeated in claim 16 on PDF page 7.

The patent gives seven numerical examples. Example II is a six-element f/2 objective using potassium bromide crystal in the front divergent component. Its dimensions are normalized to f = 1.000; multiplying all lengths by 100 gives the nominal 100 mm design shown here. No production lens is documented for this prescription.

The numerical table and claim 16 agree throughout. Figure 2 labels the central separation S2 as .1691, while both tables give .1591. The table value gives EFL 100.00 mm at this scale; the drawing value would give about 100.47 mm. The drawing also labels S3 as .0060, while both tables give .0050. The model follows the numerical tables, retaining central and rear air spaces of 15.91 and 0.50 mm respectively.

## Optical Architecture

The four groups follow a double-Gauss arrangement: a positive front singlet, two weakly negative cemented doublets with their concave air surfaces facing one another, and a positive rear singlet. Elements 2–3 meet at the planar R4 interface; elements 4–5 meet at planar R7. All refracting surfaces are spherical or planar.

The cemented pairs have focal lengths in air of −494.87 mm and −433.22 mm. Their negative members provide the dispersive contributions between the two positive outer collectors. The first-to-last surface distance is 60.94 mm.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Element focal lengths below describe each element in air, rather than its contribution inside a cemented assembly.

### Element 1 — Positive Meniscus

nD = 1.644, ν = 48.3. Dense barium flint; f = +161.49 mm. Radii +64.32 / +159.9 mm and thickness 8.45 mm form the front positive collector.

### Elements 2–3 — Front Cemented Doublet

Element 2: nD = 1.644, ν = 48.3. Dense barium flint; f = +64.83 mm. Its +41.75 mm front radius and planar rear face form a plano-convex positive element, 9.50 mm thick.

Element 3 is a potassium bromide crystal with nD = 1.558, ν = 31.5 and f = −49.84 mm. Its planar front face is cemented to element 2. The +27.81 mm rear radius forms the concave face towards the central air space. The crystal is 4.18 mm thick. The diagram marks this element as APD because the patent specifies the crystal for secondary-spectrum correction.

### Elements 4–5 — Rear Cemented Doublet

Element 4: nD = 1.651, ν = 33.5. Dense flint; f = −48.57 mm. Its −31.62 mm front radius and planar rear face form a plano-concave negative element, 3.98 mm thick, facing the central air space.

Element 5: nD = 1.644, ν = 48.3. Dense barium flint; f = +64.57 mm. Its planar front face is cemented to element 4; the −41.58 mm rear radius completes a plano-convex positive element, 10.43 mm thick.

### Element 6 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.644, ν = 48.3. Dense barium flint; f = +107.45 mm. Radii +316.7 / −87.73 mm and thickness 7.46 mm form the final positive collector.

## Glass Identification and Spectral Scope

The patent prints nD and names the D line, taken as sodium at 589.3 nm. All six elements retain the printed indices, Abbe numbers and D reference. “Unmatched” indicates that no catalogue curve meets the site's spectral-proxy tolerance for these materials at the sodium D line. The patent identifies the crystal and the glass classes. The historical suppliers and melts are unknown.

The printed relative partial dispersion is (n_e − n_g) / (n_C − n_F):

| Elements   | Material                  |    nD |    ν | Printed relative partial dispersion |
| ---------- | ------------------------- | ----: | ---: | ----------------------------------: |
| 1, 2, 5, 6 | Dense barium flint        | 1.644 | 48.3 |                                1.02 |
| 3          | Potassium bromide crystal | 1.558 | 31.5 |                                1.00 |
| 4          | Dense flint               | 1.651 | 33.5 |                                1.07 |

This e–g ratio is distinct from a g–F partial-dispersion ratio or its deviation from a normal line. The printed ratios alone do not determine complete dispersion curves or C-, F- and g-line indices. Evaluated approximately from the printed data, the patent's two secondary-spectrum conditions give sums of about 0.004 against individual terms of about 0.03. D-line rays can be assessed directly; Abbe-number estimates do not reproduce this balance or establish the crystal's secondary spectrum or broadband performance.

## Focus Mechanism and Aperture

The model is focused at infinity. The patent supplies no close-focus distances or movement law. The inferred stop lies 8.80 mm behind R5 and 7.11 mm before R6, preserving the printed central air space. Its plane is 31.46 mm behind the first vertex; neither its position nor its bore is printed.

The paraxial f/2 setting gives an inferred iris radius of 18.063 mm. Exact axial tracing gives an entrance-pupil equivalent of about f/1.99. The inferred clear semi-diameters are 26 mm at both outer singlets, 23.4 mm at R3/R4 and R7/R8, and 18.6/18.4 mm at the two concave ends, R5/R6. Figure 2 supports these relative heights and square-cut positive members of the doublets, with chamfers towards the shorter concave faces. Its inconsistent axial thicknesses permit proportions rather than precise dimensions; the straight edges joining the model's rims approximate those chamfers. The full axial opening and the chief ray at the modelled 10.0° half-field pass every rim. Wide-open geometric transmission at that field edge is about 91%, with off-axis vignetting retained. The f/22 endpoint is a model choice, not a published diaphragm limit.
