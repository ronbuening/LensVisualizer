# COOKE 100mm f/2.5 (US 2,298,993 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,298,993 A, “Optical Objective”

**Inventors:** Arthur Warmisham

**Assignee:** Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited

**Published:** 13 October 1942

**Filed:** 6 December 1940

**Priority:** Great Britain, 12 December 1939

**Example details:** Example IV, Figure 4; numerical table on PDF page 3.

The patent gives five numerical examples. Example IV is a photographic objective of five air-spaced singlets at f/2.5. Its dimensions are normalized to an equivalent focal length of 1.000; multiplying every length by 100 expresses the model in millimetres at nominal f = 100 mm. No production lens is documented as using this prescription.

## Optical Architecture

The power sequence is positive–positive–negative–negative–positive. Two front positive menisci and a rear biconvex element surround two adjacent negative elements. Every surface is spherical, and there are no cemented interfaces. The first two elements remain separated by the printed 0.1 mm axial air space.

Warmisham's stated aim is to reduce zonal spherical aberration, or increase aperture without an undue increase in it. The central negative pair is separated by 4.0 mm, less than either neighbouring air space of 4.1 and 6.5 mm and less than 45% of their sum. The third element is biconcave with its shallower face toward the front; the fourth has the weak convex front face described for Examples IV and V (specification pp. 1–2).

The patent gives no diaphragm station or bore. The model places the stop in the central air space, 3.51 mm behind R6 and 0.49 mm ahead of R7, at an axial station of 23.31 mm from R1. Calculated real-ray coma favours this air space, but does not establish the historical iris location. The stop plane clears both neighbouring glass rims.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The indices below use the patent's capital-D notation. Element focal lengths are calculated for each thick singlet in air.

### Element 1 — Positive Meniscus

nD = 1.644, ν = 48.3. Glass: Unmatched. f = +164.04 mm.

This is the weaker of the two front positive menisci. Both radii are positive, with the front face more strongly curved. It contributes converging power before the central negative pair.

### Element 2 — Positive Meniscus

nD = 1.613, ν = 59.3. Glass: Unmatched. f = +85.83 mm.

The second meniscus supplies stronger positive power. Its higher Abbe number makes it less dispersive than the other positive elements. It sits immediately behind Element 1, across the narrow front air space.

### Element 3 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.652, ν = 33.5. Glass: Unmatched. f = −59.38 mm.

The shallow concave front and stronger concave rear form the front negative component. Its negative power and greater dispersion help balance the three converging elements. This is the stronger member of the negative pair.

### Element 4 — Negative Meniscus

nD = 1.652, ν = 33.5. Glass: Unmatched. f = −80.22 mm.

The weak convex front face and more strongly curved rear face produce net negative power. It uses the same printed glass pair as Element 3, with the inferred stop between them.

### Element 5 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.644, ν = 48.3. Glass: Unmatched. f = +53.32 mm.

The final singlet supplies the strongest individual positive power and converges the emerging beam toward the image. Its printed glass pair matches Element 1.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent prints nD and Abbe ν for three distinct glasses. All three pairs recur in the patent's other examples. The model retains the printed D-line indices (sodium at 589.3 nm) and estimates dispersion from the Abbe numbers. The glass labels are Unmatched, and the suppliers and melts are unknown.

At sodium D, Sumita BAF9 differs from Elements 1 and 5 by approximately −0.00083 in index and −0.59 in Abbe number. Sumita K-PSK200 differs from Element 2 by −0.00004 and −0.33. Hoya FD9 differs from Elements 3 and 4 by +0.00229 and +0.21. Each pair falls outside the site's spectral-proxy tolerance.

The positive elements use less dispersive glass than the negative pair, consistent with chromatic balancing. No additional line indices or partial-dispersion data are printed, so the supplied pairs do not establish secondary-spectrum or apochromatic performance.

## Focus Mechanism

The model is focused at infinity. The patent gives no close-focus distances, focus-state spacings, or moving-group law. The image lies at the computed paraxial back focus of 76.97 mm behind R10; no back-focus distance is printed.

## First-Order Properties and Model Scope

The printed table gives EFL 99.62 mm against nominal 100 mm, a difference of −0.4%. Across the patent, the five examples calculate to approximately 0.990–1.008 relative to the stated focal length. Rounding of the three-decimal indices alone permits a worst-case variation of about ±0.7%. The printed Example IV table gives calculated longitudinal spherical aberration of approximately +0.005 mm at the f/2.5 marginal ray. These results support retaining the printed prescription; no particular numerical correction is established.

The paraxial aperture is f/2.50, with an inferred iris radius of 15.03 mm. At this opening, the real-ray entrance-pupil and image-space sine measures both give approximately f/2.62. A radius of approximately 15.83 mm would admit the nominal real-ray f/2.5 marginal ray; the model retains the paraxial opening. The first-to-last vertex length is 39.80 mm and the total distance from R1 to the paraxial image is 116.77 mm.

Example IV has no printed field angle. The 25.0° semi-angle statement accompanies the Example V glass arrangement and is not assigned to Example IV. The model's ±10.0° field is illustrative: its chief rays clear the rims, and the axial beam has no rim limitation. No image format or image-circle diameter is inferred from this illustration.

The estimated rims follow Figure 4's nearly level element edges, with semi-diameters of 21.2 mm at R1/R2, 21.1 mm at R3/R4 and R9/R10, and 21 mm at R5 and R7/R8. R6 has a 16.65 mm clear semi-diameter with an opaque outer land extending to 21 mm. The land approximates the drawn corner and lies 0.54 mm ahead of the inferred stop plane. The axial beam and 10.0° chief ray clear every rim. Calculated wide-open geometric transmission is 100% through 6.7°, 99.3% at 8.3° and 98.0% at 10.0°. These results describe the modeled D-line geometry.
