# COOKE 100mm f/2.67 (US 2,601,369 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [US 2,601,369 A — Three-component optical objectives](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2601369A/en)\
**Inventors:** Gordon Henry Cook\
**Assignee:** Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited\
**Filed:** 29 January 1951\
**Published:** 24 June 1952\
**Priority:** Great Britain, 2 February 1950\
**Example details:** Sole numerical example; specification table on PDF page 2, repeated in claim 4 on PDF page 3; unnumbered section drawing on PDF page 1.

All dimensional values are multiplied by 100 from the patent's F = 1 table to give millimetres at a nominal 100 mm focal length. The calculated focal length is 99.97 mm. [GB 672,822 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/GB672822A/en) belongs to the same patent family. No production lens is documented as using this prescription.

## Optical Architecture

The objective has four spherical elements in three groups: a positive front singlet, a negative middle singlet and a positive rear cemented doublet. Surface R2 is plane, and R6 is the cemented interface. This is a Tessar-type arrangement with a thick rear positive element.

The patent emphasizes the converging cemented interface, whose radius is .207F, and the .044 increase in refractive index from the front to the rear member of the doublet. Its component focal-length magnitudes, .53F, .33F and .57F, agree with the calculated signed values +53.41, −32.95 and +56.78 mm. The first-to-last surface distance is 40.30 mm, matching the stated .403F.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### Element 1 — Plano-Convex Positive

nD = 1.6570, νD = 50.8. Glass: J-SSK5 (Hikari), spectral proxy. Focal length in air: +53.41 mm.

The front singlet supplies positive power through R1 = +35.09 mm and a plane rear surface. Its 13 mm central thickness follows both numerical tables.

### Element 2 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.6535, νD = 33.5. Glass: FD9 (Hoya), spectral proxy. Focal length in air: −32.95 mm.

The middle singlet is the diverging component between the positive front component and the rear doublet. Its lower Abbe value provides a dispersive counterpart to the positive components.

### Element 3 — Negative Meniscus (convex to object)

nD = 1.7000, νD = 41.2. Glass: S-BAH27 (Ohara), spectral proxy. Focal length in air: −31.98 mm.

This thin meniscus is the front member of cemented doublet C1. Its gently curved front surface, R5 = +285.71 mm, leads to the more strongly curved cemented interface, R6 = +20.70 mm.

### Element 4 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.7440, νD = 44.7. Glass: S-LAM2 (Ohara), spectral proxy. Focal length in air: +21.96 mm.

The 14.3 mm rear element supplies the stronger positive power in C1. Its higher index makes R6 converging at the actual glass-to-glass interface; the complete doublet has a focal length of +56.78 mm. Individual element focal lengths above describe each piece in air.

## Glass Identification

The patent prints indices for the D line (taken as sodium, 589.3 nm) and Abbe V values. All four elements use present-day catalog curves as spectral proxies. Differences below are catalog minus printed at the sodium D line:

- L1: J-SSK5 (Hikari), ΔnD = +0.00133, ΔνD = +0.03.
- L2: FD9 (Hoya), ΔnD = +0.00079, ΔνD = +0.21.
- L3: S-BAH27 (Ohara), ΔnD = +0.00139, ΔνD = +0.03.
- L4: S-LAM2 (Ohara), ΔnD = −0.00015, ΔνD = +0.08.

The printed indices and Abbe numbers are retained. Each curve is within the site's spectral-proxy tolerance of 0.002 in index and 0.25 in Abbe number at the sodium D line. The specification and claim 4 give no other line indices or partial-dispersion values. The historical glass identities, suppliers, melts and measured broadband performance remain unknown.

## Focus and Aperture

The model has one focus state at infinity. The patent gives no focusing mechanism or variable spacings. The image plane is 79.47 mm behind R7, at the calculated paraxial focus, giving a total front-vertex-to-image distance of 119.77 mm. The patent gives no back-focus distance.

Neither the text nor the drawing locates an iris. The inferred stop lies 4.8 mm behind R4 and 0.2 mm before R5, preserving the printed 5 mm second air gap. Its radius is 14.689 mm for the nominal paraxial f/2.67 aperture. The R4 rim remains about 0.565 mm clear of the stop plane.

The clear semi-diameters are estimated from the unnumbered drawing on PDF page 1 and checked against the axial beam and off-axis illumination. R1–R7 use 22.3, 22.3, 16.7, 15.4, 17, 17 and 17 mm. The front singlet keeps a square rim. Its 22.3 mm radius and R3's 16.7 mm radius exceed the drawn curve ends of about 18.9 and 15.3 mm to preserve off-axis light; R3's axial marginal ray reaches 15.92 mm. R4 and the rear doublet agree with the drawing within measurement uncertainty. The iris sets the axial aperture, and wide-open geometric transmission at 26° is about 77.1%. These estimates do not establish manufactured clear apertures or measured relative illumination.

## Field and Source Discrepancy

The specification states a 26° semi-angular field. The model therefore uses a 52° full field and traces to 26°. At that angle, the real chief ray reaches an image height of 48.60 mm and clears every current rim. The catalog image circle is 97.2 mm, twice that height at the model's scale; no physical film format is assigned. Chief-ray clearance does not establish illumination of the whole off-axis bundle or measured image quality.

The drawing labels the first thickness D1 = .103F, while both numerical tables print .130F. The stated .403F overall length and .130F sum of the intervening spaces and thicknesses support the tables: the drawing label is a misprint. The model retains the tabulated first thickness without altering any radius, glass value or optical interval.
