# COOKE 100mm f/2 (US 2,194,413 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,194,413 A, *Objectives for Photographic or Like Purposes*  
**Application Number:** Serial No. 243,828  
**Filed:** December 3, 1938  
**Priority:** December 11, 1937 (Great Britain)  
**Granted:** March 19, 1940  
**Inventors:** Arthur Warmisham; Charles Gorrie Wynne  
**Assignee:** Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited  
**Example details:** Example 1, illustrated by Fig. 1; prescription table on PDF p. 2, repeated in claim 3 on PDF p. 3; equivalent focal length 1.000 and F/2; all lengths multiplied by 100

US 2,194,413 describes a wide-aperture objective made of two compound dispersive menisci, concave faces turned towards each other, between two collective components. The patent gives two numerical examples, each with an equivalent focal length of 1.000 and a relative aperture of F/2. Example 1 is the one the patent recommends for the shorter focal lengths: it states that Example 2 serves well at 3 inches, but that Example 1 should be used when the lens is only 2 inches long, because distortion grows with the cube of the field angle and only with the first power of the focal length. The description table and the claim 3 table print identical values for Example 1.

COOKE 100mm f/2 (US 2,194,413 Design) multiplies every radius, thickness and air gap by 100. No other scaling or fitting is applied, so the 100 mm design focal length is a convenient size for display, not a marketed focal length. At the patent's own intended size (2 inches, 50.8 mm) the same prescription would be scaled by 50.8 instead.

The link to a production lens is a family association only:

1. Taylor, Taylor & Hobson made the Cooke lenses, and the patent presents itself as an improvement on US 1,955,591, the patent that Cooke's own history gives for the Speed Panchro line introduced around 1930.
2. The stated purpose matches that line: an F/2 objective of 2-inch (and, in Example 2, 3-inch) focal length for 35 mm motion-picture film. Cooke describes the original Speed Panchro set as thirteen f/2 lenses from 24 mm to 121 mm.
3. No source found ties US 2,194,413 to a particular production lens. Cooke's histories mention neither this patent nor its inventors, and no published construction drawing of a pre-war Speed Panchro has been located to compare with Fig. 1.
4. The post-war Series II Speed Panchros were redesigned after Gordon Cook joined the company in 1948, so they are later designs and not this example.

The model therefore carries no product name.

## Optical Architecture

The design is a six-element, four-group double-Gauss objective with a positive–negative–negative–positive sequence of components:

- L1, a positive front meniscus that is almost plano-convex.
- C1, the front compound dispersive meniscus: L2 cemented to L3, net focal length −263.63 mm, concave R5 facing the stop.
- The central air space S2 (19.21 mm), which holds the iris.
- C2, the rear compound dispersive meniscus: L4 cemented to L5, net focal length −703.70 mm, concave R6 facing the stop.
- L6, a positive biconvex rear element.

This is the same arrangement as the earlier six-element, four-group f/2 design of US 1,955,591. What distinguishes Example 1 is the shape of the front collective component and the glass of the rear one. The rear surface of L1 has a radius of 25.04 times the focal length (nearly flat), against 3.948 in the 1-inch example of US 1,955,591. The rear collective L6 uses a glass of index 1.6437 instead of 1.6234. The patent states that these two changes give the greatly improved distortion correction that is its object.

The patent prints no diaphragm position, and Fig. 1 draws no iris; it only labels the central air space S2. The model places the stop midway through S2, 9.605 mm from R5 and from R6, with a radius of 16.54 mm chosen for f/2. The entrance pupil radius is then 25.01 mm. Both values are modelling choices, not printed data.

The patent also prints no back focus. The model's 68.40 mm image distance after R10 is the calculated paraxial back focus. The vertex-to-vertex length is 78.18 mm and the total track to the image is 146.58 mm.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Positive Meniscus

`nd = 1.6130, νd = 59.3.` Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.6130 / V 59.3). f = +152.33 mm.

L1 is the front collective component. Its front radius is 90.13 mm and its rear radius 2504 mm, so it is close to plano-convex with the curved side towards the object. The flat rear face is the main feature of the invention: claim 1 requires the rear radius of the front collective component to exceed six times the focal length, and Example 1 has 25.04 times.

### C1 — L2 Positive Meniscus and L3 Negative Meniscus (cemented)

L2: `nd = 1.6130, νd = 59.3.` Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.6130 / V 59.3). f = +132.95 mm.
L3: `nd = 1.6137, νd = 37.2.` Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.6137 / V 37.2). f = −72.85 mm.

L2 and L3 form the front dispersive meniscus, with a net focal length of −263.63 mm. Both are menisci convex to the object, and the strongly curved concave R5 (26.57 mm) faces the stop. The two glasses have almost the same index (1.6130 and 1.6137) but very different Abbe numbers (59.3 and 37.2). On this reading, the cemented surface R4 acts mainly on colour and has little effect on monochromatic aberrations. This is an interpretation of the prescription; the patent does not say so. R4 is weaker here (0.696 of the focal length) than in US 1,955,591 (1.035).

### C2 — L4 Biconcave Negative and L5 Biconvex Positive (cemented)

L4: `nd = 1.6468, νd = 33.8.` Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.6468 / V 33.8). f = −27.46 mm.
L5: `nd = 1.6437, νd = 48.3.` Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.6437 / V 48.3). f = +35.90 mm.

L4 and L5 form the rear dispersive meniscus, with a net focal length of −703.70 mm. Its outer surfaces are the concave R6 (−30.47 mm), facing the stop, and the convex R8 (−40.91 mm), facing the image. The cemented surface R7 (+45.0 mm) bulges towards the object, so L4 is biconcave and L5 biconvex, as Fig. 1 shows. Claim 1 holds these outer radii between 0.25 and 0.31 (concave) and between 0.35 and 0.42 (convex) of the focal length; Example 1 gives 0.3047 and 0.4091. As in C1, the cemented pair joins two glasses of nearly equal index (index difference 0.0031) and different dispersion. The glass pair is essentially the one printed for the rear dispersive member of US 1,955,591 (1.6469/33.7 and 1.6437/48.3).

### L6 — Biconvex Positive

`nd = 1.6437, νd = 48.3.` Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.6437 / V 48.3). f = +100.29 mm.

L6 is the rear collective component, with radii of +174.6 mm and −100.1 mm. Its index of 1.6437 meets claim 2, which calls for a rear collective glass of index above 1.63. The patent states that this higher index is part of what makes Example 1 suitable for short focal lengths.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent prints the sodium-D index nD and the Abbe number V for each element. It names no supplier or glass type. No current catalogue row matches any of the four pairs within 0.0001 in index, so each glass is labelled Unmatched with its printed values; the nearest rows (SF2-class flints at 1.6477 / 33.8 for L4, for example) differ by roughly 0.001 in index. The model uses the printed nD and V at the sodium-D reference (`indexReference "D"`), and models dispersion from those two numbers alone.

| Glass (nD / V) | Elements | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1.6130 / 59.3 | L1, L2 | Collective crowns |
| 1.6137 / 37.2 | L3 | Flint of the front dispersive meniscus |
| 1.6468 / 33.8 | L4 | Flint of the rear dispersive meniscus |
| 1.6437 / 48.3 | L5, L6 | Collective glass of the rear meniscus and the rear element |

US 1,955,591 printed the 1.6437/48.3 glass with a note that it had been newly introduced since the publication of Chance Brothers' catalogue. The present patent gives no glass numbers, so the identity of its melts is unknown. No partial-dispersion data are printed, so the model makes no secondary-spectrum claim.

## Focus Mechanism

Infinity focus only; the patent gives no close-focus data, and no focus movement is modelled.

## Distortion Correction

Distortion is the patent's subject. It states that a 2-inch F/2 lens built to US 1,955,591 compresses the line from the centre to the corner of the 35 mm motion-picture frame by 0.004 inch. Its aim is to approach the grain size of the negative, given as about 0.0005 inch. According to the patent, Example 2 at 3 inches reduces the compression of the semi-diagonal to 0.001 inch, and Example 1 achieves 0.001 inch at a focal length of only 2 inches. These are the patent's figures; they have not been checked with this model.

## Patent Conditions and Model Scope

| Patent condition | Example 1 | Result |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Rear radius of the front collective component greater than 6 × f (claim 1) | 25.04 | Satisfied |
| Concave outer radius of the rear dispersive component between 0.25 and 0.31 × f (claim 1) | 0.3047 | Satisfied |
| Convex outer radius of the rear dispersive component between 0.35 and 0.42 × f (claim 1) | 0.4091 | Satisfied |
| Rear collective index greater than 1.63 (claim 2) | 1.6437 | Satisfied |

The model's paraxial focal length is 100.04 mm, 0.04 % above the patent's 1.000 scaled by 100, which is within the table's rounding. The f-number is 2.0 with the modelled stop. The radius signs agree with Fig. 1 element by element.

The patent prints no clear apertures. The semi-diameters follow Fig. 1, which is drawn to scale: its vertex spacings agree with the table to within about 3 %. From R1 to R10 the rims are 28.1, 28.1, 26.6, 26.6, 20.3, 20.3, 23, 23, 24 and 24 mm. The front doublet is square-cut at 26.6 mm, the inner rims and the rear doublet match the drawing, and the short flat lands the figure draws outside R5 and R6 are shown to 22.2 and 22.1 mm.

Two rims are larger than drawn. L1 is drawn at about 26.6 mm, like the front doublet. At that size the illumination 12° off axis would be 74 % instead of 79 %, so the model keeps L1 at 28.1 mm. L6 is drawn at about 22.9 mm against 24 mm in the model, which is within the accuracy of the drawing; a 23 mm L6 would take the 12° illumination down to about 76 %. The f/2 axial beam clears every surface with margin.

On a 2-inch lens the 35 mm motion-picture frame corresponds to a half-field of about 15° to 17°. At 15° the front and rear rims cut up to about 32 % and 33 % of the oblique bundle's width on one side. At 17° the cuts are about 39 % and 44 %. The chief ray is never blocked. The default display field of about 24° lies beyond the coverage the patent describes.

## Design Heritage and Context

The design descends from Horace William Lee's Opic and Speed Panchro objectives for Taylor, Taylor & Hobson; Cooke's history describes the Speed Panchro as a development of the Opic. The patent names kinematography as the special purpose that calls for the extra distortion correction. Later patents that cite US 2,194,413 include Warmisham's own US 2,416,032 and US 2,416,969 (1947) and several Voigtländer double-Gauss patents of the early 1950s.
