# COOKE 100mm f/2.67 (US 2,502,508 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [US 2,502,508 A](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/2502508), _Highly Corrected Three Component Optical Objectives of Large Aperture_

**Inventors:** Gordon Henry Cook

**Assignee:** Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited

**Filed:** 29 November 1948

**Published:** 4 April 1950

**British priority:** 18 October 1948

**Example details:** Example I, PDF page 3, column 3; Figure 1 on PDF page 1.

The patent gives two f/2.67 examples that differ only in the rear cemented doublet. This model follows Example I. The patent normalizes focal length to 1.000 without specifying a physical unit; all dimensional prescription values are multiplied by 100 to express the model in millimetres at nominal f = 100 mm.

No commercial product is documented as using this prescription.

## Optical Architecture

The four elements form a positive front singlet, a negative middle singlet and a positive rear cemented doublet. The concave cemented interface R6 contributes positive power as light enters the lower-index glass of Element 4.

The patent seeks improved spherical and coma correction over a reduced angular field, allowing a larger aperture and longer focal length. It describes the stronger rear component as helping lateral colour and distortion correction. The computed component powers, normalized to the nominal focal length, are approximately +1.744, −3.230 and +1.924, reproducing the printed +1.75, −3.23 and +1.92. The front-to-rear vertex track is 46.2 mm, corresponding to the printed 0.462F.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### Element 1 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.62250, ν = 60.4. Glass: Unmatched. Standalone focal length in air: +57.35 mm.

The front positive component has a strongly curved front surface and a weakly curved rear surface. The patent associates this weak rear curvature with coma correction.

### Element 2 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.62266, ν = 36.0. Glass: TIM11 (Ohara), spectral proxy. Standalone focal length in air: −30.96 mm.

The middle component supplies the negative power between the two positive components. Its lower Abbe number makes it the most dispersive element. The patent relates its surface curvatures to coma and astigmatism correction.

### Element 3 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.61380, ν = 57.5. Glass: Unmatched. Standalone focal length in air: +31.77 mm.

Element 3 is the positive front member of the rear doublet C1. Its rear surface is the cemented R6 interface, with no intervening air gap.

### Element 4 — Negative Meniscus

nD = 1.51414, ν = 56.5. Glass: Unmatched. Standalone focal length in air: −82.97 mm.

The rear meniscus reduces the positive power of Element 3. The assembled doublet has focal length +51.97 mm; the individual in-air powers do not describe its cemented-interface power directly. The rear pair's index difference is 0.09966, satisfying the patent's minimum difference of 0.07.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent explicitly gives D-line refractive indices and Abbe V numbers. The model uses the D line (taken as sodium, 589.3 nm), preserving all four printed pairs, including the small index difference between Elements 1 and 2.

Element 2 uses TIM11 (Ohara) as a spectral proxy. At the printed D line, the catalog values differ by Δn = −0.00186 and Δν = −0.13. The model anchors the TIM11 dispersion curve to the printed D-line index and retains the printed Abbe number in the prescription. Elements 1, 3 and 4 use Abbe-number estimates because their printed pairs have no catalog match within the site's spectral-proxy tolerance. The historical supplier and melt identities are unknown.

No additional spectral-line indices or partial-dispersion data are printed. Chromatic calculations remain estimates, with Abbe-number estimates for the unmatched elements; they do not establish anomalous dispersion, apochromatic correction or measured broadband performance.

## Focus Mechanism

The model has one optical configuration at infinity. The patent gives no focusing mechanism, close-focus distance or near-focus prescription.

The patent's 11.4 mm R4–R5 air space is preserved. The inferred stop is 7.2 mm after R4 and 4.2 mm before R5, at z = 30 mm from R1. Its radius of 13.92 mm produces paraxial f/2.67. Neither the stop station nor its bore is printed or drawn.

## Aspherical Surfaces

All seven refracting surfaces are spherical. The prescription contains no aspheric coefficients, cover glass or filter plate.

## Image Plane, Aperture and Field Limits

The calculated EFL is 99.97 mm, 0.03 mm below nominal; paraxial back focus is 76.30 mm. The image plane is 76.30 mm behind R7, at z = 122.50 mm from R1; the patent supplies no back-focus value.

R1's 18.75 mm clear radius comes from the printed effective front diameter of 0.375 times the focal length, or 37.5 mm here. R2 shares that radius. The modeled middle singlet has level 18 mm rims. Figure 1 draws shallow bevels on this element. The rear doublet has a common 17 mm radius at R5–R7. Its drawn rim is about as tall as the middle singlet's, but the printed surfaces of Element 3 meet at a radius of 17.17 mm. At 17 mm its edge remains about 0.21 mm thick. These dimensions clear the axial f/2.67 beam and leave positive glass and air clearances. Geometric transmission at the illustrative 5.0° edge is about 92.3%.

The aperture label uses the paraxial entrance pupil. At this same stop opening, real marginal rays give an entrance equivalent of about f/2.73 and an image-space sine equivalent of f/2.74. A real marginal ray entering at the paraxial f/2.67 pupil edge would require a larger stop radius, 14.25 mm. That alternative opening is not used in the model.

The ±5.0° field is illustrative: the patent says only that the angular field is reduced. The axial beam and 5.0° chief ray clear the modeled rims, while the front component partly vignettes the off-axis pupil. No physical image format, image circle or wider historical field coverage is established. Figure 1 does not dimension individual apertures.
