# COOKE 100mm f/4 (US 2,601,593 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,601,593 A

**Inventors:** Gordon Henry Cook

**Assignee:** Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited

**Published:** 24 June 1952

**Filed:** 8 March 1951, serial 214,508

**British priority:** 15 February 1951

**Example details:** Example VI, Figure 6; table on PDF p. 6, column 6, and discussion on PDF p. 7, column 7.

The patent gives nine numerical examples. Example VI is an all-spherical, six-element objective with two crystalline calcium fluoride menisci, nominal aperture f/4 and a stated half-field of 36.0°. The normalized table dimensions are multiplied by 100 mm per source unit for a nominal 100 mm model. No manufactured product is documented for this prescription.

The table and Figure 6 both print R9 = −0.6897, giving −68.97 mm in the model. Its reciprocal magnitude, 1.4499, agrees with the prose's approximately 1.45 times the equivalent power. All ten radii, nine physical thicknesses and separations, six sodium-D indices and six Abbe numbers follow the table.

## Optical Architecture

Six meniscus elements form four groups in the order 1+2 | 3 | 4 | 5+6. The outer groups are cemented doublets, joined at R2 and R9; two air-spaced positive fluorite menisci surround the diaphragm. The nearly symmetric arrangement places the concave sides of the components toward the stop.

The individual elements have positive–negative–positive–positive–negative–positive power. The front doublet has an in-air focal length of −804.04 mm; the rear doublet computes to +718.47 mm. Although the patent describes both outer doublets as divergent, the printed Example VI rear group is weakly convergent. The prescription retains the printed values, and this difference remains unresolved.

The first-to-last optical vertex distance is 38.3 mm. The text places the diaphragm “approximately midway” between R5 and R6 (PDF p. 7, column 7). The model uses the exact midpoint, splitting the 4.0 mm air space into 2.0 mm on either side of the stop at 18.9 mm from the first vertex. The exact station and opening are inferred.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### Elements 1–2 — Front cemented doublet

Element 1: nD = 1.717, νD = 47.9. Glass: S-LAM3 (Ohara), spectral proxy. In-air f = +58.91 mm. Element 2: nD = 1.610, νD = 37.5. Glass: Unmatched. In-air f = −44.92 mm.

The outer positive meniscus and inner negative meniscus form a weakly divergent front group. Their 7.2 and 3.6 mm center thicknesses meet at R2. The negative element's lower Abbe number supplies the more dispersive member of the pair. The clear radii at R1–R3 are 17.0, 17.0 and 10.1 mm, following the printed effective diameters with a 0.1 mm enlargement at R3 for axial clearance.

### Element 3 — Positive fluorite meniscus

nD = 1.4339, νD = 95.4. Material: crystalline calcium fluoride. In-air f = +123.17 mm.

The +28.09/+56.98 mm surfaces enclose 3.5 mm of fluorite ahead of the stop. This low-dispersion positive component supplies converging power between the front doublet and diaphragm. Its front clear radius of 11.8 mm is inferred; the rear clear radius of 10.0 mm follows the printed effective diameter.

### Element 4 — Positive fluorite meniscus

nD = 1.4339, νD = 95.4. Material: crystalline calcium fluoride. In-air f = +139.31 mm.

The −98.72/−38.02 mm surfaces enclose 4.6 mm of fluorite behind the stop. This second low-dispersion positive component faces the first across the central air space. Its front clear radius of 10.0 mm follows the printed effective diameter; the rear clear radius of 12.4 mm is inferred.

### Elements 5–6 — Rear cemented doublet

Element 5: nD = 1.610, νD = 37.5. Glass: Unmatched. In-air f = −49.90 mm. Element 6: nD = 1.717, νD = 47.9. Glass: S-LAM3 (Ohara), spectral proxy. In-air f = +55.12 mm.

The inner negative meniscus and outer positive meniscus reverse the front pair's order and meet at R9. Center thicknesses are 3.0 and 7.2 mm; clear radii at R8–R10 are 10.0, 15.0 and 15.0 mm, following the printed effective diameters. The combined group has weak positive power despite the patent's general description of a divergent rear component.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent explicitly identifies the two simple components as crystalline calcium fluoride (PDF p. 7, column 7). Both use the catalog CaF2 dispersion curve while retaining the printed nD = 1.4339 and νD = 95.4. The curve's sodium-D coordinates are about 1.43381 and 94.99, differing by −0.000092 and −0.41. The material is identified by the patent; its historical supplier is unknown.

Elements 1 and 6 use S-LAM3 (Ohara) as a spectral proxy. At the printed sodium-D line, catalog minus printed is ΔnD = −0.00013 and ΔνD = +0.02. The printed index and Abbe number are kept; the catalog curve supplies only the dispersion shape, so colour analysis follows a real glass curve instead of an Abbe-number estimate. This does not identify Cooke's historical glass, supplier or melt.

Elements 2 and 5 remain Unmatched at nD = 1.610 and νD = 37.5 because no catalog row falls within the proxy tolerance of 0.002 in index and 0.25 in Abbe number at the printed line.

The low dispersion of fluorite contrasts with the 47.9 and 37.5 Abbe numbers of the outer pairs. This explains the material choice without establishing measured apochromatic performance. The patent gives no additional spectral indices or partial-dispersion values. The text's quantity x = 0.106 differs from 0.107 obtained from its printed indices; those indices remain unchanged.

## Focus Mechanism

The model is focused at infinity, with light entering from the larger-conjugate side and no moving groups. Its rear image gap is the computed paraxial BFD, 80.71 mm from R10. The patent prints no back focus, focus travel or finite-conjugate prescription.

The EFL is 0.01 mm below the nominal value. The inferred iris radius is 9.766 mm. A real axial marginal ray passes every clear aperture at f/4.

The patent prints effective diameters of 0.34F at R1/R2, 0.2F at the chamfers of R3/R5/R6/R8, and 0.3F at R9/R10 (PDF p. 8, column 9, lines 56–63). At this scale these give clear radii of 17.0, 10.0 and 15.0 mm. R3's 10.1 mm radius includes 0.1 mm axial f/4 clearance beyond the printed value. R4 and R7 are inferred at 11.8 and 12.4 mm; Figure 6 gives approximately 11.0 and 12.0 mm, within the drawing's accuracy. Straight chamfers join the smaller inner faces to the larger outer rims, with no additional flat lands. The printed diameters set the off-axis vignetting, giving about 41.4% relative illumination at 36.0°.

## Field Coverage and Performance Limits

The chief ray reaches the image plane throughout the stated 36.0° half-field. At 36.0°, its image height is 72.68 mm; twice the unrounded height gives the 145.36 mm image-circle diameter. This is a model-derived diameter at the stated field, rather than a documented film format. Off-axis bundles are partly vignetted by the printed effective diameters.

The patent publishes no measured MTF chart. The unmatched elements 2 and 5 and the spectral proxies for elements 1 and 6 limit wavelength-dependent conclusions.
