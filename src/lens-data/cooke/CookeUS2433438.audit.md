# COOKE 100mm f/2.8 (US 2,433,438 Design) — Audit

## Source

Arthur Cox's US 2,433,438 A, “Telephoto Optical Objective,” was filed on 1 May 1946, with British priority of 18 June 1945, and published on 30 December 1947. The assignee printed on the front page is Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited. Example I corresponds to Figure 1 and is repeated in claim 17; Examples II and III are different prescriptions.

## Transcription

Two independent readings of the patent page images agree on all eight signed radii, five glass thicknesses, two air separations, five D-line indices and five Abbe numbers. The normalized 1.000 focal length becomes nominally 100 mm in the model. Cemented interfaces R2 and R7 agree with both the figure and the text. The 3.47 mm second element and the following 0.10 mm air space are distinct. The second air space is 3.97 mm, divided into 1.50 and 2.47 mm around the inferred stop. No transcription mismatch was found.

## First-order

The initial calculation gave EFL 100.021277 mm, paraxial f/2.8 and BFD 53.776780 mm. The printed back focus is retained as the 53.75 mm image gap, 0.026780 mm before paraxial focus. The lens track is 32.34 mm and the stored total track is 86.09 mm. No radius, spacing or image-plane adjustment was made to remove the rounding difference.

The inferred iris radius is 15.015980 mm. Earlier real-ray checks independently reproduced entrance-equivalent f/2.946277 and image-space sine f/2.952537; a real f/2.8 axial beam would require radius 15.897974 mm at that stop station. The delivered rims clear that beam.

## Not printed

The stop position, iris diameter, clear semi-diameters, selected illustrative ±10° field and glass names are absent from the patent. The drawing's dash-dot line in the rear air space is a dimension leader, not a diaphragm. The patent gives no close-focus data or aspheres.

Earlier geometric checks found no drawing trims, additional baffles or enlarged rims were needed. Minimum glass clearance was 1.559596 mm; the 0.10 mm air gap widens outward. Estimated straight sidewalls had positive clearance for the sampled rays, with a smallest radial margin of 0.002654 mm. Earlier field tracing reached 0° and 5° without clipping and showed slight front-rim clipping at 10°, with no unresolved ray paths. A separate 15° total-internal-reflection result did not establish coverage.

Earlier monochromatic geometric MTF refinement at the printed image plane converged with maximum changes of 0.000070, 0.000230 and 0.001649 at 0°, 5.219487° and 10° over 0–40 lp/mm. These results do not establish measured or broadband performance. Earlier application checks covered rendering, import/export and controls; they did not establish the historical glass identities. An earlier search found no duplicate prescription in the catalog material examined.

## Product

Cooke sold a Telepanchro 6 in f/2.8, but the product references examined do not connect it to this prescription. No commercial product is documented as using this patent example.

## Corrections

The initial data used an incomplete patent number and assignee, no maker, compressed prose and generic element types. The source prescription itself required no correction. Those presentation and bibliography issues are closed by the changes below.

## Remaining questions

The estimated rear rims are larger than Figure 1. At the model's scale the drawing suggests roughly 11.9 mm for the rear doublet's outer radius and 8.8 mm for the last clear surface before its chamfer, compared with stored R6/R7/R8 radii of 15.1/14.5/12.3 mm. The front outline is about 17.4 mm against 18.6 mm stored, and the meniscus outline is about 15.9 mm against 17.7/16.3 mm stored. These observations remain for a separate rim review.
