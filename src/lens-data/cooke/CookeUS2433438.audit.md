# COOKE 100mm f/2.8 (US 2,433,438 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,433,438 A, “Telephoto Optical Objective”](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2433438A/en), by Arthur Cox, was assigned to Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited. British priority is 18 June 1945, the United States filing date is 1 May 1946, and publication was 30 December 1947. The bibliography is on PDF page 2. Example I is printed on PDF page 3, column 3, and repeated in claim 17 on PDF page 4, column 6. Figure 1 is on the drawing sheet, PDF page 1. The back-focus statement and D-line definition are on PDF page 3. The patent contains three numerical examples.

[Leitz Auction 29, lot 550](https://www.leitz-auction.com/en/Taylor-Hobson-Cooke-Telepanchro-2.8-152mm-6/AI-29-33593) documents a Taylor Hobson Cooke Telepanchro 6 in f/2.8, but supplies no construction or link to this prescription.

## Transcription Check

All eight signed radii, five glass thicknesses, two air separations and five sodium D-line index/Abbe-number pairs agree with the patent page images. No transcription mismatch was found. The printed normalized focal length of 1.000 is expressed at a chosen 100 mm scale; all radii, thicknesses, spaces and the printed back focus are multiplied by 100. The patent supplies no physical focal-length unit.

The cemented interfaces are R2 and R7. R2 at −76.18 mm and R3 at +76.18 mm enclose the 3.47 mm second element; the following 0.10 mm air gap is distinct and widens outward. The second air separation is 3.97 mm, divided into 1.50 and 2.47 mm at the inferred stop. The difference R5 − R1 is 1.014 times the nominal focal length. At R7 the index increase is 0.066, and the patent gives a dispersive-power magnitude of 0.0665 times the whole objective's power. All surfaces are spherical.

## First-Order Check

Calculated EFL is 100.021 mm, about 0.021 mm above the scaled nominal 100 mm. Paraxial aperture is f/2.80, matching the printed f/2.8. Calculated BFD is 53.777 mm, against the printed 53.75 mm at this scale. The image plane remains 53.75 mm behind R8, about 0.027 mm before paraxial focus. The first-to-last-vertex distance is 32.34 mm and the first-vertex-to-image distance is 86.09 mm.

The iris radius is 15.016 mm. It admits a real-ray entrance-equivalent aperture of f/2.95 and an image-space sine aperture of f/2.95. A real f/2.80 axial beam would require a 15.898 mm iris radius at the same station; the estimated lens rims clear that beam. This finite-aperture difference is distinct from the exact numerical transcription.

The surfaces stay clear of each other, and the iris sets the aperture without a lens rim limiting the axial beam. The smallest glass edge thickness is 1.56 mm. The minimum calculated clearance to an estimated straight sidewall is about 0.0027 mm among the traced rays. The R5 rim is at 16.85 mm from the first vertex, ahead of the stop at 17.47 mm.

At 0.0° and 5.0° the traced beams are clear. At the illustrative 10.0° half-field there is slight front-rim vignetting, with one-sided bundle-width clipping estimates of about 5.0% at L1 and 3.0% at L2. The 10.0° chief ray reaches an image height of about 18.48 mm without blockage; this does not establish transmission of the full edge bundle. Transmission at 15.0° is not established because total internal reflection can occur. No measured or broadband MTF performance is established.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The patent gives no stop station or diameter. Figure 1 has no diaphragm; the dash-dot line in the rear air space is a dimension leader. The inferred stop is 1.50 mm behind R5 and 2.47 mm before R6, at 17.47 mm from the first vertex. Its radius follows paraxial f/2.80.

All clear semi-diameters are estimates: R1–R8 use 18.6, 18.6, 17.7, 17.7, 16.3, 15.1, 14.5 and 12.3 mm. Figure 1 suggests a front outline near 17.4 mm, a meniscus outline near 15.9 mm and a rear-doublet outer radius near 11.9 mm, with R8 clear to about 8.8 mm before its chamfer. These drawing readings are smaller than the retained estimates, especially at the rear doublet.

The ±10.0° field is illustrative; the patent prints no field angle or image format. No image-circle diameter is assigned. The model is focused at infinity: `focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. No close-focus distance or movement law is given. The selectable f-stops run from f/2.8 to f/22, but the patent supplies no minimum iris opening.

The glass labels state the printed nD and ν pairs. No catalog glass is assigned, and dispersion follows an Abbe-based estimate. The patent supplies no glass names, suppliers, melts, additional spectral indices or anomalous-dispersion properties.

## Product Identification

No commercial product is documented as using this exact prescription. The Cooke Telepanchro 6 in f/2.8 is a documented product, but its focal length and aperture alone do not establish a match. The Cooke title identifies the Taylor Hobson design at the chosen 100 mm scale; it does not establish a marketed 100 mm lens. No mount, production image format, diaphragm blade count or shape, marketed focal length or aperture, or marketed minimum focus distance is assigned.

## Corrections, 2026-11-21

- The author-and-patent identity was replaced by the Cooke maker-and-patent identity, with the title “COOKE 100mm f/2.8 (US 2,433,438 Design)” and maker Cooke. Example I and the chosen 100 mm scale were retained; no production model was assigned.
- The patent number gained the publication suffix A, and the assignee gained “Limited,” matching the issued patent. Arthur Cox and the 1947 publication year were retained.
- The subtitle is “US 2,433,438 A, Example I — Taylor Hobson / Cox.” The specifications are “5 ELEMENTS / 3 GROUPS,” “ALL SPHERICAL,” “DESIGN f = 100 mm,” “DESIGN F/2.8” and “TELEPHOTO,” identifying the construction and chosen scale.
- The design focal-length value changed from 100 to 100.02 mm to record calculated EFL. The illustrative image-circle value of 36.97 mm was removed because the patent gives neither a field nor a format.
- Generic element descriptions became Element 1–Element 5, with the shapes Biconvex Positive, Biconcave Negative, Positive Meniscus, Biconvex Positive and Biconcave Negative. Optical roles and in-air focal lengths of +49.42, −53.99, +77.53, +35.81 and −19.99 mm were added. The front doublet, meniscus and rear doublet have net focal lengths of +309.58, +77.53 and −78.08 mm.
- Cemented identities C1 on L1/L2 and C2 on L4/L5 were added with brackets spanning R1–R3 and R6–R8. The three group brackets describe the front cemented doublet, convergent meniscus and divergent rear doublet, following the patent's members.
- Generic glass text was replaced by explicit unmatched pairs: nD 1.623 / ν 60.3 on L1/L3, 1.699 / 30.5 on L2, 1.547 / 45.7 on L4 and 1.613 / 57.6 on L5. No catalog row matches within the printed precision. The sodium D reference and every printed glass value were retained: ΔnD = 0 and Δν = 0 for all elements. No anomalous-dispersion tags were added.
- No radii, thicknesses, air separations, stop position, clear rims, field angle, image plane or focus values changed. The printed 53.75 mm image gap remains in place rather than moving it to remove the small paraxial back-focus difference.

After these corrections, EFL is 100.021 mm, paraxial aperture is f/2.80 and BFD is 53.777 mm. The iris radius remains 15.016 mm, half-field remains 10.0°, image gap remains 53.75 mm and total distance to the image remains 86.09 mm.

## Remaining Questions

- The glass suppliers and melt identities remain unknown; none of the four distinct printed pairs has a catalog match within its printed precision.
- The true stop position and diameter remain unknown. The difference between paraxial f/2.80 and real-ray f/2.95 remains unresolved.
- The true clear rims and rear chamfer dimensions remain unknown. The retained estimates, particularly R6–R8, exceed the drawing readings.
- The patent gives no field angle, image format or image circle, and complete transmission at the illustrative edge field is not established.
- No source connects this prescription to a production lens or supplies its mount, diaphragm blades or shape, minimum iris opening, marketed specifications or close-focus mechanism.
