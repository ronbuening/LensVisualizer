# COOKE 100mm f/2.5 (US 2,366,597 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** [US 2,366,597 A, Optical Objective](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2366597A/en)\
**Inventors:** Arthur Cox\
**Assignee:** Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited\
**Filed:** November 18, 1942\
**Published:** January 2, 1945\
**British priority:** September 17, 1941\
**Example details:** Example I, numerical table on PDF p. 4 and Figure 1 on PDF p. 1.

The patent gives seven numerical examples, corresponding to Figures 1–7. This model follows Example I, whose stated relative aperture is f/2.5. The tables in claims 35–37 reproduce Examples II, III and VII, rather than Example I.

The patent prints an equivalent focal length of 1.000 without a physical length unit. All dimensional prescription values are multiplied by 100 to give millimetres at a nominal 100 mm focal length. The computed focal length is 99.87 mm, 0.1% below that nominal value; the printed radii, thicknesses and refractive data are retained.

No production lens is documented as using this prescription; the Cooke Telepanchro name has not been tied to this patent.

## Optical Architecture

Six elements form three cemented doublets, with shared interfaces at R2, R5 and R8. The first two doublets converge light and the rear doublet diverges it. Their computed focal lengths in air are +125.79, +136.13 and −74.89 mm. All nine refracting interfaces are spherical.

Each complete doublet has a meniscus shape with its exposed surfaces convex toward the object. This group shape differs from the shapes of its individual elements: each contains a biconvex positive element followed by a biconcave negative element.

The first-to-last surface distance is 38.1 mm. Adding the computed 48.91 mm paraxial back focus gives a total length of 87.01 mm, or 0.871 of the computed focal length. This satisfies the patent's statement that Example I is shorter than seven eighths of its focal length.

The patent explains that the first surface introduces undercorrected spherical aberration, balanced by overcorrection at the cemented surfaces in the two convergent members. Distributing that correction reduces the individual surface contributions and improves zonal correction while retaining correction of coma and astigmatism (PDF p. 4). D-line axial tracing gives longitudinal spherical aberration of about −0.016 mm at the full nominal entrance radius and −0.126 mm at 70% of that radius, measured from the near-axis focus.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### C1 — First Convergent Doublet

**Element 1 — Biconvex Positive:** $n_D = 1.613$, $\nu_D = 57.6$. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.613 / ν 57.6). Focal length in air: +52.75 mm.

The positive front element collects light. Its front radius is 0.5123 of the nominal focal length, within the patent's preferred range of 0.45–0.75. It is cemented to Element 2 at R2.

**Element 2 — Biconcave Negative:** $n_D = 1.699$, $\nu_D = 30.3$. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.699 / ν 30.3). Focal length in air: −84.47 mm.

The more dispersive negative element reduces the first doublet's positive power. The concave cemented interface contributes to the spherical-aberration balance described by the patent. The index difference across it is 0.086.

### C2 — Second Convergent Doublet

**Element 3 — Biconvex Positive:** $n_D = 1.613$, $\nu_D = 57.6$. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.613 / ν 57.6). Focal length in air: +51.77 mm.

The second positive element follows the first doublet across the printed 1.4 mm air space. It provides further convergence and is cemented to Element 4 at R5.

**Element 4 — Biconcave Negative:** $n_D = 1.699$, $\nu_D = 30.3$. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.699 / ν 30.3). Focal length in air: −74.91 mm.

This element repeats the first doublet's glass pairing with different curvatures. Its cemented front surface shares the spherical correction between the two convergent members. The following 6.5 mm air space separates this doublet from the divergent rear member.

### C3 — Divergent Rear Doublet

**Element 5 — Biconvex Positive:** $n_D = 1.652$, $\nu_D = 33.5$. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.652 / ν 33.5). Focal length in air: +33.01 mm.

The positive front element moderates the rear member's divergence. It is cemented to Element 6 at R8, where the refractive-index difference is only 0.008. The patent prefers an index difference below 0.01 when this rear contact is concave toward the front (PDF p. 3).

**Element 6 — Biconcave Negative:** $n_D = 1.644$, $\nu_D = 48.3$. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.644 / ν 48.3). Focal length in air: −19.70 mm.

The strongly negative last element makes the rear doublet divergent and shortens the overall lens. Its final radius, 0.1466 of the nominal focal length, falls within the patent's preferred 0.125–0.35 range.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent prints a D-line refractive index and an Abbe number for every element, but no glass trade names, suppliers or individual spectral-line indices. The D line is taken as sodium, 589.3 nm. The repeated 1.613 / 57.6 and 1.699 / 30.3 pairs belong to the two convergent doublets; the rear doublet uses 1.652 / 33.5 and 1.644 / 48.3.

No catalog row matches both printed coordinates closely enough to justify a named assignment. The glasses therefore remain Unmatched, with the printed index and Abbe number stated in each label. Those two quantities alone do not establish the actual dispersion curve, secondary spectrum or apochromatic correction.

## Focus Mechanism and Aperture

The model is focused at infinity; the patent gives no close-focus data or group-movement schedule. The image plane is the calculated paraxial infinity plane, 48.91 mm behind R9. No numerical back focus is printed.

The stop is inferred at 25.3 mm behind the first vertex, splitting the printed 6.5 mm R6–R7 air space into 3 mm before the stop and 3.5 mm after it. Neither the text nor Figure 1 specifies a diaphragm station or bore. The nominal f/2.5 setting gives a paraxial iris radius of 14.39 mm; real-ray tracing at that iris gives approximately f/2.66. An iris radius of about 15.43 mm would admit the nominal f/2.5 real marginal ray, but the modeled iris follows the paraxial setting.

## Model Scope and Clear Apertures

The displayed ±5.0° field is illustrative. The patent states no numerical field, image format or image-circle diameter, so none is assigned as a source specification.

The inferred clear semi-diameters at R1–R9 are 23, 21, 20, 20, 17.4, 17, 14, 14 and 11.5 mm. Glass and air clearances remain positive, and the axial beam clears the rims at the modeled aperture. The chief and axial marginal rays clear the rims near the displayed field. At the larger iris needed for real f/2.5, about 1.0% of one side of the off-axis bundle is vignetted.

Figure 1 draws the second and rear doublets larger than the model. Approximate figure radii are 23.5 mm for the first two members and 16 mm for the rear member, with about 13 mm at R9. Increasing clear radii is constrained by vanishing edge thickness, near 22.2 mm for Element 3 and 16.4 mm for Element 5. These drawing differences remain unresolved; the present apertures are estimates rather than measured production dimensions.
