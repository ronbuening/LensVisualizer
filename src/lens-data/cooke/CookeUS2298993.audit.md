# COOKE 100mm f/2.5 (US 2,298,993 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,298,993 A, “Optical Objective”](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/2298993) names Arthur Warmisham as inventor and Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited as assignee. The US application was filed on 6 December 1940, with British priority dated 12 December 1939, and published on 13 October 1942. Example IV / Figure 4 is one of five examples: the drawing is on PDF page 1, the definitions and design discussion begin on PDF page 2, and the numerical table is on PDF page 3. The table states equivalent focal length 1.000 and relative aperture f/2.5.

## Transcription Check

All ten signed radii, five glass thicknesses, four air separations, five D-line indices and five Abbe numbers agree with the patent page images. Figure 4 repeats the Example IV radii and thickness/separation labels. No transcription discrepancy was found. Multiplying the normalized dimensions by 100 gives millimetres at nominal f = 100 mm. The 4.00 mm central air separation is divided into 3.51 and 0.49 mm by the inferred stop; the front air space is 0.10 mm. All surfaces are spherical, with five air-spaced singlets and no cemented interfaces.

## First-Order Check

The printed prescription gives EFL 99.62 mm against nominal 100 mm, a difference of −0.4%, and paraxial f/2.50 against the printed f/2.5. Calculated BFD is 76.97 mm, and the image plane lies this distance behind R10 at paraxial infinity focus. No back focus is printed. The first-to-last vertex length is 39.80 mm and the first-vertex-to-image distance is 116.77 mm.

The five examples calculate to approximately 0.990–1.008 times their stated focal length. The three-decimal index precision alone permits about ±0.7% worst-case EFL variation. Calculated longitudinal spherical aberration is approximately +0.005 mm at the nominal f/2.5 margin. Rounding may explain the residual, but no particular correction to the printed values is supported.

The inferred iris radius is 15.03 mm. Its real-ray entrance-pupil and image-space sine measures both give approximately f/2.62. A radius of about 15.83 mm would admit the real-ray f/2.5 marginal ray; the retained opening follows the paraxial f/2.50 aperture. The surfaces stay clear of each other, and the iris sets the axial aperture without rim clipping. At 10.0°, the chief ray reaches an image height of 17.57 mm without blockage.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The patent supplies no diaphragm station or bore, clear semi-diameters, final image distance, or field angle for Example IV. The stop is in the central air space at 23.31 mm from R1, 3.51 mm behind R6 and 0.49 mm ahead of R7. Calculated real-ray coma favours this space without establishing the historical location. The stop plane clears both neighbouring glass rims.

The ±10.0° field is illustrative. The 25.0° semi-angle statement accompanies the Example V glass arrangement and is not assigned to Example IV. No image format or image-circle diameter is inferred. The estimated rims follow Figure 4's approximately 21 mm heights. R6 retains a 16.65 mm clear radius with an opaque land extending to 21 mm, keeping the inferred iris in air. R9/R10 remain at 20.2 mm, within about 5% of the drawing. The full optical R6/R7 profiles would meet near a height of 20.9 mm, so their outer outlines require finite clearance. Calculated geometric transmission at 10.0° is 98.0%; this is not measured throughput.

`focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. No close-focus distance or moving-group law is printed. The selectable f/2.5–f/22 range is illustrative; the patent supplies no minimum iris opening.

The patent prints nD and Abbe ν but no additional line indices or partial dispersion. The capital-D reference is retained as sodium at 589.3 nm. All five glass labels remain Unmatched, with dispersion estimated from the printed pairs; the historical suppliers and melts are unknown, and broadband performance is not established.

## Product Identification

No production lens is documented as using this prescription. The Cooke title identifies the Taylor Hobson patent design at its nominal 100 mm scale. No production specifications are assigned from a possibly related lens.
