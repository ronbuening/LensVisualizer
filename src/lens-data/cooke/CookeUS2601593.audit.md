# COOKE 100mm f/4 (US 2,601,593 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,601,593 A, _Four Component Optical Objective_](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/67/c5/35/06750c6e958738/US2601593.pdf), by Gordon Henry Cook, assigned to Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited. British priority: 15 February 1951; US filing: 8 March 1951, serial 214,508; publication: 24 June 1952. The patent contains nine numerical examples and 19 claims.

This model follows Example VI and Figure 6. The drawing is on PDF p. 2; the bibliography is on p. 4; the prescription table is on p. 6, column 6; and the field, diaphragm and fluorite discussion is on p. 7, column 7. The effective-diameter recommendations are on p. 8, column 9, lines 56–63. No product source identifies a manufactured lens with this exact prescription.

## Transcription Check

All ten radii, nine physical thicknesses and air separations, six sodium-D indices and six Abbe numbers agree with the patent page images. No transcription mismatch was found. The normalized dimensions are multiplied by 100 mm per source unit. The central separation of 0.040 becomes 4.0 mm, split into 2.0 mm on each side of the diaphragm. R2 and R9 are the cemented interfaces. All surfaces are spherical, and relative group spacings are fixed.

The table and Figure 6 both print R9 = −0.6897, giving −68.97 mm. Its reciprocal magnitude is 1.4499, consistent with the prose's approximately 1.45 times the equivalent power. No alternative R9 value is supported by these page images.

## First-Order Check

Calculated EFL is 99.99 mm against nominal 100 mm at this scale, a difference of −0.01 mm. The paraxial aperture is f/4.00, with a stop radius of 9.766 mm. Calculated rear-vertex BFD is 80.71 mm; the patent prints no back focus. The image plane is at paraxial infinity focus, 80.71 mm behind R10 and 119.01 mm behind the first vertex. The first-to-last optical vertex distance is 38.30 mm.

The surfaces stay clear of each other, and the iris sets the full aperture without a limiting glass rim. The axial marginal ray clears every surface. Chief rays reach the image throughout the stated 36.0° half-field, including the corner at an image height of 72.68 mm. Off-axis marginal rays are partly vignetted.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The patent places the diaphragm approximately midway between R5 and R6. The exact midpoint, 18.9 mm behind the first vertex, and the opening required for f/4 are inferred. The image gap is calculated paraxially.

The patent recommends effective diameters of 0.34F at R1/R2, 0.2F at the chamfers of R3/R5/R6/R8, and 0.3F at R9/R10. At the chosen scale, these correspond to clear radii of 17.0, 10.0 and 15.0 mm. R3 is enlarged by 0.1 mm to clear the axial f/4 beam. R4/R7, which have no printed effective diameters, remain inferred at 11.8/12.4 mm. The clear radii therefore combine printed and inferred values. Relative illumination at 36.0° is about 41.4% with these rims.

The 36.0° half-field is printed for Examples V–VII. The 145.36 mm image circle is twice the calculated chief-ray height at that field, rounded upward to two decimals. It is not a published film format or a fully unvignetted circle.

`focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. No finite-focus prescription, moving-group mechanism or focus travel is given. The modeled aperture spans f/4 to f/22; the patent gives no minimum iris opening or stop range.

The two fluorite elements use the catalog CaF2 dispersion curve; the four ordinary-glass elements use Abbe-based dispersion estimates. All retain their printed sodium-D coordinates. No additional spectral indices, partial-dispersion values or measured MTF chart are supplied, and measured polychromatic performance is not established.

## Product Identification

No manufactured product is documented for this six-element, four-group fluorite prescription. The Cooke attribution follows the assignee, Taylor, Taylor & Hobson, maker of Cooke lenses; no exact production lens is established. No mount, production image format, diaphragm blade count or shape, marketed focal length, marketed aperture or marketed minimum focus distance is assigned.

## Remaining Questions

- The rear doublet's weak positive power conflicts with the patent's divergent description, and the text's x = 0.106 differs from 0.107 obtained from the printed indices. Neither discrepancy has a source-supported correction.
- The identities of the four ordinary glasses and the historical fluorite supplier and melt remain unknown. The patent does not establish measured polychromatic performance.
- Exact physical iris dimensions, the minimum iris opening, finite-focus behavior and a manufactured product with this exact prescription are undocumented. Mount, production format, blade count and shape, marketed specifications and close-focus data are unknown.
