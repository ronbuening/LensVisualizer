# COOKE 100mm f/2.67 (US 2,601,369 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,601,369 A, _Three-component optical objectives_](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2601369A/en), by Gordon Henry Cook and assigned to Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited, was filed on 29 January 1951 and published on 24 June 1952, with British priority dated 2 February 1950. [GB 672,822 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/GB672822A/en) is the British family publication. The sole numerical example appears in the specification table on PDF page 2 and again in claim 4 on PDF page 3; the unnumbered section drawing is on PDF page 1.

The tables normalize the equivalent focal length to F = 1 and print f/2.67. All dimensional values are multiplied by 100 to give millimetres at a nominal 100 mm focal length. The patent specifies D-line indices, taken as sodium D at 589.3 nm, and Abbe V values.

[Cooke Optics' account of the Taylor-Hobson Adotal 80 mm f/2.8](https://cookeoptics.com/news-and-events/classic-cookes-80mm-f-2-8-taylor-hobson-adotal-serial-356376-likely-produced-in-the-mid-1940s-post-wwii/) provides the product history discussed below; it establishes no connection to this patent.

## Transcription Check

Both numerical tables were re-read against the page images. All seven signed radii, four glass thicknesses, two air spaces and four index/Abbe pairs agree with the model at the stated scale. R2 is plane, R5 is +285.71 mm and R6 is the cemented interface. The 5 mm second air space is divided into 4.8 mm before the inferred iris and 0.2 mm after it, preserving the printed separation. No numerical-table disagreement was found.

The drawing labels D1 as .103F, while both tables give .130F. The stated .403F overall length, the .130F sum S1 + D2 + S2 + D3 and the drawn first-element thickness support the tables. The .103F drawing label is a misprint; the retained first thickness is 13 mm.

Drawing-based clear radii are approximately 18.9 mm at R1/R2, 15.3 mm at R3, 15.3 mm at R4 and 17.6 mm at R5–R7. The middle singlet's outer edge reaches about 16.9 mm. The retained clear radii are 22.3, 22.3, 16.7, 15.4, 17, 17 and 17 mm. The front and middle values preserve off-axis illumination; the drawn R3 curve end also lies inside the axial f/2.67 marginal ray, whose height there is about 15.92 mm. All clear radii remain estimates rather than dimensions printed in the patent.

## First-Order Check

The calculated EFL is 99.97 mm, about 0.03% below the nominal 100 mm scale, with paraxial f/2.67 matching the printed aperture. The calculated BFD is 79.47 mm; the image plane is at that paraxial infinity focus behind R7. The first-to-last surface distance is 40.30 mm, matching the stated .403F, and the front-vertex-to-image distance is 119.77 mm. The component focal lengths are +53.41, −32.95 and +56.78 mm, consistent with the printed .53F, .33F and .57F magnitudes. The patent prints no back focus.

The stop radius is 14.689 mm. The R4 rim stays approximately 0.565 mm clear of its plane. The surfaces stay clear of each other, and the iris sets the axial aperture with no rim limit; finite-aperture tracing is within 3% of the stated f-number.

At the printed 26.0° half-field, the chief ray reaches an image height of approximately 48.60 mm and clears every current rim. Chief-ray field containment at the 48.6 mm image radius is effectively 100.0%. These results do not establish full off-axis pupil illumination or measured image quality.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

Neither the text nor the drawing locates an iris or specifies its diameter. Its position and bore are inferred. All clear semi-diameters, the calculated image-plane distance and the selectable f-stop sequence are also unprinted. The f/32 endpoint is a model setting, without a documented minimum iris opening.

The patent does print the 26.0° half-field. The 52.0° full field follows from it; the 97.2 mm image-circle diameter is twice the real chief-ray height at that field and the chosen scale. It is not a published production film format or evidence of an unvignetted edge bundle.

`focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. There are no close-focus spacings or documented near-focus mechanism. The four printed glass pairs remain unchanged; present-day catalog spectral proxies supply their dispersion-curve shapes. The historical glass identities, suppliers, melts and partial dispersion remain unknown; measured broadband performance is not established.

## Product Identification

No production lens is documented as using this prescription. Cooke's Adotal account describes a likely mid-1940s lens and a twelve-leaf iris, but provides no optical section or connection to this patent. Its production specifications cannot be assigned to this design.

No mount, physical image format, diaphragm blade count or roundedness, marketed focal length or f-number, marketed minimum focus distance, or production aperture range is established. The title retains the nominal 100 mm design scale.
