# COOKE 100mm f/2.67 (US 2,601,369 Design) — Audit

## Source

Gordon Henry Cook's US 2,601,369 A, _Three-component optical objectives_, assigned to Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited, was filed on 29 January 1951 and published on 24 June 1952, with British priority dated 2 February 1950. GB 672,822 A is the British family publication. The US patent has one numerical example, repeated in the specification table on PDF page 2 and claim 4 on PDF page 3; its section drawing on PDF page 1 is unnumbered.

## Transcription

Both tables agree on seven signed radii, four element thicknesses, two air spaces, four D-line indices and four Abbe V values. The 5 mm second air space is divided 4.8/0.2 mm around an inferred stop without changing its total. The drawing labels D1 as .103F; both tables give .130F, supported by the stated .403F overall length and the .130F sum S1 + D2 + S2 + D3. The drawing label is a misprint; the table value is retained.

## First-order

The prescription gives EFL 99.9670637889 mm, paraxial f/2.670 and BFD 79.4743676405 mm. The optical length is 40.30 mm and the total front-vertex-to-image distance is 119.7743676405 mm. The component focal lengths are +53.41, −32.95 and +56.78 mm, reproducing the patent's .53/.33/.57 magnitudes. The patent prints no back focus.

## Not printed

The iris position and bore, all clear semi-diameters, image-plane distance and available f-stop sequence are inferred or calculated. The stop radius is 14.6886706888 mm; the R4 rim clears its plane by about 0.565 mm. There are no close-focus data, named glass suppliers, partial dispersions, physical film format or measured production performance.

## Product

No production lens is documented as using this prescription. An Adotal attribution is not established.

## Corrections

The initial model used a 5° half-field despite the patent's explicit 26° semi-angular field. That restriction is replaced by 26°, with a 52° full field and a 97.2 mm image circle from twice the real chief-ray height. The tabulated prescription, inferred stop, image plane and rims remain unchanged.

## Remaining questions

The stop and clear apertures remain estimates. Full-field chief-ray clearance does not establish complete pupil illumination or measured optical performance. No catalog glass identity or production-lens connection is established.
