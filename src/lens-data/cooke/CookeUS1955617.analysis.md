# COOKE 27mm f/1.5 (US 1,955,617 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 1,955,617 A

**Filed:** 27 January 1931; serial 511,454; British priority 28 January 1930

**Published:** 17 April 1934

**Inventors:** Arthur Warmisham

**Assignee:** Kapella Limited

**Example details:** Figure 1, prescription table on PDF p. 2; drawing on PDF p. 1. All printed inch dimensions are multiplied by 25.4 to obtain millimetres, without focal-length adjustment.

US 1,955,617 A, _Photographic and Projection Lens_, describes a modified Petzval objective intended to improve field flatness while retaining a large relative aperture. Figure 1 is the first of three numerical examples; Figures 2 and 3 are different f/1.4 designs. The Figure 1 dimensions, refractive indices, Abbe numbers and glass codes agree with two independent readings of the patent page images. No product is documented as using this prescription.

The printed focal length is 1.059 in, or 26.8986 mm, at f/1.5. The D-line prescription computes to 26.92907 mm, 0.11327% longer; its dimensions retain the printed values. The catalog rounds the computed focal length to 26.93 mm. The computed Petzval sum is 0.02583207 mm⁻¹, giving 0.6956 when multiplied by the computed focal length: this reproduces the patent's printed .696. The patent compares that residual with .809 for its earlier design (PDF p. 2).

## Optical Architecture

The six elements form four groups: Element 1, the cemented pair 2–3, the cemented pair 4–5, and Element 6. The front meniscus and front doublet are converging; the rear doublet is diverging and precedes a positive rear singlet. The front doublet has a computed focal length of +94.93 mm and the rear doublet −64.72 mm. All curved surfaces are spherical.

R4 is the first cemented interface; R7 is the plane cemented interface of the second doublet. The patent pairs a higher-index positive element with a lower-index negative element in the rear diverging member to improve field flatness. Separately, its convex front surface reduces incidence angles and spherical aberration (PDF p. 2). The model inserts an inferred stop halfway through the R5–R6 air space, leaving 1.651 mm on each side at z = 10.795 mm. The patent gives neither its position nor its diameter.

The first air interval after R2 is .002 in, or 0.0508 mm, and widens away from the axis. The other air intervals are 3.302 and 2.286 mm. The first-to-last refracting-vertex track is 24.892 mm. Light travels left to right as the Figure 1 arrows indicate.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### Element 1 — Positive Meniscus

nD = 1.5735, νD = 57.5. Glass: Unmatched, Chance 9002. In-air focal length: +93.86 mm.

The positive front collector has radii +.8831/+1.454 in and thickness .096 in. Its estimated clear radii are 9.6/9.3 mm. The rear face is separated from Element 2 by the 0.0508 mm central air gap.

### Element 2 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.5181, νD = 60.3. Glass: Unmatched, Chance 9322. In-air focal length: +19.25 mm.

This is the positive element of the front cemented doublet. Its +.7005/−.7930 in faces bound .230 in thickness. Estimated clear radii of 9.0/8.6 mm leave positive edge thickness; the surfaces would meet at approximately 10.087 mm height.

### Element 3 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.6521, νD = 33.5. Glass: Unmatched, Chance 5093. In-air focal length: −21.25 mm.

The more dispersive negative partner shares R4 with Element 2 and ends at the +1.775 in R5 surface after .032 in glass. Its estimated rear clear radius is 8.1 mm. The inferred stop follows in the 3.302 mm air space.

### Element 4 — Plano-Convex Positive

nD = 1.6437, νD = 48.3. Glass: Unmatched; the patent leaves the code blank. In-air focal length: +28.60 mm.

This is the higher-index positive element of the rear diverging doublet. The +.7247 in front surface and plane R7 interface bound .180 in glass. Its estimated clear radii are 7.3/6.5 mm. The doublet's negative power results from its combination with Element 5.

### Element 5 — Plano-Concave Negative

nD = 1.5635, νD = 42.9. Glass: Unmatched, Chance 8653. In-air focal length: −17.68 mm.

The negative element shares plane R7 with Element 4 and ends at the +.3922 in R8 surface after .020 in glass. Its estimated rear clear radius is 6.1 mm. The subsequent 2.286 mm air interval remains open across the shared radial extent; the facing surfaces would meet at approximately 8.024 mm height.

### Element 6 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.6437, νD = 48.3. Glass: Unmatched; the patent leaves the code blank. In-air focal length: +23.44 mm.

The rear positive singlet has +.7500/−2.559 in faces and .200 in thickness, with estimated clear radii of 6.2/5.5 mm. It repeats Element 4's printed optical constants, which do not establish an unprinted glass code. The image distance after it is computed.

## Glass Identification and Spectral Scope

The patent identifies its indices as nD and gives Abbe V and four Chance Brothers catalogue numbers. The model uses sodium D at 589.3 nm, retaining all printed indices and Abbe numbers. No available catalog row meets both |ΔnD| ≤ 0.0001 and |Δν| ≤ 0.15, so all glasses remain Unmatched. Elements 4 and 6 retain no glass code because their source cells are blank.

The two cemented pairs combine positive and negative elements with different dispersions. The patent gives no individual C-, F- or g-line indices or partial-dispersion data. Broadband calculations based only on Abbe number are approximate; the numerical ray and MTF results below refer to the D line.

## Focus Mechanism and Detector

The model represents infinity focus only. The patent supplies no close-focus data, precise finite projection conjugate or focusing mechanism, so no group movement or marketed minimum focus distance is assigned.

The computed paraxial back focal distance is 11.9366364048 mm and the front-focal-distance magnitude is 20.1158847709 mm. The image is at z = 36.8286364048 mm, the Gaussian infinity plane. The patent prints no back focus; the image plane is not adjusted to minimize aberrations.

The retained ±3° field is illustrative. Its edge chief ray reaches image height 1.4118353741 mm, but the patent states no field or format. No catalog image circle is assigned from this chosen field.

## Aperture, Rims and Sensitivity

The printed f/1.5 sets the paraxial stop radius to 6.8140049082 mm, or 13.6280098163 mm diameter, at the inferred midpoint. Real rays fill this stop from an axial input radius of 8.4378704141 mm, giving an effective aperture of about f/1.60 against the paraxial f/1.5.

An axial marginal ray entering at EFL/3 = 8.9763561909 mm, corresponding to real-ray f/1.5, reaches the stop at radius 7.3235616592 mm, or 14.6471233183 mm diameter. That larger opening would admit the ray through all element rims; the model retains the smaller paraxial opening.

Calculations with the larger opening also show some iris clipping at 1.5° and 3°. Neither opening establishes the historical diaphragm or measured illumination.

The estimated clear radii, in R1–R10 order, are 9.6, 9.3, 9.0, 8.6, 8.1, 7.3, 6.5, 6.1, 6.2 and 5.5 mm. Minimum glass thickness is 0.508 mm; minimum first-air clearance is 0.0508 mm and minimum R8–R9 clearance is 1.2030198825 mm. The facing surfaces occupy at most 47.3744583% of an axial air gap through their sag. Clearance around the stop opening is at least 1.133101792 mm in front and 1.651 mm behind. No glass crosses the stop plane.

Earlier sensitivity calculations found that common clear-radius changes of ±0.1 mm retained open gaps and positive glass thickness. Changing the stop radius by ±2% gave effective apertures f/1.624085 and f/1.568592. Moving the stop ±0.25 mm within the unchanged 3.302 mm air space gave f/1.595309, f/1.595727 and f/1.596150 at z = 10.545, 10.795 and 11.045 mm. These describe uncertainty in the assumed aperture, not manufacturing tolerances.

## Illustrative Field and Clear Apertures

Chief rays reach the image throughout the chosen 0–3° half-field. At the modeled aperture, the traced pupil passes through the element rims; rays launched beyond the admitted pupil are stopped by the iris. These calculations support the illustrative field, without establishing the patent's usable field or measured throughput.

The R5 and rear clear radii are smaller than the drawn Figure 1 section. R5 is drawn at approximately 9.0 mm against the model's 8.1 mm. Drawing half-heights are approximately 7.2 mm for Elements 4–5 and 7.5 mm for Element 6, compared with the model's rear radii of 6.5/6.1 mm and 6.2/5.5 mm. The model keeps the smaller estimated radii.
