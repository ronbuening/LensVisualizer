# COOKE 100mm f/6.3 (US 2,354,503 Design)

## Patent and Design Identification

**Patent:** [US 2,354,503 A, Optical Objective of the Telephoto Type](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/2354503)

**Inventors:** Arthur Cox

**Assignee:** Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited

**Filed:** 12 December 1942

**Published:** 25 July 1944

**Priority:** British filing, 1 December 1941

**Example details:** Sole numerical example, repeated in claim 9.

The patent describes a four-element telephoto objective at f/6.3. Its dimensions, normalized to an equivalent focal length of 1.000, are multiplied by 100 for a nominal 100 mm model. The printed table computes to EFL 99.49 mm, 0.5% below nominal, with paraxial back focus 50.28 mm. Rounding may explain the difference, but remains unproven; the figure and both tables agree, and no misprint has been identified. The image plane is the computed paraxial plane because the patent supplies no back focus.

No commercial lens is documented as using this numerical prescription.

## Optical Architecture

Four elements form three air-separated glass groups, arranged **1+2 | 3 | 4**. The front convergent member is a cemented doublet, joined at R2. Elements 3 and 4 form the rear divergent member, an air-spaced doublet with a 0.1 mm axial gap. All seven refracting boundaries are spherical.

The positive front member and negative rear member shorten the objective relative to its focal length. The glass-to-glass vertex span is 32.8 mm; the first vertex to the paraxial image plane is 83.08 mm. The front cemented member has a computed focal length of +56.11 mm in air.

The rear air lens is thicker at its axis than at its margin. The patent gives particular importance to this pair of nearly matching curvatures and to a rear clear aperture large enough to pass oblique light (description, PDF p. 2; claims, PDF p. 3).

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### Element 1 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.613, ν = 55.7. Glass: BACD8 (Hoya), spectral proxy. Focal length in air: +23.34 mm.

This positive crown element supplies the convergent power of the front member. Its rear surface is cemented to Element 2.

### Element 2 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.676, ν = 32.3. Glass: Unmatched. Focal length in air: −36.17 mm.

This more dispersive negative element opposes the front crown's power. The index step across their cemented interface is 0.063, exceeding the patent's minimum of 0.06.

### Element 3 — Negative Meniscus

nD = 1.613, ν = 55.7. Glass: BACD8 (Hoya), spectral proxy. Focal length in air: −37.51 mm.

This meniscus gives the rear doublet member its divergent power. Its 1.4 mm central thickness and strongly curved front surface follow the printed prescription.

### Element 4 — Positive Meniscus

nD = 1.652, ν = 33.5. Glass: Unmatched. Focal length in air: +84.42 mm.

This positive meniscus completes the divergent rear member. The 0.1 mm axial space between Elements 3 and 4 is preserved as air, rather than a cemented interface.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent prints indices for the D line, taken as sodium at 589.3 nm, and Abbe numbers. It names no supplier or glass catalogue code. Elements 1 and 3 use Hoya BACD8 as a spectral proxy, with Δn = −0.00193 and Δν = +0.07 (catalog minus printed at sodium D). Their printed index and Abbe number are retained; the catalog curve supplies only the dispersion shape and does not identify the historical supplier or melt. Elements 2 and 4 use Abbe-based dispersion because no catalog row meets the site's spectral-proxy tolerance of 0.002 in index and 0.25 in Abbe number at the printed D line.

The glass choices pair a less dispersive positive crown with a more dispersive negative element in front, then reverse the element-power order in the rear member. The patent does not give C-, F- or g-line indices or partial dispersions. Chromatic predictions combine the BACD8 dispersion shape with Abbe-based estimates for Elements 2 and 4; these data do not establish secondary-spectrum or apochromatic performance.

## Focus Mechanism

The model is focused at infinity; the patent gives no close-focus data or movement schedule.

The diaphragm is not drawn or dimensioned. Its inferred position is 1.5 mm behind R3 and 22 mm before R4, retaining the printed 23.5 mm inter-member gap. This places the stop at 6.6 mm from the first vertex, with 0.784 mm clearance behind R3's outer rim. No glass crosses the diaphragm plane.

The inferred stop follows the patent's oblique-beam mechanism more closely than a stop near the middle of the gap. At this station, the rear/front clear-aperture ratio needed by the external faces is about 1.09 at 10.0° and 1.18 at 12.0°, close to the printed 1.20. The computed iris radius is 6.895 mm for paraxial f/6.3; its position and bore are modeling choices rather than historical measurements. At this iris the real marginal ray gives about f/6.48, because the front member's spherical aberration carries the real f/6.3 ray to 7.11 mm at the stop.

## Field, Clear Apertures and Model Limits

The patent gives no exact field angle for the numerical example. It describes the design as especially suitable for semi-angular fields of 10.0° or more and bounds the rear/front aperture ratio between 3.825 tan θ and 6.50 tan θ. The example's ratio of 1.20 implies a half-field between about 10.5° and 17.4°. The model uses ±12.0°, within that interval.

Real chief rays at ±12.0° clear every rim and reach image semi-height 21.60 mm at the paraxial plane. This is a traced field height, not a patent-specified image circle or film format. The off-axis bundles remain partly vignetted at the internal rear rims.

The inferred clear radii are 8.2 mm at R1–R2, 8.1 mm at R3, 8.3 mm at R4–R6 and 9.84 mm at R7. The external ratio is 9.84/8.2 = 1.20. On the drawing, the front cemented member reaches about 10.3 mm and R3's curve ends inside it near 9.4 mm. The model preserves this inward step. The rear outlines reach about 10.7 mm, with R4's curve ending near 9.0 mm. The overall vertex spacing and the R1/R7 arcs support that approximate scale, but the drawn rear air gap is about 0.35–0.40 mm rather than the printed 0.10 mm, and the outline ratio is about 1.04 rather than 1.20. The drawing therefore supplies proportions rather than consistent clear-aperture dimensions. With the printed curves, R5 and R6 meet at about 8.88 mm height; their inferred 8.3 mm limits preserve a real air space. No flat rim land is established by the schematic outlines.

At the 8.3 mm internal rear rims, the 0.1 mm axial air gap narrows to 0.014 mm, consuming 85.8% of that gap. The minimum glass edge thickness is 1.031 mm. Wide-open geometric transmission is 98.6% at 10.0° and 86.7% at 12.0°; the rear member preserves real off-axis vignetting. The paraxial image plane, inferred diaphragm and estimated clear apertures support optical exploration; measured production performance is unknown.
