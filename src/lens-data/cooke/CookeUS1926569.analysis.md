# COOKE 97mm f/1 (US 1,926,569 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 1,926,569 A, _Photographic and Projection Lens_  
**Application Number:** Serial No. 529,168  
**Filed:** April 10, 1931 (British priority April 11, 1930)  
**Granted:** September 12, 1933  
**Inventor:** Arthur Warmisham  
**Assignee:** Kapella Limited, Leicester, England  
**Example details:** The Figure 1 table on specification page 1; printed at an equivalent focal length of .97 and multiplied by 100 here.

The patent starts from an older five-member objective, which it identifies with Fig. 10 of British Patent No. 168,923: a positive front member, two biconcave negative members and two positive rear members, all single lenses. Its stated aim is a larger aperture than that type had reached, with better correction of zonal spherical aberration and a flatter field. It gives two numerical examples. Figure 1 makes the first and fifth members cemented pairs; Figure 2 makes the first and fourth members cemented pairs. This file follows the Figure 1 table alone, which the patent describes as "spherically corrected for an aperture F/1.0" with a flattened field of 14 degrees semi-angle.

Arthur Warmisham was a lens designer at Taylor, Taylor & Hobson of Leicester, the makers of Cooke lenses, and Kapella Limited, also of Leicester, was the assignee of other Warmisham and Lee patents of these years. The design is therefore listed under Cooke as work from the Taylor-Hobson lens office. Warmisham's position at the firm rests here on search excerpts of Cooke company-history pages; the pages themselves were not read.

No production lens has been identified with this example. The patent names no product, and no Cooke or Taylor-Hobson catalogue lens at f/1 from the early 1930s was found. A 1949 survey of objectives of f/1 and faster, quoted in a forum thread (see Sources), mentions two Warmisham f/1 designs, one of them derived from the four-element air-spaced Dogmar type. That description fits this patent's layout, but the survey itself and its reference list were not seen, so this is weak evidence: it does not confirm that the survey cites this patent, and it describes a patent design, not a product. The 97 mm focal length is a modelling scale that comes from multiplying the printed table by 100. It is not a marketed focal length.

The scaled prescription traces to a paraxial focal length of 97.06 mm, 0.06 % above the printed .97. No radius, thickness, index or Abbe number is altered.

## Optical Architecture

The objective has seven spherical elements in five air-spaced members, with the power sequence positive, negative, negative, positive, positive:

1. a cemented front pair, L1 + L2, focal length +113.67 mm;
2. a biconcave negative lens, L3, −119.88 mm;
3. a second biconcave negative lens, L4, −72.79 mm;
4. a single biconvex lens, L5, +102.63 mm;
5. a cemented rear pair, L6 + L7, +99.27 mm.

The patent's two distinguishing features are both present in the table. First, each cemented surface is convergent and concave toward the long conjugate: R2 and R11 both have a higher-index crown in front of a lower-index glass, so the buried surface adds positive power while its strong curvature works against the spherical aberration of the outer faces. Second, the two negative lenses are unsymmetrical and turn their weaker faces toward each other: L3 has radii of 95.24 and 666.6 mm, L4 has 175.5 and 71.43 mm, and the two weak faces (666.6 and 175.5 mm) bound the central air space.

Figure 1 draws the diaphragm in that central air space, S2, between the two negative lenses. The patent prints no diaphragm position or diameter.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.6234, V = 56.3. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.6234 / V 56.3); not named in the patent. f = +81.79 mm.

The front element is a thick biconvex crown, 30.5 mm on axis against a 97 mm focal length. Its front radius of 84.3 mm carries most of the first member's power. Its rear face is the cemented surface R2.

### L2 — Negative Meniscus

nD = 1.529, V = 51.6. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.529 / V 51.6); not named in the patent. f = −252.49 mm.

A thin (3 mm) meniscus of low index cemented behind L1. Taken alone it is weakly negative, but the cemented surface it shares with L1 is convergent because the index falls from 1.6234 to 1.529 across a face that is concave toward the object. The pair has a focal length of +113.67 mm.

### L3 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.694, V = 30.7. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.694 / V 30.7); not named in the patent. f = −119.88 mm.

The first of the two dense-flint negative lenses. Its stronger face (95.24 mm) is toward the front and its nearly flat face (666.6 mm) is toward the diaphragm.

### L4 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.694, V = 30.7. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.694 / V 30.7); not named in the patent. f = −72.79 mm.

The second negative lens, in the same glass as L3 and the stronger of the two. Its weaker face (175.5 mm) is toward the diaphragm and its strong rear face (71.43 mm) is toward the rear members. Together L3 and L4 supply the negative power that flattens the field and carry the chromatic correction.

### L5 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.6437, V = 48.3. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.6437 / V 48.3); not named in the patent. f = +102.63 mm.

A single positive lens, almost plano-convex: the front radius is 833.3 mm and the rear radius 71.01 mm. It is 22 mm thick and is separated from the rear pair by only 0.2 mm.

### L6 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.6234, V = 56.3. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.6234 / V 56.3); not named in the patent. f = +69.75 mm.

The thickest element, 47 mm on axis, in the same crown as L1. Its rear face is the second cemented surface, R11, with a radius of 63.05 mm.

### L7 — Negative Meniscus

nD = 1.529, V = 51.6. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.529 / V 51.6); not named in the patent. f = −199.27 mm.

A 4.9 mm meniscus in the same low-index glass as L2, cemented behind L6. As in the front member, the cemented surface is convergent and concave toward the object. The pair has a focal length of +99.27 mm.

## Glass Identification

The patent defines each material by "the mean refractive index nD" followed by "the Abbe V number". These are sodium D-line values, and the data file records them as such. Four glasses are used:

| Elements | nD     | V    | Kind                 |
| -------- | ------ | ---- | -------------------- |
| L1, L6   | 1.6234 | 56.3 | Dense barium crown   |
| L2, L7   | 1.529  | 51.6 | Low-index light flint |
| L3, L4   | 1.694  | 30.7 | Dense flint          |
| L5       | 1.6437 | 48.3 | Barium flint         |

The patent names no maker or glass type, and none of the four pairs matches a catalogue glass closely enough to be named, so each element is labelled as unmatched with its printed values. Colour in the model is estimated from nD and V alone.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent gives data for an object at infinity only and describes no focusing arrangement. The model is fixed at infinity. The image distance of 60.5498 mm is the computed paraxial back focus; the patent prints none.

## Aperture, Stop and Clear Diameters

The patent prints one diameter: "Clear aperture (front) .97", equal to the focal length, which is the f/1 beam at the front surface. The 48.5 mm semi-diameter of the front cemented pair is that printed value.

Everything else about the apertures is inferred:

- The diaphragm sits 1.9 mm behind the rear face of L3 and 5.6 mm in front of L4, inside the 7.5 mm air space where Figure 1 draws it. In that position it clears both glass faces.
- The stop radius is set by the paraxial rule for f/1 and comes to 34.04 mm. This design has strong pupil aberration, so the real axial ray that grazes the stop enters the front surface at a height of 44.6 mm, not 48.5 mm. By real rays the model therefore passes about f/1.09. The full 48.5 mm ray would need a stop radius of about 37.9 mm.
- The other semi-diameters are measured from Figure 1, which is drawn close to scale (the first-to-last vertex span matches the table within about 1 %, and the drawn diaphragm opening, about 35 mm in radius, agrees with the 34.04 mm stop). The two biconcave elements are drawn square-cut at about 41 mm and carry 42 mm. L5 is drawn running to a knife edge at about 50 mm, where its nearly flat front face meets the strongly curved rear face; it carries 49.8 mm, leaving an edge of about 0.1 mm. The cemented rear pair is drawn square-cut at about 50 mm and carries 50 mm on all three faces.
- These rims clear the model's axial beam and the chief ray of the patent's 14 degree semi-field (19.4 mm at most, at R12). Geometric illumination at 12.9 degrees off axis is about 76 % of the axial value with the iris wide open.

On axis the printed prescription has a longitudinal spherical aberration of −0.89 mm at half aperture, −0.47 mm at 0.7 of the aperture and +5.58 mm at the full f/1 margin, with the zero crossing between 0.7 and 0.85 of the aperture.
