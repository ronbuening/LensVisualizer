# COOKE 100mm f/2.8 (US 2,433,438 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,433,438 A, “Telephoto Optical Objective”

**Inventors:** Arthur Cox

**Assignee:** Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited

**Filed:** 1 May 1946; British priority 18 June 1945

**Published:** 30 December 1947

**Example details:** Example I, Figure 1; the same numerical prescription appears in claim 17.

The patent gives three numerical examples. This model uses Example I, with its normalized focal length of 1.000 multiplied by 100 to give dimensions in millimetres. The nominal focal length is therefore 100 mm at f/2.8. No commercial product is documented as using this prescription.

The eight signed radii, five glass thicknesses, two air separations and five D-line index/Abbe-number pairs follow the printed table. The printed back focus gives a 53.75 mm image gap. Calculation gives EFL 100.02 mm and paraxial back focus 53.777 mm, placing paraxial focus 0.027 mm beyond the retained image plane.

## Optical Architecture

Five spherical elements form three groups: a weakly convergent front cemented doublet, a convergent meniscus and a divergent rear cemented doublet. Their focal lengths in air are approximately +309.58, +77.53 and −78.08 mm. The convergent front system and divergent rear member produce the telephoto arrangement described by the patent. The front-to-last-surface distance is 32.34 mm; the distance from the first surface to the printed image plane is 86.09 mm.

The cemented interfaces are R2 and R7. R2 (−76.18 mm) and R3 (+76.18 mm) bound the 3.47 mm thickness of element 2. A separate 0.10 mm air gap between R3 and R4 opens with height. These surfaces remain distinct.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### Element 1 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.623, ν = 60.3. Glass: Unmatched. Focal length in air: +49.42 mm.

The first element supplies positive power to the front doublet and is cemented to the more dispersive negative element 2.

### Element 2 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.699, ν = 30.5. Glass: Unmatched. Focal length in air: −53.99 mm.

This element reduces the front doublet's net positive power. Its lower Abbe number provides the dispersive contrast used for chromatic correction.

### Element 3 — Positive Meniscus

nD = 1.623, ν = 60.3. Glass: Unmatched. Focal length in air: +77.53 mm.

The air-spaced meniscus completes the convergent front system. Its front surface is more strongly curved than its rear surface.

### Element 4 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.547, ν = 45.7. Glass: LLF1 (Schott), spectral proxy. Focal length in air: +35.81 mm.

The positive front element of the rear doublet is cemented to element 5 at R7. The patent uses this interface to contribute to spherical-aberration correction.

### Element 5 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.613, ν = 57.6. Glass: Unmatched. Focal length in air: −19.99 mm.

The strong negative rear element gives the final doublet its divergent power. The index increase across R7 is 0.066; the patent states the magnitude of that surface's dispersive power as 0.0665 times the power of the whole objective.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent's sodium D-line indices and Abbe numbers are retained for all five elements. Element 4 uses Schott LLF1 as a spectral proxy. Its catalog values differ by ΔnD = +0.00103 and Δν = +0.04 at the sodium D line, within the site's spectral-proxy tolerance of 0.002 in index and 0.25 in Abbe number. The catalog curve supplies the dispersion shape, while the historical glass, supplier and melt remain unknown. Elements 1, 2, 3 and 5 use Abbe-based dispersion because their printed pairs have no catalog row within that tolerance. The patent supplies no additional spectral indices or anomalous-dispersion properties.

## Focus Mechanism

The model is focused at infinity. The patent gives no close-focus distances or group-movement law, so no finite-focus state or marketed minimum focus distance is assigned.

## Aperture, Field and Model Limits

The patent gives neither a stop position nor a stop diameter. Figure 1 draws no diaphragm; the dash-dot line in the rear air space is a dimension leader. The model places the stop 1.50 mm behind R5 and 2.47 mm before R6, at 17.47 mm from the first vertex. Its 15.016 mm radius follows paraxial f/2.80. At that iris the real-ray entrance-equivalent aperture and image-space sine aperture are both about f/2.95. A real f/2.80 axial beam would require a 15.898 mm iris radius at the same station.

All clear semi-diameters are estimates: 18.6, 18.6, 17.7, 16.5, 16.3, 13.9, 13.9 and 10.2 mm on R1–R8. Figure 1 supplies their relative proportions. The front doublet's short rear chamfer appears as a tapered edge, and the rear doublet has a square front rim and a narrower rear face. The drawn meniscus is thinner than the printed thickness. The 16.5 mm R4 and 13.9 mm R6 radii clear the f/2.8 axial ray heights of 16.42 and 13.83 mm. R7 shares R6's height. R8's 10.2 mm radius retains edge illumination while narrowing the rear outline. All rims clear the 10.0° chief ray. The smallest glass edge thickness is 1.56 mm, and the 0.10 mm front air gap widens away from the axis.

The patent states no field angle or image format. The selected ±10.0° field is illustrative. Wide-open geometric transmission is 100% through 5.0°, 99.9% at 6.7°, 99.4% at 8.3° and 97.4% at 10.0°. This describes pupil clipping, without accounting for reflection, absorption or the cosine-fourth falloff.
