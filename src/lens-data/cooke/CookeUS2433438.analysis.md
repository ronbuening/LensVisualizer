# COOKE 100mm f/2.8 (US 2,433,438 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,433,438 A, “Telephoto Optical Objective”

**Inventors:** Arthur Cox

**Assignee:** Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited

**Filed:** 1 May 1946; British priority 18 June 1945

**Published:** 30 December 1947

**Example details:** Example I, Figure 1; the same numerical prescription appears in claim 17.

The patent gives three numerical examples. This model uses Example I, with its normalized focal length of 1.000 multiplied by 100 to give dimensions in millimetres. The nominal focal length is therefore 100 mm at f/2.8. No commercial product is documented as using this prescription.

The eight signed radii, five glass thicknesses, two air separations and five D-line index/Abbe-number pairs follow the printed table. The printed back focus gives a 53.75 mm image gap. Calculation gives EFL 100.021277 mm and paraxial back focus 53.776780 mm, placing paraxial focus 0.026780 mm beyond the retained image plane. The catalog design focal length records the calculated EFL rounded to 100.02 mm.

## Optical Architecture

Five spherical elements form three groups: a weakly convergent front cemented doublet, a convergent meniscus and a divergent rear cemented doublet. Their focal lengths in air are approximately +309.58, +77.53 and −78.08 mm. The convergent front system and divergent rear member produce the telephoto arrangement described by the patent. The front-to-last-surface distance is 32.34 mm; the distance from the first surface to the printed image plane is 86.09 mm.

The cemented interfaces are R2 and R7. R2 (−76.18 mm) and R3 (+76.18 mm) bound the 3.47 mm thickness of element 2. A separate 0.10 mm air gap between R3 and R4 opens with height. These surfaces remain distinct.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### Element 1 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.623, ν = 60.3. Glass: Unmatched. Focal length in air: +49.42 mm.

The first element supplies positive power to the front doublet and is cemented to the more dispersive negative element 2.

### Element 2 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.699, ν = 30.5. Glass: Unmatched. Focal length in air: −53.99 mm.

This element reduces the front doublet's net positive power. Its lower Abbe number provides the dispersive contrast used for chromatic correction.

### Element 3 — Positive Meniscus

nD = 1.623, ν = 60.3. Glass: Unmatched. Focal length in air: +77.53 mm.

The air-spaced meniscus completes the convergent front system. Its front surface is more strongly curved than its rear surface.

### Element 4 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.547, ν = 45.7. Glass: Unmatched. Focal length in air: +35.81 mm.

The positive front element of the rear doublet is cemented to element 5 at R7. The patent uses this interface to contribute to spherical-aberration correction.

### Element 5 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.613, ν = 57.6. Glass: Unmatched. Focal length in air: −19.99 mm.

The strong negative rear element gives the final doublet its divergent power. The index increase across R7 is 0.066; the patent states the magnitude of that surface's dispersive power as 0.0665 times the power of the whole objective.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent explicitly specifies the sodium D line and Abbe numbers. All five elements retain `indexReference: "D"`. No catalog row matches the printed coordinates closely enough to name a glass, so each label states its published nD and ν. The glass suppliers and melts remain unknown, and no additional spectral indices or anomalous-dispersion properties are supplied.

## Focus Mechanism

The model represents infinity focus. The patent gives no close-focus distances or group-movement law, so no finite-focus state or marketed minimum focus distance is assigned.

## Aperture, Field and Model Limits

The patent gives neither a stop position nor a stop diameter. Figure 1 draws no diaphragm; the dash-dot line in the rear air space is a dimension leader. The model places the stop 1.50 mm behind R5 and 2.47 mm before R6, at 17.47 mm from the first vertex. Its 15.015980 mm radius follows the paraxial f/2.8 aperture. At that iris the real-ray entrance-equivalent aperture is f/2.946277 and the image-space sine aperture is f/2.952537. A real f/2.8 axial beam would require a 15.897974 mm iris radius at the same station.

All clear semi-diameters are estimates: 18.6, 18.6, 17.7, 17.7, 16.3, 15.1, 14.5 and 12.3 mm on R1–R8. They clear the axial beam and the 10° chief ray. Figure 1 draws the rear doublet narrower than these estimates. The smallest glass edge thickness is 1.559596 mm, and the 0.10 mm front air gap widens away from the axis.

The patent states no field angle or image format. The selected ±10° field is illustrative. The stored 36.968666 mm circle is twice the real 10° chief-ray height at the printed image plane, rather than a published image circle. Earlier tracing found slight front-rim clipping at 10°, while the chief ray remained clear.
