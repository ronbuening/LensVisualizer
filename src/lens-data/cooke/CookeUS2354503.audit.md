# COOKE 100mm f/6.3 (US 2,354,503 Design) — Audit

## Source

US 2,354,503 A, Optical Objective of the Telephoto Type, names Arthur Cox and Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited. The US application was filed on 12 December 1942, claiming British priority of 1 December 1941, and published on 25 July 1944. There is one numerical example, repeated in claim 9.

## Transcription

Two independent readings of the page images agree on all seven signed radii, six glass thicknesses and air separations, four D-line indices and four Abbe numbers. The figure, description table and claim 9 table agree. No prescription transcription error or supported digit correction was found.

The topology is four elements in three air-separated glass groups. R2 is the front cemented interface; Elements III and IV form the patent's air-spaced rear doublet member. The printed checks reproduce: index step 0.063, rear air-lens curvature difference about 0.22 of the objective power, inter-member space 0.235, and rear-element thicknesses 0.014 and 0.027 in the patent's normalized units.

## First-Order Properties

The patent states equivalent focal length 1.000 and f/6.3. At the model's nominal 100 mm focal length, the printed prescription gives EFL 99.4874033503 mm, paraxial BFD 50.2778896576 mm, glass-to-glass track 32.8 mm and first-vertex-to-image distance 83.0778896576 mm. The −0.5126% focal-length difference is consistent with the printed precision; no misprint has been identified. The stored image gap coincides with the paraxial image plane.

The initial inferred stop was 13 mm behind R3, with iris radius 5.2763231238 mm. That station caused front-member clipping inconsistent with the patent's explanation of an enlarged rear aperture for oblique beams. It has been replaced by the frontward station described below.

## Values Not Printed

The absolute clear radii, diaphragm station and bore, back focus, image-plane position, physical film format and near-focus behavior are not specified. The exact field of the numerical example is also unstated; the aperture-ratio inequalities imply about 10.5–17.4° from the axis. The earlier ±5° display field was a modeling choice.

The clear radii remain inferred. Their external rear/front ratio is 1.20. At the 8.3 mm R5–R6 rims, the 0.1 mm axial air gap retains 0.01419 mm edge clearance, with no geometric allowance changed. The minimum glass edge thickness is 1.0310 mm.

## Product

No documented commercial embodiment links this numerical prescription to a named lens. The Cooke Design name identifies the patent design without assigning a production model.

## Corrections

The earlier description of Elements III and IV as separate rear singlets has been replaced by the patent's air-spaced rear doublet terminology. Statements restricting the design to ±5° and aperture calculations tied to the former stop position are superseded by the present inferred stop and ±12° field. Earlier text about file preparation and software exercises has been removed.

## Remaining Questions

Open: the exact historical iris position, absolute apertures and manufactured glass identities remain unknown. The figure suggests front/rear outlines near 10.3/10.7 mm, larger than the retained external clear radii; the internal R5–R6 space closes near 8.94 mm height, so those drawing outlines cannot be applied throughout the rear member. R5 and R6 remain 8.3 mm, below the 8.9 mm geometric cap. No image format or exact image circle is established.
