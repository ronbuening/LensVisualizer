# COOKE 100mm f/2.67 (US 2,502,508 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,502,508 A, _Highly Corrected Three Component Optical Objectives of Large Aperture_](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/2502508), by Gordon Henry Cook, assigned to Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited. The application was filed on 29 November 1948, with British priority of 18 October 1948, and published on 4 April 1950.

Example I is in column 3 on PDF page 3; Figure 1 is on PDF page 1. The bibliography and definition of D-line indices are on PDF page 2. The two numerical examples differ only in their rear cemented doublets.

## Transcription Check

The page images confirm all seven signed radii, four glass thicknesses, two air separations and four D-line index/Abbe-number pairs. No transcription mismatch was found. The dimensions are normalized to focal length 1.000 without a physical unit; multiplication by 100 gives the nominal 100 mm scale used here.

The 0.114F rear air separation becomes 11.4 mm, divided into 7.2 mm before the stop and 4.2 mm after it. R6 is the cemented contact between Elements 3 and 4, with no intervening air space. Figure 1 confirms the biconvex, biconcave, biconvex and negative-meniscus shapes. All seven refracting surfaces are spherical; no variable spacings, cover glass or filter plate are given.

## First-Order Check

The calculated EFL is 99.97 mm, 0.03 mm below the nominal 100 mm scale. Paraxial f/2.67 agrees with the printed aperture. Back focus is 76.30 mm; the patent prints no value for comparison. The image plane is at paraxial focus, 76.30 mm behind R7 and 122.50 mm from R1.

The 46.2 mm front-to-rear track agrees with the printed 0.462F. Normalized component powers of +1.744, −3.230 and +1.924 agree with the printed approximate +1.75, −3.23 and +1.92. The rear pair's index difference is 0.09966, above the required 0.07.

The stop radius is 13.92 mm. At this opening, real marginal rays give an entrance equivalent of f/2.73 and an image-space sine equivalent of f/2.74. A ray entering at the paraxial f/2.67 pupil edge would require a 14.25 mm stop radius; the model uses the smaller opening.

The surfaces stay clear of one another and the middle element stays clear of the stop plane. The iris sets the full aperture. The axial beam and 5.0° chief ray clear the rims, while the front component partly vignettes the off-axis pupil.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The stop position and bore are inferred. The stop is 30 mm behind R1, inside the printed R4–R5 air space. The final image gap is calculated from paraxial focus.

The clear radii at R1–R7 are 18.75, 18.75, 18, 17, 17, 16 and 17 mm. All are estimates. R1 uses the printed effective front diameter of 0.375F, or 37.5 mm at this scale; an effective aperture does not establish a physical glass rim. Figure 1 suggests larger rear rims and an almost closed edge on Element 3 where R5 meets R6. That element closes near a radius of 17.1 mm, constraining any interpretation of the undimensioned outline.

The ±5.0° field is illustrative. The patent describes a reduced angular field without giving an angle or image format, so no image-circle diameter is assigned. `focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. No practical minimum focus distance or near-focus movement is documented.

The selectable aperture range extends from f/2.67 to f/22, with `aperture.maxFNumber` set to 22; the patent gives no minimum iris opening. The four printed D-line index/Abbe pairs are preserved. No exact catalog pair, supplier, melt identity, additional spectral-line index or partial dispersion is established. Colour estimates from the printed pairs do not establish anomalous dispersion or measured broadband performance.

## Product Identification

No commercial product is documented as using this prescription. Period Taylor Hobson Ortal and Adotal lenses are not tied to it. US 2,502,509 concerns a separate six-element design and does not identify this four-element objective.

The nominal 100 mm title expresses the chosen scale of the patent's unit-focal-length table. No mount, production image format, blade count or shape, marketed focal length, marketed aperture or marketed close-focus distance is assigned.

## Corrections, 2026-11-21

- The maker is identified as Cooke, and the title and catalog key use the maker-and-patent form. The patent number gained the publication suffix A. Example I, Gordon Henry Cook, Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited and the 1950 publication year identify the same source.
- The subtitle is “US 2,502,508 A, Example I — Taylor Hobson / Cook”. The specification chips are “4 ELEMENTS / 3 GROUPS”, “ALL SPHERICAL”, “DESIGN f = 100 mm” and “DESIGN F/2.67”; the 100 mm scale comes from multiplying the printed unit-focal-length dimensions by 100.
- The catalog design focal length changed from 100 to 99.97 mm to state the calculated EFL. The nominal title remains at the chosen 100 mm scale.
- Element labels became Element 1 through Element 4. Their shape descriptions became Biconvex Positive, Biconcave Negative, Biconvex Positive and Negative Meniscus, matching the signed radii. Optical roles and standalone focal lengths of +57.35, −30.96, +31.77 and −82.97 mm were added.
- Elements 3 and 4 share the cemented identifier C1 and a bracket spanning R5–R7. The group brackets identify the front, middle and rear components described by the patent. The assembled rear doublet has focal length +51.97 mm.
- The glass labels explicitly record the unmatched printed pairs: nD 1.62250 / ν 60.4, nD 1.62266 / ν 36.0, nD 1.61380 / ν 57.5 and nD 1.51414 / ν 56.5. For every element, Δn = 0 and Δν = 0; the D-line reference remains as printed. No catalog curve is assigned.

The resulting model has EFL 99.97 mm, paraxial f/2.67, back focus 76.30 mm, stop radius 13.92 mm and half-field 5.0°. The printed radii, thicknesses, spaces and glass values, the inferred stop and rims, and the calculated image plane retain their original values.

## Remaining Questions

- The individual glass rims are undimensioned. The rear outline in Figure 1 differs from the estimated R5/R6/R7 clear radii of 17/16/17 mm, especially at Element 3's almost closed edge.
- The historical stop position and bore, glass suppliers and melt identities, and full dispersion curves remain unknown.
- No source establishes a production product, mount, image format, image circle, diaphragm blade count or shape, minimum iris opening, marketed specifications or close-focus mechanism.
- The usable historical field is unknown; chief-ray passage at the illustrative 5.0° half-field does not establish an unvignetted edge bundle.
