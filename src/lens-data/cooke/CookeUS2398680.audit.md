# COOKE 100mm f/1.5 (US 2,398,680 Design) — Audit

## Source

Arthur Warmisham's US 2,398,680 A, “Optical Objective,” was assigned to Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited, filed on 21 September 1944 and published on 16 April 1946, with British priority dated 6 July 1943. It gives five numerical examples, I–V. This model follows Example I and Figure 1. The prescription appears on PDF page 4 and is repeated in claim 15 on PDF page 6; the back focal lengths appear on PDF page 5.

## Transcription

Two independent readings of the patent page images agree on all twelve signed radii, eleven axial thicknesses and separations, seven sodium-D indices and seven Abbe numbers. Every dimensional prescription entry is multiplied by 100. The central 20.06 mm space is divided into 11.10 and 8.96 mm around an inferred stop. R4 and R7 remain cemented boundaries, and the three 0.2 mm airspaces remain air. No numerical transcription error was found.

## First-order

The calculated effective focal length is 100.001709099 mm, the paraxial f-number is 1.500 and the back focal distance is 57.321981470 mm. The patent's corresponding values are 100 mm, f/1.5 and 57.31 mm. The image plane is the calculated paraxial plane, 0.011981470 mm beyond the printed back focus. Optical vertex-to-vertex track is 80.52 mm; first vertex to image is 137.841981470 mm.

The inferred stop radius is 20.723959727 mm; an exact axial ray at nominal marginal height reaches 20.651007860 mm there. The sum of the magnitudes of R5 and R6 is 0.6304 of the nominal focal length, within the printed 0.63–0.64 interval. The rear-component power ratio is about 42.1% by thick-lens calculation or 42.2% by thin-lens calculation, against the printed 42.3%.

## Not Printed

The patent supplies no iris station or diameter, clear semi-diameters, numerical field, image format, image-circle diameter or close-focus data. The iris station, opening and rims are inferred; the 10° half-field is illustrative. The 17.6172 mm chief-ray height at that angle does not establish coverage.

Earlier geometry checks found positive glass and air clearances, no drawing trim, a minimum shared-radius glass thickness of 1.887 mm, central air clearance of 3.210 mm and minimum glass rim-endpoint thickness of 0.463 mm. The central gap uses about 84% of its axial length in sag; the maximum rim angle is about 56.18°. A separate comparison at the individual rim endpoints gave 2.527 mm central clearance. Checks against straight tapered sidewalls between the estimated rims found a minimum radial margin of about 0.00655 mm. These results concern the authored blank shapes, not actual manufacturing tolerances.

Earlier ray calculations confirmed that all chief rays reach the image over the illustrative field, with marginal-ray clipping off axis. Earlier display inspection retained the 41.447919 mm iris diameter after reloading. A 2% increase in iris radius introduced axial clipping; shifts of 0.5 mm in either direction changed off-axis illumination. Monochromatic geometric MTF at the paraxial plane converged numerically at 0°, approximately 5.0349° and 10° over 0–40 cycles/mm. The largest final refinement changes were approximately 0.000063, 0.001060 and 0.000988 at those fields. These results do not establish measured brightness or broadband image quality.

## Product

No commercial product is documented. The patent describes photographic, projection or similar uses without identifying a marketed lens. Earlier comparisons found no matching prescription among the designs examined, but they do not establish an exhaustive production history.

## Corrections

An earlier rounding discrepancy in the inferred image-circle value was corrected without changing the prescription. That diameter is now removed because its illustrative field supplies no evidence of an image circle. No printed prescription value has been corrected or adjusted.

## Remaining Questions

Figure 1 is drawn approximately to scale, but several inferred rims differ materially from its optical outlines. Narrowing the model to the drawn rims preserves the axial aperture but increases off-axis vignetting. Historical glass identities, actual iris geometry, field coverage and any production association remain unknown.
