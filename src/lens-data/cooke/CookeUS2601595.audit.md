# COOKE 100mm f/3.5 (US 2,601,595 Design) — Audit

## Source

US 2,601,595 A, Example VII and Figure 7, by Gordon Henry Cook, assigned to Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited. British priority: 15 February 1951; US filing: 13 March 1951; publication: 24 June 1952. The table is on PDF p. 6, column 7, and Figure 7 is on PDF p. 2. The patent gives F = 1.000, f/3.5 and a 25° half-field. The model expresses the dimensions at nominal 100 mm focal length.

## Transcription

Two independent readings of the page images agree on all eleven signed radii, six glass thicknesses, four air separations, six sodium-D indices and six Abbe numbers. Figure 7 agrees with the table. R2 is cemented; R9/R10 are separated by the printed 0.005F rear air gap. Example VII is the broken-contact variant of Example IV, whose rear pair is cemented.

## First-order

The initial and current prescriptions give EFL 100.024915929 mm, nominal f/3.5, paraxial BFD 80.319697025 mm and first-vertex-to-image distance 118.519697025 mm. The EFL is 0.024916% above nominal. The stored image plane is paraxial, with no additional defocus. The iris radius is 11.066614058 mm. The rear air gap remains 0.5 mm on axis and widens toward the edge. Initial 11 mm rims at R3/R5 restricted the axial aperture; that issue is closed by the changes below.

## Not printed

The patent gives no back focus, total track, close-focus distance, exact iris station or iris diameter. The modeled iris bisects the 5.1 mm R5–R6 air space. Its radius follows the stated f/3.5. R4, R7 and R9 clear semi-diameters are inferred; other rims interpret suggested diameters and chamfers. Detailed bevel depths are unknown. The image circle is computed from the stated field, with no production film format assigned.

## Product

No production lens is documented for this prescription. The companion US 2,601,593 A describes a different, wider-coverage design. GB 295,519 A, cited in the patent, supplies historical context rather than this numerical prescription.

## Corrections

The initial rim interpretation was too restrictive for the printed aperture. The patent says its proposed diameters exceed the axial beam's needs, supporting the small R3/R5 increases below. No optical radius, thickness, refractive index, Abbe number, stop station or image plane is changed.

The rear-diameter prose repeats R8 in its 0.26F list. This is probably a misprint for R9, because Example IV names R9/R10. No source radius is corrected, and R9 remains an inferred aperture.

## Remaining questions

The clear rims have not been established by measurement of Figure 7. The inferred R4/R7/R9 apertures and detailed chamfers remain uncertain. Historical glass suppliers, measured broadband performance and a production product identity are unknown.
