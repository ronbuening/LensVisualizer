# COOKE 100mm f/3.5 (US 2,601,595 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,601,595 A, _Four Component Optical Objective_](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/d6/6a/80/c3a3f738372978/US2601595.pdf), by Gordon Henry Cook, assigned to Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited. British priority: 15 February 1951; US filing: 13 March 1951, serial 215,255; publication: 24 June 1952. The attribution is on PDF p. 3. Example VII is tabulated on PDF p. 6, column 7; Figure 7 is on PDF p. 2. The patent prints F = 1.000, f/3.5 and a 25.0° half-field. All lengths are expressed here at nominal f = 100 mm, using a factor of 100; the patent gives no absolute focal length or film format.

## Transcription Check

All eleven signed radii, six glass thicknesses, four air separations, six sodium-D indices and six Abbe numbers agree with the page images and Figure 7. No numerical transcription disagreement was found. The positive and negative radius signs are printed explicitly. The 0.051F R5–R6 air space is preserved as two equal spaces around the iris. R2 is cemented; the printed 0.005F rear air gap separates R9 and R10. There are no aspherical surfaces or variable spacings.

Example VII is the broken-contact variant of Example IV. Its rear element is 0.052F thick, with R10 = −1.1655F and R11 = −0.2412F; Example IV has a cemented rear pair, a 0.060F rear element and final radius −0.2419F. These are distinct prescriptions.

The source's mean-radius relations are 0.3358F for R4/R7, 0.1828F for R3/R8 and 0.2369F for R1/R11. The stated cemented- and broken-contact curvatures are +1.94 and +0.86 in equivalent-power units, with mean +1.40. The calculated Petzval sum is 0.001186 mm⁻¹, or 0.1186 relative to nominal optical power, about 1.2% below the printed 0.120. This source discrepancy remains unresolved.

## First-Order Check

Paraxial EFL is 100.025 mm, 0.025 mm above the scaled nominal 100 mm. The paraxial f-number is 3.50, matching the printed f/3.5; the stop radius is 11.067 mm. Calculated BFD is 80.32 mm, and the image plane lies at paraxial focus, 118.52 mm behind the first vertex. The first-to-last refracting vertex distance is 38.20 mm. Neither BFD nor total track is printed.

The surfaces stay clear of each other, with positive glass thicknesses and air clearances and no stop-plane intersection. The 0.5 mm rear air gap widens toward the edge. The corrected rims admit the axial f/3.5 beam. The original 11 mm R3/R5 rims limited the axial aperture to about f/3.64 when measured relative to the iris-defined beam; an entrance-radius calculation gave f/3.59 under a different convention.

The 25.0° chief ray clears all rims and reaches an image height of 46.66 mm. The inferred 93.32 mm image circle describes this stated field at the chosen scale. Off-axis marginal rays remain vignetted; a clear chief ray does not establish a fully unvignetted pupil.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The iris is described as approximately midway between R5 and R6. Its exact position, 19.55 mm behind the first vertex, follows an equal 2.55/2.55 mm split of the 5.1 mm air space. Its radius follows paraxial f/3.5. The image plane follows calculated paraxial back focus.

The diameter suggestions are 0.30F at R1/R2, 0.22F for the chamfers at R3/R5/R6/R8, and 0.26F for the rear surfaces. They are possible diameters rather than measured clear apertures. R1/R2 use 15 mm clear radii; R6/R8 use 11 mm; R10/R11 use 13 mm. R3/R5 use 11.3/11.05 mm to clear axial marginal heights of 11.278/11.016 mm. R4/R7/R9 clear radii of 12.3/12.6/13 mm are inferred. Detailed chamfers and bevel depths, and the exact relation of these clear radii to Figure 7, remain uncertain.

The rear-diameter sentence repeats R8 in its 0.26F list for Example VII, despite already giving R8 a 0.22F chamfer. This is probably a misprint for R9, since the corresponding Example IV list names R9/R10. No source radius is altered; R9's clear radius remains inferred.

The field angle is printed, but the image circle is calculated from its chief-ray height. `focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. No close-focus distance, travel or movement law is printed. The selectable f/3.5–f/22 range is not a published stop range; f/22 is not an established minimum opening.

The glass pairs retain the printed sodium-D reference. F15 (Hoya) supplies a matching dispersion curve for Element 5, without establishing a historical supplier or melt. Elements 1/6, 2 and 3/4 retain their three distinct unmatched pairs and use Abbe-based dispersion estimates. No extra spectral indices, partial dispersions, anomalous-dispersion designation or measured broadband performance is established.

## Product Identification

No production lens is documented for this prescription. Cooke identifies the maker attribution used for this Taylor, Taylor & Hobson design; it does not establish a manufactured 100 mm product. No mount, production image format, diaphragm blade count or shape, marketed focal length, marketed f-number or marketed minimum focus distance is assigned.

The companion US 2,601,593 A, serial 214,508, concerns wider coverage beyond a 30.0° half-field. US 2,601,595 instead describes a higher degree of correction with smaller coverage, no greater than 30.0°; Example VII specifies 25.0°. The calcium-fluoride Example VI of US 2,601,593 is a different prescription. GB 295,519 A supplies earlier four-component context rather than the numerical data used here.

## Corrections, 2026-11-21

- The key and title were replaced by the maker-and-patent form, and `maker` changed from null to Cooke, identifying Gordon Henry Cook's Taylor, Taylor & Hobson design. The title retains the nominal 100 mm scale rather than claiming a marketed focal length.
- The subtitle reads “US 2,601,595 A, Example VII — Taylor Hobson / Cook.” The specifications are “6 ELEMENTS / 5 GROUPS”, “ALL SPHERICAL”, “DESIGN f = 100 mm” and “DESIGN F/3.5”. The 100 mm value is the chosen scale of the printed F = 1.000 prescription. The earlier wide-angle description was removed because this is the smaller-coverage design.
- R3's clear radius increased from 11 to 11.3 mm, and R5's from 11 to 11.05 mm. The original rims clipped the printed f/3.5 axial beam; the patent says its suggested diameters exceed the axial requirement. All other rims, radii, thicknesses, spaces, glass coordinates, stop position, field and image-plane spacing remain as first given.
- A single inferred-rim classification was removed because the clear radii combine source diameter suggestions with inferred apertures.
- Element labels became Element 1 through Element 6. Elements 1/6 are identified as positive menisci and Elements 2/5 as negative menisci; Elements 3/4 remain positive menisci. Optical roles and calculated in-air focal lengths of +58.67, −42.54, +114.13, +120.95, −39.57 and +45.28 mm were added. The front cemented pair has focal length −471.74 mm. C1 identifies Elements 1/2 and spans R1–R3; the rear annotations identify the separate negative and positive menisci.
- Generic unmatched glass descriptions were replaced by the printed-coordinate labels for Elements 1/6 (1.6570/50.8), Element 2 (1.6205/36.2) and Elements 3/4 (1.6234/56.2). They remain unmatched because no catalog row meets the stated coordinate limits.
- Element 5 changed from unmatched to F15 (Hoya). At sodium D, catalog minus printed values are ΔnD = +0.000011 and ΔνD = −0.11. The printed 1.6055/38.0 pair is retained; the curve match does not identify the historical supplier.
- `catalog.focalLengthMm.design` changed from 100 to 100.02 mm to report the calculated EFL. The inferred image circle is 93.32 mm, from twice the 25.0° chief-ray height; the patent supplies the field angle rather than a production format.

After these changes, EFL is 100.025 mm, f-number 3.50, BFD 80.32 mm, stop radius 11.067 mm and half-field 25.0°. These first-order values are identical to those of the original prescription; the rim changes remove axial clipping without changing paraxial power or focus.

## Remaining Questions

- R4/R7/R9 clear radii, exact Figure 7 rim dimensions, and detailed chamfers and bevel depths are not established.
- The repeated R8 in the rear-diameter sentence and the approximately 1.2% Petzval discrepancy remain unresolved.
- Historical glass suppliers and melts, full spectral data and measured broadband performance are unknown; five elements have no matching catalog dispersion curve.
- No production product, mount, film format, blade count or shape, published stop range, marketed specifications or close-focus mechanism is documented.
