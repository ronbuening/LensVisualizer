# COOKE 27mm f/1.5 (US 1,955,617 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

Arthur Warmisham, [US 1,955,617 A, _Photographic and Projection Lens_](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/1955617), assigned to Kapella Limited, Leicester, England. Filed 27 January 1931, serial 511,454, with British priority 28 January 1930; published 17 April 1934. Figure 1 is on PDF p. 1 and its numerical table is on PDF p. 2. Figures 2 and 3 are separate f/1.4 examples. The drawing arrows establish left-to-right propagation.

The [Cooke Compendium](https://cookeoptics.com/compendium/), Series I KINIC sample list, records a 1-inch f/1.5 Kinic, serial 265542. It supplies no construction diagram or link between that lens and Figure 1.

## Transcription Check

All ten radii and signs, six glass thicknesses, three air separations, six nD/Abbe pairs, four Chance Brothers numbers and two blank glass-code cells agree with the patent page images. No transcription mismatch was found. The dimensions retain the printed inch scale, converted to millimetres by exactly 25.4. The first air interval is .002 in = 0.0508 mm. The central .130 in interval is 3.302 mm, divided equally around the inferred stop. R4 is cemented and R7 is a plane cemented interface. There are no aspherical surfaces or variable spacings.

The patent specifies sodium-D indices and Abbe V. Chance numbers 9002, 9322, 5093 and 8653 belong to Elements 1, 2, 3 and 5; the code cells for Elements 4 and 6 are blank. All printed optical constants are retained.

The Figure 1 section supports the six-element/four-group arrangement and the element shapes. Drawing half-heights are approximately 9.4 mm for Element 1, 9.0 mm for Elements 2–3, 7.2 mm for Elements 4–5 and 7.5 mm for Element 6. The smaller retained radii at R5, R7, R8, R9 and R10 are 8.1, 6.5, 6.1, 6.2 and 5.5 mm. The rear rims lie roughly 1–2 mm inside the drawing; R10 is about 27.0% smaller.

## First-Order Check

The patent prints focal length 1.059 in = 26.899 mm and f/1.5. The calculated D-line EFL is 26.929 mm, 0.1% longer, with paraxial aperture f/1.50. The calculated back focal distance is 11.94 mm; the image lies that distance behind R10 at z = 36.83 mm, the Gaussian infinity plane. The refracting-vertex track is 24.892 mm. The patent gives no back focus or total track, and the image plane is not shifted to minimize aberrations.

The Petzval sum is 0.025832 mm⁻¹; multiplied by EFL it gives 0.6956, reproducing the printed .696. The patent compares this residual with .809 for the earlier design.

The 6.814 mm stop radius admits an axial input radius of 8.438 mm, giving real-ray f/1.60. Real-ray f/1.50 would require an input radius of 8.976 mm and a stop radius of 7.324 mm, or 14.647 mm diameter; that ray clears the element rims. The retained iris uses the paraxial f/1.50 opening. The iris sets the axial aperture, with no additional limit from the rims. The 3.0° chief ray reaches image height 1.412 mm.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The patent gives no diaphragm position or diameter, clear radii, back focus, field angle, image format, finite projection conjugate or close-focus data. The stop is inferred midway between R5 and R6 at z = 10.795 mm, leaving 1.651 mm on each side. Its opening follows paraxial f/1.50. All clear radii are estimates; in R1–R10 order they are 9.6, 9.3, 9.0, 8.6, 8.1, 7.3, 6.5, 6.1, 6.2 and 5.5 mm.

The surfaces stay clear of each other. Minimum glass thickness is 0.508 mm, minimum first-air clearance is 0.0508 mm and minimum R8–R9 clearance is 1.203 mm. Facing-surface sag occupies at most 47.4% of an axial air gap. Clearance around the stop opening is at least 1.133 mm in front and 1.651 mm behind; no glass crosses the stop plane. Element 2's faces would meet at approximately 10.087 mm height, and R8/R9 at approximately 8.024 mm.

The ±3.0° field is illustrative. The admitted pupil clears the rims throughout this field; a larger incident pupil is clipped at the iris. Even the larger 7.324 mm stop radius gives some iris clipping at 1.5° and 3.0°. The chosen field does not establish a source-supported image circle or measured illumination, so `catalog.imageCircleMm` is omitted.

`focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. The relative element spacings are fixed; no finite-focus mechanism is described. The selectable f-stops run from f/1.5 to f/16, but the patent supplies no minimum iris opening. This range does not establish a production diaphragm limit.

Common clear-radius changes of ±0.1 mm retain open gaps and positive glass thickness. A stop-radius change of −2% gives f/1.62 and +2% gives f/1.57. Stop positions z = 10.545, 10.795 and 11.045 mm all give approximately f/1.60 within the unchanged 3.302 mm air space and leave EFL unchanged. These describe sensitivity to assumed aperture dimensions, not manufacturing tolerances.

No available catalog row satisfies both |ΔnD| ≤ 0.0001 and |Δν| ≤ 0.15, so all six glass labels remain Unmatched. The closest sodium-D candidates fall outside these limits; differences below are catalog minus printed values.

The patent gives no individual C-, F- or g-line indices, partial dispersion or measured broadband performance. Abbe-based dispersion remains approximate. The geometric MTF values in the analysis use D-line light at the Gaussian infinity plane and exclude diffraction, coatings, tolerances, detector response and broadband effects. Numerical variation is at most 0.0012 through 40 lp/mm; the patent supplies no MTF curve for comparison.

## Product Identification

No production lens is documented as using this prescription. A proposed Kinic connection lacks a matching construction diagram; the compendium's 1-inch f/1.5 entry alone cannot establish that connection. The Cooke name does not establish a production model. No mount, production image format, blade count or roundedness, marketed focal length, marketed aperture, marketed minimum focus distance or converter compatibility is assigned.

The higher-index positive/lower-index negative rear pair improves field flatness, while its convex front surface separately reduces incidence angles and spherical aberration.
