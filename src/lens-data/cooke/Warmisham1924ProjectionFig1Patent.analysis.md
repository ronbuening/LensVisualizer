# Warmisham Projection Lens — US 1,484,853 Figure 1

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 1,484,853 A, *Projection Lens*  
**Application Number:** Serial No. 585,160  
**Filed:** August 30, 1922  
**Granted:** February 26, 1924  
**Inventor:** Arthur Warmisham, of Leicester, England  
**Assignee:** none printed  
**Example details:** the patent's only numerical example of the invention, Figure 1 and the table on specification page 2; dimensions in inches, multiplied here by 25.4

The model is the single numerical example of US 1,484,853, a projection lens for kinematograph (cinema) machines. The patent's second table, shown in Figure 2, is a conventional Petzval portrait lens quoted from von Rohr's 1899 book for comparison only; it is not part of the invention and is not modelled.

The patent states an equivalent focal length of 10 inches and a back focal length of 2.508 inches, and it carries out its ray computations at a free aperture of the front component of 3.20 inches, which it calls F/3.1. It does not state a maximum aperture. The half-field used in those computations is 5°.

No production lens is identified. The patent prints no assignee. Arthur Warmisham was the optical designer of Taylor, Taylor & Hobson in Leicester, which is why the file sits with the Cooke lenses. The Science Museum Group holds a "Taylor-Hobson Maximum Projection Lens" engraved 6 in (152 mm) f/3.1 (object 1979-559/1376), the same relative aperture as the patent's worked case, but its record gives no construction and no patent number, and no source found ties it to this patent. The link is therefore possible but unproven, and the model is kept at the patent's own 10 inch scale.

## Optical Architecture

The lens is of the Petzval type: two positive components separated by a long air space. Both components are cemented doublets, four elements in two groups, with only four glass-to-air surfaces. A conventional Petzval lens has an uncemented rear pair and six such surfaces; the patent counts the two surfaces saved as roughly ten per cent more light on the screen.

What distinguishes the design is the power distribution. The front component is weak (focal length 529.1 mm in the model; the patent states 20.86 in, 529.8 mm) and the rear component is strong (170.9 mm; the patent states 6.73 in). The air space of 332.74 mm (13.10 in) is longer than the focal length of the rear component and shorter than that of the front one. The result is a back focal length of about one quarter of the focal length, 63.5 mm against 254.2 mm, where the quoted conventional Petzval lens has 58.6 per cent. The patent claims any such lens with a back focal length below 35 per cent of the focal length.

The purpose is illumination. With the lens close to the film, the image of the front aperture seen from the film lies behind the film gate (7.6 in behind, by the patent's computation) and subtends a larger angle there than in the conventional lens, so the lens collects more of the lamp's light. The patent reports a measured 52 per cent gain in screen-centre illumination.

The file draws the lens as Figure 1 does, with the screen side on the left and the film on the right, and traces light from the screen side. This is the reverse of the direction of use but gives the same focal length, back focus and aperture.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Biconvex Positive (front doublet)

nd = 1.509, νd = 64.3 (printed as nD and V). Glass: Chance type 646, a hard crown; no modern catalogue match. f = +246.0 mm.

The crown of the front component. Its radii are 271.78 mm and −227.076 mm (10.700 in and 8.940 in), so it is only moderately curved and carries the weak positive power of the front component.

### L2 — Negative Meniscus (front doublet)

nd = 1.651, νd = 33.5. Glass: Chance type 5093, a dense flint; no modern catalogue match. f = −452.5 mm.

Cemented to L1 at the −227.076 mm surface; its rear face is nearly flat (−1005.84 mm, 39.60 in). It corrects the colour of the front component and, through the cemented surface, part of its spherical aberration. The doublet's net focal length is 529.1 mm.

### L3 — Biconvex Positive (rear doublet)

nd = 1.509, νd = 64.3. Glass: Chance type 646. f = +97.2 mm.

A thick, strongly curved crown (radii 74.6252 mm and −127.762 mm, centre thickness 28.65 mm). It supplies most of the power of the whole lens and brings the beam to a focus a short distance behind the lens.

### L4 — Plano-Concave Negative (rear doublet)

nd = 1.651, νd = 33.5. Glass: Chance type 5093. f = −196.3 mm.

Cemented to L3, with a flat face toward the film. It achromatises the rear component, whose net focal length is 170.9 mm.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent prints, for each element, the sodium D-line index, the V value and a type number from Chance Brothers' optical glass catalogue: type 646 (nD 1.509, V 64.3) for both crowns and type 5093 (nD 1.651, V 33.5) for both flints. The data file uses the printed nD and V directly and marks the index line as sodium D. Neither glass matches a current catalogue glass at these values, so colour in the model is estimated from nD and V alone rather than from measured dispersion data.

Using the same crown and flint pair in both doublets is the simplest possible palette; each doublet is separately achromatised by a crown-in-front pairing.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent describes no focusing arrangement; a projection lens of this kind is focused by moving the whole lens in its mount. The model is fixed at infinity (an infinitely distant screen), which is the condition for which the patent defines its back focal length.

## Patent Values and Model Verification

All six radii, four thicknesses, the separation, and the four index and V pairs were read from the page image of the patent table and agree with the data file after multiplying by 25.4.

| Quantity | Patent | Model |
|---|---|---|
| Equivalent focal length | 10 in (254.0 mm) | 254.20 mm (10.008 in) |
| Back focal length | 2.508 in (63.70 mm) | 63.48 mm (2.499 in) |
| Front component focal length | 20.86 in | 20.83 in |
| Rear component focal length | 6.73 in | 6.726 in |
| Aperture | 3.20 in free aperture, "F/3.1" | 81.28 mm pupil, f/3.13 |

The focal length is 0.08 per cent long and the back focus 0.23 mm short. The whole difference sits in the front doublet and is consistent with the indices being printed to three decimals; no single misread figure was found, and nothing was adjusted to remove it. The image plane is the computed paraxial focus.

Inferred values, not printed in the patent:

- The stop plane, 1 mm in front of the first surface. The patent defines the working aperture as the free aperture of the front component, so the stop is placed there with a radius of 40.64 mm.
- The clear semi-diameters: 42 mm for the front doublet and 32 mm for the rear doublet. The rear value is the smallest that lets the central ray of the patent's 5° half-field pass (it meets the rear doublet 31.1 mm from the axis). Figure 1, which is drawn to scale, shows both doublets about 39 mm in semi-diameter; at 5° the full beam would need about 44 mm at the rear doublet, so the model, like the figure, vignettes at the edge of that field.
- The back focus, as above.
