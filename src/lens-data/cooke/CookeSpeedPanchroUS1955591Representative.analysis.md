# Cooke Speed Panchro: 1-inch f/2 patent representative

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 1955591 A  
**Inventor:** Horace William Lee  
**Assignee:** Kapella Limited  
**Filed:** 11 November 1932  
**Granted:** 17 April 1934  
**British application stated:** 14 November 1931  
**Example details:** Single 1-inch, f/2 example with a 35° field, expressly adapted for 16 mm motion-picture cameras.

Cooke's manufacturer-authored retrospective explicitly associates US1955591 with the Speed Panchro line. It is a primary family attribution, not a contemporary factory prescription or proof of a particular retail 1-inch lens. A 1930 Bell & Howell advertisement on Cooke's site names Speed-Panchro products but lists no 1-inch focal length; it also predates the stated 1931 British application. Product-history dates and patent dates are not conflated.

The model is a qualified representative of the patent example, using the exact unit conversion of 25.4 mm per inch. It is not scaled to a catalog focal length or asserted to be an actual factory 25.4 mm product. The canonical image-format label remains 16 mm cinema. This six-element design is distinct from the eight-element Ultra Speed Panchro; it is not a second version of that held model.

## Optical Architecture

Six spherical elements form four air-separated components: a positive front singlet, a cemented positive/negative pair, a cemented negative/positive pair, and a positive rear singlet. The opposed inner components form the double-Gauss arrangement described in the source.

Both cemented interfaces and all three source air gaps remain literal. The central S2 = 0.22 inch label denotes an air separation, not an iris position. The patent's condition involving a midpoint between vertex images is not a physical stop prescription.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1: front positive meniscus

Source nD = 1.6100, V = 53.3; Chance Brothers No. 8894. The front surface is more strongly curved than the rear. This is a historical catalog number from the source, not a certified present melt or modern replacement.

### L2 and L3: front cemented component

L2: nD = 1.6150, V = 56.1, Chance No. 3265. L3: nD = 1.6134, V = 36.9, Chance No. 4743. The positive front meniscus and negative rear element share S4. Their source thicknesses and common boundary are retained without inserting air.

### L4 and L5: rear cemented component

L4: nD = 1.6469, V = 33.7, Chance No. 337. L5: nD = 1.6437, V = 48.3, an unnumbered new glass in the patent's note. The first is biconcave and the second biconvex. No catalog number is invented for the unnumbered glass.

### L6: rear positive singlet

Source nD = 1.6234, V = 56.3; Chance No. 6665. The source's unequal biconvex radii are preserved. Isolated-element focal lengths and quantitative aberration allocations are not independently certified for these elements.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The original text expressly identifies its V values as Abbe numbers and supplies Chance Brothers catalog identifiers. The nD convention is historical; the engine d-reference representation is an approximation rather than an exact modern helium-d melt. No measured full dispersion curve, partial dispersion, historical melt record or modern catalog substitution is claimed.

All numerical indices and Abbe numbers remain literal. The unnamed fifth glass remains unnamed. The source discusses comparative glass indices and weathering behavior; these are patent statements, not experimentally verified material properties here.

## Focus Mechanism

The model represents the infinity prescription only. Literal paraxial EFL is 0.999416508237 inch, or 25.385179309 mm, versus nominal 1 inch. The small residual remains without focal fitting.

The terminal image interval is calculated as 0.678397625613 inch, approximately 17.231299691 mm. It is not a published source BFL. No retail near-focus movement, focusing travel, minimum distance or camera mount coupling is reconstructed.

## Inferred Iris and Clear Apertures

Neither exact physical iris position nor clear apertures are supplied. An iris at the midpoint of the 0.22-inch central gap is an explicit engineering hypothesis, giving 0.11 inch on each side. It is not derived from the source's vertex-image midpoint claim.

Inferred semi-diameters are 9 mm for the front singlet, 6.0 mm at S3 and 5.1 mm at S4 in the front doublet, 5 mm at the remaining doublet surfaces, and 7 mm for the rear singlet. The delivered 5 mm rims at S3 and S4 clipped the axial beam to f/2.41. They are raised only to the aperture audit’s minimum 6.0/5.1 mm values, within the validator’s approximately 6.9 mm front-doublet rim from the patent figure. The remaining inferred rims retain their delivered geometry-grid values; they are not measured mechanical apertures.

Native f/2 construction gives an iris radius of 4.156363225 mm. This is a model result, not a source hole diameter. The f-stop range through f/22 is authored convenience, not a validated retail specification. No source radius, spacing, glass value, cemented interface or geometry limit is changed to improve acceptance.

## Verification Summary and Field Limitation

Native construction and isolated strict TypeScript pass, with independent scoped source/geometry reviews accompanying the handoff. These checks do not validate all commercial performance claims.

The original sparse source-field test used the stated 17.5° half-field, not a 36 × 24 mm full-frame assumption. Its claim of 26 successful axial samples is superseded by the independent validator’s finding that the delivered S3/S4 rims clipped the f/2 axial beam to f/2.41. The original off-axis sample counts have not been revalidated after the 6.0/5.1 mm rim correction.

Strong peripheral clipping remains. These traces terminate at the rear optical surface, not at a separately tested image-plane landing. Counts are not throughput fractions, full pupil integration, MTF or verified 35° image quality/coverage. The 16 mm cinema designation is source context, not proof of every ray or production-format tolerance.

The representative remains hidden. Full current-corpus/app tests, lint/format integration, build, browser and MTF checks are deferred, not passed. No release or deployment follows from qualified pre-integration acceptance.
