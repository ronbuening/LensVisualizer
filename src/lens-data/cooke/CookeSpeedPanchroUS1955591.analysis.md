# Cooke Speed Panchro: 1-inch f/2 patent design for 16 mm

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 1,955,591 A  
**Inventor:** Horace William Lee  
**Assignee:** Kapella Limited  
**Filed:** 11 November 1932  
**Granted:** 17 April 1934  
**British application stated:** 14 November 1931  
**Example details:** Single 1-inch, f/2 example with a 35° field, expressly adapted for 16 mm motion-picture cameras.

Cooke's [History of Panchro, Part 1](https://cookeoptics.com/news-and-events/the-history-of-panchro-part-1/) explicitly associates US 1,955,591 A with Lee's Speed Panchro family. The 35 mm cinema line and this patent's 1-inch example for 16 mm cameras share that family association; the history does not establish a particular retail 1-inch lens. A 1930 Bell & Howell advertisement on Cooke's site names Speed-Panchro products but lists no 1-inch focal length; it also predates the stated 1931 British application.

The prescription uses the exact unit conversion of 25.4 mm per inch, without further scaling to a catalog focal length. The 16 mm cinema format follows the patent's stated application. This six-element design differs from the eight-element Ultra Speed Panchro.

## Optical Architecture

Six spherical elements form four air-separated components: a positive front singlet, a cemented positive/negative pair, a cemented negative/positive pair, and a positive rear singlet. Both cemented components have net negative power, giving a positive–negative–negative–positive group sequence. The opposed inner components form the double-Gauss arrangement described in the source.

The two cemented interfaces and all three air gaps follow the patent. The central S2 = 0.22 inch label denotes an air separation, not an iris position. The patent's condition involving a midpoint between vertex images does not specify a physical stop position.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1: front positive meniscus

Source nD = 1.6100, νD = 53.3; Chance Brothers No. 8894; isolated-element focal length approximately +39.20 mm. The front surface is more strongly curved than the rear. The glass number identifies the historical source glass; no exact current catalog row is established.

### L2 and L3: front cemented component

L2: nD = 1.6150, νD = 56.1, Chance No. 3265, isolated-element focal length approximately +24.80 mm. L3: nD = 1.6134, νD = 36.9, Chance No. 4743, approximately −14.47 mm. The positive and negative menisci share S4 and form cemented pair C1, with a calculated focal length of approximately −61.25 mm. Their source thicknesses and common boundary are retained without inserting air.

### L4 and L5: rear cemented component

L4: nD = 1.6469, νD = 33.7, Chance No. 337, isolated-element focal length approximately −7.35 mm. L5: nD = 1.6437, νD = 48.3, approximately +9.59 mm, an unnumbered new glass in the patent's note. The biconcave negative and biconvex positive elements form cemented pair C2, with a calculated focal length of approximately −176.58 mm. The fifth glass remains unnumbered.

### L6: rear positive singlet

Source nD = 1.6234, νD = 56.3; Chance No. 6665; isolated-element focal length approximately +24.22 mm. The source's unequal biconvex radii are preserved. These paraxial focal lengths describe power, rather than quantitative aberration contributions.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent expressly identifies its V values as Abbe numbers and supplies Chance Brothers catalog identifiers. It prints sodium-D indices; the model retains the existing helium-d reference, so the wavelength convention differs slightly. The six glasses carry Unmatched labels because no catalog row agrees within the stated index precision and 0.15 in Abbe number. No measured full dispersion or partial-dispersion data are available.

The printed numerical indices and Abbe numbers are retained. The unnamed fifth glass remains unnamed. The source discusses comparative glass indices and weathering behavior; these are patent statements rather than independently measured material properties.

## Focus Mechanism

The model represents the infinity prescription only. Calculated paraxial EFL is 0.999416508237 inch, or 25.385179309 mm, versus nominal 1 inch. The small residual remains without focal fitting.

The terminal image interval is calculated as 0.678397625613 inch, approximately 17.231299691 mm. The patent does not print this back focal distance or provide finite-focus spacings, a focusing mechanism, minimum focus distance or camera mount coupling.

## Inferred Iris and Clear Apertures

Neither exact physical iris position nor clear apertures are supplied. The model assumes an iris at the midpoint of the 0.22-inch central gap, giving 0.11 inch on each side. The source's vertex-image midpoint condition does not determine this location.

Inferred semi-diameters are 9 mm for the front singlet, 6.0 mm at S3 and 5.1 mm at S4 in the front doublet, 5 mm at the remaining doublet surfaces, and 7 mm for the rear singlet. The S3/S4 apertures pass the f/2 axial beam. The figure suggests approximately 6.9 mm at the front singlet and front-doublet outer rim, 4.4 mm at the inner R5/R6 openings, and 6.2 mm at the rear doublet and rear singlet. The inferred rims therefore differ from the drawing and should not be read as measured mechanical apertures.

At f/2 the calculated iris radius is 4.156363225 mm, rather than a printed source diameter. The selectable f-stop range through f/22 has no established retail source.

## Model Scope and Field Limitation

The patent states a 35° full field, or 17.5° half-field, for its 16 mm application. Peripheral clipping limits the modeled beam; passing the 16 mm frame corner does not establish uniform illumination or image quality across the full stated field. The format designation does not establish 35 mm cinema coverage. Exact retail identity, mechanical clear apertures and finite-focus behavior remain undocumented.
