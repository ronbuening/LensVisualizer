# COOKE SPEED PANCHRO 1 in f/2 (US 1,955,591 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 1,955,591 A  
**Inventor:** Horace William Lee  
**Assignee:** Kapella Limited  
**Filed:** 11 November 1932  
**Granted:** 17 April 1934  
**British application stated:** 14 November 1931  
**Example details:** Single 1-inch, f/2 example with a 35° field, expressly adapted for 16 mm motion-picture cameras.

Cooke's [History of Panchro, Part 1](https://cookeoptics.com/news-and-events/the-history-of-panchro-part-1/) explicitly associates US 1,955,591 A with Lee's Speed Panchro family. The 35 mm cinema line and this patent's 1-inch example for 16 mm cameras share that family association; the history does not establish a particular retail 1-inch lens. A 1930 Bell & Howell advertisement on Cooke's site names Speed-Panchro products but lists no 1-inch focal length; it also predates the stated 1931 British application.

The prescription uses the exact unit conversion of 25.4 mm per inch, without further scaling to a catalog focal length. The nominal 1-inch f/2 values describe the patent design; the catalog records a calculated design focal length of 25.385 mm and design f-number of 2, with no marketing focal length or f-number because no retail 1-inch lens is established. Its specifications are “6 ELEMENTS / 4 GROUPS”, “DESIGN f = 1 in”, “F/2” and “ALL SPHERICAL”. The 16 mm cinema format follows the patent's stated application. This six-element design differs from the eight-element Ultra Speed Panchro.

## Optical Architecture

Six spherical elements form four air-separated components: a positive front singlet, a cemented positive/negative pair, a cemented negative/positive pair, and a positive rear singlet. Both cemented components have net negative power, giving a positive–negative–negative–positive group sequence. The opposed inner components form the double-Gauss arrangement described in the source.

The two cemented interfaces and all three air gaps follow the patent. The central S2 = 0.22 inch label denotes an air separation, not an iris position. The patent's condition involving a midpoint between vertex images does not specify a physical stop position.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1: front positive meniscus

Source nD = 1.6100, νD = 53.3; Chance Brothers No. 8894; isolated-element focal length approximately +39.20 mm. The front surface is more strongly curved than the rear. The glass number identifies the historical source glass; the model labels L1 Unmatched.

### L2 and L3: front cemented component

L2: nD = 1.6150, νD = 56.1, Chance No. 3265, Unmatched, isolated-element focal length approximately +24.80 mm. L3: nD = 1.6134, νD = 36.9, Chance No. 4743, F3 spectral proxy, approximately −14.47 mm. The positive and negative menisci share S4 and form cemented pair C1, with a calculated focal length of approximately −61.25 mm. Their source thicknesses and common boundary are retained without inserting air.

### L4 and L5: rear cemented component

L4: nD = 1.6469, νD = 33.7, Chance No. 337, J-SF2 spectral proxy, isolated-element focal length approximately −7.35 mm. L5: nD = 1.6437, νD = 48.3, Unmatched, approximately +9.59 mm, an unnumbered new glass in the patent's note. The biconcave negative and biconvex positive elements form cemented pair C2, with a calculated focal length of approximately −176.58 mm. The fifth glass remains unnumbered.

### L6: rear positive singlet

Source nD = 1.6234, νD = 56.3; Chance No. 6665; Unmatched; isolated-element focal length approximately +24.22 mm. The source's unequal biconvex radii are preserved. These paraxial focal lengths describe power, rather than quantitative aberration contributions.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent expressly identifies its V values as Abbe numbers and supplies Chance Brothers catalog identifiers. It prints sodium-D indices; the model uses a helium-d reference, so the wavelength convention differs slightly. L3 uses an F3 spectral proxy and L4 uses a J-SF2 spectral proxy. Each label preserves the historical Chance number and states that the supplier and melt are unspecified.

The engine keeps the printed index at the element's reference line and borrows only the catalog row's wavelength dependence. The printed nD/V pairs stay unchanged. These rows meet the site's proxy tolerance: Abbe number within about 0.25 and index within about 0.002 of the printed values. They are not exact matches and do not identify the historical glass, supplier or melt. Catalog vendors below identify the proxy rows only. Differences are catalog minus printed.

## Focus Mechanism

The model represents the infinity prescription only. Calculated paraxial EFL is 0.999416508237 inch, or 25.385179309 mm, versus nominal 1 inch. The small residual remains without focal fitting.

The terminal image interval is calculated as 0.678397625613 inch, approximately 17.231299691 mm. The patent does not print this back focal distance or provide finite-focus spacings, a focusing mechanism, minimum focus distance or camera mount coupling.

## Inferred Iris and Clear Apertures

Neither exact physical iris position nor clear apertures are supplied. The model assumes an iris at the midpoint of the 0.22-inch central gap, giving 0.11 inch on each side. The source's vertex-image midpoint condition does not determine this location.

The sole patent figure supplies approximate rim proportions. Its overall axial scale is about 33.7 native pixels per millimetre, supported by the front arc's transverse scale of about 34 pixels per millimetre. Individual element thicknesses disagree with the prescription by up to about 20%, so the drawing is schematic rather than an exact aperture specification.

The front singlet has equal 8.0 mm semi-diameters. The front cemented component has 6.9 mm rims at S3/S4 and a 4.4 mm optical cap at S5, followed by a flat land out to 6.9 mm. The rear cemented component has a 4.4 mm optical cap at S6, a flat land out to 6.3 mm, and 6.3 mm rims at S7/S8. The rear singlet has equal 6.7 mm rims. The two lands follow the figure's short optical curves and taller square edges; they do not enlarge the clear apertures. The singlets retain rims larger than the drawing's approximately 6.9/6.3 mm proportions to limit each rim reduction's geometric-transmission cost to less than three percentage points. These inferred values are not measured mechanical apertures.

The f/2 axial beam and the chief rays to both the 16 mm corner and the stated 17.5° half-field clear all optical rims. At the 6.35 mm format semi-diagonal, reached at approximately 14.17°, calculated geometric transmission is about 86.0%. This is pupil transmission, not a measured photographic relative-illumination curve. Real off-axis clipping remains. The S5/S6 caps and lands clear the assumed iris plane by approximately 1.09/1.45 mm respectively.

At f/2 the calculated iris radius is 4.156363225 mm, rather than a printed source diameter. The selectable f-stop range through f/22 has no established retail source.

## Model Scope and Field Limitation

The patent states a 35° full field, or 17.5° half-field, for its 16 mm application. Peripheral clipping limits the modeled beam; passing the 16 mm frame corner does not establish uniform illumination or image quality across the full stated field. The format designation does not establish 35 mm cinema coverage. Exact retail identity, mechanical clear apertures and finite-focus behavior remain undocumented.
