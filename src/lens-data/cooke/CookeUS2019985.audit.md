# Audit

- Table and drawing of the patent transcribed and visually checked; no optical constants adjusted.
- Independent paraxial matrix, exact axial Snell trace and 1,001-point adjacent-surface sag sampling passed. Minimum sampled separation 0.053 mm as delivered. No gap override.
- Type check of the data file passed. As delivered, the on-axis beam passed through the central 90 % of the pupil; outer and off-axis clipping was recorded.
- Publication number and radius fingerprint screened against the catalog and earlier historical models for duplicates. These sweeps are duplicate screens, not proof of a production relationship.
- Full application regression and viewer checks were not run by the author.

## 2026-11-17 — Deployment validation

**Checked against the page images.** An independent re-read of US 2,019,985 at 400 dpi: all 12 radii, 7 thicknesses, 4 separations, 7 nD and 7 V values, the printed Plus / Minus signs, the ×25.4 inch conversion on every length, and the drawing (seven elements in five groups, two cemented pairs concave to each other, split rear collective member). No mismatch. Front page: Horace William Lee, assignor to Kapella Limited; filed December 26, 1930; granted November 5, 1935; one numerical example.

**Verdict.** Valid as a hidden patent reference. No production lens is documented for this design: Cooke's history pages mention Lee's f/1.4 Ultra Speed Panchro (US 1,779,257) and a 58 mm f/1.3 Super Speed Panchro of 1930, but nothing found ties either to this seven-element form, and no construction diagram was found.

**Focal length.** The printed table computes to 26.31 mm against the patent's nominal one inch (25.4 mm), +3.6 %. A scan of 393 single-value variants (digit, sign, transposition in one radius or thickness) found no change supported by the page; the nearest (1.12 → 1.02, 0.523 → 0.503) contradict clearly printed digits. Radii are printed to one to three figures, so the difference is taken as table rounding. Printed values kept; no rescaling. On-axis spherical aberration as printed: +1.04 / +0.33 / +0.09 / −0.005 mm at pupil 1 / 0.85 / 0.7 / 0.5.

## 2026-11-18 — Semi-diameter pass against the patent figure

**Source and scale.** US 2,019,985, sheet 1, the single drawing (native 300 dpi scan, read at 600 dpi). No axis is drawn, so the axis was taken midway between the upper and lower silhouettes, and glass and air were read from the ink along it. Spans against the table: front cemented pair 278 px for 4.953 mm (56.1 px/mm); central air space 270 px for 4.953 mm (54.5 px/mm); front of the front pair to the back of the rear pair 894 px for 15.748 mm (56.8 px/mm); first to last vertex 1252 px for 23.495 mm (53.3 px/mm). Arc fits of the deep radii give 54–58 px/mm, so the main body is drawn at about 55 px/mm. The thin parts are not to scale. The two 0.508 mm flints are drawn about twice as thick, the 0.127 mm air spaces are drawn 16–24 px wide, and the front lens and the two rear single lenses are drawn thinner (37–46 px/mm). With spacings off by more than 10 %, the drawing was used for proportions only.

**Drawn heights** (mean of the upper and lower silhouettes, in px, with mm at 55 px/mm): front single lens 413.5 (7.5); front cemented pair, flat top 392.5 (7.1), end of its concave face 300.5 (5.5); rear cemented pair, end of its concave face 300.5 (5.5), flat top 373.5 (6.8); first rear single lens 371 (6.7); second rear single lens 370.5 (6.7). At that scale every rim except the two concave faces lies below the f/1.4 on-axis beam, which needs 9.40 / 9.05 mm at the front lens, 8.23 at the front pair, 7.24 at surface 8, 7.00 / 6.93 at the first rear lens and 6.52 / 6.29 at the second. So the drawing does not show the f/1.4 aperture at scale. Its proportions: front lens : front pair : rear pair : rear lenses = 1 : 0.95 : 0.90 : 0.90. The rear pair and the two rear single lenses share one flat top line. The rear pair is square-topped, with its cemented junction running up to the flat top, and chamfered at about 47° to its concave face. The front pair has a flat top, so its biconvex element has an edge, and a 45° chamfer.

The front pair can follow neither the drawing's flat top nor its 45° chamfer. In the model it comes to a point at 8.24 mm and slopes down at about 35°, because the printed radii leave the biconvex element no edge there. No land or rim extension was added: a vertical land would match the drawn chamfer less well than the present slope.

**Clearance and light.** Engine numbers are identical before and after: focal length 26.3105 mm, f/1.4, half-field 27.61°, iris semi-diameter 6.094 mm. Corner illumination against the committed rims is unchanged at every field: 85.4 % on axis, 72.6 % at 17.2°, 45.8 % at 22.9° and 6.1 % at 28.6°, with a worst loss of 0.0 points. The chief ray passes the rims unblocked out to 34.8°, limited by surface 12 as before. The surface audit is clean. The aperture audit keeps its single limit, surface 5 (f/1.40 → f/1.51 at the rim), which is the gap cap. At f/1.4 the real-ray trace clips the axial beam on surfaces 5 and 6 only (10 % and 9 % on one side), and no chief ray is blocked. Iris in air: at the full 6.09 mm iris radius the rim of surface 5 stays 0.053 mm in front of the iris plane and surface 6 sits 0.450 mm behind it. Nothing is trimmed in the drawing. The image-circle and field-coverage audits skip the file, which has no image format and is hidden.

**Metadata.** None changed. The patent states no mount, image format or field, and no source ties the design to a product, so `lensMounts`, `imageFormat` and `imageCircleMm` stay unset. The f-stop scale (f/1.4 to f/16) is kept; f/1.4 is the patent's stated aperture.

**Name.** The patent describes "a lens of one inch focal length" at f/1.4 and names no product. The catalogue name uses the computed focal length, 26.31 mm, rounded to 26 mm.
