# COOKE 100mm f/2.5 (US 2,366,597 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

Arthur Cox, [US 2,366,597 A, “Optical Objective”](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2366597A/en), assigned to Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited. Filed November 18, 1942; British priority September 17, 1941; published January 2, 1945. Example I appears on PDF p. 4, printed specification p. 2, and Figure 1 on PDF p. 1. The patent gives seven examples; claims 35–37 reproduce Examples II, III and VII, not Example I.

## Transcription Check

All nine signed radii, six element thicknesses, two air spaces and six D-line index/Abbe pairs agree with the Example I page images. Every radius sign is printed. The source gives unit focal length 1.000 without a physical length unit; multiplying all dimensions by 100 gives the chosen nominal 100 mm scale. No transcription mismatch or source correction is identified.

Figure 1 confirms six elements in three cemented doublets, with shared interfaces at R2, R5 and R8. The first two doublets converge and the rear doublet diverges. Each consists of a biconvex positive element followed by a biconcave negative element; each complete doublet is meniscus-shaped, with its exposed surfaces convex toward the object. No stop is drawn.

## First-Order Check

The calculated EFL is 99.87 mm, 0.1% below the nominal 100 mm chosen scale. The paraxial aperture is f/2.50, matching the printed f/2.5. Calculated BFD is 48.91 mm, placing the image plane that distance behind R9. The first-to-last vertex distance is 38.10 mm and the first-vertex-to-image distance is 87.01 mm, or 0.871 of EFL, below the patent's seven-eighths limit. The patent prints no numerical BFD.

The iris radius is 14.39 mm. Its real-ray entrance-pupil and image-sine equivalents both give approximately f/2.66. A radius near 15.43 mm would admit the nominal f/2.5 real marginal ray; the present iris follows the paraxial setting. Axial D-line longitudinal spherical aberration is about −0.016 mm at the full nominal entrance radius and −0.126 mm at 70% of that radius, relative to near-axis focus. These values agree with the patent's discussion of zonal correction without establishing measured or broadband image quality.

The surfaces stay clear of each other. Minimum glass clearance over a shared clear radius is about 1.46 mm, minimum air clearance is 1.40 mm and minimum glass thickness at an individual rim is 0.51 mm. The iris sets the axial aperture. Chief and axial marginal rays clear the rims near the displayed 5.0° half-field; at the larger iris needed for real f/2.5, about 1% of one side of the off-axis bundle is vignetted.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The patent supplies no physical length unit, stop station, iris bore, clear radii, numerical back focus, numerical field, image format, image-circle diameter or close-focus schedule. The nominal 100 mm scale is a choice. The inferred stop is 25.3 mm behind the first vertex, dividing the printed 6.5 mm R6–R7 air space into 3 mm before the stop and 3.5 mm after it. Its radius follows paraxial f/2.5, and the image plane is the calculated paraxial infinity plane.

The clear semi-diameters at R1–R9 are estimated as 23, 21, 20, 20, 17.4, 17, 14, 14 and 11.5 mm. Figure 1 suggests about 23.5 mm for the first two doublets and 16 mm for the rear doublet, with about 13 mm at R9. The second and rear doublets are therefore smaller than drawn. Larger rims are constrained by vanishing edge thickness near 22.2 mm for Element 3 and 16.4 mm for Element 5.

The ±5.0° field is illustrative. No image format or image-circle diameter is assigned, so a specified format's coverage is not established. `focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. All element spacings remain fixed. The selectable aperture range extends to f/22, but the source supplies no minimum iris opening.

The printed D-line indices are retained as sodium-D values. All six glasses retain their printed Abbe numbers and remain unidentified. Dispersion is estimated from these pairs; no individual spectral-line indices, partial dispersion, glass trade names, historical suppliers or melts are given. The source does not establish transmission, secondary spectrum or apochromatic performance.

## Product Identification

No production lens is documented as using Example I. A connection to Cooke Telepanchro remains unestablished. The Cooke attribution follows the assignee, Taylor, Taylor & Hobson, maker of Cooke lenses; it does not establish production specifications. No marketed focal length or aperture, mount, image format, diaphragm blade count or shape, marketed minimum focus distance or close-focus mechanism is assigned.

## Corrections, 2026-11-21

- The key and title changed to the maker-and-patent form, and the previously absent maker became Cooke. The title identifies the chosen 100 mm scale and the patent design; it does not establish a production lens.
- The patent number gained the publication suffix A, becoming US 2,366,597 A. Example I, Arthur Cox, the 1945 publication year and Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited were retained from the source.
- The subtitle identifies Example I, Taylor Hobson and Cox. The specifications state six elements in three groups, spherical surfaces, the chosen 100 mm design scale, design f/2.5 and telephoto construction.
- `catalog.focalLengthMm.design` changed from the nominal 100 mm scale to the calculated 99.87 mm EFL. The design f-number remains the printed f/2.5.
- Element labels changed from L1–L6 to Element 1–Element 6. Generic shape descriptions became Biconvex Positive on Elements 1, 3 and 5 and Biconcave Negative on Elements 2, 4 and 6, following the printed radius signs. Optical roles and focal lengths in air were added: +52.75, −84.47, +51.77, −74.91, +33.01 and −19.70 mm.
- The cemented pairs received C1–C3 identifiers and matching doublet brackets. Group captions identify the first convergent, second convergent and divergent rear doublets. Their calculated focal lengths in air are +125.79, +136.13 and −74.89 mm.
- Generic glass descriptions became Unmatched labels retaining the printed pairs: 1.613 / 57.6 on Elements 1 and 3; 1.699 / 30.3 on Elements 2 and 4; 1.652 / 33.5 on Element 5; and 1.644 / 48.3 on Element 6. Every relabel has Δn = 0 and Δν = 0. No catalog row reproduces both coordinates within the printed precision, so no trade name is assigned.

No radius, thickness, air-space total, index, Abbe number, stop station, clear radius, field angle, image-plane position or focus geometry changed. The resulting EFL is 99.87 mm, paraxial aperture f/2.50, BFD 48.91 mm, iris radius 14.39 mm and half-field 5.0°, identical to the original optical values.

## Remaining Questions

- The drawing's larger second and rear doublets remain inconsistent with the estimated clear radii, particularly where larger rims would approach a knife edge. Their actual physical outlines are unknown.
- The actual diaphragm station and bore, minimum iris opening and any wider useful field are unknown.
- All four distinct glass pairs lack matching catalog curves at the printed precision. Historical suppliers, melt identities and measured spectral dispersion remain unknown.
- No cited source identifies a production lens using Example I or supplies its mount, image format, blade count or shape, marketed specifications or near-focus mechanism.
