# COOKE 100mm f/3.5 (US 2,601,595 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,601,595 A

**Inventors:** Gordon Henry Cook

**Assignee:** Taylor, Taylor & Hobson Limited

**Published:** 24 June 1952

**Filed:** 13 March 1951, serial 215,255

**British priority:** 15 February 1951

**Title:** Four Component Optical Objective

**Example details:** Example VII, Figure 7 and the table in column 7; nominal f/3.5 and a 25° half-field.

The patent describes a higher-correction objective for half-fields up to 30°, the smaller-coverage counterpart of US 2,601,593, with six elements arranged as four functional components. Example VII is the broken-contact variant of Example IV: Example IV has a cemented rear doublet, while Example VII separates its two rear elements with a small air gap and changes the rear element's thickness and radii. No production lens is documented for this prescription.

The patent prints focal length F = 1.000. Every dimensional prescription value is multiplied by 100 to express the model in millimetres at nominal f = 100 mm. The resulting paraxial EFL is 100.025 mm, 0.025 mm above nominal. The patent specifies neither an absolute focal length nor a film format. Figure 7 on PDF p. 2 agrees with the table on PDF p. 6. [Primary facsimile](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/d6/6a/80/c3a3f738372978/US2601595.pdf).

The patent distinguishes the companion US 2,601,593 A, serial 214,508, as a wider-coverage design. Its calcium-fluoride Example VI is a different prescription. The cited GB 295,519 A provides earlier four-component context rather than the numerical data used here.

## Optical Architecture

Six elements form five groups separated by air: 1+2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6. Elements 1 and 2 form the cemented front doublet C1, sharing R2. The rear pair is separated between R9 and R10 by 0.5 mm of air. The patent's four functional components therefore correspond to five physical groups in this example.

The two positive inner menisci sit on either side of the central stop, between the negative outer components. All eleven refracting boundaries are spherical. The model contains no additional plate, filter or detailed bevel geometry.

The first-to-last refracting vertex distance is 38.2 mm. The patent places the stop approximately midway between R5 and R6. An equal 2.55/2.55 mm split of that air space places the modeled stop 19.55 mm behind the first vertex. Its exact station is inferred, and its 11.067 mm radius is derived from the nominal f/3.5 aperture.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### Elements 1–2 — Front cemented meniscus component

Element 1: nD = 1.6570, νD = 50.8. Glass: J-SSK5 (Hikari), spectral proxy. Standalone focal length in air: +58.67 mm.

Element 2: nD = 1.6205, νD = 36.2. Glass: S-TIM2 (Ohara), spectral proxy. Standalone focal length in air: −42.54 mm.

The positive front meniscus and negative rear meniscus together form a weak negative component with focal length −471.74 mm. Their shared cemented surface distinguishes it from the air-spaced rear pair. The suggested front diameters give 15 mm semi-diameters at R1 and R2; R3 uses 11.3 mm to clear the f/3.5 axial marginal ray.

### Element 3 — Inner positive meniscus

nD = 1.6234, νD = 56.2. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.6234 / ν 56.2). Standalone focal length in air: +114.13 mm.

This positive meniscus lies ahead of the stop. Its +28.41/+45.25 mm faces enclose 3.2 mm of glass. R4's 12.3 mm semi-diameter is inferred; R5 uses 11.05 mm to clear the axial beam.

### Element 4 — Inner positive meniscus

nD = 1.6234, νD = 56.2. Glass: Unmatched (nD 1.6234 / ν 56.2). Standalone focal length in air: +120.95 mm.

The second positive inner meniscus lies behind the stop and is 3.6 mm thick. Its curvature faces the opposite direction from Element 3. R6 follows the suggested chamfer diameter with an 11 mm semi-diameter; R7's 12.6 mm semi-diameter is inferred.

### Element 5 — Negative meniscus of the rear component

nD = 1.6055, νD = 38.0. Glass: F15 (Hoya), a catalog match at the printed sodium-D coordinates. Standalone focal length in air: −39.57 mm.

This negative meniscus is 4.0 mm thick and forms the front member of the rear component. R8 has an 11 mm semi-diameter from the suggested chamfer diameter; R9's 13 mm semi-diameter is inferred. The 0.5 mm air gap behind it replaces the cemented rear contact of Example IV.

### Element 6 — Positive meniscus of the rear component

nD = 1.6570, νD = 50.8. Glass: J-SSK5 (Hikari), spectral proxy. Standalone focal length in air: +45.28 mm.

The final positive meniscus is 5.2 mm thick. Its glass coordinates repeat those of Element 1. The suggested rear diameter gives 13 mm semi-diameters at R10 and R11. R9 and R10 remain separate glass-to-air and air-to-glass boundaries.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent explicitly specifies sodium-D indices, at about 589.29 nm, and Abbe numbers. The six printed pairs are retained. No supplier, melt, extra spectral index or partial dispersion is printed.

Element 5 matches Hoya F15 within |ΔnD| ≤ 0.0001 and |ΔνD| ≤ 0.15. The catalog gives nD = 1.60551 and νD = 37.89, differences of +0.000011 and −0.11.

Elements 1 and 6 use Hikari J-SSK5 as a spectral proxy, with catalog-minus-printed differences ΔnD = +0.00133 and ΔνD = +0.03. Element 2 uses Ohara S-TIM2 as a spectral proxy, with ΔnD = −0.00061 and ΔνD = +0.05. Both rows are within the proxy limits of |ΔnD| ≤ 0.002 and |ΔνD| ≤ 0.25 at the printed sodium-D line. The printed indices and Abbe numbers are kept; these present-day rows supply only the shape of the dispersion curves. None of the catalog assignments establishes Cooke's historical supplier or melt.

Elements 3 and 4 remain unmatched because no catalog row is within the proxy limits for their 1.6234/56.2 pair. Their chromatic calculations use Abbe-based estimates. Measured broadband correction is not established.

## Focus Mechanism

The model is focused at infinity. The patent provides no close-focus distance, focus travel or internal movement law for this example. No production minimum focus distance is assigned.

The computed sodium-D paraxial back focus is 80.32 mm behind the final vertex, placing the image plane 118.52 mm behind the first vertex. The image lies at this paraxial plane. The patent prints no back focus or total track.

## Aperture and Suggested Diameters

The patent describes possible diameters rather than measured clear apertures or bevel depths. It suggests 0.30F for R1/R2 and 0.22F for the chamfer diameters at R3/R5/R6/R8. At R3 and R5, interpreting the latter as 11 mm spherical semi-diameters clips the nominal f/3.5 axial beam. The model uses 11.3 mm at R3 and 11.05 mm at R5, just above the required marginal heights of 11.278 and 11.016 mm. This interpretation follows the patent's statement that the suggested diameters exceed the axial requirement.

The rear-diameter sentence prints “.26F for R8 R10 and R11.” The repeated R8 is probably a text misprint for R9: Example IV lists R9/R10 for its cemented rear pair. The uncertain label is recorded without changing any radius or assigning a printed diameter to R9. Its 13 mm semi-diameter remains inferred, as do R4 at 12.3 mm and R7 at 12.6 mm.

Figure 7 supports the relative rim heights: the front pair is largest, the rear pair is smaller, and the inner curve ends lie near the chamfer radii. Its overall vertex spacing agrees approximately with the prescription, but Element 3's drawn axial thickness is about 14% short, so the drawing supplies proportions rather than exact clear diameters. Relative to the suggested 30 mm front diameter, R4 and R7 each measure about 11.8 mm, within about 7% of the inferred 12.3 and 12.6 mm radii. R9 agrees with the 13 mm rear rims. These inferred values and the axial-clearance radii at R3/R5 are retained; detailed bevel depths remain unknown.

The resulting surfaces retain positive glass thickness and air clearance, with no stop-plane intersection. The axial aperture is f/3.5 without a rim limit. The suggested diameters still vignette off-axis bundles; a clear chief ray does not imply an unvignetted pupil. Wide-open geometric transmission is about 83.0% at 11.1°, 69.5% at 16.7° and 45.9% at 25.0°; these are calculated pupil fractions rather than measured relative illumination.

## Field and First-Order Behavior

The patent states a 25.0° half-field, modeled as a 50.0° full field. At 25.0°, the real chief ray reaches an image height of 46.66 mm and clears every lens rim. The 93.33 mm image circle is twice that computed height at the model's scale, rounded up to two decimals. It describes the patent's stated field rather than a documented film format or measured illumination circle.

The source's mean-radius relations are retained: 0.3358F for R4/R7, 0.1828F for R3/R8 and 0.2369F for R1/R11. The stated cemented- and broken-contact curvatures are +1.94 and +0.86 in equivalent-power units, with mean +1.40. The computed Petzval sum is 0.001186 mm⁻¹, or 0.1186 relative to nominal optical power, compared with the printed 0.120. That approximately 1.2% difference is retained.

No measured MTF curve or full spectral prescription is supplied. Image-quality calculations describe the modeled spherical surfaces, inferred apertures and computed paraxial image plane.
