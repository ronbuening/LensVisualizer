# COOKE 100mm f/2 (US 2,130,760 Design)

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,130,760 A, *Lens*  
**Filed:** May 5, 1937 (Serial No. 140,789); British priority May 5, 1936  
**Granted:** September 20, 1938  
**Published:** September 20, 1938  
**Inventors:** Arthur Warmisham  
**Assignee:** Kapella Limited, Leicester, England  
**Example details:** Example II of two, Fig. 6, table on specification page 2; printed at equivalent focal length 1.004

The model is the second of the patent's two numerical examples. The patent concerns f/2 objectives with two collective outer components enclosing two compound dispersive menisci whose concave faces look at a central diaphragm. It names US 1,955,591 (H. W. Lee's six-element f/2 objective, the type sold by Taylor, Taylor & Hobson as the Cooke Speed Panchro) as the best prior lens of this kind, and sets out to reduce the over-corrected oblique spherical aberration that such lenses share. Its remedy is to make each inner meniscus a cemented triplet rather than a doublet.

No production lens is documented for this example. Kapella Limited was the patent-holding company of Taylor, Taylor & Hobson, the makers of Cooke lenses, which is the reason for the Cooke name here. Search summaries of Cooke Optics' company history and of the Cine Lens Wiki pages on the Speed Panchro series describe those lenses as six-element designs of the earlier Lee type; the pages themselves were not read in full, so this rests on excerpts. No Cooke lens with eight elements and two cemented triplets was found.

## Optical Architecture

Eight spherical elements stand in four air-spaced groups: a positive front meniscus (L1), a cemented triplet (L2, L3, L4; C1), the diaphragm space, a second cemented triplet (L5, L6, L7; C2), and a biconvex rear element (L8). Both triplets are weak negative menisci as a whole, with focal lengths of −287.3 mm and −814.3 mm.

In each triplet the element next to the diaphragm is a positive meniscus of higher index (1.652) than the negative element cemented to it (1.605). The cemented surfaces R5 and R8 are therefore strongly curved and convergent. The patent explains that the outer rays of an oblique pencil meet these surfaces at large angles and are bent back toward the chief ray, while the rays near the chief ray are little affected. This works on one half of the pencil in the front triplet and on the other half in the rear triplet.

## Prescription and Scaling

The table prints signed radii, eight thicknesses, three separations, and a refractive index and Abbe number for every element. Each length is multiplied by 100. Nothing is fitted or adjusted. The printed prescription traces to a paraxial focal length of 100.40 mm against the printed 1.004, a difference of 0.001 %.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### Element 1 (L1) — Positive Meniscus

`nD = 1.613, V = 59.3.` Focal length +150.9 mm. The front collective element, convex to the object.

### Element 2 (L2) — Positive Meniscus (front triplet)

`nD = 1.621, V = 60.4.` Focal length +78.6 mm. The outer crown of the front triplet. The patent asks for this element to have relatively low power and a higher index than the negative element behind it.

### Element 3 (L3) — Negative Meniscus (front triplet)

`nD = 1.605, V = 38.2.` Focal length −39.2 mm. The dispersive element of the front triplet, with its strong concave face R5 toward the diaphragm.

### Element 4 (L4) — Positive Meniscus (front triplet)

`nD = 1.652, V = 33.5.` Focal length +117.6 mm. The meniscus next to the diaphragm. Its index and dispersion are both higher than those of L3, which the patent claims as the feature that corrects the oblique pencil and helps the axial spherical correction.

### Element 5 (L5) — Positive Meniscus (rear triplet)

`nD = 1.652, V = 33.5.` Focal length +97.9 mm. The mirror counterpart of L4 behind the diaphragm.

### Element 6 (L6) — Biconcave Negative (rear triplet)

`nD = 1.605, V = 38.2.` Focal length −31.7 mm. The dispersive element of the rear triplet.

### Element 7 (L7) — Biconvex Positive (rear triplet)

`nD = 1.621, V = 60.4.` Focal length +52.7 mm. The outer crown of the rear triplet. Its front face R9 is nearly flat (radius 200 mm).

### Element 8 (L8) — Biconvex Positive

`nD = 1.621, V = 60.4.` Focal length +109.7 mm. The rear collective element.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent defines each glass by its mean index for the sodium D line and its Abbe number, and names no glass type or maker. The model keeps the printed pairs as D-line values. None of the four pairs matches a current catalogue glass within the printed precision, so each element is labelled unmatched with its printed values. Four glasses are used: a dense crown at 1.613 / 59.3, a dense crown at 1.621 / 60.4 (three elements), a flint at 1.605 / 38.2 (two elements) and a dense flint at 1.652 / 33.5 (two elements). Colour in the model follows from the index and Abbe number alone, so it supports no claim about secondary spectrum.

## Stop, Clear Apertures, and Focus

The patent says the diaphragm lies between the two triplets but prints no position, and Fig. 6 does not draw it. The model places it midway through the 16 mm central air space.

No clear apertures are printed. Fig. 6 is drawn to scale: its vertex spacings and the curves of R1, R6 and R10 agree with the table. It draws L1 at about 24 mm, the front triplet with a square top at about 22.4 mm, the rims beside the diaphragm at about 16.4 mm, the rear triplet with a square top at about 19 mm, and L8 at about 19 mm. The model's semi-diameters are 25.8 mm for L1, 23 mm for the front of the front triplet, 17.5 mm for the faces next to the diaphragm, 18.3 mm for L6 and L7, and 19.1 mm for L8. L1 is larger than drawn because the f/2 entrance beam is 25.1 mm in radius and a smaller front element would darken the field. The rims beside the diaphragm are larger than drawn because the axial beam needs about 17.3 mm at R5.

The f/2 aperture is a paraxial figure. A real marginal ray entering at the f/2 height of 25.10 mm would need 23.2 mm of clear glass at R3 and R4 and about 19.6 mm at R9 and R10. With the printed thicknesses, the edge of L2 closes to zero at about 23.06 mm and the edge of L7 at about 18.4 mm, so these elements cannot be made large enough. The drawing gives L7 a slightly thicker edge than the table allows. In the model the widest axial ray that passes enters at 23.74 mm, about f/2.11, limited by L7. Inside that aperture the longitudinal spherical aberration stays within about ±0.06 mm.

The patent states only that the field should exceed the 45 to 50 degrees total of the earlier type. In the model the chief ray clears every rim out to about 29° off axis, a total field of about 58°. The oblique bundle is vignetted on one side by the rear triplet and L8: by 14 to 17 % at 10° at f/2.2. Geometric transmission at 12.2° off axis is about 76 % of the axial value.

Only infinity focus is modelled. The patent gives no focusing data.

## Model Scope and Limitations

- Every radius, thickness, separation, index and Abbe number is the patent's printed value at the stated scale. Fig. 6 agrees on the element count, the two cemented triplets and the sign of every radius.
- The calculated paraxial focal length is 100.40 mm against the printed 1.004, the paraxial aperture is f/2.00 and the back focus is 71.1054 mm.
- The diaphragm position, the clear semi-diameters and the back focus are not printed and are set as described above; the semi-diameters follow Fig. 6 except where the rays or the edge thickness require otherwise. The real-ray aperture is limited by the edge thickness of L7.
- The patent prints no field angle, image format or close-focus data, so the model claims none.
