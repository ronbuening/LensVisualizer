# Warmisham Example II 100.4 mm f/2 — US 2,130,760 Example II

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,130,760 A, *Lens*  
**Filed:** May 5, 1937 (Serial No. 140,789); British priority May 5, 1936  
**Granted:** September 20, 1938  
**Published:** September 20, 1938  
**Inventors:** Arthur Warmisham  
**Assignee:** Kapella Limited, Leicester, England  
**Example details:** Example II of two, Fig. 6, table on specification page 2; printed at equivalent focal length 1.004 and scaled ×100 here

The model is the second of the patent's two numerical examples. The patent concerns f/2 objectives with two collective outer components enclosing two compound dispersive menisci whose concave faces look at a central diaphragm. It names US 1,955,591 (H. W. Lee's six-element f/2 objective, the type sold by Taylor, Taylor & Hobson as the Cooke Speed Panchro) as the best prior lens of this kind, and sets out to reduce the over-corrected oblique spherical aberration that such lenses share. Its remedy is to make each inner meniscus a cemented triplet rather than a doublet.

No production lens has been matched to this example. Kapella Limited was the patent-holding company for Taylor, Taylor & Hobson, which is why the file is kept with the Cooke designs, but the Speed Panchro lenses described by Cooke Optics' own history and by Cine Lens Wiki are six-element lenses of the earlier Lee type. No Cooke lens with eight elements and two cemented triplets was found. The model is therefore a patent design reference at the patent's own scale, not a reconstruction of a lens that was sold.

## Optical Architecture

Eight spherical elements stand in four air-spaced groups: a positive front meniscus (L1), a cemented triplet (L2, L3, L4), the diaphragm space, a second cemented triplet (L5, L6, L7), and a biconvex rear element (L8). Both triplets are weak negative menisci as a whole, with focal lengths of −287.3 mm and −814.3 mm.

In each triplet the element next to the diaphragm is a positive meniscus of higher index (1.652) than the negative element cemented to it (1.605). The cemented surfaces R5 and R8 are therefore strongly curved and convergent. The patent explains that the outer rays of an oblique pencil meet these surfaces at large angles and are bent back toward the chief ray, while the rays near the chief ray are little affected. This works on one half of the pencil in the front triplet and on the other half in the rear triplet.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Positive Meniscus

`nD = 1.613, V = 59.3.` Focal length +150.9 mm. The front collective element, convex to the object.

### L2 — Positive Meniscus (front triplet)

`nD = 1.621, V = 60.4.` Focal length +78.6 mm. The outer crown of the front triplet. The patent asks for this element to have relatively low power and a higher index than the negative element behind it.

### L3 — Negative Meniscus (front triplet)

`nD = 1.605, V = 38.2.` Focal length −39.2 mm. The dispersive element of the front triplet, with its strong concave face R5 toward the diaphragm.

### L4 — Positive Meniscus (front triplet)

`nD = 1.652, V = 33.5.` Focal length +117.6 mm. The meniscus next to the diaphragm. Its index and dispersion are both higher than those of L3, which the patent claims as the feature that corrects the oblique pencil and helps the axial spherical correction.

### L5 — Positive Meniscus (rear triplet)

`nD = 1.652, V = 33.5.` Focal length +97.9 mm. The mirror counterpart of L4 behind the diaphragm.

### L6 — Biconcave (rear triplet)

`nD = 1.605, V = 38.2.` Focal length −31.7 mm. The dispersive element of the rear triplet.

### L7 — Biconvex (rear triplet)

`nD = 1.621, V = 60.4.` Focal length +52.7 mm. The outer crown of the rear triplet. Its front face R9 is nearly flat (radius 200 mm).

### L8 — Biconvex

`nD = 1.621, V = 60.4.` Focal length +109.7 mm. The rear collective element.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent defines each glass by its mean index for the sodium D line and its Abbe number, and names no glass type or maker. The data file keeps the printed pairs and marks the indices as D-line values. Four glasses are used: a dense crown at 1.613 / 59.3, a dense crown at 1.621 / 60.4 (three elements), a flint at 1.605 / 38.2 (two elements) and a dense flint at 1.652 / 33.5 (two elements). Colour in the model follows from the index and Abbe number alone, so it supports no claim about secondary spectrum.

## Stop, Clear Apertures, and Focus

The patent says the diaphragm lies between the two triplets but prints no position, and Fig. 6 does not draw it. The model places it midway through the 16 mm central air space.

No clear apertures are printed. The stored semi-diameters are estimates: 28 mm for L1, 23 mm for the front of the front triplet, 17.5 mm for the faces next to the diaphragm, 18 mm for the rear of the rear triplet and 25 mm for L8. They have not been measured from Fig. 6.

The f/2 aperture is a paraxial figure. A real marginal ray entering at the f/2 height of 25.10 mm would need 23.2 mm of clear glass at R3 and R4 and about 19.6 mm at R9 and R10. With the printed thicknesses, the edge of L2 closes to zero at about 23.06 mm and the edge of L7 at about 18.4 mm, so these elements cannot be made large enough. With the rims as stored, the widest axial ray that passes enters at 23.39 mm, which corresponds to about f/2.15. Inside that aperture the longitudinal spherical aberration stays within about ±0.06 mm.

The patent states only that the field should exceed the 45 to 50 degrees total of the earlier type. In the model the chief ray is clear to at least 22.65° off axis; the oblique bundle is vignetted by the rear triplet, by 16 to 18 % on one side at 10°.

Only infinity focus is modelled. The patent gives no focusing data.

## Verification Summary

- All 12 radii, 8 thicknesses, 3 separations, 8 indices and 8 Abbe numbers were compared with the page image of the patent table. There are no differences.
- Fig. 6 agrees on the element count, the two cemented triplets and the sign of every radius.
- Calculated paraxial focal length 100.40 mm (printed 1.004 × 100 = 100.4), paraxial aperture f/2.00, back focus 71.1054 mm.
- The repository's surface validator reports no errors.
- Sources for the product comparison: Cooke Optics' company history (cookeoptics.com) and the Cine Lens Wiki pages on the Speed Panchro series; US 1,955,591 for the earlier Lee design.
