# Rudolph 100mm f/3.2 patent reference

## Patent Reference / Design Identification
**Patent:** [US1812717](https://patents.google.com/patent/US1812717A/en)
**Inventor:** Paul Rudolph
**Filed:** October 14, 1926 (Germany August 18, 1926)
**Granted:** June 30, 1931
**Title:** Lens System

Sole numerical example. Patent-only, no exact commercial product or factory prescription identity. Historical five-lens terminology counts the front cemented pair as one group; the model has six glass elements in five groups. The original [scan](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/pdfs/US1812717.pdf) supplies prescription on PDF2 and glass data PDF3.

## Optical Architecture
Front collector, front cemented positive/negative pair, central negative meniscus and two rear positive menisci. The stop is inside the central air gap: 5.41mm after R5, 2.32mm before R6. Zero axial AIR at R2/R3 is retained, not cemented or artificially opened. R4 is the actual flat cement interface.

## Element-by-Element Analysis
### L1: positive meniscus
nD=1.5331, νD=58.0. Unidentified source glass, no named vendor. Front collecting element.
### L2′/L2: cemented pair
L2′ nD=1.5795, νD=53.8; L2 nD=1.5749, νD=41.3. Plano-convex followed by plano-concave element, shared flat interface. Together form the front thick dispersive group described in the patent. No invented isolated-element performance figures.
### L3: negative meniscus
nD=1.5331, νD=58.0. Central dispersive element following iris.
### L4: positive meniscus
nD=1.5749, νD=41.3. Rear collecting element.
### L5: positive meniscus
nD=1.5795, νD=53.8. Final collecting element.

## Glass Identification / Selection
Three literal source pairs repeated across six elements; no factory glass identity is known. D reference is explicit. C/d/F calculations use native estimated partial dispersions from nD/ν, not a measured full dispersion curve.
