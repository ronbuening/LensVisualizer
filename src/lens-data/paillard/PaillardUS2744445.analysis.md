# PAILLARD 100mm f/1.4 (US 2,744,445 Design)

## Patent and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,744,445 A, _Photographic and Projection Objective of the Petzval Type_

**Inventors:** Arnold Werfeli

**Assignee:** Paillard S.A., Sainte-Croix, Switzerland

**Published:** May 8, 1956

**Filed:** October 6, 1954, serial 460,650

**Priority:** Switzerland, November 4, 1953

**Example details:** Figure 1; the description table on PDF page 2 is repeated in claim 2 on PDF page 3.

The patent gives this four-element objective at f = 100 mm, f/1.4 and a field of ±8°. Claim 3 supplies a second numerical set with a modified rear group; that set is not modeled here. DE 1,086,911 B is a member of the same patent family and gives the same radii. [US patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2744445A/en), [German family member](https://patents.google.com/patent/DE1086911B/en).

The Petzval-type layout and narrow field suit cinematographic projection, including an 8 mm gate at a shorter focal length. This is an application inference, not a documented production identity. The Cinémathèque française's Bolex M8 record identifies a Kern-Paillard 20 mm f/1.6 lens, and the patent itself describes known ±8° Petzvals as limited to f/1.6. No product is documented for the present f/1.4 prescription. [Bolex M8 collection record](https://www.cinematheque.fr/fr/catalogues/appareils/collection/projecteur-de-film-8-mmap-00-2191-1-2.html).

## Optical Architecture

Four air-spaced singlets form two converging achromatic pairs, each with a positive element followed by a negative element. There are no cemented interfaces or aspherical surfaces. The front pair has a 1.15 mm air gap, the pairs are separated by 73.8 mm, and the rear pair has a 1.75 mm air gap.

Figure 1 places the object to the left and the film plane F to the right. The model traces collimated light from left to right in that reduction direction. The 119.2 mm first-to-last-surface track follows the printed thicknesses and separations. The patent does not specify the finite conjugates of a projector installation.

The invention reduces the Petzval sum through its distribution of curvature, thickness and glass index. It requires the magnitudes of R2 and R3 to exceed 1.3 times R1, R1 to exceed 0.8 f, and the rear positive element to have a thickness between 0.10 f and 0.25 f. The printed example satisfies these conditions. Its paraxial Petzval sum is 0.00839325 mm⁻¹.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### Element 1 / L1 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.67741, ν = 55.6. Glass: Unmatched. Standalone focal length in air: +87.79 mm.

The +89.8/−162.2 mm surfaces enclose 17.5 mm of glass. This is the positive member of the front achromatic pair. Its inferred front and rear clear semi-diameters are 39 and 36 mm.

### Element 2 / L2 — Biconcave Negative

nD = 1.68873, ν = 31.2. Glass: FD8 (Hoya), a catalogue match in index and Abbe number. Standalone focal length in air: −139.33 mm.

The −131.55/+365.7 mm surfaces enclose 10 mm of glass. Its stronger dispersion offsets colour from the positive front member. The inferred clear semi-diameters are 36 and 38 mm; the 1.15 mm air gap separates it from Element 1.

### Element 3 / L3 — Biconvex Positive

nD = 1.67741, ν = 55.6. Glass: Unmatched. Standalone focal length in air: +48.77 mm.

The +57.85/−70.3 mm surfaces enclose 12.5 mm of glass. This element supplies positive power in the rear pair. It has the same printed glass values as Element 1 and inferred clear semi-diameters of 26 and 25.6 mm.

### Element 4 / L4 — Plano-Concave Negative

nD = 1.74174, ν = 27.5. Glass: Unmatched. Standalone focal length in air: −80.82 mm.

The −59.95 mm front surface and plane rear surface enclose 2.5 mm of glass. This is the negative, more dispersive member of the rear achromatic pair. The patent emphasizes its index above 1.72 and its index difference from Element 3 above 0.05. Its inferred clear semi-diameters are 27 and 26.5 mm. R8 is an optical exit surface.

## Glass Identification and Spectral Limits

The patent explicitly gives indices for the D line, taken as sodium at 589.3 nm, together with Abbe numbers. Those printed values are retained. It supplies no individual C-, F- or g-line indices or partial-dispersion data.

Hoya FD8 evaluates to nD = 1.6887384, with catalogue Abbe number 31.158857, compared with Element 2's printed 1.68873 and 31.2. The differences are +0.0000084 and −0.041143. This match supports use of its catalogue dispersion curve; it does not identify the historical supplier or melt. No sufficiently close catalogue row was found for Elements 1, 3 or 4, which retain their printed values and Unmatched labels.

Chromatic estimates for the unmatched glasses depend on Abbe-based dispersion. The available data do not establish broadband performance or anomalous partial dispersion.

## Focus Mechanism and Image Plane

The model represents infinity focus only. The patent gives no close-focus distances, focus travel or movement law, so the elements remain fixed.

The printed prescription computes to EFL 99.7065 mm, 0.2935% below the stated 100 mm. Its paraxial back focal distance is 37.7458 mm, placing the image plane 156.9458 mm behind the first vertex. Back focus is not printed; the model uses this paraxial plane without a shift to improve image sharpness.

The patent's ±8° field is retained. With the inferred stop described below, the real 8° chief ray reaches image height 13.8562 mm at that plane. Twice this height gives the catalogued 27.7124 mm image circle at the model's focal length. This is derived from the printed field, not a measured film-format dimension.

## Inferred Stop and Clear Apertures

Figure 1 shows no iris, and the patent gives neither a stop station nor its diameter. The inferred stop is 3 mm behind R4, splitting the 73.8 mm air space into 3 and 70.8 mm. At the 38 mm R4 rim, the surface sag is 1.98 mm, leaving the stop clear of the glass.

This position is supported by Figure 3 and the text's limits of 0.17% f for mean field curvature and below 0.65% f for astigmatism. Real-ray fits with a stop 0–4 mm behind R4 give mean curvature of about 0.18–0.20% f and astigmatism of about 0.64–0.68% f. These are closer to the printed claims than a midpoint stop, which gives mean curvature about 0.37% f and the wrong sign for the edge tangential focus. The exact historical stop station remains unknown, and the inferred position does not reproduce every printed bound exactly.

The paraxial f/1.4 stop has radius 30.4070 mm. Its accepted real axial entrance radius is 34.1842 mm, corresponding to f/1.4584. The iris sets that aperture; the glass rims do not limit the axial beam. The 8° chief ray clears every rim, while approximately 95% of the on-axis pupil area remains at 8°. This area ratio describes modeled vignetting, not measured transmission.

The clear semi-diameters remain inferred. The minimum front-pair air clearance is about 0.174 mm, the rear positive element retains about 1.701 mm minimum glass thickness, and the rear-pair air clearance is about 0.836 mm. Their precise relation to the drawn rims remains unresolved.

## Aberration Curves and MTF Scope

Figure 2 plots spherical aberration and the sine condition; Figure 3 plots tangential and sagittal field curvature. The prescription reproduces the undercorrected spherical zone, with longitudinal shifts of approximately +0.10, −0.05, −0.13 and −0.11 mm across the pupil zones. These curves support the design's correction strategy rather than a manufactured-lens performance claim.

The patent publishes no MTF curve. Geometric MTF estimates depend on the inferred stop, pupil vignetting and chosen image plane. D-line results describe monochromatic geometric performance; diffraction, coatings, glass dispersion beyond the supplied data and manufacturing tolerances require additional information.
