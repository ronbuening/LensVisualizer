# PAILLARD 100mm f/1.4 (US 2,744,445 Design) — Audit

## Source

US 2,744,445 A names Arnold Werfeli of Yverdon and Paillard S. A. of Sainte-Croix. The catalog spelling is Paillard S.A. The application was filed October 6, 1954, with Swiss priority November 4, 1953, and was published May 8, 1956. Figure 1 and claim 2 supply this prescription. Claim 3 supplies a second numerical set with a modified rear group. DE 1,086,911 B belongs to the same family and has the same radii.

## Transcription

Two independent readings of the page images agree with all eight surface radii and signs, seven thicknesses and air separations, four D-line indices and four Abbe numbers. The main table on PDF page 2 agrees with claim 2 on page 3. There are four air-spaced singlets, no cemented interfaces, no aspheres and no variable gaps. The plane R8 is an optical exit surface.

## First-order Properties

The printed focal length is 100 mm, the relative aperture f/1.4 and the half-field 8°. The prescription computes to EFL 99.7065 mm, a −0.2935% difference, and paraxial BFD 37.7458 mm. The optical track before back focus is 119.2 mm and the image plane is at 156.9458 mm from the first vertex. No correction to a printed prescription value is supported.

## Not Printed

The patent prints no stop position, stop diameter, clear semi-diameters, back focal distance or finite-conjugate focus law. The image plane is paraxial, the rims are inferred, and the stop diameter follows the paraxial f/1.4 aperture. The image circle is derived from the real chief ray at the printed ±8° field.

The initial model placed the stop at the midpoint of the 73.8 mm gap and admitted a real axial aperture of f/1.4684. That position disagrees with Figure 3: it gives mean field curvature near 0.37% f and an edge tangential shift near −0.15 mm, where the drawing is near +0.25 mm. A stop 0–4 mm behind R4 gives closer agreement, with mean curvature about 0.18–0.20% f and astigmatism about 0.64–0.68% f. The patent claims at most 0.17% f and less than 0.65% f respectively. The model uses 3 mm behind R4, clear of its 1.98 mm rim sag.

## Product

No production lens is documented for this prescription. Its Petzval-type form suits 8 mm projection at a shorter focal length. The Cinémathèque française documents a Bolex M8 with a Kern-Paillard 20 mm f/1.6 lens; this does not establish a connection to Werfeli's f/1.4 design. The patent notes that known Petzvals at ±8° were limited to f/1.6. The taking-lens Switar is not an established identification.

## Corrections

The inferred midpoint stop is replaced by a station supported more closely by the patent's field curves. The prescription's radii, glass thicknesses, total air gaps, indices, Abbe numbers, clear semi-diameters and image plane remain intact. Numerical MTF claims for the midpoint stop are removed because they describe a different pupil geometry.

## Remaining Questions

The exact stop station, iris diameter, factory rims, glass suppliers, finite conjugates and production identity remain unknown. The selected stop improves agreement with Figure 3 but leaves a small difference from the text's field-curvature and astigmatism limits. Abbe-based dispersion for the unmatched glasses cannot establish the original broadband correction.
