# PAILLARD 100mm f/1.4 (US 2,744,445 Design) — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,744,445 A, _Photographic and Projection Objective of the Petzval Type_](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2744445A/en), names Arnold Werfeli of Yverdon, Switzerland, as inventor and Paillard S. A., Sainte-Croix, Switzerland, as assignee. The application was filed October 6, 1954, serial 460,650, with Swiss priority November 4, 1953, and was published May 8, 1956. The assignee is recorded as Paillard S.A.

Figure 1 on PDF page 1 illustrates the main prescription, printed in the description on PDF page 2 and repeated in claim 2 on PDF page 3. Claim 3 gives a second numerical set with a modified rear group; it is not the design described here. [DE 1,086,911 B](https://patents.google.com/patent/DE1086911B/en) is a German family member with the same radii.

The [Cinémathèque française's Bolex M8 record, AP-00-2191(1/2)](https://www.cinematheque.fr/fr/catalogues/appareils/collection/projecteur-de-film-8-mmap-00-2191-1-2.html), documents a Kern-Paillard 20 mm f/1.6 projection lens, providing application context without identifying this f/1.4 design.

## Transcription Check

All eight radii and signs, seven glass thicknesses and air separations, four D-line indices and four Abbe numbers agree with the page images. The main table on PDF page 2 agrees with claim 2 on page 3. No transcription mismatch was found. Dimensions retain the printed millimetre units and 100 mm focal-length scale. The 73.8 mm central air gap remains intact despite its division at the inferred stop.

Four air-spaced singlets form two converging achromatic pairs. There are no cemented interfaces, aspherical surfaces or variable gaps. Their shapes are biconvex positive, biconcave negative, biconvex positive and plano-concave negative. R8 is an optical exit surface. The patent explicitly identifies the sodium D line; the printed indices have not been relabeled as helium d.

## First-Order Check

The patent prints f = 100 mm, f/1.4 and a half-field of 8.0°. Calculated EFL is 99.71 mm, 0.3% below the stated focal length; the paraxial f-number is 1.40. No correction to a printed prescription value is supported. Calculated BFD is 37.75 mm, and the image plane is at paraxial focus, 156.95 mm behind the first vertex. The first-to-last-surface track is 119.20 mm. Back focus is not printed.

The stop radius is 30.41 mm. Its accepted real axial entrance radius is 34.18 mm, equivalent to f/1.46. The iris limits that beam; no glass rim blocks it. The 8.0° chief ray clears every rim and reaches image height 13.86 mm. Approximately 95.0% of the on-axis pupil area remains at 8.0°; this is a geometric area ratio, not measured transmission. The surfaces stay clear of each other, and the inferred rims contain the field-derived image circle.

The paraxial Petzval sum is 0.008393 mm⁻¹. Longitudinal spherical shifts of approximately +0.10, −0.05, −0.13 and −0.11 mm across the pupil follow Figure 2's undercorrected zone. The patent publishes no MTF curves; no numerical MTF performance is established for a manufactured lens.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

Figure 1 shows no iris, and the patent gives no stop station or diameter, clear semi-diameters, back focus or finite-conjugate focus law. The stop is inferred 3 mm behind R4, dividing the central air space into 3 and 70.8 mm. R4's sag at its 38 mm rim is 1.98 mm, leaving the stop clear of the glass.

The inferred clear semi-diameters at R1–R8 are 39, 36, 36, 38, 26, 25.6, 27 and 26.5 mm. Minimum front-pair air clearance is about 0.174 mm, minimum rear-positive glass thickness about 1.701 mm, and minimum rear-pair air clearance about 0.836 mm. Their precise relation to Figure 1's optical rims and chamfers remains unknown.

The image circle is not a printed diameter. Twice the real chief-ray height at the printed ±8.0° field gives 27.72 mm when rounded upward to two decimals. It does not establish a production film format or a fully unvignetted edge pupil. `focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. No finite-focus movement is described. The selectable f/1.4–f/16 range is not a published diaphragm range.

The patent names no glass supplier or catalogue glass and gives no individual C-, F- or g-line indices or partial dispersion. FD8 (Hoya) supplies an approximate dispersion curve for Element 2; the other three elements use their printed index and Abbe values with Abbe-based dispersion. No anomalous-dispersion designation is assigned.

## Product Identification

No production lens is documented for this prescription. The Petzval-type form and narrow field suit cinematographic projection, including an 8 mm gate at a shorter focal length. This is an application inference. The museum's Kern-Paillard 20 mm f/1.6 lens does not establish a connection to Werfeli's f/1.4 design; the patent itself describes known ±8.0° Petzvals as limited to f/1.6. A Switar taking-lens identity is not established. Paillard identifies the patent assignee here, without establishing who manufactured a lens to this prescription.

## Corrections, 2026-11-21

- The identity changed from an inventor-based title to PAILLARD 100mm f/1.4 (US 2,744,445 Design), listed under Paillard with a maker-and-patent key and visible in the catalogue. Figure 1, Arnold Werfeli, Paillard S.A. and the 1956 publication year identify the source.
- The subtitle identifies the patent, Figure 1, Paillard and Werfeli. The specifications give four elements in four groups, spherical surfaces, the printed 100 mm scale, f/1.4 and the projection application.
- Element labels became Element 1–4, retaining L1–L4 in the diagram. Optical roles and standalone focal lengths of +87.79, −139.33, +48.77 and −80.82 mm were added. Brackets identify the front and rear air-spaced achromatic pairs named in the patent.
- Generic glass descriptions became explicit Unmatched labels with printed nD/ν for Elements 1, 3 and 4, and FD8 (Hoya) for Element 2. FD8 differs from the printed pair by ΔnD = +0.0000084 and Δν = −0.04, using its catalogue Abbe number. Its index lies slightly beyond the printed rounding range, so the curve is approximate and does not establish historical material identity. LAC12 (Hoya), considered for Elements 1 and 3, differs by about +0.00038 in nD and −0.08 in catalogue ν; E-FD13 (Hoya), considered for Element 4, differs by about −0.0012 and +0.26. Those glasses were not assigned because their indices do not match the printed precision. All printed glass values and their D-line reference were retained.
- The midpoint stop split of 36.9/36.9 mm became 3/70.8 mm. The midpoint gives mean field curvature near 0.37% f and edge tangential shift near −0.15 mm, against about +0.25 mm in Figure 3. Stations 0–4 mm behind R4 give mean curvature about 0.18–0.20% f and astigmatism about 0.64–0.68% f, closer to the printed limits of 0.17% f and below 0.65% f. The chosen 3 mm station clears the glass but does not reproduce every bound exactly. Stop radius increased from 24.37 to 30.41 mm; the accepted real axial aperture improved from about f/1.47 to f/1.46. Total air space, rims and paraxial image plane were retained.
- The design focal-length value changed from the printed 100 mm to the calculated 99.71 mm; the printed scale remains explicit. The inferred image-circle diameter changed from about 27.716 to 27.72 mm to follow the revised 8.0° chief ray. The field angles remain as printed.

After these corrections: EFL 99.71 mm, paraxial f/1.40, BFD 37.75 mm, first-vertex-to-image distance 156.95 mm, stop radius 30.41 mm and half-field 8.0°.

## Remaining Questions

- The exact historical stop station, iris diameter, clear rims and edge chamfers are unknown. The inferred stop leaves small differences from the printed field-curvature and astigmatism limits.
- Elements 1, 3 and 4 lack catalogue matches at the printed precision. Element 2's FD8 curve is approximate. Historical suppliers, melts and broadband correction remain unknown.
- The 0.3% focal-length difference is unexplained; the identical tables supply no evidence for changing a printed value.
- Finite conjugates, focus travel, production identity, mount, image format, marketed focal length and aperture, minimum focus distance, diaphragm blade count and shape, minimum opening and teleconverter compatibility are undocumented and remain unassigned.
