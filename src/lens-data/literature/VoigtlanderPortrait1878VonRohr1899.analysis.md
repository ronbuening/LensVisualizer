# Voigtländer Cemented-Rear Petzval Portrait f/3.5 — von Rohr 1899, Fig. 107

## Source and Design Identification

**Source:** Moritz von Rohr, *Theorie und Geschichte des photographischen Objektivs* (Berlin: Julius Springer, 1899), p. 276, Fig. 107
**Data after:** Fr. von Voigtländer (radii), J. M. Eder (lens and stop distances)
**Designer:** Fr. von Voigtländer, as the caption credits him; von Rohr suspects H. Zincke gen. Sommer
**Manufacturer:** Voigtländer & Sohn, Braunschweig
**Source dated:** 1899
**Design dated:** 1878

Fig. 107 is captioned "Fr. von Voigtländer: Modifikation des Petzval-Objektivs" and is drawn again as No. 107 on
Tafel I, with spherical-aberration curves (107a) and astigmatism curves (107b). On p. 276 the Voigtländer firm is
introducing, in 1878, "ein Portraitobjektiv mit verkitteter Hinterlinse", a portrait objective with a cemented rear
lens; p. 319 calls it the modification of the Petzval portrait objective with cemented rear lens, protected by patents
granted in November 1878, and notes that the firm pointed to the smaller light loss of the cemented rear member.

The caption takes the radii from "Fr. von Voigtländer. 2." and the distances of the lenses and the stop from
"J. M. Eder. 1. 125". The bibliography resolves both. Voigtländer entry 2 (p. 433) is the German patent D.R.P. 5761
of 14 November 1878, "Neuerungen in der Construction von Doppelobjectiven für photographische Apparate", for a
portrait objective with cemented rear lens, with the British patent 4756 of 22 November 1878 on the same subject taken
through G. W. von Nawrocki. Eder entry 1 (p. 411) is *Die photographischen Objektive, ihre Eigenschaften und Prüfung*
(Halle: W. Knapp, 1891), whose p. 125 describes the "Porträtobjectiv von Voigtländer vom Jahre 1878" and its Fig. 81.

Page 318 dates the firm: Fr. Voigtländer handed the optical works formally to his only son, Friedrich von Voigtländer,
on 1 October 1876 and died on 7 April 1878; the firm then traded as Voigtländer & Sohn in Braunschweig. The 1878
patentee is therefore the son. Who computed the design von Rohr leaves open: p. 276 says the type may go back to the
author of Fig. 106, H. Zincke gen. Sommer, Fr. Voigtländer's stepson, who probably computed for the firm (p. 318), and
p. 315 says only the firm could confirm it. The design date, 1878, and the maker are established; the designer is not.

## Reconstruction Status

**Printed by von Rohr.** Reduction to fD = 100 mm; computation for 1:3.5 and ω = 12°; the radius magnitudes
r1 = 64.7, r2 = 52.3, r3 = 579.4, r4 = ∞, r5 = 24.1 and r6 = 64.7; the distances b1 = 22.2 (front cell to stop) and
b2 = 29.0 (stop to rear cell); and two indices, nD = 1.536 for L1 and L3 and nD = 1.602 for L2 and L4. Tafel I adds
the computed curves, and pp. 315–316 state the correction.

**Assumed or derived by von Rohr.** The thicknesses d1 = 5.3, d2 = 3.9, d3 = 7.7 and d4 = 3.9 are "Willkürlich
angenommen" (arbitrarily assumed); b1 and b2 are "nach der von J. M. Eder angegebenen Figur bestimmt", determined from
Eder's figure. Each lens was computed from its source data and only then reduced to f = 100 mm (notes to the plates).

**Relation to the patent numbers (computed here).** The patent text, as Eder reprints it, gives the new construction
for a total focal length F = 10: radii 6.700, 5.417, 5.417, 60.00, ∞, 2.5, 2.5, 6.7, the separation a = 5.3 of the two
cells, and the indices. Multiplied by 10 and divided by 1.0356, these radii are von Rohr's table to within its last
digit, and 53.0 / 1.0356 = 51.2 = b1 + b2. The common factor is his reduction of the computed lens to fD = 100; the
split of the separation at the stop comes from Eder's figure.

**Added by this model.**

- Radius signs. Eder's reprint marks each face convex (+) or concave (−) per lens: L1 +/+ (biconvex), L2 −/−
  (biconcave), L3 ∞/+ (plano-convex), L4 −/+ (concave toward the object, convex behind). Fig. 107 and Tafel I draw the
  same shapes, with r3 slightly concave toward the image. With r3 convex instead the focal length would be 89.3 mm.
- The glass order of the rear pair as von Rohr prints and hatches it, crown first; see Glass Selection for the
  disagreement with the patent wording.
- The last gap, 60.288 mm, the computed paraxial back focus; von Rohr prints none.
- Clear radii (17 mm throughout), the iris radius derived from 1:3.5, the f-stop list 3.5–22, the field limit at the
  printed 12°, and element focal lengths computed thick in air.

**Printed against modelled.** The table traces to an EFL of 100.062 mm, 0.06 % long; 0.05 mm on r1, r5 or r6 alone
moves it by about 0.04 mm, so the residual is rounding. At f/3.5 the entrance pupil has a radius of 14.295 mm and the
exact marginal ray falls 0.410 mm short of the paraxial focus. Curve 107a, read at its three computed heights against
the model scaled to f = 100:

The model's zonal focal length is h/sin u′. Von Rohr judges the spherical aberration not far behind Petzval's and

## Optical Architecture

Four elements in two cemented groups, both positive, with the stop between them: a biconvex crown cemented to a
biconcave flint (f = +164.8 mm), 22.2 mm of air, the stop, 29.0 mm of air, then a plano-convex crown cemented to a flint
meniscus (f = +150.9 mm). The back focus is 60.3 mm and the total track 132.3 mm.

Two changes to Petzval's arrangement define the type, as Eder's p. 125 and the patent describe them. The cells were
given equal focal lengths, each 3:2 of the total, where Petzval's differ (139.5 and 209.1 mm in the Fig. 100 model).
And the rear pair, held apart in Petzval's lens by a ring with unequal inner radii, became a doublet cemented on equal
radii, removing two reflecting glass-air surfaces. The outer faces r1 and r6 are equally curved (64.7 mm).

The Petzval sum is 0.00893 mm⁻¹ (radius about 112 mm, 1.1 f), slightly above the 0.00835 mm⁻¹ of Fig. 100. Where the
1840 design keeps the sagittal field nearly flat and lets the meridional field recede, this one does the reverse. The
patent claims greater light, depth, flatness and correctness of image; von Rohr grants a probable advance in the
oblique pencils over the old form.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 + L2 — Front cemented achromat

L1: nD = 1.536 (sodium D), no νd published. Glass: old low-index crown (figure hatching), unmatched. f = +54.8 mm.
L2: nD = 1.602 (sodium D), no νd published. Glass: flint (figure hatching), unmatched. f = −79.5 mm.

The convex front face r1 (surface power +0.0083 mm⁻¹) meets the axial beam at its full height. The cemented junction
r2 = −52.3 separates glasses 0.066 apart in index, so it refracts weakly at the D line and carries the chromatic
correction of the cell. The flint's rear face r3 = 579.4 is nearly plane, concave toward the image. Von Rohr's assumed
d1 = 5.3 mm brings L1 to a knife edge at a height of 17.3 mm, where both drawings end the cell.

### L3 + L4 — Rear cemented doublet

L3: nD = 1.536 (sodium D), no νd published. Glass: old low-index crown (figure hatching), unmatched. f = +45.0 mm.
L4: nD = 1.602 (sodium D), no νd published. Glass: flint (figure hatching), unmatched. f = −66.2 mm.

L3 has a flat front face r4 and a rear face r5 = −24.1, the shortest radius in the lens; L4 follows it on the same
radius and closes with r6 = −64.7, so the rear cell is an 11.6 mm thick block, flat toward the stop and convex toward
the image. Its positive power sits almost entirely in the last face r6 (+0.0093 mm⁻¹). The junction r5 refracts weakly
at the D line (−0.0027 mm⁻¹, the flint being the denser glass behind it) and does the chromatic work of the cell; the
flat r4 has no power at all.

In Petzval's lens the air meniscus between the separated rear lenses is the lever on the oblique pencils; here the
bending of the thick cemented cell takes that role. This reading is an interpretation; the sources describe only the
result.

## Glass Selection

| Glass class (figure hatching) | nD | Elements | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Old (low-index) crown | 1.536 | L1, L3 | collective lens of each cell |
| Flint | 1.602 | L2, L4 | dispersive lens of each cell |

The table prints only nD under "Glasarten nD". Eder, after the patent, names one crown of density 2.51 and "mean"
index 1.536 and one flint of density 3.21 and mean index 1.602; von Rohr tabulates both as nD. The classes come from the
hatching of Fig. 107 and Tafel I, which his notes to the plates define (falling to the right for old, low-index crown;
rising to the right for flint): L1 and L3 are hatched as crown, L2 and L4 as flint, matching his table. Where he does
print dispersion elsewhere in the book, his ν̄ is (nD − 1)/(nG′ − nD), defined by the column heads of his glass table
on p. 339: a figure for the photographic blue, not an Abbe number. No such figure is given here, so the model keeps nD
only, with no νd and no modern catalog glass as a proxy, and is monochromatic.

**The rear glass order.** Eder's p. 125, reprinting the patent's wording, makes A and D crown and B and C flint, and
in the patent's old construction C is the front lens of the rear pair. Lettered the same way, the new rear pair would
be a flint plano-convex lens and a crown meniscus, the reverse of von Rohr. That order contradicts the patent's own
numbers: with von Rohr's thicknesses the focal length becomes 78.9 mm and the rear cell +91.2 mm against +164.8 mm in
front, where the patent requires equal cells (as thin lenses at F = 10 it gives F = 7.6). Von Rohr's order gives
100.06 mm, cells of 164.8 and 150.9 mm, and his Tafel I curves. The patent drawing, which would settle the lettering,
was not available. Eder's report that the 1885 successor changed the rear glass order after Dallmeyer fits his own
flint-first reading; in von Rohr's table the 1878 rear pair is already crown-first, like Fig. 106. The model keeps
von Rohr's order.

## Aperture, Field and Rims

The model's iris follows from 1:3.5 by the engine's paraxial stop rule, a stop radius of 11.715 mm. The exact f/3.5
marginal ray, entering at 14.295 mm, meets r1–r3 at 14.29, 14.16 and 13.78 mm, the stop at 11.85 mm and the rear cell at
9.32, 8.98 and 8.79 mm. At the printed ω = 12° (an image circle of about 42.6 mm at f = 100) the chief ray passes
every rim, at 5.8–7.3 mm in front and 7.6–9.5 mm behind; the rims cut 17–29 % of the outer half of the oblique pencil
in front and 11 % at r6, ordinary vignetting that is kept. The paraxial rim limit is 20.8°, set by r6.

| Surface | Model clear radius (mm) | f/3.5 axial ray (mm) | Fig. 107 (mm) | Tafel I (mm) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| r1, r2, r3 | 17 | 14.29, 14.16, 13.78 | 17.3 | 17.3 |
| r4, r5, r6 | 17 | 9.32, 8.98, 8.79 | 17.4 | 17.45 |

The 17 mm value is an earlier modelling envelope, kept. Both drawings, scaled from the 72.0 mm vertex length, draw
the cells 2–3 % larger, where L1 has no edge left; at 17 mm it keeps 0.19 mm. Their incident-beam lines stand
14.5–14.6 mm from the axis, like the largest height on curve 107a (14.5 mm). The stop marks of Fig. 107 sit 22.5 mm
behind r3 (printed 22.2); their inner ends, 13.4 mm from the axis, are wider than the iris and not read as its size.

## Design Heritage and Context

Von Rohr places the lens among the modifications of the Petzval portrait objective (pp. 275–277). J. H. Dallmeyer
altered the rear member in 1866, and H. Zincke gen. Sommer computed in 1870 a portrait objective of 1:2.37 on the same
rearrangement of glasses (Fig. 106), of which von Rohr knew no execution. The 1878 type followed with its cemented rear
lens. The patent's second claim also covers the new rear lens fitted behind the front lens of the old construction.

The construction did not last (p. 316). Citing Eder (p. 126), von Rohr reports that in 1885 Voigtländer replaced it
by a type with the rear glass order changed after Dallmeyer; reduced to about f = 100 its radii, 52.0, 44.9, 251, 393,
33.5, 29.0 and 60.5 mm, are close to Dallmeyer's 50.0, 43.5, 250, 533, 33.3, 29.8 and 59.7 mm, with lens diameters of
31.1 and 33.3 mm; the distances are unknown (pp. 276–277).

**Patent context.** The radii of the table come from D.R.P. 5761, whose text prints the new construction at F = 10
with the cell separation and the two indices, but no thicknesses and no stop distance, beside the radii of the firm's
older Petzval construction for comparison. The file transcribes von Rohr's reduced table, not the patent.
