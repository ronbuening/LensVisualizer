## 2026-11-18 — Literature reconstruction audit

discussion on pp. 297–298, the 1866 Aplanat, its patent and the first form (Fig. 110) on pp. 294–297. Further

### Signs and their basis

Signs follow Fig. 111 and Tafel II: in each cell a thin-centred outer meniscus (negative) is cemented to a
thick-centred inner meniscus (positive), both convex toward the object in front and concave toward the central
stop, so r1, r2, r3 > 0; the rear cell mirrors the front. Page 296 describes the Aplanat as pairs of flint menisci
with the more strongly refracting glass outside. Both drawings are to scale. Fig. 111 at 600 dpi, scaled from the
24.1 mm vertex length (23.61 px/mm), gives vertex spacings 1.02, 1.86, 18.28, 1.80 and 1.14 mm against the printed
1.05, 2.0, 18.0, 2.0 and 1.05 mm; Tafel II at 400 dpi (15.77 px/mm measured, 15.75 nominal for natural size) gives
0.95, 1.93, 18.20, 1.84 and 1.17 mm. The astigmatism match below also confirms the signs and the central stop.

The hatching was checked by a diagonal autocorrelation of the hatch lines in each lens of both cells, in both
drawings: the outer lenses are hatched rising to the right (flint in the plate legend), the inner lenses descending
(old low-index crown). The text calls both glasses flints, and the labels follow the text.

### Rim decisions

Exact meridional trace at the printed ω = 30° and f/7.7, with the staged 6.8 mm rims: no on-axis clip (f/7.7
axial heights 6.46, 6.25, 6.20 mm in front and 5.60, 5.63, 5.72 mm behind the stop), but the chief ray crosses r1
and r6 at 6.824 mm, so it is blocked by 0.024 mm; it passes up to 29.9°. Both drawings show the cells larger than
that, and the printed radii allow up to the 7.20 mm knife edge of L2 and L3, so the blocking rims were raised to just
above the chief-ray height. Each cell is drawn square, with one flat top across its three surfaces, so all six
surfaces take the same 6.85 mm. After the change the trace at 30° shows no axial clip and no blocked chief ray. L2
and L3 keep a 0.224 mm edge (0.255 mm at 6.8); L1 and L4 are 2.535 mm thick at the rim.

The drawing columns are the cell rim lines (and the inner ends of the stop marks), measured on both sides of the
axis and averaged. Both drawings also show the incident axial beam as lines 6.56 and 6.58 mm from the axis, the
1:7.7 pupil at f = 100 (6.49 mm), and run the inner meniscus out to an edge at the rear corner of each cell.

Meridional transmission (share of the stop diameter, exact trace) with the 6.85 mm rims: 100 % on axis, 80 % at 10°,
62 % at 15°, 43 % at 20°, 23 % at 25°, 10 % at 28°, 5 % at 29°, 3 % at 29.5° and only the chief ray at 30° (with
6.8 mm: 79, 61, 42, 22, 9, 4 and 2 %). The full-stop edge rays at 30° would cross the cell surfaces 9.5–12.0 mm
from the axis, so the clear-aperture probe finds no full-bundle edge rays there; even 7.15 mm rims would pass only
6 % at 30°. The paraxial rim limit rises from 28.9° to 29.1° (r1, r6). The engine's `tracingHalfField` rises from
28.91° to 29.09°: its slope-launched chief ray through the paraxial pupil meets r1 and r6 there, while the exact
30° chief ray passes. Real vignetting is kept and the field limit stays at the printed 30°. `source.sdSource` is
"heuristic": the 6.85 mm value is set by the chief ray, not measured from the drawings.

The package's notes counted edge-pupil clipping on axis and no transmission at 30° as open faults. Neither is a
defect: the f/7.7 axial beam passes inside the rims, and at 30° only the chief ray's neighbourhood can pass with any
rim the drawings and radii allow.

### Patent check (D6)

Von Rohr names no patent for the second form. The patent he names for the Aplanat, A. Steinheil 3 ("Aplanatpatent",
taken 12 November 1866 and granted in Bavaria on 14 January 1867, communicated by J. M. Eder (1.) pp. 71–72), is
the source of the first form, Fig. 110 (Quelle: J. M. Eder 1. 71): 1:8, ω = 22°, r 25.3 / 10.4 / 35.9, d 0.8 / 1.3,
b 10.1 and nD 1.61603 / 1.58329. Result: **table differs** in every radius, thickness, gap and index; von Rohr
himself contrasts the second form's dispersion ratio (1.244) with that of the type of 1867 (1.213). The Bavarian
patent carries no number in the bibliography and was not searched on the patent office sites. The file was not
converted to a patent lens.

### Open limitations

- The model keeps the rounded book table; its EFL is 0.56 % short and its zero of spherical aberration lies at
  1:8.94 instead of 1:9.1, with a zone of −0.148 mm instead of about −0.12 mm.
- Rims are an estimate set by the 30° chief ray and smaller than both drawings; at 30° only the chief ray passes,
  and the engine's own chief-ray estimate stops at 29.09°.
- nD only; von Rohr prints only the ratio of the two glasses' dispersions, so no chromatic behaviour is modelled.
- The drawings hatch the inner lens as old crown; the labels follow the text, which calls both glasses flints.
- Design date unknown (p. 297): after the 1866/67 patent form in von Rohr's sequence, before Eder's book of 1891.
- `maker` stays null: von Rohr credits A. Steinheil, whose firm C. A. Steinheil Söhne (Munich) is not a catalog
  maker.
