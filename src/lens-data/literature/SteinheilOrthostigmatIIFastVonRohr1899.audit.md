# Audit log — Steinheil Orthostigmat Type II f/4.02 (von Rohr 1899, Fig. 142)

## 2026-11-18 — Literature reconstruction audit

### Book pages and figure used

Moritz von Rohr, *Theorie und Geschichte des photographischen Objektivs* (Berlin: Julius Springer, 1899). The table and
Fig. 142 are on printed p. 383 (e-rara chapter PDF 4637944, page 139); pp. 382 and 384 (pages 138 and 140) give the
history of the Orthostigmat, the glass names with ν̄, the text's aberration summary and the commercial series. Values
were read on the page image (scan 164 ppi, rendered at 300–800 dpi), not from the OCR layer. The caption reads
"Fig. 142, s. Taf. I. R. Steinheil: Orthostigmat. Typus II. Quelle: R. Steinheil. 3. Reducirt auf fD = 100 mm.
Durchgerechnet für 1 : 4.02 und ω = 29°."

Further sections of the same e-rara record: the Litteraturverzeichniss p. 430 (PDF 4637806, page 33), entry
R. Steinheil (3.); the Bemerkungen zu den Figurentafeln (PDF 4637812, page 2), which give the hatching legend, the
curve scales, the computed-point convention and the statement that the plate drawings are at natural size for
f = 100 mm; and Tafel I (PDF 4637814, page 2), which redraws the lens as No. 142 with curves 142a and 142b. The
hatching of Fig. 142 (rising to the right for L1, L3, L4, L6; falling for L2, L5) matches the legend's flint and old
crown and the glass names in the text.

### Signs and their basis

Fig. 142 and Tafel I: r1 bulges toward the object; the junction r2 bulges slightly toward the stop (negative); the
junction r3 bulges strongly toward the stop (negative); r4 is concave toward the stop (positive); the rear triplet
mirrors the front. The D.R.P. 88 505 claim fixes the same shapes: a positive lens enclosed by a biconvex and a
biconcave lens, both of higher index. On Fig. 142 (400 dpi, 15.55 px/mm from the 30.8 mm vertex length) the vertex
spacings read 3.95 / 5.66 / 0.77 / 10.26 / 0.84 / 5.24 / 4.08 mm against 4.2 / 5.2 / 0.8 / 10.4 / 0.8 / 5.2 / 4.2;
Tafel I (326 dpi, 12.69 px/mm) gives 3.90 / 5.67 / 0.75 / 10.36 / 0.83 / 5.20 / 4.10, the same pattern, so both come
from one drawing whose front L1/L2 boundary sits about 0.3 mm too far forward. The drawn sag of r1 (1.90 mm at the
rim) agrees with the computed 1.93 mm. The stop is drawn 0.2–0.4 mm in front of the midpoint, i.e. central.

### Rim decisions

The rims are not printed. Fig. 142 draws each triplet as a single cylinder: rim lines (median of the line centres)
at 13.44 mm (front) and 13.34–13.54 mm (rear), stop bars ending at 11.1 and 11.6 mm, incident axial beam at
12.7–12.8 mm. Tafel I gives 13.44–13.55 mm rims scaled from the vertex length (12.69 px/mm) and 13.29–13.41 mm at its
stated natural size (12.83 px/mm at 326 dpi), a stop of 11.3–11.4 mm and a beam of 12.6–12.8 mm.

The exact meridional trace at ω = 29° and 1:4.02 with the stored 14 mm rims shows no axial clip (largest axial height
12.46 mm at r1) and no blocked chief ray (largest chief height 7.37 mm at r1 and r8); one-sided rim vignetting is
34 / 31 / 22 % at r1–r3, none at r4 and r5, and 15 / 25 / 29 % at r6–r8. The aperture census finds the lens within
3 % of the stated f-number with no rim limiter. The paraxial rim limit is 45.8° (r1, r8). No rim was changed.

### Patent check (D6)

Von Rohr's bibliography (p. 430) lists R. Steinheil (3.), "Objectiv für photographische Zwecke": D.R.P. 88 505 of
28 November 1893 on the Orthostigmat Type II, used through the English-patent excerpt "Steinheil's improvements in
lenses", B. J. 1896, 43, No. 1891, pp. 489–490. The D.R.P. (DEPATISnet DE000000088505A; issued 4 September 1896, the
scan being the second printing of 12 September 1899) prints two examples with nD and nF. Example 1, f = 493 mm,
1:6.2, is Fig. 143. Example 2, f = 476 mm, 1:4, X0 = 59.5: R0 = R14 = +229, R2 = R12 = ±562.95, R4 = R10 = ±142.03,
R6 = R8 = −490.196, D1 = D13 = 20, D3 = D11 = 25, D5 = D9 = 4, D7 = 50; nD / nF 1.62356 / 1.63498,
1.50786 / 1.51351, 1.60304 / 1.61428. Result: **table identical** to Fig. 142 after reduction to fD = 100 and
rounding to 0.1 mm (reduced radii 47.87, 117.68, 29.69, 102.47; spacings 4.18, 5.23, 0.84, 5.23). The patent's two
drawings are schematic (the inner face is drawn far deeper than R = 490 mm) and give no usable rim. The English
patent and the B. J. excerpt were not consulted; the bibliography gives no E.P. number.

### Open limitations

- nD only. The patent adds nF but no nC, which is not enough for a νd or a measured C/F pair, and the book prints
  only ν̄ (G′-based). Whether to transcribe the patent itself, which prints the same prescription, is for the
  coordinator under the literature rule.
- The 14 mm rims are about 0.5 mm larger than both drawings; a figure-fidelity pass could adopt about 13.5 mm.
- The middle-zone spherical aberration differs from curve 142a by 0.07–0.09 mm for reasons not found; the oblique
  curves 142b were not compared numerically.
- No manufacturer is established for this fast form; von Rohr knows of commercial series only for the universal form.
