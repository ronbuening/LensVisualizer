# Carl Zeiss Jena Abbe–Rudolph Triplet f/7.2 (von Rohr 1899, Fig. 125)

## Source and Design Identification

**Source:** Moritz von Rohr, *Theorie und Geschichte des photographischen Objektivs* (Berlin: Julius Springer, 1899), p. 357, Fig. 125
**Designer:** Ernst Abbe (concept) and Paul Rudolph (computation)
**Manufacturer:** Carl Zeiss Jena (the Zeiss optical workshop in Jena)
**Source dated:** 1899
**Design dated:** 1888–89

Von Rohr captions Fig. 125 "E. Abbe und P. Rudolph: Symmetrisches Triplet (achromatisch)" and gives the source as "Angaben des Verfassers", the author's own data. He names no earlier publication and no patent for the numbers. The table is reduced to fD = 100 mm and, in his words, computed through for 1:7.2 and ω = 26½°. The figure also refers to Plate II of the book, which was not consulted for this model.

The surrounding text (pp. 356–358) places the design at the Zeiss workshop. Rudolph joined the firm in January 1886 to help Abbe with the computations for the apochromatic microscope objectives. When the workshop turned to new photographic objectives, Abbe proposed a triplet in which two single lenses provide the converging power and a cemented middle lens carries the correction of the chromatic and spherical errors; Rudolph carried out the computation. Von Rohr dates the preparation of this type to 1888/89, the same years in which Rudolph began the experiments that led to his anastigmat doublet.

On production, von Rohr says the result was a disappointment and that practice made only little use of the new instrument; he also says users noticed its good sharpness near the axis. Both statements imply that the lens reached photographers, which is the basis for the manufacturer line above. Von Rohr gives no production figures, dates, catalog name or focal lengths.

## Reconstruction Status

**Printed by von Rohr (p. 357).** All eight radii as magnitudes (r1 = r8 = 26.2, r2 = r7 = 39.2, r3 = r6 = 235, r4 = r5 = 14.0), the thicknesses d1 = d5 = 3.4, d2 = d4 = 1.1 and d3 = 5.7, the air spaces b1 = b2 + b3 = 4.3 with b2 = 0.8 and b3 = 3.5, the three indices nD 1.51660, 1.56760 and 1.50846, the reduced focal length fD = 100 mm, the aperture 1:7.2 and the field angle ω = 26½°. The text adds two performance statements: the longitudinal spherical aberrations do not exceed −0.4 mm, and the astigmatic difference at an obliquity of about 26° is still nearly 6 mm, with a concave field.

**Assumed, measured or estimated by von Rohr.** Nothing in the table is marked as assumed or measured from a drawing. The only stated operation is the reduction to fD = 100 mm.

**Added by this model.**

- Radius signs, read from Fig. 125: both outer lenses are menisci bent around the stop (L1 convex to the object, L5 convex to the image); the middle lens is drawn as a square block with a biconvex core, so r3 and r4 are positive and r5 and r6 negative.
- Stop position: von Rohr's own b2 = 0.8 behind the middle lens and b3 = 3.5 before L5. Fig. 125 draws the stop at the same place (about 1.2 and 3.3 mm on the drawing's scale), so the asymmetric stop in a symmetric glass system is the printed design, not a transcription slip.
- Back focus: the paraxial value 90.238 mm, computed; von Rohr prints none.
- Clear radii: 10 mm on L1 and L5, 8 mm on the cemented middle lens. These are heuristic values carried over from the package that first transcribed the table. Measured on Fig. 125, scaled by the 23.3 mm vertex length, the drawing shows about 11.9, 8.5 and 11.9 mm. The narrower model rims clip neither the axial f/7.2 beam nor the chief ray at 26.5°, so they were kept.
- Stop radius 6.24 mm, derived by the engine from 1:7.2; f-stops f/7.2, 8, 11 and 16; infinity focus only.

The focal-length residual is a rounding effect of the one-decimal radii: a change of 0.05 mm in r1 = r8 alone moves the focal length by 0.68 mm, so a printed 26.2 standing for about 26.16 accounts for the whole difference. The spherical-aberration and astigmatism figures were computed for this model (an exact meridional trace and a Coddington trace along the real chief ray) and agree with both of von Rohr's statements, which also confirms the stop position and the radius signs.

## Optical Architecture

The lens is a five-element, three-group symmetrical triplet: a positive meniscus, a cemented three-element middle lens of weak negative power, and a second positive meniscus. With the computed focal lengths the group powers are +140.4 mm, −277 mm and +140.4 mm. The radii, thicknesses and glasses are exactly symmetric about the middle of L3; only the stop breaks the symmetry, sitting 0.8 mm behind the cemented group instead of midway in the 4.3 mm rear air space.

The arrangement follows Abbe's plan as von Rohr reports it. The two outer menisci do the collecting; the middle lens, almost plane on its outer faces (r = ±235) and with two deeply curved inner junctions (r = ±14), corrects color and spherical aberration. The vertex length is 23.3 mm, under a quarter of the focal length, and the back focus is 90.2 mm.

The Petzval sum computed from the printed data is 0.0081 mm⁻¹, a Petzval radius of about 123 mm or 1.2 f. The two outer menisci contribute 0.0086 mm⁻¹; the middle lens takes back only 0.0005 mm⁻¹, because the index steps at its junctions are small. A field this curved fits von Rohr's verdict that the correction of the oblique pencils was no better than in the aplanats and antiplanets of the time.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Positive Meniscus, convex to the object

nD = 1.51660 (sodium D); no dispersion published. Glass: Unmatched. f = +140.4 mm (computed).

The front collective lens. Its strongly curved front face (r = 26.2) and weaker rear face (r = 39.2) bend it around the stop, the usual shape of an outer member in a symmetrical objective, which keeps the chief-ray incidence moderate across the 26.5° field. At the f/7.2 aperture it carries the full axial beam (marginal height 6.98 mm) and is the surface that vignettes the oblique beam first.

### D1 — Cemented middle lens (L2 + L3 + L4)

- **L2, Negative Meniscus.** nD = 1.56760; no dispersion published. Glass: Unmatched. f = −26.3 mm (computed).
- **L3, Biconvex Positive.** nD = 1.50846; no dispersion published. Glass: Unmatched. f = +14.8 mm (computed).
- **L4, Negative Meniscus.** nD = 1.56760; no dispersion published. Glass: Unmatched. f = −26.3 mm (computed).

The middle lens is a cemented triplet whose net focal length is −277 mm. Its outer faces are nearly flat; all its structure lies in the two junctions of radius 14 mm between the higher-index shells L2 and L4 and the lower-index core L3. Because the core has the lower index, each junction has negative power (about −0.0042 mm⁻¹ each in nD), so the junctions add overcorrecting spherical aberration that balances the undercorrection of the two collective menisci. A third-order (Seidel) breakdown computed for this model confirms the split: the two junctions are the main overcorrecting surfaces, L1's front face and L5's rear face the main undercorrecting ones. Von Rohr's statement that the longitudinal aberration stays within −0.4 mm is reproduced by the exact trace.

The chromatic role cannot be checked from the page, since no dispersions are printed. A net-negative middle lens can only cancel the color of the two positive outer lenses if its negative shells are made of the more dispersive glass. That is an inference from the layout, not something von Rohr states.

Fig. 125 draws the middle lens as a square block in which the two r = 14 faces of L3 meet at the block's top and bottom edge. With d3 = 5.7 those faces intersect at a height of 8.47 mm, and the drawing's block measures about 8.5 mm, so the drawing is a true section of the printed radii. The model's 8 mm rim leaves L3 an edge of 0.68 mm.

### L5 — Positive Meniscus, concave to the object

nD = 1.51660 (sodium D); no dispersion published. Glass: Unmatched. f = +140.4 mm (computed).

The rear collective lens, the mirror image of L1 about the middle lens. It sits 3.5 mm behind the stop and carries the exit side of the oblique beams; the model's chief ray at 26.5° crosses its rear face at 3.2 mm.

## Glass Identification

| nD (von Rohr) | Elements | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1.51660 | L1, L5 | Collective outer menisci |
| 1.56760 | L2, L4 | Negative shells of the cemented middle lens |
| 1.50846 | L3 | Positive core of the cemented middle lens, the lowest index in the lens |

Von Rohr prints only nD for this lens: no glass names, catalog numbers or dispersion figures. Elsewhere in the same chapter he prints a barred ν̄ beside some indices; his glass table on p. 339 defines it as (nD − 1)/(nG′ − nD), a blue-region figure, not the Abbe number. No such figure appears on p. 357, so the model keeps all five elements at nD only and substitutes no modern catalog glass. The chromatic analyses therefore show a monochromatic lens.

Von Rohr adds two remarks about the glasses. The choice of glass types was such that Abbe could also have reached apochromatic correction, and the design was planned in symmetric and asymmetric, achromatic and apochromatic forms. The printed version is the achromatic triplet, chosen because both its spherical and its astigmatic computations were available. With slightly different glasses, he says, the spherical aberration for yellow light stays nearly the same and approaches full aplanatism for the other colors as well. Neither the apochromatic glasses nor their data are given.

## Aperture, Field and Focus

The nominal aperture is von Rohr's 1:7.2. The engine sets the stop radius to 6.24 mm from the paraxial entrance pupil; an exact marginal ray at the same entrance height crosses the stop at 6.29 mm, a 0.8 % difference within the aperture audit's tolerance. The f-stop list adds f/8, f/11 and f/16, which the book does not print. The entrance pupil lies 1.9 mm in front of the stop, inside the middle lens.

The trace field is limited to von Rohr's ω = 26½°. At that angle the chief ray passes every rim and reaches an image height of 50.1 mm. The off-axis beam is vignetted by about 41 % on one side at L1's front face and by 22–23 % at L1's rear face and the middle lens's front face; that loss is kept as the consequence of the heuristic rims. With the wider rims of the drawing the vignetting would be smaller. A paraxial estimate puts the chief-ray limit of the 10 mm front rim at about 34.6°.

The book gives no close-focus data. The model is infinity-only.

## Design Heritage and Context

Von Rohr presents this triplet as the Zeiss workshop's first plan for photographic objectives of a new kind; before it, aplanats had only been computed there for other firms. The approach followed Abbe's microscope work. He explains apochromatic correction as Abbe's microscope concept: removing the secondary spectrum and also making the lens aplanatic for several colors. He also credits Abbe with new formulas for the astigmatism of chief-ray pencils, developed alongside Seidel's.

He notes that the construction matched, in its essentials and apart from the apochromatism, a triplet scheme published by H. Schroeder (p. 230 of the book) that had remained unknown in Jena. This is context reported by von Rohr; the Schroeder scheme was not used for this model.

The triplet's modest success led directly to the next stage. Von Rohr continues on p. 358 with Rudolph's experiments of the same years, 1888/89, on a construction in which astigmatism, the main fault of the objectives of the day, would be less troublesome. Those experiments led to Rudolph's anastigmat doublet. The astigmatic difference of nearly 6 mm that von Rohr reports for this triplet is the shortcoming that work addressed.

No patent is associated with the design in von Rohr's text, and none was used for this model.
