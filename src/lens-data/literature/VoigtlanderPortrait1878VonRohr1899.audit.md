### Signs and their basis

- Eder's per-lens convention (+ convex, − concave) gives L1 biconvex, L2 biconcave, L3 plano-convex, L4 concave-convex
  meniscus; in vertex convention R1 +, R2 −, R3 +, R5 −, R6 −. All staged signs agree.
- Tafel I No. 107 at 600 dpi (24.00 px/mm from the 72.0 mm vertex length, 1728 px): r1 and r6 meet the cell rim 55.5 px
  (2.31 mm) inside their vertices (sag of 64.7 mm at 17.1 mm); r3 meets it 16 px (0.67 mm) behind its vertex, concave
  toward the image (drawn more curved than 579.4 mm). r3 negative would give EFL 89.34 mm; R6 positive gives −820.9 mm.
- Hatching (directional pixel-difference test inside each lens, both drawings): L1 and L3 falling to the right (old,
  low-index crown), L2 and L4 rising to the right (flint); mean grey-level differences of 5–17 along the hatch
  direction against 21–45 across it at 2, 3 and 5 px offsets. The drawings confirm von Rohr's glass order.

### Identity

Von Rohr (pp. 276, 319) has the Voigtländer firm introduce the portrait objective with cemented rear lens in 1878 under
patents granted in November 1878, and drop it in 1885. Page 318: Fr. Voigtländer transferred the firm to his son
Friedrich von Voigtländer on 1 October 1876 and died on 7 April 1878; the firm traded as Voigtländer & Sohn,
Braunschweig. Maker Voigtländer and design year 1878 confirmed. Designer not established: the caption credits
Fr. von Voigtländer; pp. 276 and 315 suggest H. Zincke gen. Sommer, unconfirmed.

### Patent check (D6)

The caption's radius source, bibliography Voigtländer 2 (p. 433), is D.R.P. 5761 of 14 November 1878, "Neuerungen in
der Construction von Doppelobjectiven für photographische Apparate", with E.P. 4756 of 22 November 1878 through
G. W. von Nawrocki. DEPATISnet answered with a JavaScript challenge page and was not pursued; Espacenet returned 403;
Google Patents (DE 5761 C) gave the text layer without the drawing. The patent prints a numerical description of the
new construction (Fig. 2) for F = 10: the eight radii, a = 5.3, the two indices and densities and the 3:2 focal-length
statement, plus radii of the old construction (Fig. 1) for comparison. It prints no thicknesses and no stop distance.
Result: table differs. Its radii and separation equal von Rohr's after his reduction by 1.0356; his thicknesses are
assumed and his stop split comes from Eder's figure. The patent wording (OCR "A und B" for crown, which Eder's reprint
gives as "A und D") assigns flint to C; whether C is the plano-convex lens depends on the unseen Fig. 2.

### Open limitations

- nD only; von Rohr prints no dispersion, so chromatic results of the model carry no source meaning.
- Thicknesses are von Rohr's assumptions and the stop position comes from Eder's drawing, not from the patent.
- Rear glass order rests on von Rohr's table, hatching and the focal-length checks; the patent drawing that would show
  the C/D lettering was not seen.
- Rims stay 2–3 % inside the drawn cells; the model's sagittal shell lies 0.06–0.13 mm closer to the paraxial plane
  than curve 107b; not traced to a cause.
- The edition of the Eder facsimile was not checked against von Rohr's 1891 citation; the page number and text match.
