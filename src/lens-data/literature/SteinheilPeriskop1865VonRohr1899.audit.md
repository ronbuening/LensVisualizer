# Steinheil Periskop f/40 (von Rohr 1899, Fig. 109) — audit log

## 2026-11-18 — Literature reconstruction audit

### Pages and figure used

Von Rohr (1899), printed pp. 286–290 and 294 (e-rara chapter PDF pages 42–46 and 50): table, caption and Fig. 109 on
p. 288, the firm's history on pp. 286–289, the later Aplanat on p. 294. Further sections of the same e-rara record:
the Litteraturverzeichniss pp. 410–411 (J. M. Eder 1) and 428 (C. A. Steinheil 3–5, A. Steinheil 1) in PDF 4637806,
pages 13, 14 and 31 (the held p. 430 holds only the K. Steinheil entries); the Bemerkungen zu den Figurentafeln (PDF
4637812, page 2), which state that the plate drawings are at natural size for f = 100 and give the curve scales; and
Tafel III (PDF 4637814, page 4), which redraws the lens as No. 109 with curve 109b. Eder's p. 146, the source von
Rohr names, was read on the page image kept with the package evidence. Every value was read on page renders at
150–1000 dpi; the native scan resolutions are 154 ppi (p. 288) and 162 ppi (Tafel III). The page prints no ν̄.

The staged numbers all match the print; no transcription error was found. The OCR layer misreads the caption source
("J. M. Eher . / . 146"), the specimen year ("Mitte 1805") and the patent reference number; the image reads
"J. M. Eder. 1. 146.", "Mitte 1865" and "(3.)". Eder 1 is, by the bibliography (pp. 410–411), J. M. Eder, Die
photographischen Objektive, ihre Eigenschaften und Prüfung (Halle: W. Knapp, 1891), cited there for A. Steinheil
p. 288. Eder's p. 146 prints: lens diameter 12.56; outer radii +17.53, inner radii −20.76; separation of the lenses
12.56 "or" 8.29; thickness 1.26; nv = 1.5233, no = 1.5360; optical focus 100.00, chemical focus 97.54; diaphragm
f/40 (printed "251,3", evidently 2.513). Von Rohr marks no value as assumed; by his notes on the plates his table is
the reduction of Eder's data rounded to one decimal.

### Signs and their basis

Fig. 109 and Tafel III draw two menisci with their convex faces outward and their concave faces toward a central
stop; the text calls the lens symmetric with a central stop, and the English patent describes the concave sides
facing each other. Hence r1, r2 > 0 and r3, r4 < 0. Both drawings are to scale. Tafel III at 486 dpi (nominal
natural size 19.13 px/mm) gives a vertex length of 290.5 px, 19.11 px/mm for 15.2 mm, and spacings 1.28, 6.23,
6.36 and 1.33 mm against 1.3, 6.3, 6.3 and 1.3. Fig. 109 at 450 dpi gives 270.5 px, 17.80 px/mm (17.72 nominal),
with thicknesses of 1.32 mm and the stop 6.21 and 6.35 mm from the lenses. Both lenses carry the descending hatching
that the plate legend assigns to old low-index crown. The astigmatism comparison below also confirms the signs, the
central stop and the 12.6 mm separation.

### Rim decisions

Exact meridional trace at f/40 with the staged 6.28 mm rims: no on-axis clip (axial heights 1.23, 1.20, 1.12 and
1.14 mm). The chief ray meets r1 and r4 at 6.218 mm at 42.0°, 6.271 mm at 42.3° and 6.306 mm at 42.5°, so it is
blocked from about 42.33°; at the printed 46° it needs 6.934 mm on r1 and r4 and 6.279 mm on r2 and r3. The staging
note's "about 42.5°" is slightly high, and the package's 46° field limit let the chief ray strike the rims from
42.3° to 46°.

Both drawings were profiled on the clean side of each lens. The lens rims lie 6.07–6.18 mm (line centre to outer
edge) from the axis for the front lens and 6.18–6.29 mm for the rear lens in Fig. 109, and 6.28–6.38 mm and
6.25–6.36 mm on Tafel III. Both draw the stop opening at a radius of 1.18 mm and the incident axial beam 1.25 mm
(Fig. 109) and 1.33 mm (Tafel III) from the axis, the f/40 pupil at f = 100 being 1.25 mm. The 6.93 mm needed for
46° lies 0.55 mm outside the largest drawn rim, so the rims were not raised. The staged 6.28 mm is half of Eder's
printed lens diameter of 12.56 mm, a value of his more precise data rather than of von Rohr's rounded table; it lies
inside the band both drawings show, and Eder's text example (11.2 mm diameter at f = 89 mm, 12.58 mm at f = 100)
agrees. It is kept unchanged on all four surfaces, and `projection.maxTraceFieldDeg` is capped at 42°, the whole
degree below the chief-ray limit. `source.sdSource` is "figure-measured" because the drawings, measured at natural
size, confirm the value; the number itself is Eder's.

### Patent check (D6)

Von Rohr names the English patent: C. A. Steinheil 3, "Improvements in photographic lenses", E.P. 2937 of
15 November [1865] "auf das Periskop", taken through W. Bünger (bibliography p. 428). DEPATISnet holds it as
GB000186502937A, 9 pages with empty bibliographic fields; Google Patents has no record. The scan is the printed
specification of William Bünger, a communication from Charles Augustus Steinheil of Munich, dated 15 November 1865
and sealed 11 May 1866, with one drawing sheet. It prints a table of radius ratios r1 : r2 for image angles of 45°,
70° and 90° (1 : −1.1407, −1.1529, −1.1828) and two numerical examples in lines, without an index (the glass is
called mirror glass):

| Example | Focus | r1 / r2 | Thickness | Separation | Lens diameter | Stop | Image |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 90° | 78 | 13.95 / 16.50 | 1.00 | 6.60 | 10 | 2 | 156 |
| 75° | 78.0 | 12.502 / 14.413 | 1.0 | 6.26 | 7.5 | 2.0 | 120.0 |
| 90° at f = 100 | 100 | 17.88 / 21.15 | 1.28 | 8.46 | 12.82 | 2.56 | — |
| Von Rohr | 100 | 17.5 / 20.8 | 1.3 | 12.6 | — | — | — |

Result: **table differs**. The patent's 90° example has a radius ratio (1.1828) close to Eder's (1.1843) but larger
radii, thinner lenses and a separation of 8.46 against 12.6 at f = 100, and no index. The file was not converted to
a patent lens.

### Open limitations

- The model keeps the rounded table: EFL 1.3 % short, and the oblique foci differ from curve 109b by up to 0.5 mm,
  where Eder's two-decimal data match within 0.15 mm.
- Field capped at 42° against the printed 46°; at the cap only 7 % of the meridional beam passes, and the engine's
  own chief-ray estimate stops at 39.02°.
- The 6.28 mm rims are Eder's lens diameter, confirmed but not set by the drawings; von Rohr prints no clear
  aperture.
- nD only; Eder's second index (1.5360, for the chemical focus) is not used, so the f/40 chemical focus shift is not
  modelled. Eder's index subscripts appear interchanged relative to his focus labels.
- Eder's alternative separation "8.29" is unexplained; the patent's 90° example scales to 8.46.
- `maker` stays null. Von Rohr credits A. Steinheil and the Optisch-astronomische Werkstätte of C. A. Steinheil in
  Munich (C. A. Steinheil Söhne from 1865), with a Voigtländer manufacturing licence of 1865; Steinheil is not a
  catalog maker.
