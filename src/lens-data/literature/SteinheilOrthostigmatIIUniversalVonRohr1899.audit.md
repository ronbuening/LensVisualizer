# Steinheil Orthostigmat Type II f/6 (von Rohr 1899, Fig. 143) — audit log

## 2026-11-18 — Literature reconstruction audit

### Pages and figure used

Von Rohr (1899), printed pp. 382–384 (e-rara PDF pages 138–140): history of the Orthostigmat on p. 382, table and
Fig. 143 on p. 383 (beside the companion Fig. 142), glass names, ν̄ and the text results for this form on p. 384,
the commercial series and the Voigtländer agreement at the foot of p. 384, and von Höegh's priority argument at the
top of p. 385. The bibliography entry R. Steinheil 3 was read on p. 430, the glass-table definition of ν̄ on p. 339.
Three further e-rara sections of the same record were consulted: Tafel II (PDF 4637814, page 3) with No. 143 and
curves 143a/143b, and the Bemerkungen zu den Figurentafeln (PDF 4637812, page 2), which give the curve scales, the
hatching code and the statement that every lens was traced with its source data before reduction to f = 100. All
values were read on page renders up to 600 dpi; the scan's native resolution is 164 ppi.

### Signs and their basis

Fig. 143 shows a convex outer face, two cemented junctions bulging toward the stop and a concave stop-side face in
each cell. The patent claim (D.R.P. 88 505) fixes the same shapes: a positive lens enclosed by a biconvex lens and a
biconcave lens. L1 biconvex needs r1 > 0 and r2 < 0; L2 positive with r2 < 0 then needs r3 < 0 with |r3| < |r2|;
L3 biconcave needs r4 > 0. The rear cell mirrors the front. The patent drawing of its first example shows the same
profile.

- EFL 99.587 mm against the printed 100 (−0.41 %, inside the 0.8–1.25 window). Paraxial back focus 85.498 mm, stored
  as the last gap (was 85.49822408612587). Total vertex length 30.12 mm. Petzval sum 0.00431 mm⁻¹.
- f-number 6.000 from the stop: entrance-pupil radius 8.299 mm, stop radius 7.034 mm.
- Longitudinal spherical aberration at the f/6 pupil: −0.101 (rim), −0.489 (0.85), −0.517 (0.7), −0.343 mm (0.5);

  −0.08 (r1), −0.06 (d1) and +0.07 mm (r2); the rest are below 0.02 mm.

### Patent check (D6)

Von Rohr's source "R. Steinheil 3" is, by his bibliography on p. 430, D.R.P. 88 505 of 28 November 1893, "Objectiv für
photographische Zwecke", on the Orthostigmat Type II, which he used through the abstract of the English patent in The
British Journal of Photography 1896 (vol. 43, No. 1891, pp. 489–490). The DEPATISnet scan (issued 4 September 1896;
second edition of 12 September 1899) was read digit by digit. Its first example, f = 493 mm at 1:6.2, prints R0 = R14
= +173.0, R2 = R12 = ±139.66, R4 = R10 = ±77.805, R6 = R8 = −202.47 mm, D1 = D13 = 20, D3 = D11 = 25, D5 = D9 = 4 mm,
a distance D7 = 50 mm between the cells, and nD/nF of 1.61003/1.61759, 1.51874/1.52561 and 1.56370/1.57160. Result:
**table identical up to von Rohr's reduction and rounding.** The example traces to EFL 494.15 mm; scaled to 100 mm it
gives r = 35.01, 28.26, 15.75, 40.97, d = 4.05, 5.06, 0.81 and b = 5.06, which round to von Rohr's table except d1
(4.047, printed 4.1) and r3 (15.745, printed 15.8), both within 0.005 mm of the rounding boundary. The patent gives no
stop position; the central stop is von Rohr's. Its second example (476 mm, 1:4) carries the glasses of Fig. 142. The
English abstract von Rohr cites was not seen. The file was not converted to a patent lens.

### Open limitations

- The model keeps the rounded book table: EFL 0.41 % short, and the f/6 marginal aberration about 0.16 mm more
  negative than von Rohr's own curve, which the unrounded patent values reproduce.
- Rims are a package estimate, up to 0.2 mm inside von Rohr's drawing and 0.7 mm inside the patent drawing; oblique
  beams may be slightly more vignetted than in the drawn lens.
- nD only; no chromatic behaviour is modelled although the patent prints nF (not nC).
- `maker` stays null: von Rohr credits C. A. Steinheil Söhne, which is not a catalog maker. Voigtländer & Sohn made
  the same type under the joint agreement as the Collinear Type I, not this Orthostigmat.
- The key carries no design year; the design dates from the patent application of November 1893.
