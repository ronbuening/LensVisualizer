# Audit log — Voigtländer Petzval Portrait f/3.4 (von Rohr 1899, Fig. 100)

## 2026-11-18 — Literature reconstruction audit

### Book pages and figure used

Moritz von Rohr, *Theorie und Geschichte des photographischen Objektivs* (Berlin: Julius Springer, 1899). The table
and Fig. 100 are on printed p. 250 (e-rara chapter PDF 4637944, page 6; scan 160 ppi, rendered at 300–600 dpi); the
history is on pp. 248–255 (pages 4–11). Values were read on the page image, not from the OCR layer. The caption reads

1858. 4. 237. Reducirt auf fD = 100 mm. Durchgerechnet für 1 : 3.4 und ω = 12°."

Further sections of the same e-rara record, not among the held source pages: the Litteraturverzeichniss (PDF 4637806)
pp. 411, 421 and 433 for the Eder, Petzval and Voigtländer entries; the Bemerkungen zu den Figurentafeln (PDF 4637812,
page 2) for the hatching legend and curve scales; and Tafel I (PDF 4637814, page 2), which redraws the lens as No. 100
with curves 100a and 100b. The bibliography resolves the caption's sources: Voigtländer (3.) is his 1859 Braunschweig

pencil notes ("1840", "1:3,7 f=149", angle notes by the drawing); they touch no printed digit and were not used.

### Signs and their basis

Fig. 100 rendered at 600 dpi (24.06 px/mm from the 60.4 mm vertex length):

- r1 positive, r2 negative: L1 is drawn biconvex.
- r3 positive: the flint's rear face meets its rim 13–16 px (0.55–0.65 mm) behind its vertex, so it is concave toward
  the image (drawn more curved than 436 mm). With r3 negative the EFL is 83.90 mm; positive gives 99.93 mm.
- r4 positive: L3's front face reaches its rim about 25–30 px behind the vertex (the radius gives 0.98 mm at 14.3 mm).
- r5, r6 positive: the air crescent between L3 and L4 narrows toward the rim (0.095 mm at a height of 14.5 mm).
- r7 negative: L4's rear rim lies about 20 px ahead of its vertex (the radius gives 0.68 mm).

Tafel I No. 100 shows the same shapes. Hatching per the plate legend: L1 and L4 falling to the right (old, low-index
crown), L2 and L3 rising to the right (flint).

### Identity

Von Rohr (p. 251) states that Petzval's types were finished by mid-1840 and that Petzval gave their manufacture to
Fr. Voigtländer, the Vienna optician; trial pictures followed in autumn 1840 and the portrait objective was announced
before the end of 1840. The staged maker (Voigtländer) and the key's design year (1840) are confirmed. Designer:
Joseph Petzval.

### Patent check (D6)

Von Rohr names no patent for this design. The text (pp. 248–255) says only that Petzval made no contract with
Voigtländer about his rights, and the bibliography entries for Petzval (p. 421) and Voigtländer (p. 433) list no
patent for the 1840 objective; Voigtländer's D.R.P. 5761 of 1878 listed there is a later portrait objective with a
cemented rear lens. Result: no patent, so no table to compare.

### Open limitations

- nD only; von Rohr prints no dispersion for this lens, so chromatic results of the model carry no source meaning.
  The text notes that the first objectives were chromatically overcorrected.
- The model is von Rohr's computed design, not the lens as issued: Voigtländer separated the rear lenses with a ring
  (p. 253), and Eder's 1899 data, summarised in von Rohr's footnote (pp. 253–254), give a much smaller cell
  separation, fourth-lens radii 5.2 % and 1.7 % larger and four glasses. None of this is modelled.
- The front rim stays 1.1 mm above the drawn cell; a figure-fidelity pass could lower r1–r3 to the exact f/3.4 ray
  height (about 14.7 mm), which would add some off-axis vignetting.
- The model's meridional astigmatism lies 0.14–0.6 mm further from the lens than curve 100b; not traced to a cause.
