# Ross Doublet f/19.4 — von Rohr 1899, Fig. 76

## Source and Design Identification

**Source:** Moritz von Rohr, *Theorie und Geschichte des photographischen Objektivs* (Berlin: Julius Springer, 1899), p. 177, Fig. 76
**Data after:** H. Schroeder, *Die Elemente der photographischen Optik* (Berlin: Robert Oppenheim, 1891), p. 189 (von Rohr's "Quelle: H. Schroeder 4. 189")
**Designer:** Thomas Ross ("Th. Ross" in von Rohr), English optician, son of Andrew Ross
**Manufacturer:** Th. Ross, who brought the doublet to market at the end of 1864 (p. 177)
**Source dated:** 1899
**Design dated:** 1864

Fig. 76 is captioned "Th. Ross: Doublet". Von Rohr places it in the historical part of the book, in the section
on English and American optics, and prints the table beside the figure on p. 177. He reduces the data to
fD = 100 mm and computes the lens for 1:19.4 and a half-field ω = 36°. Tafel III redraws it at natural size among
the wide-angle objectives as No. 76, with curves 76a (spherical aberration) and 76b (astigmatism).

The date is von Rohr's: the doublet was put on the market by Th. Ross at the end of 1864. He calls it a step that was
made "in all probability" following the globe lens, and describes it as Ross's rework of his father's old Collen
lens, whose components he bent more deeply into a new wide-angle doublet. The bibliography's index lists the Collen
lens as Andrew Ross's portrait objective (p. 150).

## Reconstruction Status

**Printed by von Rohr.** The six radius magnitudes (r1 35.8, r2 241.0, r3 62.5, r4 36.9, r5 15.3, r6 25.7), the
glass thicknesses d1 3.6, d2 1.4, d3 4.8 and d4 1.6, the distances b1 5.9 (front cell to stop) and b2 10.1 (stop
to rear cell), the indices nD 1.516 for L1 and L3 and 1.5738 for L2 and L4, the reduction to fD = 100 mm, the
computation aperture 1:19.4 and the half-field ω = 36°. The text gives two numerical results: a spherical
undercorrection of about −0.6 mm at 1:19.4, and an astigmatic difference of about 10.3 mm at an inclination of
about 36°.

**Taken from the drawing or estimated, as von Rohr states.** A note under the table reads "Dicken und Abstände aus
der Zeichnung entnommen. Glasarten nach der Angabe des specifischen Gewichts geschätzt": the thicknesses and the
distances were taken from the drawing, and the glasses were estimated from the stated specific gravity. All four d,
both b and both indices are therefore drawing measurements or density estimates, not maker's data. Von Rohr does
not say whose drawing or whose density statement; that both belong to Schroeder, his named source, is an inference.
The radii carry no qualification. All values stay as printed.

**Added by this model.**

- Radius signs, read from Fig. 76 and Tafel III: the front cell is a meniscus convex toward the object, a biconvex
  crown cemented to a biconcave flint; the rear cell is a meniscus concave toward the stop, a crown meniscus cemented
  to a flint meniscus. The nearly flat cemented face r2 bows toward the image in both drawings by about 0.35 mm at
  the rim, as r2 = −241.0 requires; the opposite sign would give an EFL of 96.25 mm.
- The last gap, 89.708 mm, is the computed paraxial back focus; von Rohr prints no back focus.
- Clear radii, heuristic: 10.9 mm on the rear cell, set by the chief ray at 36° and bounded by the drawings, and
  10 mm on the front cell, which the chief ray does not approach (see Aperture, Field and Rims). The stop radius,
  2.373 mm, is derived from 1:19.4.
- The f-stop list 19.4–22–32–45, the 36° field limit (the printed ω), the element focal lengths (computed thick, in
  air) and the glass labels, which follow the hatching legend of the plates.

**Printed against modelled.** The rounded table traces to an EFL of 99.940 mm, 0.06 % short and well inside the
rounding of the radii (0.05 mm on r1 moves it between 99.77 and 100.11 mm). At that EFL f/19.4 is an entrance-pupil
radius of 2.576 mm, and the longitudinal spherical aberration at its rim is −0.576 mm against the printed −0.6 mm. At
36° the astigmatic difference is 10.60 mm against the text's 10.3 mm, with the mean image 4.65 mm in front of the
paraxial plane against the text's ground-glass shift of about 5 mm. The traced field is the printed 36°.

## Optical Architecture

The doublet is two cemented crown–flint cells about a stop, with the stop much nearer the front cell (5.9 mm)
than the rear (10.1 mm). Both cells are menisci bent around the stop: the front cell convex toward the object, the
rear cell concave toward it. The cells differ in every radius and thickness and are of unequal power, +184.1 mm
in front and +172.6 mm behind, so the system is the "völlig unsymmetrisches Doublet" von Rohr names: neither the
cells nor the stop distances repeat. The vertex length is 27.4 mm for f = 100.

The surface powers are +0.0144, −0.0002 and −0.0092 mm⁻¹ in the front cell and −0.0140, −0.0038 and +0.0223 mm⁻¹ in
the rear; the strongest surface is the rear face r6, and the junction r2 is nearly powerless. The deep cemented face
r5 has its centre of curvature 0.4 mm in front of the stop (computed), so the oblique chief rays cross it close to its
normal, as in the globe lens whose surfaces are centred on the stop. The Petzval sum is 0.00695 mm⁻¹, a Petzval radius
of about 144 mm or 1.44 f, so the image field stays markedly curved; the deep bending flattens the meridional image
instead. The computed distortion is barrel and at most −0.22 % (at 28°), −0.12 % at 36°, which bears out von Rohr's
"praktisch verzeichnungsfreies System".

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Biconvex Positive, front crown

nD = 1.516 (sodium D, estimated from density), no νd published. Glass: old low-index crown (plate hatching),
unmatched. f = +60.7 mm.

The front collective lens, 3.6 mm thick, strongly convex toward the object (r1 = +35.8) and almost flat at its
cemented face (r2 = −241.0). It carries the front cell's positive power and thins to 1.97 mm at the 10 mm rim.

### L2 — Biconcave Negative, front flint

nD = 1.5738 (sodium D, estimated from density), no νd published. Glass: flint (plate hatching), unmatched.
f = −86.4 mm.

A 1.4 mm flint behind the nearly flat junction; its rear face (r3 = +62.5) is concave toward the stop and thickens
the lens to 2.41 mm at the rim. With an index step of only 0.058 at r2 the junction has almost no power; L2 is the
flint partner of L1, whose colour correction the nD-only data cannot show. The front cell has f = +184.1 mm.

### L3 — Positive Meniscus, rear crown

nD = 1.516 (sodium D, estimated from density), no νd published. Glass: old low-index crown (plate hatching),
unmatched. f = +47.1 mm.

The thick (4.8 mm) crown meniscus of the rear cell, concave toward the stop (r4 = −36.9), with a deeply curved
cemented face (r5 = −15.3). With the printed radii it runs out to a knife edge at a radius of 12.95 mm; both drawings
bring r5 into the corner where r4 meets the cell's rim line, which is where the drawings place the rim. At the
model's 10.9 mm rim it is 1.88 mm thick.

### L4 — Negative Meniscus, rear flint

nD = 1.5738 (sodium D, estimated from density), no νd published. Glass: flint (plate hatching), unmatched.
f = −69.8 mm.

The flint meniscus closes the rear cell, convex toward the image (r6 = −25.7), 1.6 mm on the axis and 3.74 mm at the
10.9 mm rim. Its rear face is the strongest collective surface of the system. The cemented rear cell has a focal
length of +172.6 mm.

## Glass Selection

| Von Rohr's data | nD | Elements | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crown (drawn as old low-index crown) | 1.516 | L1, L3 | positive lenses of each cell |
| Flint (drawn as flint) | 1.5738 | L2, L4 | negative partners |

Von Rohr prints the two indices under "Glasarten nD" without glass names and without his barred dispersion figure ν̄,
which elsewhere in the book is (nD − 1)/(nG′ − nD), a photographic blue figure rather than an Abbe number. He states
that both indices were estimated from the specific gravity, so even the nD values are inferred. Both drawings hatch L1
and L3 descending to the right, the plate legend's old low-index crown, and L2 and L4 rising to the right, the
legend's flint. Each cell is the conventional pairing of a positive crown with a negative flint. With no dispersion
published, the model keeps nD only, with no Abbe number and no modern catalog glass as a proxy, and is monochromatic.

## Aperture, Field and Rims

Von Rohr computes the lens for 1:19.4. The model sets the iris from that aperture: the entrance-pupil radius is
2.576 mm, 0.75 mm in front of the stop, and the physical stop radius 2.373 mm. The f/19.4 axial beam reaches only
2.58 mm at r1 and 2.31 mm at r6. Fig. 76 draws the incident axial beam at about 2.55 mm and Tafel III at about
2.49 mm.

The printed half-field is ω = 36°, and curve 76b on Tafel III carries its last computed point at 36.0° on the plate's
angle scale, so von Rohr's own trace passed the chief ray at the full angle. The exact chief ray at 36° crosses r1 at
7.09 mm, r4 at 7.81, r5 at 9.27 and r6 at 10.69 mm; with 10 mm rims throughout it would be blocked at r6 from 33.9°.
The engine's chief-ray solve, which converges 0.21 mm from the stop centre, crosses r6 at 10.84 mm. Only r6 blocks, so
only the rear cell was raised, to 10.9 mm, keeping its three faces at one height as both drawings show the cell with a
single flat rim line; the front cell keeps 10 mm. Both drawings support the change. Scaled from the printed 27.4 mm
vertex length, Tafel III (drawn at natural size, 23.80 px/mm measured at 600 dpi against 23.62 nominal) draws both
cells to a rim radius of about 12.9 mm, and Fig. 76 to about 12.88 mm. The drawn cells are thus larger than the
model's rims, which matches von Rohr's closing remark on the "gewaltige Grösse der Linsendurchmesser", the enormous
lens diameters relative to the aperture used that the lens separation forces. Rims at the drawn 12.9 mm would pass the
whole f/19.4 beam unvignetted at 36°.

With the model's rims the meridional beam passes the full stop diameter up to 31°, 92 % at 32°, 75 % at 34°, 65 %
at 35° and 56 % at 36°. With 10 mm rims throughout it would fall from 28° and lose the chief ray beyond 33.9°. Real
vignetting is kept, and the field limit is the printed 36°. The paraxial rim-limited field of the model is 33.9°
(31.7° with 10 mm rims throughout), short of what the exact trace passes, so the printed angle is declared as the
trace limit.

## Spherical Aberration and Astigmatism

The spherical aberration is undercorrected and small for a wide-angle lens, as von Rohr says: −0.144, −0.282, −0.416
and −0.576 mm at 0.5, 0.7, 0.85 and 1.0 of the f/19.4 pupil, close to a pure third-order growth. Curve 76a ends at an
incidence height of about 2.52 mm with about −0.56 mm; the model gives −0.551 mm at that height. Von Rohr adds that
the focal lengths vary as the back focal distances do, so the zones differ in focal length as well as in focus.

A Coddington trace along the exact chief ray, compared with the computed points of curve 76b (solid curve sagittal,
dashed meridional, displacement of the ground glass from the paraxial focus):

| ω (plate) | Sagittal, model | Sagittal, plate | Meridional, model | Meridional, plate |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 17.9° | −2.73 | −2.75 | −1.33 | −1.34 |
| 25.1° | −5.20 | −5.21 | −1.90 | −1.86 |
| 30.75° | −7.56 | −7.54 | −1.51 | −1.50 |
| 36.0° | −9.95 | −9.93 | +0.65 | +0.47 |

The model reproduces the plate within the reading accuracy of the curves, which confirms the transcription, the
radius signs and the stop position together. The meridional image stays within 2 mm of the paraxial plane over the
whole field while the sagittal image curves toward the lens. That is von Rohr's reading: a field concave toward the
lens, astigmatism small for so large an angle (about 10.3 mm at 36°), and a curvature that would require bringing
the ground glass about 5 mm closer to focus the margin of a flat object.

## Design Heritage and Context

Von Rohr relates the doublet to the American globe lens of C. C. Harrison and Schnitzer (No. 74 on Tafel III), a
symmetric pair of deep menisci about a central stop. Just before Ross, on pp. 176–177, he describes C. B. Boyle's
ratio lens of 1863, a hemisymmetric pair of menisci with the stop at the centre of the outer surfaces, with which,
he says, hemisymmetry was realized for the first time in a wide-angle doublet. Ross reached his design by bending
the components of his father's Collen lens more deeply, and von Rohr calls him the first representative of
trial-and-error optics ("tatonnirende Optik") to make a completely unsymmetrical doublet with better flattening of
the field and insignificant distortion. On p. 178 he dates J. H. Dallmeyer's triple landscape lens of early 1865 as
coinciding with Ross's novelty.

The bibliography lists a paper by Th. Ross on triplets, doublets and single view lenses with reference to marginal
distortion (Photographic Journal 1865/66), cited at p. 193, and names J. Stuart as Ross's partner and later
successor. Von Rohr cites Schroeder's *Elemente* of 1891, the source of the numbers, for A. and Th. Ross on his
pp. 150, 152 and 177; the index also records Schroeder's own position at Ross & Co. (pp. 229, 346), but not whether
it gave him the firm's data. Von Rohr names no patent for the doublet. The only patent among his Th. Ross entries,
E.P. 174 of 23 January 1861 on achromatic objectives of crown glass alone or flint glass alone, is indexed at p. 211
and is not this design.
