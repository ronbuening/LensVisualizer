# Audit log — Petzval Landscape Second Form f/8.1 (von Rohr 1899, Fig. 103)

## 2026-11-18 — Literature reconstruction audit

### Book pages and figure used

Moritz von Rohr, *Theorie und Geschichte des photographischen Objektivs* (Berlin: Julius Springer, 1899). Fig. 103
and its caption are on printed p. 258 (e-rara chapter PDF 4637944, page 14); the caption refers the numbers to the
table under Fig. 101 on p. 250 (page 6). Pages 249–262 (pages 5–18) were read for context: Petzval's Satz (Fig. 99),
the 1840 trial (p. 251), the 1856 presentation (pp. 258–259), the Orthoskop, the correction notes and the patent
(pp. 260–261) and the dispute (pp. 261–262). Values were read on the page images (rendered at 400–800 dpi; the

4637812, page 2), which give the hatching legend, the curve scales and the rule that every lens was computed from its
source's data before reduction to f = 100; and the Litteraturverzeichniss (PDF 4637806), pp. 410–411 (Dietzler,
Eder), 421 (Petzval) and 433 (Voigtländer).

### Signs and their basis

Fig. 103 is to scale along the axis. With the 11.4 mm vertex length as scale (369.5 px = 32.4 px/mm at 800 dpi),
the measured stations are: L2 rear 3.09 (table 3.1), stop 6.89 (6.8), L3 front 8.02 (8.0), L3 rear 8.70 (8.6), L4
front 10.22 (10.2) mm. Face displacements 3.73 mm off the axis, against the sags of the printed radii: L1 front
+0.37 mm toward the image (r1 = +22.5 gives 0.31); junction −0.32 mm (r2 = −17.6 gives 0.40, biconvex L1); L2 rear
face nearly straight (r3 = 185.7); L3 front −0.22 mm (31.5 gives 0.22) and rear +0.23 mm (53.5 gives 0.13), both
concave; L4 front −0.08 mm (78.2 gives 0.09) and rear −0.31 mm (22.8 gives 0.31), a meniscus concave toward the
stop. The figure cannot separate the two depths of L3 at this resolution; which face of L3 is the deeper one comes
from the caption ("nur L3 ist umgekehrt") applied to Fig. 101's r4 = 53.5, r5 = 31.5. Tafel II No. 103 shows the same
shapes. Exchanging the indices of L3 and L4 would give f = 165.5, so the printed glass order stands.

the front pair. `sdSource` is "heuristic". The field limit stays at the printed 30°.

### Patent check (D6)

Von Rohr names no patent number. He refers to a patent bearing Petzval's signature that Voigtländer reprinted
(1859, p. 103); a patent attorney's search in Vienna reported on 29 December 1898 that it could not be found, and a
note in Kr. Z. 1863 (vol. 7, p. 72) shows it granted to C. Dietzler on 28 December 1857 and extended to a sixth year
(pp. 260–261). With no number, and an Austrian grant of 1857 outside the coverage of the allowed patent-office
databases, no patent-office search was made. Result: **not checkable; table unknown.** Every number in Fig. 103
comes from Fig. 101, which suggests (inference) that the text von Rohr saw printed none.

### Open limitations

- Not Petzval's final data: von Rohr says he could not find them (pp. 260–262), and Petzval's 1858 description of a
  variable lens separation is not modelled. All thicknesses and distances are von Rohr's assumptions.
- nD only; no dispersion is printed, so chromatic results of the model carry no source meaning.
- The rear rims (6.5 mm) are 2 mm above both drawings, which cannot be taken literally at 1:8.1. A figure-fidelity
  pass could set them to the axial beam (about 5.5 mm), at the cost of 30° transmission (69 % to 57 %).
- Near 30° the model's sagittal focus lies about 1 mm behind curve 103b's last value, in the steepest part of the
  curve; the radius rounding was not explored further there.
- L4's hatching is not resolvable on the scan; its glass follows the table and the focal-length check.
