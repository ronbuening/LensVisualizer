# Enna München Tele-Sandmar 100 mm f/4.5

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,631,497 A  
**Inventor:** Franz Schlegel  
**Assignee:** Harry J. Graw  
**Filed:** 21 February 1952  
**Granted:** 17 March 1953  
**Title:** Telephoto Objective  
**Example details:** Sole 100 mm f/4.5 numerical example and matching repeated claim.

Franz Schlegel of Munich assigned the patent to Harry J. Graw of Chicago; the assignee's name does not establish the manufacturer. The patent does not name Enna or Tele-Sandmar. The [Geiss-America sales manual](https://www.arguscg.org/manuals/sandmar/trutele.pdf) describes a German-made 100 mm f/4.5 Tele-Sandmar for the Argus C3, with stops from f/4.5 to f/22 and rangefinder coupling. Collector sources name Enna-Werk. The focal length, aperture and the C3's in-body shutter behind the lens support a strong but circumstantial association with the patent. No published production element count or cross-section was found, and an engraved patent number has not been verified.

The manual carries a 1951 copyright and indicates that US patents were pending, preceding the patent application in 1952. The commercial literature and matching focal length support the association, but do not establish the glass supplier or identical optics across production versions. The manual is mirrored by the Argus collectors group. The catalog mount is `argus-c`, labelled “Argus C-series”: the geared screw mount of the Argus C2/C3 and later C-series rangefinders.

## Optical Architecture

Four elements form three air-separated optical groups: a cemented front doublet and two separate rear singlets. The patent's two groups are functional positive-front and negative-rear groups, not an assertion of two air-separated components.

The front group is positive and the rear group negative, giving a telephoto arrangement. The 29 mm inter-group interval is retained. The source addresses camera-wall or shutter apertures that can cause vignetting; these housing components are absent from the model, so its illumination does not establish the assembled camera's performance.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 and L2: positive front cemented doublet

L1: nD = 1.62398, νD = 47.0; E-BAF8 spectral proxy (catalog vendor Hikari; historical supplier and melt unspecified). L2: nD = 1.74066, νD = 27.2; Unmatched glass. L1 is biconvex and L2 biconcave, sharing S2 in cemented group C1. Their center thicknesses are 10 mm and 1.2 mm respectively.

The index/dispersion contrast is consistent with a positive cemented correction group. The calculated focal lengths of L1 and L2 treated separately as thick elements in air are +25.77 mm and −34.86 mm; the cemented doublet's calculated focal length is +66.71 mm. These are prescription calculations, not measured production values. No exact modern glass pair or measured color correction is established.

### L3: rear negative meniscus

nD = 1.61008, νD = 46.0; H-BaF6 spectral proxy (catalog vendor CDGM; historical supplier and melt unspecified). Both radii are negative; the stronger concave surface faces the object. Its center thickness is 0.8 mm. Its calculated isolated thick-element focal length in air is −25.54 mm.

### L4: rear plano-convex positive singlet

nD = 1.72719, νD = 28.0; Unmatched glass. The front surface is flat. The 0.03 mm separation from L3 is an air gap. The negative/positive rear pair forms the functional rear group. Its calculated isolated thick-element focal length in air is +37.99 mm.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent explicitly defines its index column as sodium D at 589.3 nm and identifies the dispersion numbers as Abbe numbers. All four elements use the sodium-D reference; the sodium-D to helium-d index difference is about 1.2–2.3 × 10⁻⁴, larger than the patent's five-decimal print precision. L1 uses E-BAF8 and L3 uses H-BaF6 as spectral proxies under the site's proxy tolerance of an Abbe difference within about 0.25 and an index difference within about 0.002. These are not exact glass matches and do not identify the historical glass, supplier or melt.

## Focus Mechanism

The model is at infinity focus only. The production lens couples to the Argus C3 rangefinder, as described in the Geiss-America manual; no finite-focus motion or retail minimum focus distance is established here. Paraxial calculation gives EFL 100.026642611 mm and BFL 39.071980372 mm. The image interval is calculated rather than tabulated in the patent. Dimensions are already in millimeters, with a scale factor of 1 and no unit conversion.

The patent prose suggests a back focus of about 0.2f, whereas its numerical example gives approximately 0.39f (39.071980372 mm); the tabulated prescription is retained.

## Stop and Aperture

The source says the diaphragm or shutter is shown substantially midway between the second and third elements. The model uses an inferred 14.5/14.5 mm split of the 29 mm gap; the patent does not tabulate a precise stop coordinate or mechanical tolerance.

Clear apertures are not tabulated. The inferred front-group semi-diameter is 15.1 mm and the rear-group semi-diameter 9 mm, with square rims throughout. The sole figure's long axial spans agree with the numerical example within 1 %, but an independent front-surface arc fit gives a scale about 6 % smaller. The thin rear element and tiny rear air gap are schematic: their axial dimensions do not share that scale. The optical rims suggest proportional targets of approximately 11.1 mm at the front and 6.4 mm at the rear using the long-span scale, or about 11.8/6.9 mm using the arc scale. The larger modeled caps preserve the traced field and off-axis illumination; they do not reproduce the drawn apertures. Neither reading is a printed clear diameter.

Clearance from the stop plane to the inferred surface caps is 12.874357 mm on the front side and 11.025989 mm on the rear side. At f/4.5, the calculated physical iris radius is 6.738905554 mm; the patent does not print an iris diameter. The f/4.5–22 range follows the click stops illustrated in the Geiss-America manual.

No source radius, spacing, plane surface or glass value is modified. No rim exception, spacer, aperture clipping surface or geometry-limit override is applied.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The calculated EFL is 100.026642611 mm at f/4.5, a +0.0266 % difference from the patent's 100 mm. Optical length is 42.73 mm and the calculated image plane lies 81.801980372 mm from the first vertex.

Off-axis clipping remains with the larger inferred rims. The engine's wide-open infinity pupil-lattice calculation gives approximately 83.6 % geometric transmission at the 12.1° full-frame corner. The f/4.5 axial beam and corner chief ray pass every optical rim. Illumination depends on those apertures and on the omitted camera housing; no measured throughput, MTF or production cross-section is available to establish identical commercial performance.
