## Patent Reference / Design Identification

**Patent:** US 3,856,386 A  
**Application Number:** 358,266  
**Filed:** May 8, 1973  
**Granted:** December 24, 1974  
**Inventor:** Yoshisato Fujioka  
**Assignee:** Kabushiki Kaisha Yashica (Yashica Co., Ltd.)  
**Title:** Extrawide-angle retrofocus type of objective  
**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 1, Fig. 1 and Table I  
**Priority:** Japan, May 9, 1972, application 47-45044

The prescription is the first numerical embodiment of Fujioka's patent, rather than a measured production lens.
The association with the Auto Yashinon-DS 20mm f/3.3 is supported by convergent specifications:

1. Table I and Fig. 1 describe eleven glass elements in nine air-separated groups. Yashica's lens brochure gives the same counts for the Auto Yashinon 20mm.
2. The patent and brochure agree on f/3.3 and a full viewing angle of 94.5°, the brochure expressing the angle as 94°30′.
3. The patent assignee is Yashica. The contemporary product family is for the company's single-lens reflex system.
4. The brochure specifies a Praktica screw mount and minimum focus of 0.3 m. These supply production metadata; they do not establish the patent's focusing law.

The brochure does not identify this patent or select its first example. A separate Yashica TL-ELECTRO brochure names
the Auto Yashinon-DS 20mm with the same specifications. [6] Whether the production lens uses exactly this prescription,
its release date and its glass supplier remain unconfirmed. The product name therefore expresses a correlation, not
manufacturer confirmation that these radii describe production copies. [1, 2]

## Optical Architecture

The design has eleven elements in nine air-separated groups, organized by the patent into four functional groups.
These functional groups must not be confused with the nine physical groups: the first contains several air-spaced
lenses, while the rear functional group contains a cemented pair and separate lenses.
The functional power sequence is negative, weakly negative, positive and positive in the published infinity state.
A strongly divergent front section precedes the principal converging section and leaves substantial image-side clearance.

Fig. 1 numbers the elements 1 to 11 and Table I lists them the same way; the patent text names the same lenses
IA–ID, IIA–IIB, IIIA and IVA–IVD, the Roman numeral being the functional group. L1–L11 below follow the figure's
numbers, so L5 is the patent's IIA and L8 its IVA. The diagram brackets the functional groups as I (−), II (−),
III (+) and IV (+); their focal lengths are −24.3 mm, −510 mm, +20.8 mm and +74.9 mm. It marks the two cemented
pairs as D1 (L5–L6, which is the whole of group II) and D2 (L8–L9, the front of group IV). [1, Fig. 1; cols. 3–4]

The patent normalizes Example 1 to a focal length of 99.8. All lengths in the model are uniformly scaled by 20/99.8,
without changing indices or Abbe numbers. The model's infinity EFL is 20.003 mm and the back focal distance from the
final refracting vertex is 37.124 mm. Their ratio is 1.8559, which is what makes the design a retrofocus. The
first-to-last vertex track is 60.685 mm; first vertex to paraxial image plane is 97.809 mm. These optical distances
are not barrel dimensions or the flange focal distance.

The front principal plane lies 36.000 mm imageward of the first vertex. The rear principal plane lies 17.121 mm
imageward of the final vertex, behind the glass.

The patent's Seidel analysis assumes a stop between the third and fourth functional groups but does not tabulate its
position. The model places it at the midpoint of that air space. The stop semi-diameter in the data file, 4.922 mm,
is set so that the paraxial entrance pupil, 3.031 mm in semi-diameter, gives f/3.3. It is a calibration to the stated
aperture, not evidence for the physical diaphragm diameter. [1, cols. 5–6]

Fig. 1 does not draw the diaphragm. The coefficients of Table II can be reproduced only with an entrance pupil 123.0
normalized units behind the first vertex, 24.65 mm at this scale. The paraxial image of that pupil falls 0.38 mm
behind the front vertex of L8, outside the air space, so the table records the pupil assumed for the third-order
calculation and does not locate a physical diaphragm. The midpoint placement remains an inference; it gives an
entrance pupil 23.72 mm behind the first vertex. [1, Table II; col. 6]

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Element focal lengths below are standalone thick-element values in air at the d-line, computed from the final
scaled surfaces. They are not the element's in-situ contribution to total system power. At a cemented interface,
replacing the neighboring material by air for this calculation is an explicitly hypothetical isolation.

### L1 — Negative Meniscus, convex toward the object

nd = 1.72000, νd = 50.3. Glass: K-LaK10 (SUMITA; modern coordinate equivalent, supplier unconfirmed). f = -77.43 mm.

The first front meniscus contributes negative power to the divergent entrance section. The patent relates the combined first-pair focal length to distortion/coma balance and the long back focal distance. This is the patent's statement about the pair, not a computed aberration contribution of this element. [1, Fig. 1; Table I; cols. 3–6]

### L2 — Negative Meniscus, convex toward the object

nd = 1.71700, νd = 47.9. Glass: S-LAM3 (OHARA; modern coordinate equivalent, supplier unconfirmed). f = -75.26 mm.

The second front meniscus continues the negative entrance pair. The pair's power includes the air space between the two menisci; it is not the sum of the two standalone powers. [1, Fig. 1; Table I; cols. 3–6]

### L3 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.69680, νd = 55.6. Glass: K-LaK14 (SUMITA; modern coordinate equivalent, supplier unconfirmed). f = +49.48 mm.

The positive third lens interrupts the sequence of front negative menisci. It lies inside functional group I, so its positive standalone power does not make that functional group positive. The patent explicitly identifies this intervening positive lens in its front-group description. [1, Fig. 1; Table I; cols. 3–6]

### L4 — Negative Meniscus, convex toward the object

nd = 1.72000, νd = 50.3. Glass: K-LaK10 (SUMITA; modern coordinate equivalent, supplier unconfirmed). f = -31.64 mm.

The last member of functional group I restores negative power before the air space that separates it from group II. Its strongly curved rear face nearly meets the front rim of the following cemented pair, which bounds how large that face can be modeled. [1, Fig. 1; Table I; cols. 3–6]

### L5 — Biconcave Negative, cemented to L6

nd = 1.70154, νd = 41.1. Glass: BASF7 (SUMITA; modern coordinate equivalent, supplier unconfirmed). f = -15.38 mm.

L5 begins the second functional group. Its weakly concave front surface satisfies the patent requirement on the sign of the group entrance curvature. The following cemented interface has negative refracting power because the downstream index decreases across a positive radius. [1, Fig. 1; Table I; cols. 3–6]

### L6 — Biconvex Positive, cemented to L5

nd = 1.67270, νd = 32.2. Glass: SF5 (SCHOTT; modern coordinate equivalent, supplier unconfirmed). f = +16.56 mm.

This thick positive member shares its entrance surface with the rear of L5. The two elements form a weakly negative net doublet when computed as an isolated cemented group, despite the positive standalone power of L6. The patent discusses this group in relation to image-field and chromatic correction; the model's chromatic behavior follows the catalog equivalents named below, not measured data for the original glasses. [1, Fig. 1; Table I; cols. 3–6]

### L7 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.60717, νd = 40.2. Glass: BASF3 (SUMITA; modern coordinate equivalent, supplier unconfirmed). f = +20.80 mm.

The separate positive lens forms functional group III. It lies immediately before the air space that contains the inferred stop. Its standalone power is strongly positive; its individual aberration contribution is not computed here. [1, Fig. 1; Table I; cols. 3–6]

### L8 — Biconcave Negative, cemented to L9

nd = 1.74000, νd = 28.2. Glass: FD3 (HOYA; modern coordinate equivalent, supplier unconfirmed). f = -9.52 mm.

This element starts the fourth functional group and forms the negative member of the rear cemented pair. The positive-radius cemented interface again has a drop in index. The patent bounds the magnitude of this negative interface power to avoid excessive field-curvature and coma consequences. [1, Fig. 1; Table I; cols. 3–6]

### L9 — Biconvex Positive, cemented to L8

nd = 1.58144, νd = 40.8. Glass: S-TIL25 (OHARA; modern coordinate equivalent, supplier unconfirmed). f = +24.67 mm.

The following positive element completes the second cemented pair. Its lower refractive index and different Abbe number are as printed. Neither the pair nor the complete lens is described as apochromatic: the patent gives no partial-dispersion data and the original glasses are not identified. [1, Fig. 1; Table I; cols. 3–6]

### L10 — Positive Meniscus, convex toward the image

nd = 1.69100, νd = 54.8. Glass: K-LaK9 (SUMITA; modern coordinate equivalent, supplier unconfirmed). f = +34.70 mm.

Both radii are negative, with the image-side surface more strongly curved, and the standalone power is positive. The air spaces on either side are thin (0.95 mm and 0.10 mm), so its rim is bounded by its neighbors; the figure draws it square-cut and smaller than the final element. [1, Fig. 1; Table I; cols. 3–6]

### L11 — Positive Meniscus, convex toward the image

nd = 1.64000, νd = 60.2. Glass: LACL60 (HOYA; modern coordinate equivalent, supplier unconfirmed). f = +39.89 mm.

The final positive lens has a very weakly curved front face, not an exactly plane surface; the large finite negative radius is kept as printed. Its rear vertex is the reference for the back focal distance. The model's image distance is the computed paraxial back focus, which the table's rounded back-focus value agrees with. [1, Fig. 1; Table I; cols. 3–6]

## Glass Identification / Selection

The patent publishes a d-line index and a one-decimal Abbe number for each element, without supplier names, melt
identifiers or partial-dispersion data. The data file names, for each element, a catalog glass whose index and Abbe
number match the printed pair within 0.0001 in nd and 0.15 in νd. Six of the ten distinct glasses reproduce the printed
values exactly and the other four agree within 0.05 in νd. These are modern coordinate equivalents that supply a
dispersion curve for the model; they do not identify the glass or the supplier used in 1974.

The exact matches come from legacy Japanese catalog types that SUMITA's all-glass catalog and HOYA's catalog of
obsolete glasses still list; the patent's Abbe numbers equal those catalog values to the printed decimal. The OHARA
types in the last column are current-catalog glasses at the same index, except BAM23, which is an archived row. Similar
coordinates occur across suppliers, so the choice of catalog row is not evidence of who melted the original glass, and
a modern equivalent may be an environmentally reformulated composition. [3–5, 7, 8]

The model takes its C-, F- and g-line behavior from these catalog curves, not from measurements of the patent glasses.
No element carries an anomalous-dispersion flag. The patent's index and Abbe values do not support an APO claim or a
verified secondary-spectrum estimate. The computed d-line surface-by-surface Petzval sum is 0.005672 mm⁻¹. It is a
first-order surface sum, not a complete sagittal or tangential field-curvature prediction.

## Focus Mechanism

No focusing movement is modeled. The patent supplies one infinity prescription, with no close-focus spacings,
moving-group description or mechanism data from which a focusing law could be derived. The manufacturer's 0.3 m is the
marketed minimum focus distance and is recorded as product data only; it does not correspond to a modeled finite
conjugate, helicoid travel or focus breathing. The model has no variable air spaces. [1, 2]

## Conditional Expressions

The five applicable conditions are evaluated using the published native coordinates and the patent's 99.8 mm
normalization. The homogeneous ratios are unchanged by the model's uniform length scaling. [1, cols. 3–6]

- Requirement 1: the magnitude of the combined first-pair focal length divided by f is 1.8403, between 1.2 and 3.
- Requirement 2: the reciprocal radius of the entrance surface of functional group II is -0.0018804 mm⁻¹, satisfying its required negative sign in native units.
- Requirement 3: f times the index drop divided by the first cemented-interface radius is 0.04784, between 0 and 0.5.
- Requirement 4: the specified air gap plus the reduced thicknesses of functional group II, divided by f, is 0.53438, between 0.3 and 0.7.
- Requirement 5: f times the rear-doublet index drop divided by its cemented-interface radius is 0.19426, between 0 and 0.5.

These calculations verify the inequalities for the chosen table. They do not establish image quality at all
apertures and field angles.

Table I can also be checked against the patent's own aberration data. Third-order coefficients computed from the
printed radii, spacings and indices match 98 of the 100 surface entries of Table II to the last printed digit. One
entry differs by one unit in the fifth decimal. The other, the coma term of surface 16, is printed as −0.38712
against a computed −0.38742; the printed column sum agrees with the computed value, so that entry is a misprint in
Table II. The spherical and Petzval columns do not depend on the stop, so this agreement rules out a wrong sign or
digit in any radius, thickness or index of Table I.

An exact ray trace of the model follows Figs. 1B–1D in sign and shape. Longitudinal spherical aberration is
+0.08 mm at f/3.3 and −0.19 mm at f/4.7; the figure reads about +0.10 mm and −0.20 mm at this scale. The tangential
and sagittal field curves cross zero near 28° and 44°, as drawn, and barrel distortion peaks at −3.0% near 42°
against about −3.4% in the figure. The drawn astigmatism excursions are larger than the model's: −0.54 mm against
−0.32 mm for the tangential curve at 44°, and −0.21 mm against −0.16 mm for the sagittal curve at 33°. These
values are sensitive to the unpublished stop position. Moving the model stop 0.65 mm toward L7 reproduces the drawn
tangential excursion but moves the zero crossings to about 21° and 45°, away from the drawn ones, so the figures do
not settle the stop position either. [1, Table II; Figs. 1B–1D]

## Aperture Geometry and Model Limits

No source semi-diameters are published. The modeled rims are estimates read from the outline of the patent's Fig. 1,
which is drawn to scale with Table I (its vertex positions reproduce the scaled prescription within 0.9 mm over the
60.7 mm track), and each value was then checked against an exact ray trace, spherical rim slope, positive glass edge
thickness and air-gap clearance. The three front surfaces and the rear face of the fourth element instead carry
real-ray envelope heights, which lie within 2–7% of the drawing. No source radius or axial spacing was altered to
resolve a rim conflict.

The figure draws the first, second and fourth elements with a flange: a flat land and a cylindrical edge beyond the
end of the rear face. The model gives each of these elements the two curve-end heights, and the diagram joins them
with a straight edge, so the flange appears as a slanted edge. A square rim at the flange height is not possible on
the first element, whose rear radius is smaller than that height, and on the second and fourth it would carry the
rear face over the front corner of the element behind it, which the figure does not show.

The figure draws the cemented pair of functional group II, the separate positive lens of group III and the three
rear-group components with square-cut rims that are markedly smaller than an unvignetted oblique bundle would need,
and the model follows it. At the modeled maximum aperture the on-axis beam clears every rim, and the chief ray reaching
the 21.63 mm corner of the 135 format, at a 48.0° semi-field, also clears. Oblique bundles are clipped: in a meridional
trace the stop height that passes every rim is about 94% of the full aperture at 20°, 80% at 30°, 63% at 40° and 33%
at the patent's 47.25°. Over the whole pupil the share of the stop area that passes every rim is about 96%, 81%, 58%
and 26% at the same angles. The upper side is limited by the rear cemented pair and the meniscus behind it; the lower
side by the group III lens at intermediate fields, by the rear face of the fourth element near 40° and by the strongly
curved rear face of the first element near the corner. Consequently, the patent's 94.5° full viewing angle is a source
specification, not a full-pupil coverage claim. These fractions describe figure-derived rims, not measured production
vignetting.

All surfaces are spherical; there are no aspherical coefficients or conic conversions. There are no rear plates,
cover glass or cement layers in the prescription. The image plane is set at the calculated paraxial back focus,
37.124 mm, which the patent's rounded back-focus value agrees with after scaling.

The viewer sets its wide-open iris from an exact on-axis marginal ray at f/3.3, which gives a semi-diameter of
4.970 mm rather than the paraxial 4.922 mm; neither is a patent diaphragm dimension. Its estimate of the usable
half-field, 38.66°, is a paraxial chief-ray bound set by the rear face of the first element. It is narrower than the
published 47.25° semi-field, so off-axis displays that use it stop short of the full field even though the traced
corner chief ray clears.
