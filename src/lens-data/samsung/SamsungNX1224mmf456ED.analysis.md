# SAMSUNG 12-24mm f/4-5.6 ED

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2013/0235465 A1, _Zoom lens and photographing apparatus including the same_; granted as US 9,341,825 B2.\
**Inventors:** Tetsuya Arimoto.\
**Assignee:** Samsung Electronics Co., Ltd.\
**Published:** September 12, 2013; grant May 17, 2016.\
**Priority:** March 9, 2012, KR 10-2012-0024545.\
**Filed:** January 29, 2013.\
**Example details:** Example 5, Figure 9 and Tables 13–15, at the printed millimetre scale.

The patent family has a strong circumstantial association with the Samsung NX 12-24mm f/4-5.6 ED. The product has ten elements in eight groups, one ED element, two aspherical elements, 0.24 m minimum focus and 0.14× maximum magnification. These agree with Examples 2, 3 and 5; the published product specifications do not distinguish those examples. Example 5 is selected because its approximately 95.9 mm wide-end optical track is shorter than the approximately 99.0 mm alternatives, making it a plausible compact-barrel choice. Optical track and external barrel length measure different things, so this does not establish the production prescription. No manufacturer construction diagram establishes a unique example. Examples 1 and 4 have eleven elements and do not fit the published count. [LensTip](https://www.lenstip.com/1086-Samsung_NX_12-24_mm_f_4.0-5.6_ED-lens_specifications.html), [Imaging Resource](https://www.imaging-resource.com/lenses/samsung/12-24mm-f4-5.6-ed-nx/specifications/), [DPReview](https://www.dpreview.com/products/samsung/lenses/samsung_12-24_4-5p6/).

The marketed range is 12–24 mm at f/4–5.6 for Samsung NX, APS-C. Example 5 instead prints 12.36 / 18.00 / 23.40 mm at f/4.1 / 4.73 / 5.8, with full angular fields of 103° / 79° / 64°. Those source values remain distinct from the retail specifications. The catalog stores the wide-end marketed f-number, 4, because that field accepts one number; the full marketed range appears in the lens name and here.

## Optical Architecture

The four optical groups have negative, positive, positive and positive power. G1 contains L1–L2, G2 is L3, G3 is the cemented L4–L5 focusing doublet, and G4 contains the iris and L6–L10, including cemented L8–L9. The ten elements form eight air-separated components. S10 is the iris, 2.5 mm before S11. The optional image-side filter mentioned in the patent has no prescribed geometry and is omitted.

The three printed infinity states retain D1 = 4.500 / 5.250 / 5.100 mm, D2 = 14.244 / 4.551 / 1.302 mm and D3 = 12.668 / 7.645 / 3.913 mm. Internal group spacings remain fixed. The calculated first-order values are:

| Quantity                          |    Wide |  Middle |    Tele |
| --------------------------------- | ------: | ------: | ------: |
| Effective focal length (mm)       | 12.3637 | 18.0055 | 23.4008 |
| Nominal f-number                  |    4.10 |    4.73 |    5.80 |
| Paraxial back focal distance (mm) | 21.6314 | 29.4076 | 36.8514 |
| First vertex to image (mm)        | 95.9134 | 89.7236 | 90.0364 |

Back focal distance is not printed. The image plane is the calculated paraxial plane, without a shift to minimize aberrations. The iris radius is calculated from each station's nominal aperture; its wide / middle / tele values are 4.5957 / 4.7832 / 4.5249 mm. Agreement with the printed f-numbers follows from that iris calculation.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The individual focal lengths below are thick-lens values in air. A cemented member's individual value does not describe its power in the assembled doublet.

### L1 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.83481, νd = 42.72. Glass: TAFD5F (Hoya catalog equivalent). f = −25.12 mm.

The 3 mm front meniscus contributes negative power to G1. Its front clear radius is 17.7 mm. The rear spherical cap remains 11.2 mm, with an opaque vertical land extending to 17.7 mm, following the rear edge drawn in Figure 9.

### L2 — Aspherical Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.62. Glass: TAF1 (Hoya catalog equivalent). f = −19.26 mm.

The 1.7 mm rear element completes the negative front group. Both surfaces are aspherical, providing correction within the strongly diverging front section. The fixed L1–L2 separation is 8.110 mm.

### L3 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.78. Glass: FDS90 (Hoya catalog equivalent). f = +43.47 mm.

The 3.210 mm singlet forms positive group G2. Variable gaps D1 and D2 separate it from G1 and G3.

### L4–L5 — Positive Cemented Focusing Doublet C1

L4: nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.78. Glass: FDS90 (Hoya catalog equivalent). f = −48.58 mm.\
L5: nd = 1.60342, νd = 38.01. Glass: E-F5 (Hoya catalog equivalent). f = +24.53 mm.

The 1 mm negative meniscus is cemented to the 4 mm biconvex positive element at S8. Together they have a focal length of +51.02 mm. The lower-Abbe negative member and higher-Abbe positive member provide a chromatic power balance. Table 15 describes focusing by changing the air gaps on either side of this group. The adopted clear radii at S7–S9 are 8.2 / 7.8 / 7.4 mm, within the Figure 9 rim bounds and large enough for its approximately 8.1 mm G3 ray height at the front face.

### L6 — Low-Dispersion Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.61. Glass: FCD1 (Hoya catalog equivalent). f = +30.06 mm.

The 3 mm meniscus is the first powered element behind the iris. Its low index and high Abbe number fit the product's single ED element. The ED position is inferred from the example and product count; the supplier and production melt are unconfirmed.

### L7 — Biconvex Positive Singlet

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.44. Glass: FC5 (Hoya catalog equivalent). f = +25.28 mm.

The 2.4 mm singlet adds positive power in the rear train. It is separated from L6 by 2.55 mm and from the following doublet by 1.2 mm.

### L8–L9 — Negative Cemented Doublet C2

L8: nd = 1.88300, νd = 40.80. Glass: TAFD30 (Hoya catalog equivalent). f = −9.55 mm.\
L9: nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.44. Glass: FC5 (Hoya catalog equivalent). f = +16.41 mm.

The 1.2 mm biconcave element is cemented to the 6.5 mm biconvex element at S16. The assembled doublet has f = −36.49 mm. This negative component offsets positive power elsewhere in G4; its differing glass dispersions also contribute to chromatic balance.

### L10 — Aspherical Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.62. Glass: TAF1 (Hoya catalog equivalent). f = −75.01 mm.

The 1.5 mm final element is separated from L9 by 1 mm. Its rear surface S19 is aspherical, providing an additional correction surface near the image side.

## Focus Mechanism and Zoom Motion

The model includes infinity focus only. The product's 0.24 m minimum focus is catalog metadata. Table 15 prints finite states whose object-to-image distance is 240 mm and whose magnifications are 0.076 / 0.107 / 0.140×. D1 stays fixed; D2 increases while D3 decreases, moving G3 toward the image side. A direct infinity-to-near interpolation misses focus at mid-travel by approximately 0.09 / 0.16 / 0.27 mm, so these states require intermediate focus positions before a continuous focus range can be included.

There are 101 infinity zoom stations. The three printed stations are retained exactly. Between them, D1 follows a piecewise-linear motion, including its reversal at the middle station. D2 and D3 are calculated so that effective focal length and paraxial back focal distance interpolate between the printed states; both gaps decrease monotonically. These 98 intermediate states describe an inferred optical motion, without establishing the production cam. Maximum independently sampled interpolation defocus is 0.00037 mm; interpolation from only the three printed gap rows would miss focus by 0.705 mm.

## Aspherical Surfaces

L2 has aspheres on S3 and S4; L10 has one on S19. All use K = 0 with the standard base sag c·y² / [1 + √(1 − (1 + K)·c²·y²)]. The printed A–D coefficients multiply y⁴, y⁶, y⁸ and y¹⁰ respectively, without scaling.

| Surface |          A4 |          A6 |           A8 |          A10 |
| ------- | ----------: | ----------: | -----------: | -----------: |
| S3      |  2.82208e−5 | −1.70632e−7 |  7.83184e−10 | −2.30535e−12 |
| S4      | −3.54777e−5 | −2.90803e−7 |  4.57762e−11 | −6.09647e−12 |
| S19     |  6.71962e−5 |  3.58213e−7 | −3.76745e−11 |  1.81612e−11 |

The opposite signs of the leading terms on L2 provide different peripheral departures on its two faces. The final rear asphere adds image-side correction. The coefficients alone do not establish a manufacturing process.

## Apertures, Field and Model Limits

The clear radii are inferred. Selected undersized rims have been widened within Figure 9's measured bounds, retaining the existing smaller front-group caps elsewhere. S1 remains below the approximately 18.1 mm drawing reading; the G3 radii remain below approximately 9.1 / 8.7 mm. Rear radii are bounded by approximately 5.9 mm at L6, 5.7 mm at L7 and L8, 6.8 mm at L9's rear and 7.4 mm at L10. The whole pupil is not transmitted at every field, and the drawing does not establish all physical clear apertures.

The retained 28 mm image circle is an estimate. At the printed middle and tele half-fields of 39.5° and 32°, real chief rays reach about 14.35 and 14.38 mm, suggesting approximately 28.7 mm diameter. The modeled 14 mm corner is reached at every zoom station. At wide and tele the corresponding half-fields are 50.9538° and 31.3106°, with calculated geometric pupil transmission of 48.82% and 99.44%. These are properties of the adopted apertures, without coatings, sensor cover glass or production baffles; they are not measured retail illumination.

Remaining work concerns figure-to-rim agreement, the 28 versus approximately 28.7 mm field extent, and a continuous finite-focus motion. Prescription fidelity and the product correlation do not establish measured product MTF.
