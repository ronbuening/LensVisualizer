# CANON NEW FD 200mm f/4

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 4,240,703, _Telephoto Lens_\
**Inventors:** Kikuo Momiyama\
**Assignee:** Canon Inc. (printed as Canon Kabushiki Kaisha)\
**Priority:** June 28, 1977, Japan\
**Filed:** June 23, 1978\
**Published:** December 23, 1980\
**Example details:** Example I, Figure 1, prescription in columns 3–4 and member focal lengths in column 5.

Example I is a strong match for the New FD 200mm f/4 that Canon introduced in June 1979. Canon lists seven elements in six groups and describes rear-group focusing. Figure 1 and the retail cutaway both show a broad air-spaced front triplet, a forward-convex negative meniscus, a small cemented focusing doublet and a fixed rear meniscus. Example II separates the elements of the focusing member with air. Example III has a second cemented pair at the rear. Neither has the construction of the retail lens. The patent gives no product name, and the cutaway does not establish the production radii. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/nfd224.html) · [Canon FD Lenses](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/03873/03873.pdf) · [Retail cutaway](https://mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/canon/fdresources/fdlenses/200mmf4optic.jpg)

## Optical Architecture

The patent divides the optics into four members. Member I collects the beam. Member II diverges it just before the diaphragm. Member III, the negative doublet, moves toward the film to focus closer. Member IV stays fixed so its weak positive power can help correct residual spherical aberration and distortion as the doublet moves. All 13 refracting surfaces are spherical. Neither the patent nor Canon's product description identifies an ED element.

The patent normalizes its lengths to a focal length of 1 mm. Multiplying all lengths by 200 puts the prescription at the marketed scale. The table then gives an effective focal length of 200.89 mm at infinity, 0.45% longer than the printed 200 mm. The first vertex is 164.00 mm from the film, including 61.156 mm of calculated back focus. The patent gives f/4 and a 12.3° full field. Canon specifies f/4 to f/32, eight blades and a 12° diagonal field for the 35 mm FD lens. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/nfd224.html) · [Canon FD Lens Guide](https://www.galimbertipaolo.it/CanonFD/FDLensGuide1.pdf)

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Focal lengths below are calculated for the individual elements in air. The named glasses are catalog equivalents.

### L1 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.10. Ohara S-FSL5 equivalent. f = +85.16 mm. This is the stronger of the two positive crowns in member I. Its broad front surface admits the f/4 axial bundle.

### L2 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.72342, νd = 38.00. Sumita BASF8 equivalent. f = −95.22 mm. This more dispersive negative element sits between the crowns. Its power opposes their longitudinal chromatic aberration.

### L3 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.10. Ohara S-FSL5 equivalent. f = +121.89 mm. This crown completes the positive front member. Most of its bending is at the front surface, with a rear radius of 6,773 mm.

### L4 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.58913, νd = 61.10. Ohara S-BAL35 equivalent. f = −288.83 mm. Both surfaces curve toward the object. The stronger rear curvature makes this fixed singlet negative. The patent associates its power and its spacing from member I with control of spherical aberration.

### L5 and L6 — Cemented Focusing Doublet

- **L5, biconvex positive.** nd = 1.75520, νd = 27.50. Ohara S-TIH4 equivalent. f = +103.93 mm. This element supplies positive power at the front of the doublet. Its rear surface is the nearly flat cemented interface R10.
- **L6, biconcave negative.** nd = 1.66755, νd = 41.90. Hikari J-BASF6 equivalent. f = −59.50 mm. Its rear curvature gives the assembled doublet a net focal length of −145.94 mm. The two elements move together as member III.

### L7 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.75520, νd = 27.50. Ohara S-TIH4 equivalent. f = +936.58 mm. The front and rear radii are close, +42.32 and +43.86 mm, leaving weak positive power. This is member IV, the fixed rear corrector. Its calculated focal length differs substantially from the +687.52 mm in the patent's condition table.

## Glass Identification

The two positive front crowns share the highest Abbe number in the lens. The negative element between them has stronger dispersion. In the cemented focusing pair, the positive glass is more dispersive than its negative partner. The patent gives the refractive indices and Abbe numbers for all seven elements, but leaves their trade names and suppliers unspecified.

Column 3 calls the reference wavelength the “spectral d line of sodium.” Lowercase d conventionally denotes helium. All five distinct refractive indices agree with helium d-line catalog values to the printed precision, so the model interprets the indices as helium d. The catalog glasses estimate dispersion at other wavelengths. The production melts remain unidentified.

## Focus Mechanism

The focus table gives the air space D8 ahead of member III and D11 behind it. At the nearer position the doublet has moved 18.68 mm toward the film. The sum of the air spaces is 57.42 mm throughout its travel.

| Focus position |       D8 |      D11 | Patent focal length | Calculated focal length |
| -------------- | -------: | -------: | ------------------: | ----------------------: |
| Infinity       | 26.40 mm | 31.02 mm |           200.00 mm |               200.89 mm |
| 9.13f          | 45.08 mm | 12.34 mm |           168.78 mm |               169.44 mm |

The near distance of 9.13f corresponds to 1.826 m from object to film. At that distance the printed spacings put the paraxial image 0.0206 mm behind the film. With the film fixed, the lens focuses at 1.82737 m with signed magnification −0.1225×. The model ends at this printed position. Canon's production lens focuses to 1.5 m and reaches 0.15×. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/nfd224.html)

Between the two printed positions, nine doublet positions are calculated at equal steps in reciprocal object-to-film distance, using the focused near distance. They hold the paraxial image within 0.019 mm of the film between positions. The patent does not describe the focusing cam profile.

## Aperture and Field

Figure 1 places the iris about 46.4 mm behind the first vertex, near member II. Its calculated paraxial radius at f/4 is 15.86 mm. The clear radii of the glass follow the upper and lower optical edges in that figure. At infinity the chief ray reaches the 21.65 mm format corner at a half-field of 6.056°. Some oblique marginal rays meet the rims, giving a calculated corner illumination of 72.1% relative to the center. At the patent's 6.15° half-field the chief ray reaches 22.00 mm. The patent supplies no measured illumination or image height.
