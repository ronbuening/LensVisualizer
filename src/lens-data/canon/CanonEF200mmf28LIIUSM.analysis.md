# CANON EF 200mm f/2.8 L II USM

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP H05-027163 A, *Telephoto lens* (望遠レンズ)\
**Application number:** JP H03-202396\
**Filed:** 1991-07-17\
**Published:** 1993-02-05\
**Inventors:** Tsunefumi Tanaka\
**Assignee:** Canon Inc.\
**Example details:** Numerical Example 2 (数値実施例 2), p. 4. Figure 2 shows the section. Figure 5 gives the aberrations at infinity and at −0.15×.

The two blue elements in Canon's Mark II section distinguish this example from the other two in the patent. They are the second and fourth elements, both positive menisci. Example 2 alone puts the glass with index 1.49700 and Abbe number 81.6 in those positions. Examples 1 and 3 put it in the first and second elements. Example 3 also separates the two focusing elements with a 2.00 mm air gap, giving nine elements in eight groups. Example 2 has the five separate front elements and two cemented rear pairs shown by Canon. These details strongly identify the design, although Canon does not publish the production radii. [Patent, Figures 1–3](https://patents.google.com/patent/JPH0527163A/ja) · [Canon optical section](https://global.canon/ja/c-museum/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/ef333-lens-construction.gif)

The patent describes a telephoto of about 200 mm and f/2.8 for 35 mm film, suited for example to autofocus cameras (¶0001). Canon filed it five months before the original EF 200mm f/2.8L USM went on sale in December 1991. The March 1996 Mark II has the same published optical specification: 200 mm f/2.8, nine elements in seven groups, two UD elements, an eight-blade diaphragm closing to f/32, a 12° diagonal angle of view, and 0.16× maximum magnification at 1.5 m. Canon's documents show the versions differing in hood and weight. The original has a built-in pull-out hood and weighs 790 g; the Mark II comes with a detachable ET-83B II hood and weighs 765 g. These sources do not say whether the optical design changed. Both versions focus with an ultrasonic motor that allows full-time manual focusing; Canon specifies a ring-type motor for the Mark II. With Canon's Extender EF 1.4× III the Mark II becomes a 280 mm f/4 lens, and with the EF 2× III a 400 mm f/5.6, keeping autofocus with both. ## Optical Architecture

Tanaka divides the seven air-separated groups into three optical units. Five singlets ahead of the diaphragm form a positive front unit (+126.09 mm). The first doublet behind the diaphragm is the negative focusing unit (−77.84 mm). A fixed positive doublet (+130.35 mm) converges the beam onto the film. The patent labels these units L₁, L₂ and L₃ in Figure 2. Here G1–G3 denote the units and L1–L9 denote individual elements in order from the front.

All sixteen refracting surfaces are spherical. The table gives the radii, thicknesses, air spaces, glass indices and Abbe numbers in the infinity position. The dimensions are used in millimetres, without scaling to the marketed 200 mm. They give a focal length of 195.17 mm against the patent's 195.2 mm. The calculated distance from the front vertex to the image is 178.91 mm, shorter than the focal length as expected of a telephoto design.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are for individual thick lenses in air. The two cemented pairs have the combined focal lengths given above.

### L1 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.2, Ohara S-FSL5 equivalent, f = +149.56 mm. The front element collects the wide axial beam. Its front surface provides most of its power.

### L2 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6, Hoya FCD1 equivalent, f = +142.03 mm. The first UD meniscus sits 0.50 mm behind L1. Its steep front and shallow rear faces add convergence with little chromatic dispersion.

### L3 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.80610, νd = 41.0, Ohara S-LAH53 equivalent, f = −85.98 mm. This element separates the two UD menisci. Its front looks flat in Canon's drawing, but the patent gives it a shallow concave radius of −1971.05 mm. The rear surface does most of the diverging.

### L4 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6, Hoya FCD1 equivalent, f = +127.70 mm. L4 supplies the strongest positive power in G1. The patent pairs its shape with that of L5 to balance the change in aberrations as focusing alters the ray angles (¶0015 and ¶0021–0022).

### L5 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.58267, νd = 46.4, Ohara S-BAM3 equivalent, f = −241.31 mm. A 0.23 mm axial air space separates L4 and L5. The patent places this negative meniscus toward the front so that the axial beam enters the focusing unit high, which corrects the change in aberrations during focusing more effectively (¶0015).

### L6 and L7 — Focusing doublet C1

- **L6, positive meniscus.** nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4, Ohara S-TIH6 equivalent, f = +85.26 mm. Its stronger convex rear face is cemented to L7 at surface 13.
- **L7, biconcave negative.** nd = 1.63930, νd = 44.9, Ohara S-BAM12 equivalent, f = −40.69 mm. Its negative power dominates C1. Moving just this pair changes focus while keeping the large front elements stationary.

### L8 and L9 — Rear doublet C2

- **L8, negative meniscus.** nd = 1.68893, νd = 31.1, Ohara S-TIM28 equivalent, f = −122.41 mm. This thin, more dispersive member shares surface 16 with L9.
- **L9, biconvex positive.** nd = 1.78590, νd = 44.2, Ohara S-LAH51 equivalent, f = +62.97 mm. It provides the net positive power of the fixed rear pair. Its image-side surface is only weakly curved (−642.68 mm).

## Glass Identification and Selection

The table identifies glasses by index and Abbe number. Its aberration legend identifies the d and g lines. Reading the pairs at the d line gives the catalog equivalents below. The largest index difference is 0.000004, and the largest Abbe-number difference is 0.074. Canon names the two UD elements, while the suppliers of the production glasses remain unknown.

| Elements | Catalog equivalent | Optical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | Ohara S-FSL5 | Low-dispersion front collector |
| L2, L4 | Hoya FCD1 | Two UD menisci in the front unit |
| L3 | Ohara S-LAH53 | High-index negative element between the UD menisci |
| L5 | Ohara S-BAM3 | Negative power at the rear of the front unit |
| L6, L7 | Ohara S-TIH6 and S-BAM12 | More dispersive positive glass paired with less dispersive negative glass |
| L8, L9 | Ohara S-TIM28 and S-LAH51 | More dispersive negative glass paired with less dispersive positive glass |

For L2 and L4, Ohara S-FPL51 also fits the patent's printed values. Hoya FCD1 is closer at the d line (1.49700, 81.61). With FCD1 the paraxial g-line focus falls 0.073 mm behind the d-line focus, consistent with the roughly 0.07 mm separation at the foot of Figure 5A. The drawing cannot distinguish suppliers at that precision.

## Focus Mechanism

To focus closer, G2 moves toward the image while G1, the diaphragm and G3 stay fixed (¶0010–0012). Its strong negative power keeps the travel short for fast autofocus (¶0014). At infinity C1 is 3.00 mm behind the stop and 29.00 mm in front of C2. As it moves, the first gap opens and the second closes by the same amount. Canon describes the production lens as rear focusing. [Canon Mark II specifications](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/ef333.html)

The model shows infinity, the only position with published spacings. Figure 5B shows performance at −0.15× but supplies neither an object distance nor the doublet position. Canon's 1.5 m and 0.16× describe the production lens at its closest setting. A paraxial calculation that moves only C1 reaches −0.15× after 14.10 mm of travel, with the object 1.56 m from the image, and 1.5 m from the image after 14.82 mm, at 0.157×. Measured from the front vertex instead, 1.5 m would give 0.137×, so Canon's two figures fit a distance taken from the film plane. These positions are calculated, not printed, and the model stays at infinity.

## Aperture and Field

The table locates the diaphragm 23.40 mm behind surface 10. The patent prints neither its opening nor the image distance; both are calculated. The paraxial iris radius is 16.92 mm at f/2.8, and the infinity image lies 69.852 mm behind surface 17. The clear radii are measured from the glass edges in Figure 2. Six surfaces in the front unit need openings up to 0.86 mm larger than their drawing measurements to pass the full axial marginal ray, and each of the five front elements keeps the square edge the figure draws, at the larger of its two face radii. Figure 2 ends the concave rear faces of L3, L5 and L7 short of the element edge, with a flat land out to the rim. The model draws these lands at 27.6, 21.5 and 15.6 mm from the axis, just outside the f/2.8 beams; they block no traced ray.

The printed 12.6° diagonal field reaches the full 36 × 24 mm frame. Canon gives 12° for the 200 mm product. At 6.3° the front and rear rims vignette the beam, with 53.3% of the pupil area transmitting in the geometric calculation. Canon does not give a measured illumination value in its product description.
