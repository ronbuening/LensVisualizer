# CANON FD 300mm f/5.6 S.C.

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP S47-8749 B, _Short, high-performance telephoto lens_ (全長の短い高写力望遠レンズ)\
**Inventors:** Michiyoshi Suwa (諏訪道美)\
**Assignee:** Canon Inc.\
**Filed:** 1 October 1968, application Shō 43-71246\
**Published:** 13 March 1972\
**Example details:** Example 3 (実施例 3), numerical table on page 5, section in Figure 5 and aberration plots in Figure 10.

Example 3 is the probable design of the FD 300mm f/5.6 S.C., introduced in March 1973. The patent names ordinary optical glasses for all six elements and gives a first-vertex-to-image length of 0.7198 times the nominal focal length. Canon describes the S.C. as an ordinary-glass telephoto with a ratio of 0.72. The patent's 8.2° field also agrees closely with Canon's 8°15′ for the 36 × 24 mm frame. [Canon Camera Museum, 1973 S.C.](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd179.html) · [Canon lens catalog, p. 32](https://www.360icon.com/PDF/CanonLenses.pdf) · [Canon FD lens specifications](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00354/00354.pdf)

Canon lists six elements in five groups. Its cutaway shows two air-spaced rear singlets, as in Figure 5. In the front assembly the cement line between L3 and L4 is indistinct, which leaves the exact prescription uncertain. [Canon lens catalog, p. 32](https://www.360icon.com/PDF/CanonLenses.pdf) · [Canon Camera Museum, 1973 S.C.](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd179.html)

The specifications here apply to the 1973 Spectra Coating (S.C.) lens. The March 1971 FD version shares its construction count, 173 mm barrel length and 4 m minimum focus. The March 1977 Super Spectra Coating (S.S.C.) version has rear focusing, a longer, lighter barrel and a 3 m minimum. The June 1979 New FD also focuses to 3 m and closes to f/32. The S.C. closes to f/22. [Canon system brochure, coating designations](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00354/00354.pdf) · [1971 FD](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd178.html) · [1973 S.C.](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd179.html) · [1977 S.S.C.](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd180.html) · [1979 New FD](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/nfd229.html)

Of the other examples in JP S47-8749 B, Example 1 covers a wider 12.4° field. Example 2 uses two fluorite elements, a feature Canon identifies in its FL-F 300mm f/5.6. Example 4 has six separate groups. Example 5 also has six elements in five groups, but L3 and L4 are separated by air and the rear pair is cemented. That rear construction differs from Canon's S.C. cutaway. [Canon Camera Museum, FL-F 300mm f/5.6](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fl117.html) · [Canon lens catalog, p. 32](https://www.360icon.com/PDF/CanonLenses.pdf)

## Optical Architecture

A positive front group and a negative rear group give this lens a 300.47 mm effective focal length in just 216.24 mm from the first vertex to the image. All dimensions use one ×3 scale from the patent's nominal focal length of 100 to Canon's 300 mm. The aperture is f/5.6 in both.

The front group consists of two positive singlets followed by a negative cemented doublet. Its focal length is +204.84 mm. An 87.441 mm air space contains the diaphragm and separates the doublet from the rear group. The rear negative meniscus and positive singlet together have a focal length of −296.03 mm. All eleven refracting surfaces are spherical.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are for the individual elements in air. L3 and L4 have one common glass-to-glass surface in the assembled lens.

### L1 — Biconvex positive

FK5, nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.0, f = +122.13 mm. The front face supplies most of the element's converging power. Its 62.778 mm radius is much steeper than the nearly flat rear face.

### L2 — Positive meniscus

FK5, nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.0, f = +174.37 mm. Only 0.516 mm of air separates L2 from L1 on the axis. This second low-dispersion positive lens shares the front group's power, with its more strongly convex surface facing the object.

### L3 — Biconcave negative

LaSFo1, nd = 1.78590, νd = 44.0, f = −49.92 mm. This high-index element turns part of the front singlets' convergence back at the cemented interface. Together, L3 and L4 form a negative doublet of −78.99 mm focal length.

### L4 — Positive meniscus

FK1, nd = 1.47069, νd = 67.2, f = +128.66 mm. Its convex face fits the concave rear face of L3 at surface 6. It supplies the doublet's lower-dispersion positive component.

### L5 — Negative meniscus

LaK8, nd = 1.71300, νd = 53.9, f = −57.72 mm. Its strongly concave face looks toward the diaphragm. This is the stronger of the two rear elements and gives the rear group its negative power.

### L6 — Biconvex positive

F16, nd = 1.59270, νd = 35.4, f = +87.30 mm. A 5.643 mm air gap separates it from L5. Its image-side face supplies most of its positive power and partly offsets L5.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent names all six glasses and prints N and V without a spectral subscript. Their catalog values support reading them at the helium d line. L3, L5 and L6 use the dispersion curves of Hoya NBFD11, LAC8 and FF5 respectively. Each index agrees to all five printed decimal places, and each Abbe number differs by 0.07 or less. The original glass supplier and melt are unknown.

The FK5 catalog glass has an Abbe number of 70.4, compared with 70.0 for both front singlets in the patent. A corresponding catalog curve for the printed FK1 values is also unavailable. Dispersion for L1, L2 and L4 therefore follows the printed index and Abbe number. The patent supplies no partial dispersions.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent gives one prescription at infinity, without finite spacings or close-focus magnification. All six elements stay at their infinity positions in the model. The production S.C. focuses to 4 m from the film plane and reaches 0.09×. Canon provided extra focusing travel to compensate for temperature changes down to −30 °C. Its cutaway leaves the motion of the individual groups uncertain. [Canon Camera Museum, 1973 S.C.](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd179.html) · [Canon FD instructions, “Distance Scale”](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/03486/03486.pdf) · [Canon lens catalog, p. 32](https://www.360icon.com/PDF/CanonLenses.pdf)

## Image Plane, Aperture and Field

The distance from the first glass vertex to the last is 142.800 mm. Subtracting this from the patent's scaled total length of 215.952 mm gives 73.152 mm behind the last vertex. The image plane lies at the calculated paraxial focus, 73.438 mm behind the last vertex and 0.286 mm beyond the plane implied by the printed length.

Figure 5 places the diaphragm about 44% of the way across the air space from surface 7 to surface 8. At this position, f/5.6 gives a paraxial stop radius of 13.12 mm. Canon specifies an eight-blade diaphragm closing to f/22. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd179.html)

The glass rims follow the outlines in Figure 5. The 26.9 mm front radius just clears the 26.83 mm axial marginal ray. At a half-field of 4.1°, the chief ray lands 21.61 mm off axis, close to the 21.63 mm corner of a 36 × 24 mm frame. The corner chief ray clears all eleven surfaces, although the front and rear rims cut some of the oblique bundle. About 53% of the pupil area transmits at the corner with these inferred rims. Canon gives no measured corner transmission here.
