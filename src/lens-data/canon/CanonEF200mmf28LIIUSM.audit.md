# CANON EF 200mm f/2.8 L II USM — audit

## Source

Tsunefumi Tanaka, *Telephoto lens* (望遠レンズ), Canon Inc. Application JP H03-202396, filed 17 July 1991, published 5 February 1993. Numerical Example 2 on p. 4 gives the infinity prescription. Figure 2 is its optical section, Table 1 gives the condition values, and Figures 5A and 5B show infinity and −0.15×. [JP H05-027163 A](https://patents.google.com/patent/JPH0527163A/ja)

## Transcription

All sixteen radii, with their signs, sixteen axial intervals and nine glass index/Abbe-number pairs agree with p. 4 at the printed scale in millimetres. Surface 11 is the diaphragm. C1 spans surfaces 12–14 and C2 spans 15–17. Surface 5 has a finite radius of −1971.05 mm. The table contains no aspheres, camera-side plate, back focus or finite-focus spacings. The d- and g-line legend supports the d-line interpretation of the indices.

At six surfaces the f/2.8 axial marginal ray falls outside the drawn glass. Those radii are the ray heights rounded upward to 0.01 mm.

The stop position is printed. Its radius of 16.92 mm follows from paraxial pupil magnification at f/2.8. The real axial ray that just reaches this iris enters at a height of 34.00 mm, giving f/2.87 from its entrance beam diameter. A ray entering at the nominal pupil radius of 34.851 mm reaches the stop at 17.36 mm. Figure 2 indicates about 17.4 mm. The glass rims clear this latter ray as well. The image-side gap is the calculated paraxial back focus. The only lens position is infinity.

The surface geometry clears at every rim. The corner chief ray reaches an image height of 21.633 mm at 6.26°. At 6.3° the calculated pupil transmission is 53.3%.

The patent gives no trade names or wavelength-specific indices. Eight distinct catalog equivalents cover the nine printed glass pairs. The largest departures are 0.000004 in index (L9) and 0.074 in Abbe number (L3). The UD identification at L2 and L4 follows Canon's marked section and their printed Abbe number of 81.6.

## Product Identification

Canon shows five singlets, a diaphragm and two cemented doublets, with the UD menisci at elements 2 and 4. Only Example 2 combines those positions with the cemented negative focusing pair and fixed positive rear pair. Examples 1 and 3 use the same low-dispersion glass at elements 1 and 2. Figure 3 and its 2.00 mm gap show that Example 3 separates its focusing pair, giving nine elements in eight air-separated groups. The layout strongly identifies Example 2 with the Mark II. [Canon optical section](https://global.canon/ja/c-museum/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/ef333-lens-construction.gif)

Canon's original lens of December 1991 and the Mark II of March 1996 share the 200 mm f/2.8 specification, nine elements in seven groups, two UD elements and 1.5 m closest focus at 0.16×. ## Corrections

### 2026-11-25

The infinity prescription reproduces the published table. The printed 195.2 mm design focal length accounts for the difference from the 200 mm product marking. The front clear radii above Figure 2 are the minimum openings for the nominal f/2.8 axial ray. Canon's 0.16× maximum magnification describes a different condition from the patent's −0.15× aberration plot.

## Remaining Questions

Canon's optical section does not dimension the clear apertures or iris. Exact production radii, glass suppliers and the travel to closest focus remain undocumented in these sources. The difference between the calculated corner transmission and that of a production lens is unknown.
