# CANON EF 24-85mm f/3.5-4.5 USM

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP H09-304697 A, _Zoom Lens_ (ズームレンズ)\
**Inventors:** Makoto Misaka (三坂 誠)\
**Assignee:** Canon Inc.\
**Filed:** 13 May 1996, Japanese application H08-142230\
**Published:** 28 November 1997\
**Example details:** Numerical Example 1 (数値実施例１), prescription on pp. 4–5, sections in Figure 1 and aberration curves in Figures 5–7.

The strongest evidence for the identification is the fourth element from the front. Canon marks it green in the production section. It occupies the same position as the hybrid asphere at the front of the patent's negative second group. Three glass doublets, the two openings on either side of the third group, the closely spaced fourth-group pair and the nearly flat front face of the fifth group also align. Both designs have 15 glass elements in 12 air-separated components. Canon identifies one aspherical element and marks no UD element. The May filing preceded the lens's September 1996 introduction by four months. [Canon construction diagram](https://global.canon/ja/c-museum/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/ef337-lens-construction.gif) · [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/ef337.html) The lens was finished in silver when sold with the EOS IX and in black when sold separately. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_EF_24-85mm_lens)

All four examples have much the same construction. Example 1 appears on the patent's front page, has the fastest wide aperture (f/3.58), and is the only example with a separate 0.05 mm layer on the asphere. Canon's section cannot resolve that layer or distinguish Examples 2 and 3 by outline alone. Example 4 has a more strongly curved front face on the rear group and moves G4 during zooming. Example 1 is a strong, non-unique match to the production lens. [Patent, Figures 1–4 and Numerical Examples 1–4](https://patents.google.com/patent/JPH09304697A/en)

## Optical Architecture

The lens has five groups, which the patent labels L1–L5; here they are called G1–G5, and the elements are numbered L1–L15. From front to rear the group focal lengths in air are +86.49, −15.60, +22.08, −31.35 and +35.24 mm. Toward telephoto, G1, G3 and G5 advance toward the subject, G2 moves toward the image, and G4 stays fixed. The main diaphragm (SP) travels with G3, 0.20 mm ahead of its first glass surface. The second stop (MP) moves independently between G3 and G4. The three variable air spaces between G3 and G5 total 11.46 mm at all three printed positions, so the two groups advance together. Canon describes the production lens as a multi-group zoom. Canon specifies 24–85 mm and f/3.5–4.5 for the production lens. The example is 3.5% longer than 24 mm at the wide end and 3.5% shorter than 85 mm at the long end, and its f/4.68 at 82.06 mm is slightly slower than the marked f/4.5. The example prints a diagonal field of 82.1°–29.5°; Imaging Resource lists 84°–28.5° for the product. Canon's six-bladed diaphragm closes to f/22 at wide and f/32 at tele. Figures 5–7 give the patent apertures, and the main diaphragm radius is calculated from them at each zoom position. The separate opening of 6.6 mm radius read from Figure 1 clips the axial beam around 50 mm. A chief ray still reaches the 35 mm frame corner at every zoom position. At wide, barrel distortion puts the corner at a 42.9° half-field, compared with the 41.1° half-field marked on Figure 5. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/ef337.html) · [Imaging Resource specifications](https://imaging-resource.com/lenses/canon/ef-24-85mm-f3.5-4.5-usm/specifications) · [Patent, Figures 1 and 5–7](https://patents.google.com/patent/JPH09304697A/en)

The patent tabulates five air spaces at three infinity positions. It gives neither the final air space nor the continuous zoom motion. The back focal distance at each printed position follows from the prescription. At twelve intermediate positions, the spacing from G2 to SP is calculated for infinity focus on the fixed image plane. The other gaps and the maximum aperture vary linearly between adjacent printed positions. The largest paraxial focus error between the resulting positions is 0.0175 mm. The patent prints no clear radii: they are scaled from Figure 1, and six are held slightly inside the drawing where neighbouring surfaces would otherwise meet.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Focal lengths below are for each element in air. The glass names identify Ohara catalog equivalents. The separate resin entry describes the optical surface of L4 and shares its element number.

### L1 and L2 — front cemented menisci

- **L1, negative meniscus.** nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.8, S-TIH53, f = −126.96 mm. The high-dispersion flint is the negative partner of the front doublet.
- **L2, positive meniscus.** nd = 1.69679, νd = 55.5, S-LAL14, f = +94.79 mm. Its lower dispersion and opposite power give the pair a weak positive focal length of +389.26 mm.

### L3 — positive meniscus

nd = 1.69679, νd = 55.5, S-LAL14, f = +109.36 mm. This air-spaced element supplies most of G1's convergence, leaving the front pair free to balance dispersion.

### L4 — hybrid negative meniscus

The aspherical layer has nd = 1.51640, νd = 52.2 and f = −89,886.92 mm in air. Its material is unidentified. The glass substrate has nd = 1.80400, νd = 46.6, S-LAH65 and f = −18.72 mm. The rear face of the glass provides most of the negative power. The thin layer changes the shape encountered by off-axis rays with little effect on axial power.

### L5 and L6 — negative cemented doublet

- **L5, positive meniscus.** nd = 1.80517, νd = 25.4, S-TIH6, f = +47.90 mm. The more dispersive member supplies the positive power of the pair.
- **L6, biconcave negative.** nd = 1.88299, νd = 40.8, S-LAH58, f = −16.69 mm. Its stronger divergence gives the doublet a net focal length of −25.26 mm in G2.

### L7 — positive meniscus

nd = 1.84665, νd = 23.9, S-TIH53, f = +32.68 mm. L7 partly offsets the preceding negative components and bends the beam toward the diaphragm.

### L8 and L9 — positive cemented doublet

- **L8, negative meniscus.** nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.8, S-TIH53, f = −49.26 mm. This flint sits immediately behind SP.
- **L9, biconvex positive.** nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.2, S-FSL5, f = +26.84 mm. The low-dispersion crown gives the pair positive power while balancing the color contribution of L8. Its combined focal length with L8 is +59.87 mm.

### L10 — biconvex positive

nd = 1.71299, νd = 53.8, S-LAL8, f = +33.32 mm. L10 adds convergence behind the G3 doublet. The patent attributes spherical-aberration correction across the zoom to this group arrangement (¶0031).

### L11 — positive meniscus

nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.8, S-TIH53, f = +28.92 mm. The strongly curved image-side face sits across a 0.22 mm axial air gap from L12. The patent describes contact at the margin (¶0025).

### L12 — biconcave negative

nd = 1.80609, νd = 41.0, S-LAH53, f = −15.23 mm. Its stronger power makes the stationary G4 negative. The patent associates this pair with wide-end coma and tele-end spherical-aberration correction (¶0025).

### L13 — biconvex positive

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.2, S-FSL5, f = +34.20 mm. The front face of G5 has a radius of +291.11 mm and appears nearly flat in section. Most of the bending occurs at the rear face of this low-dispersion element.

### L14 — biconvex positive

nd = 1.75699, νd = 47.8, S-LAM54, f = +36.55 mm. L14 shares the convergence of the rear group with L13, using glass of higher index and dispersion.

### L15 — negative meniscus

nd = 1.84665, νd = 23.9, S-TIH53, f = −29.52 mm. The rear flint partially offsets the two positive members of G5. The patent describes this positive-positive-negative sequence as balancing aberrations through the zoom (¶0032).

## Glass Identification

The patent gives an index and an Abbe number for each of the sixteen refracting media. Nine Ohara glasses match the fifteen glass elements within 0.000011 in nd and 0.122 in νd. The greatest Abbe-number difference occurs for L7 and L15, whose printed νd is 23.9. These matches supply dispersion curves. The original glass suppliers and resin formulation remain unknown. ## Focus Mechanism

Claim 3 and ¶0029 assign focusing to G2, chosen because the group is small, light and of low lateral magnification. Its four glass elements move together along the axis. Canon drives focus with a ring USM, allows full-time manual focus and keeps the filter ring from rotating; Imaging Resource lists the lens as inner focusing. Canon gives a closest distance of 0.5 m and a maximum magnification of 0.16×. Canon measures minimum focusing distance from the focal plane, so the 0.5 m runs from the image plane to the subject. The patent gives no finite-distance gap or reproduction ratio. The model therefore stays focused at infinity throughout the zoom and does not reach Canon's closest distance. ## Aspherical Surface

Surface 6A is the object-side face of L4. Canon calls the element a replica aspherical lens, its term for an ultraviolet-cured resin layer that forms the aspherical surface on a spherical glass lens; the 0.05 mm layer with its own index fits that construction. The sag equation in ¶0034 starts with a sphere of radius +72.48 mm (K = 0). Its four even-power coefficients, with heights and sag in millimetres, are A4 = +6.122 × 10⁻⁷, A6 = +2.798 × 10⁻¹⁰, A8 = +5.866 × 10⁻¹¹ and A10 = −6.974 × 10⁻¹³. Paragraph 0027 places the asphere on this surface to correct negative distortion at the wide end. Canon highlights the same element in its production section.
