# CANON NEW FD 35mm f/2.8 — audit

## Source

US 4,257,678, _Wide Angle Photographic Lens_, by Kikuo Momiyama and Hideo Yokota, assigned to Canon Inc. (Canon Kabushiki Kaisha). Japanese priority September 14, 1977, US filing September 11, 1978, grant March 24, 1981. Figure 1 shows the section. Example 1 in column 4 (PDF page 7) gives the prescription. Figures 2A–2D show its aberrations. [US 4,257,678](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4257678A/en)

## Transcription

All eleven radii, with their signs, ten axial separations and the back focus agree with the table at a uniform scale of 35 mm per unit of its normalized focal length. All six d-line indices and Abbe numbers agree at the printed precision. R4 occurs once, at the cemented junction of L2 and L3. D5 includes the diaphragm. All surfaces are spherical, and the table gives one infinity spacing for each separation.

At the nominal focal length the front element has f₁/f = −1.76431, its curvature ratio is 0.26298 and the mean index of the positive elements is 1.73103. All three agree with the printed values when rounded. The normalized cemented-surface power from the printed entries is 0.41287, within 0.00008 of the listed 0.4128.

The printed image plane lies 0.00344 mm ahead of paraxial focus. At full aperture the diaphragm limits the axial beam, and the real axial marginal rays give f/2.88 from their entrance diameter. The corner chief ray reaches 21.65 mm with clearance at every face. Tracing across the pupil gives about 56% geometric transmission and about 39% relative illumination at the corner.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

- **Diaphragm.** The section places the stop in D5. An estimated axial position of 24.50 mm from R1 divides the 6.4995 mm space into 1.3230 and 5.1765 mm. An entrance pupil radius of 6.251 mm at paraxial f/2.8 corresponds to a physical stop radius of 7.030 mm.
- **Clear radii.** In order from R1 to R11 the semi-diameters are 12.90, 10.80, 8.70, 8.70, 8.00, 8.45, 7.02, 7.04, 8.45, 9.60 and 9.60 mm. Nine come from measuring both limbs of Figure 1 against the known 37.261 mm vertex span, ending each measurement at the curved face. Reading uncertainty is about 0.1–0.2 mm.
- **R7 and R8.** The drawing places both outer shoulders near 8.45 mm. The spherical faces would meet at a radius of 7.3605 mm and overlap by 0.2514 mm at those shoulders. The estimated clear radii of 7.02 and 7.04 mm accommodate the axial beam and leave 0.0714 mm of air at the common radius of 7.02 mm. The space at the vertices is 0.7875 mm.
- **Glass.** The present-day equivalents are Sumita K-BK7 (L1), Ohara S-LAH53 (L2), Schott F5 (L3), Ohara S-TIH13 (L4) and Hoya LAC13 (L5 and L6). Their catalog indices differ from the printed indices by at most 0.000002, and the Abbe numbers by at most 0.04. The production glass suppliers are unknown.

## Product Identification

Canon's June 1979 New FD 35mm f/2.8 uses six elements in five groups, a five-blade diaphragm closing to f/22, and a 63° diagonal field on 35 mm film. Canon gives a 0.35 m minimum focusing distance and 0.13× maximum magnification. MIR lists helicoid focusing. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/nfd205.html) · [FD Lens Guide, SG2-A13](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/01152/01152.pdf#page=13) · [MIR, New FD 35mm f/2.8](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/canon/fdresources/fdlenses/35mm.htm)

The MIR cutaway agrees with Figure 1 from the front negative meniscus through the doublet ahead of the diaphragm to the biconcave, positive meniscus and biconvex rear elements. At a common drawing scale the eleven vertex positions give RMS deviations of 0.63, 1.75 and 1.87 pixels for Examples 1, 2 and 3 respectively. Example 2's second element is too thick, and Example 3's diaphragm gap too short, for the production drawing. The agreement strongly favors Example 1. ## Corrections

### 2026-11-25

The prescription reproduces the table as printed. Its rounded values give a focal length 0.0068 mm longer than the nominal 35 mm. The printed back focus sets the image plane. Canon's 63° diagonal specification and the patent's 64° design field refer to their respective lenses.

## Remaining Questions

The original publisher of the MIR cutaway and the factory glass types are unknown. Figure 1 draws the diaphragm about 0.9 mm behind R5, about 24.1 mm from R1, while the model uses the estimated 24.50 mm. At the drawing's precision the exact stop position remains uncertain, as do the iris opening and the bevel profiles at R7 and R8. Neither the patent nor the cutaway gives group travel at close focus or measured illumination at the field corner.
