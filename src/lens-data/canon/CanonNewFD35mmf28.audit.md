# CANON NEW FD 35mm f/2.8 — Audit Log

## Source

Kikuo Momiyama and Hideo Yokota, _Wide Angle Photographic Lens_, US 4,257,678, assigned to Canon Inc. (Canon Kabushiki Kaisha). Japanese priority September 14, 1977, US filing September 11, 1978, and grant March 24, 1981. Figure 1 shows Example 1 in section. Column 4 (PDF p. 7) gives the prescription at f = 1, and Figures 2A–2D give its aberrations. [US 4,257,678](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4257678A/en)

## Transcription Check

All eleven signed spherical radii, ten axial separations, six d-line index and Abbe-number pairs, and the back focus agree with the printed table. At 35 mm per unit, the first-to-last vertex distance is 37.261 mm. R4 is the single cemented surface between L2 and L3. The diaphragm lies within D5. The table gives one infinity spacing for each separation.

Column 1 describes the stronger convex faces of the last two groups as facing the object. The abstract, Figure 1 and the signed radii R8–R11 agree that both face the image. The model follows those radii. [Patent, abstract, Fig. 1 and Example 1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4257678A/en)

The printed back focus places the film 3.44 µm ahead of paraxial focus. The diaphragm limits the axial beam at f/2.8. The diameter of the real axial marginal-ray bundle gives f/2.88. The full-frame corner chief ray clears every face. At the corner, the inferred apertures pass about 56% of the pupil and give about 39% relative illumination.

Using the nominal 35 mm focal length, the table gives −1.76431 for f₁/f, 0.26298 for the front meniscus curvature ratio and 1.73103 for the mean positive-element index. All round to the patent values. The normalized power at R4 is 0.41287, within 0.00008 of the printed 0.4128.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

- **Diaphragm.** The estimated position, 24.50 mm behind R1, divides D5 into 1.3230 mm before the iris and 5.1765 mm after it. The paraxial f/2.8 entrance pupil has a radius of 6.251 mm, which gives a physical iris radius of 7.030 mm at that position.
- **Clear radii.** From R1 to R11 the radii are 12.90, 10.80, 8.70, 8.70, 8.00, 8.45, 7.02, 7.04, 8.45, 9.60 and 9.60 mm. Nine come from the curved faces in Figure 1. The 37.261 mm first-to-last vertex span sets the scale. The two limbs of each face can be read to about 0.1–0.2 mm.
- **R7 and R8.** The vertices are 0.7875 mm apart and the drawing places both shoulders near 8.45 mm radius. The spheres meet at 7.3605 mm and would overlap by 0.2514 mm at the shoulders. Clear radii of 7.02 and 7.04 mm pass the axial beam and leave 0.0714 mm of air at their common clear edge.
- **Focus and field.** `focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. The patent prints the 64° design field but gives no image height. The modeled corner height comes from the 24 × 36 mm film format.

## Product Identification

Canon specifies 35 mm, f/2.8, six elements in five groups, a five-blade diaphragm closing to f/22 and a 63° diagonal field for the New FD lens. The manual helicoid focuses to 0.35 m from the film plane, at 0.13× magnification. Example 1 gives the same aperture and a 64° field at infinity. Example 2's second element is too thick and Example 3's diaphragm gap too short for the drawing. The proportions strongly favor Example 1. ## Corrections, 2026-11-25

- The earlier model showed the central ±3° at f/2.8, with wider fields described at f/4 through 20° and f/8 through 32°. The 32° limit placed the chief ray at 21.41 mm. The field now extends to the 21.65 mm full-frame corner at the printed f/2.8.
- Figure 1 gives the optical edges for nine faces. The R1 radius decreased from 13.0 to 12.9 mm. R3/R4 increased from 8.0 to 8.7 mm, R6 from 7.7 to 8.45 mm, R9 from 7.6 to 8.45 mm and R10/R11 from 9.2 to 9.6 mm. R7/R8 retain the smaller clear radii above because extending the spherical faces to the drawn shoulders would make them intersect.
- All lengths retain the same 35 mm scale, including the printed back focus of 1.1033 times the nominal focal length. The rounded entries give an excess of 0.0068 mm in focal length. The original image plane and the sole printed focus state are preserved because the table supplies no finite spacings.
- The cutaway and the patent both place the cemented pair ahead of the diaphragm. The five optical groups are labeled G1–G5, and both members of G2 bear the doublet label C1. The New FD specifications establish the FD mount, 35 mm film format, marketed 35 mm focal length, f/2.8 aperture and 0.35 m closest focus. The five-blade iris has full stops at f/2.8, 4, 5.6, 8, 11, 16 and 22. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/nfd205.html) · [Canon FD Lens Guide, SG2-A13](https://www.galimbertipaolo.it/CanonFD/FDLensGuide1.pdf) · [Canon AE-1 instructions](https://flynngraphics.ca/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/AE-1-Manual.pdf)

## Remaining Questions

- The cutaway's original publisher and the factory glass types and suppliers are unknown.
- Figure 1 places the diaphragm about 0.9 mm behind R5, or about 24.1 mm from R1. The exact position, physical opening and blade curvature are undocumented, as are the bevel profiles at R7 and R8.
- The production group travel at close focus and the measured corner illumination remain unknown.
