# CANON FD 17mm f/4 S.S.C. — Audit Log

## Source

Akira Tajima and Kikuo Momiyama, _Reverse Telephoto Lens for Near-Distance Photography_, US 3,748,021, assigned to Canon Kabushiki Kaisha (Canon Inc.). The US application was filed April 5, 1971, with Japanese priority of April 11, 1970, and granted July 24, 1973. Embodiment 1 is in columns 3–4 (PDF p. 7). Figure 1 shows the lens section, Figures 2–4 the aberrations at infinity, and Figures 5–10 the effect of floating focus at one near distance. [US patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3748021A/en)

Canon’s FD lens instructions give the production lens’s 104° field, eleven elements in nine groups and Super Spectra Coating designation. Its period catalog shows the S.S.C. lens in section and describes its close-focus mechanism. The Camera Museum gives the specifications for the March 1973 lens. [Canon FD lens instructions](https://cameramanuals.org/lenses/canon_fd_lenses-lang.pdf) · [Canon lens catalog, p. 16](https://www.360icon.com/PDF/CanonLenses.pdf) · [Canon Camera Museum, FD 17mm f/4 S.S.C.](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd130.html)

## Transcription Check

The printed page accounts for all 20 spherical refracting radii, 19 axial intervals through R20, the infinity back focus, the eleven glass index and Abbe-number pairs, and both values of D8. R14 is the cemented interface between E7 and E8, and R17 joins E9 and E10. At 17 mm per focal-length unit the dimensions agree with the table apart from the missing minus sign on R12. The German publication specifies the d line for the indices. Figures 3–4 mark an image height of 1.235 focal lengths at 52°. [US patent, Embodiment 1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3748021A/en) · [German publication, Example 1](https://patents.google.com/patent/DE2117367A1/en)

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

- **Clear radii.** Scaling the drawing by the distance between the first and last vertices gives the following heights of the curved optical faces, in millimetres from R1 to R20. R1–R5 measure 20.9080, 16.2184, 17.7816, 12.7989 and 14.5575. R6–R10 measure 14.5575, 10.7471, 10.7471, 7.2299 and 5.2759. R11–R15 measure 5.0805, 5.0805, 5.0805, 6.2529 and 5.1782. R16–R20 measure 7.2299, 7.2299, 7.0833, 9.2816 and 9.2816. The fourth decimal place preserves the scale conversion. The drawing is less precise.
- **Iris.** Figure 1 puts the diaphragm between R12 and R13. The estimated midpoint divides the 2.1488 mm space into two 1.0744 mm intervals. A 3.878 mm iris radius gives the 4.250 mm entrance pupil diameter required at f/4.
- **Focus travel.** The printed near object distance and D8 determine the rear spacing by paraxial focus. From infinity, the rear vertex advances 1.3586 mm toward the subject and the front vertex advances 0.8061 mm. The model varies D8 and the rear spacing linearly between the printed settings.
- **Field clearance.** The axial marginal ray clears the glass at both settings. At the 21.633 mm frame corner the chief ray passes closest to R4 at infinity, with 0.257 mm to spare, and to R10 at the near setting, with 0.375 mm to spare. At infinity the estimated geometric pupil transmission is 18.0% at the patent image height and 13.3% at the frame corner.

## Product Identification

Embodiment 1 is a strong match for the FD 17mm f/4 S.S.C. The 104° field, f/4 aperture, eleven elements in nine groups and floating air space agree with Canon’s description. The cutaway shows the two front menisci, positive singlet ahead of the iris and compact rear section. Figure 1 supplies the individual cemented curvatures. The cutaway is too coarse to confirm them. The only other embodiment has an 84° field and nine elements in eight groups, matching Canon’s FD 24mm f/2.8. [Canon lens catalog, p. 16](https://www.360icon.com/PDF/CanonLenses.pdf) · [Canon FD lens instructions](https://cameramanuals.org/lenses/canon_fd_lenses-lang.pdf) · [US patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3748021A/en)

The numerical model takes its scale from the marketed 17 mm focal length. Canon’s March 1971 FD 17mm f/4 shares the S.S.C. lens’s 11/9 construction and 75 × 56 mm dimensions, suggesting the prescription also applies to that version. The product specifications here are for the 450 g March 1973 S.S.C. Canon lists the December 1979 New FD separately at 360 g and 76.5 × 56 mm. Its 11/9 count alone cannot establish its prescription. [1971 FD](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd129.html) · [1973 FD S.S.C.](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd130.html) · [1979 New FD](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/nfd197.html)

## Corrections, 2026-11-25

- The table uses f = 1. A uniform scale of ×17 replaces ×17.4 so one focal-length unit equals Canon’s marketed 17 mm. This sets the infinity film spacing to 2.0714 × 17 = 35.2138 mm.
- Column 3 prints R12 as +0.7380. That sign gives f = −4.60 and a virtual back focus of −19.41 in focal-length units. Restoring the minus sign gives R12 = −12.546 mm, f = 0.999995 and back focus = 2.07139 in the normalized prescription. These reproduce the printed 1 and 2.0714. Figure 1 also draws E6 biconvex, and Figures 2–4 describe a real image behind the lens. [US patent, Fig. 1 and columns 3–4](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3748021A/en)
- The former ±5° field limit and narrow rim estimates gave way to the patent’s full field at f/4. All 20 clear radii follow the measured drawing at the new scale. Its 1.235-unit image height gives a 41.99 mm image circle at ×17. The chief ray also reaches the 36 × 24 mm frame corner at 52.63°.
- The infinity-only prescription now includes the printed near setting. Closing D8 by 0.5525 mm and extending the rear spacing to 36.5724 mm puts the 293.08 mm conjugate in focus to within 0.0001 mm. Only the two endpoints have published D8 values. Canon’s S.S.C. lens focuses to 0.25 m from the film plane at 0.10×, beyond the patent’s 0.29308 m at 0.0799×. [Canon S.S.C. specifications](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd130.html) · [Canon F-1 instructions, distance scale](https://www.cameramanuals.org/canon_pdf/canon_f-1-original.pdf)
- The construction and floating gap identify the likely 17 mm f/4 S.S.C. design for 35 mm Canon FD cameras. The full-stop scale of f/4, 5.6, 8, 11, 16 and 22 replaces the former f/4 and f/8 settings. Canon specifies six diaphragm blades. ## Remaining Questions

- Figure 1 places the iris mark closer to R12 than the assumed midpoint. Its exact axial position, the mechanical clear apertures and the production rim heights require a dimensioned section.
- The production glass suppliers and melts are unknown. E2, E6 and E10 have no catalog equivalents within 0.0001 in index and 0.15 in Abbe number.
- The patent gives two focus settings. The actual spacing between those settings and the travel from 0.29308 m to the production minimum of 0.25 m remain undocumented. Canon specifies the diaphragm blade count but gives no blade curvature.
