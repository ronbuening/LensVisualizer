# CANON FD 17mm f/4 S.S.C. — audit

## Source

Akira Tajima and Kikuo Momiyama, US 3,748,021, _Reverse Telephoto Lens for Near-Distance Photography_, assigned to Canon Inc. and published July 24, 1973. Embodiment 1 appears in columns 3–4. Figure 1 shows the section, Figures 2–4 describe the infinity image, and Figures 5–10 compare two close-focus configurations. The patent gives lengths in units of focal length. The scale is ×17 throughout. [US patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3748021A/en)

## Transcription

The prescription has 20 spherical refracting surfaces, 19 glass thicknesses and air spaces through R20, and eleven index and Abbe-number pairs. R14 is shared by E7/E8 and R17 by E9/E10. The only changed table entry is the sign of R12. The d-line reference is explicit in the German publication. Figures 3–4 label the edge height as 1.235 at a field angle of 52°. [German publication](https://patents.google.com/patent/DE2117367A1/en)

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

- **Glass rims.** The heights of the curved optical faces in the patent drawing give the clear radii. The distance from the first to last vertex sets the drawing scale. The axis-to-rim readings, in millimetres and in order from R1 to R20, are 20.9080, 16.2184, 17.7816, 12.7989, 14.5575, 14.5575, 10.7471, 10.7471, 7.2299, 5.2759, 5.0805, 5.0805, 5.0805, 6.2529, 5.1782, 7.2299, 7.2299, 7.0833, 9.2816 and 9.2816. The four decimal places preserve the scale conversion. The drawing itself is less precise.
- **Iris.** Figure 1 puts the iris between R12 and R13. The estimated midpoint divides the 2.1488 mm air space into two 1.0744 mm lengths. The derived iris radius of 3.878 mm gives a 4.250 mm entrance pupil diameter at f/4.
- **Near image spacing.** The tabulated D8 and object distance determine the rear spacing paraxially. The rear vertex advances 1.3586 mm from infinity, while the front vertex advances 0.8061 mm. Between the two published settings the spacings vary linearly.
- **Field clearance.** At the 21.633 mm frame corner, the chief ray clears every optical rim. The closest approach is 0.257 mm inside R4 at infinity and 0.375 mm inside R10 at the near setting. At both settings the axial marginal ray passes every glass surface. At infinity the estimated geometric pupil transmission is 18.0% at the patent image height and 13.3% at the full-frame corner.

## Product Identification

The 104° field, f/4 aperture, eleven elements in nine groups and floating front gap identify Embodiment 1 as the likely design of the FD 17mm f/4 S.S.C. Canon’s cutaway agrees on the two front menisci, thick central singlet, iris and compact rear section. Its resolution limits comparison of the two cemented pairs and their individual curvatures. The 1971 FD 17mm f/4 shares the S.S.C. lens’s construction count and dimensions. The patent’s other example is an 84° f/2.8 design with nine elements in eight groups. [Canon cutaway, p. 16](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00253/00253.pdf#page=16) · [1971 lens](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd129.html) · [1973 S.S.C. lens](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd130.html)

## Corrections

### 2026-11-25

Column 3 prints R12 = +0.7380. This sign gives f = −4.60 and a virtual back focus of −19.41 in the patent’s units. R12 = −0.7380 (−12.546 mm) gives f = 0.999995 and back focus 2.07139, reproducing the printed 1 and 2.0714. Figure 1 independently shows E6 biconvex, and Figures 2–4 describe a real image behind the lens.

Canon gives a production minimum focus of 0.25 m at 0.10×. The closest setting shown here follows the patent’s 0.29308 m example at 0.0799×. [Canon specifications](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd130.html)

## Remaining Questions

The precise iris location and mechanical clear apertures are unknown. The drawn iris mark appears closer to R12 than the assumed midpoint. A dimensioned production section would settle both the iris position and the rim heights. Canon’s glass suppliers and melts are also unknown. E2, E6 and E10 lack catalog equivalents within 0.0001 in refractive index and 0.15 in Abbe number. The patent gives no spacing schedule from its nearest example to the production minimum focus.
