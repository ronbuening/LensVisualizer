# CANON NEW FD 24mm f/2 — Audit Log

## Source

Kikuo Momiyama, _Retrofocus Type Wide-Angle Objective Lens_, US 4,188,092. Canon Inc., named Canon Kabushiki Kaisha on the grant, filed the US application on March 31, 1978, claiming Japanese priority of April 5, 1977 (52-38690). The patent was granted February 12, 1980, and carries no certificate of correction. Figure 1 shows Example 1 (PDF p. 2). Column 5 gives its focal length, aperture and half-field and begins the prescription, which ends in column 6 with R20 and the sum of the spacings (PDF p. 12). Columns 7–8 print the third-order aberration coefficients and the condition values of all four examples (PDF p. 13). Claim 5, in column 10, names the air space that changes during focusing (PDF p. 14). [Patent text and drawings](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4188092A/en)

Canon's museum record gives the release date and production specifications of the New FD 24mm f/2. MIR's lens page gives the angle of view, the all-spherical construction, the coating, the helicoid focusing and the floating system, and reproduces a production cutaway. Canon's New FD lens instructions list double-helicoid focusing with the Canon floating system. Canon's F-1N manual states that lens distance scales are calibrated from the film plane. ## Transcription Check

All 20 spherical radii, 19 axial spacings and 11 helium d-line index and Abbe-number pairs were reread against the page image of the Example 1 table, including the continuation for R20, and agree. The negative signs at R10, R11, R13, R14, R15, R17, R18 and R20 agree with the page image. R10 joins L5 and L6, and R15 joins L8 and L9. Each cemented interface contributes one refraction. The section contains no plate. The printed sum of the spacings, 2.2496, matches the table.

The patent's third-order coefficient table for Example 1, columns I–V at all 20 surfaces, is reproduced by the prescription to the fourth decimal. The Petzval sum is 0.1028 against the printed 0.1026.

The condition values agree with the prescription: |fI| = 0.8471f, f1 = 7.516f and f3 = 4.02f (4.023f from the prescription), |f2| = 1.529f and |f4| = 1.169f, ν1 = 61.1 against ν3 = 39.2, DII = 0.6509f, Ra = 0.9078f, −Ra/Rb = 0.7907 and NIII = 1.7425. The one exception is the combined focal length of Groups I and II, printed as 1.8923f in magnitude, where R1–R11 give −2.4205f. The same calculation reproduces the printed values of Examples 2, 3 and 4, so the Example 1 entry in the condition table is a misprint. The prescription is unaffected and still lies inside the stated 1.5f to 6f interval.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

**Iris.** The prescription gives a single air space D11 of 1.9584 mm, and Figure 1 draws the diaphragm inside it. Fitting the iris position to the printed third-order coefficients gives 0.0203f, which places it 0.488 mm behind R11 and 1.4704 mm before R12. The diaphragm stroke in Figure 1 reads about 0.66 mm behind R11. The coefficient fit sets the position. The iris radius of 9.098 mm follows from the paraxial f/2 aperture.

**Clear radii.** The patent prints none. Figure 1, read at 600 dpi, measures 37.5 px/mm over the 53.99 mm from the R1 vertex to the R20 vertex, and the clear radii follow its drawn edges.

| Surfaces | Clear radii (mm)              |
| -------- | ----------------------------- |
| R1–R5    | 22.2, 22.2, 16.6, 11.35, 12.3 |
| R6–R10   | 12.3, 9.9, 8.7, 10.6, 10.6    |
| R11–R15  | 10.6, 11.3, 11.3, 10.5, 10.5  |
| R16–R20  | 9.1, 10.6, 10.6, 12.2, 12.2   |

Three clear radii depart from the drawing. At R4 the figure brings the glass nearly into contact with R5 at about 11.6 mm; the 11.35 mm radius keeps the combined sag within 90% of the axial gap and leaves air between the surfaces. At R16 the figure suggests 10.5 mm, but the printed curvatures of R16 and R17 meet at a radius of 9.602 mm; the 9.1 mm radius leaves about a tenth of the axial gap at the edge. At R8 the figure ends the concave arc at about 8.3 mm. That radius would lower geometric transmission by up to 7.9 points, at 30°; the 8.7 mm radius costs at most 2.6 points, at 27°, and nothing at 42°.

Three flat lands, square to the axis and opaque, complete the drawn element edges outside these clear radii. L2's rear land runs from 11.35 mm to its 16.6 mm flange, L4's from 8.7 mm to its 9.9 mm edge, and L9's from 9.1 mm to 10.3 mm. L9's land ends 0.03 mm from R17, within 98% of that air gap; the figure draws the two elements meeting there. The lands carry no light and change no ray.

**Real axial pupil.** At the 9.098 mm iris the axial marginal ray enters at 5.454 mm and reaches R16 at 8.550 mm, inside its clear radius. Pupil spherical aberration gives a focal-length-to-entrance-diameter ratio of 2.20. A 5.998 mm entrance radius, corresponding to a real f/2 bundle, would require a 10.347 mm iris and 9.626 mm at R16, beyond the 9.602 mm at which R16 and R17 meet.

**Image plane and focus.** The image distance is the d-line paraxial back focal distance. `focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. Column 5 describes whole-lens advance with a widening of D6 for nearer objects, and claim 5 names that air space. The patent gives only the infinity spacing, 2.2512 mm, so no finite distance, magnification or intermediate position can be calculated from a printed focus setting.

- The iris moved from the midpoint of D11 (0.9792 mm on each side) to 0.488 mm behind R11 and 1.4704 mm before R12, the position that reproduces the printed third-order coefficients. The paraxial f/2 iris radius changed from 9.149 mm to 9.098 mm with it. The aperture stays at the printed f/2.
- Clear radii read from Figure 1 replace the first narrow estimates, which were 18 mm at the front element, 13 mm at R3 and between 9 and 12 mm elsewhere. At 18 mm the front element cut the corner chief ray at R1, which needs 19.44 mm, and passed 4.5% of the pupil at 42°. The figure rims pass the corner chief ray and 27.3% of the pupil at 42°. R4, R8 and R16 depart from the drawing for the reasons given above. - Focus stays at infinity only, the one state the patent prints. D6 is 2.2512 mm there and widens as the lens moves toward a nearer subject. No near spacing is printed, so no intermediate positions are calculated.
- Element shapes replace the generic element type, following the signs of the radii. The two cemented pairs are marked C1 (R9–R11) and C2 (R14–R16), and the three optical groups follow the patent's Groups I–III.
- The name, patent number and assignee take their standard forms, the subtitle gives the patent, example, assignee and inventor, and the specification chips give the construction, all-spherical design and floating elements.

## Remaining Questions

- The condition table prints 1.8923f for the magnitude of the combined focal length of Groups I and II, where R1–R11 give −2.4205f. The same calculation reproduces the printed values of Examples 2–4, so the Example 1 entry appears to be a misprint. The prescription still satisfies the stated 1.5f to 6f interval.
- The production iris diameter and the clear edges at R4, R8 and R16 are undocumented. R8's clear radius lies 0.4 mm beyond the drawn end of its arc, and R16 is limited by its meeting with R17, so the wide-open illumination remains an estimate.
- Canon gives 0.11× at 0.3 m. The patent gives neither a finite conjugate nor a second value of D6 from which to determine the floating travel.
- The factory radii, spacings and glass suppliers and melts, and the original print source of the cutaway, are unknown.
