# CANON NEW FD 24mm f/2 — audit

## Source

Kikuo Momiyama, US 4,188,092, _Retrofocus Type Wide-Angle Objective Lens_, assigned to Canon Kabushiki Kaisha (Canon Inc.) and granted February 12, 1980. Example 1 appears in columns 5–6, including the continuation for R20. Fig. 1 shows the section and diaphragm. Columns 7–8 give the surface aberration coefficients and design conditions. [Patent and drawings](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4188092A/en)

## Transcription

All 20 spherical radii, 19 axial intervals and 11 d-line index and Abbe-number pairs agree with the printed table. Multiplying its dimensions by 24 converts the normalized focal length of one to millimetres. The negative signs at R10, R11, R13, R14, R15, R17, R18 and R20 agree with the page image. R10 joins L5/L6 and R15 joins L8/L9. Each cemented interface contributes one refraction. The section contains no plate.

The image plane coincides with paraxial infinity focus to within 0.0001 mm. All surfaces retain positive edge clearance, and the nominal f/2 axial bundle clears the glass. At the published field the chief ray reaches 98% of the full-frame corner height. The 36 × 24 mm film corner at 21.633 mm is reached at 42.49°, with 0.40 mm of chief-ray clearance at the tightest surface, R4.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

**Diaphragm.** The prescription gives a single air space D11 of 1.9584 mm. Matching the printed Seidel coefficients places the iris 0.488 mm behind R11 and 1.4704 mm before R12. The diaphragm mark in Fig. 1 suggests about 0.66 mm behind R11. The axial position comes from the numerical evidence, and the iris radius of 9.098 mm follows from the paraxial f/2 aperture.

**Clear radii.** The first-to-last vertex distance sets the scale of Fig. 1. The table lists the clear radius at each refracting surface in order from the object to the image.

| Surfaces | Clear radii (mm)              |
| -------- | ----------------------------- |
| R1–R5    | 22.2, 22.2, 16.6, 11.35, 12.3 |
| R6–R10   | 12.3, 9.9, 9.7, 10.5, 10.5    |
| R11–R15  | 10.6, 11.3, 11.3, 10.5, 10.5  |
| R16–R20  | 9.1, 10.6, 10.6, 12.2, 12.2   |

At R4 the figure brings the glass nearly into contact with R5 at about 11.6 mm. A radius of 11.35 mm leaves air between the two surfaces. At R16 the figure suggests 10.5 mm, but its printed curvature meets R17 at a radius of 9.602 mm. A 9.1 mm radius leaves about a tenth of the axial air gap at the edge. At R8 the drawing shows a flange where the concave arc reaches about 7 mm. Treating that line as an opaque edge would reduce geometric pupil transmission at 30° from 78% to 50%. The 9.7 mm clear radius is an estimate. With these openings, geometric transmission at 42° is 27.3%.

**Real axial pupil.** At the 9.098 mm iris the axial marginal ray enters at 5.454 mm and reaches R16 at 8.550 mm, inside the clear radius. Pupil spherical aberration gives a focal-length-to-entrance-diameter ratio of 2.20. A 5.998 mm entrance radius, corresponding to a real f/2 bundle, would require a 10.347 mm iris and 9.626 mm at R16. That height exceeds the R16/R17 contact radius.

**Image plane and focus.** The image distance is the d-line paraxial back focal distance. The patent describes whole-lens advance and widening of D6 for nearer objects. It gives only the infinity spacing, so no finite distance, magnification or intermediate motion can be calculated from a printed focus setting.

## Product Identification

Canon lists a June 1979 New FD 24mm f/2 with 11 elements in nine groups, eight blades, f/22 minimum aperture, 0.30 m closest focus and 0.11× maximum magnification. The MIR production section shows the two cemented components, the seam in the rear biconcave component, the positive singlet behind it and the nearly flat front of L7. These details strongly favor Example 1. The other 24 mm example (Example 3) places the second seam in the positive component and bends L7 more sharply. Examples 2 and 4 give 28 mm lenses. The 84° angle of view and the description of floating focus also agree. ## Corrections

### 2026-11-25

The prescription reproduces the Example 1 table as printed. Its rounded dimensions yield a focal length 0.0074 mm shorter than Canon's nominal 24 mm. The model uses that focal length and the patent's infinity spacing. Canon's closest focus and maximum magnification describe the production lens.

## Remaining Questions

- The conditions table prints 1.8923 f for the magnitude of the combined focal length of Groups I and II. R1–R11 give a focal length of −2.4205 f. The same calculation reproduces the tabulated magnitudes for Examples 2–4. The Example 1 condition appears to be a misprint. The prescription still satisfies the stated 1.5 f to 6 f interval.
- The production iris diameter and the clear edges at R4, R8 and R16 are undocumented. The drawn flange at R8 and the R16/R17 contact limit leave uncertainty in wide-open illumination. The exact close-focus motion, production glass suppliers and melts, and original print source of the cutaway are also unknown.
