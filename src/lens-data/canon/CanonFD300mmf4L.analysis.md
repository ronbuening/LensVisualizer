# CANON FD 300mm f/4 L

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 4,251,133, _Large Aperture Telephoto-Lens_\
**Inventors:** Sadahiko Tsuji\
**Assignee:** Canon Inc. (printed as Canon Kabushiki Kaisha)\
**Filed:** November 8, 1978, with Japanese priority of November 16, 1977\
**Published:** February 17, 1981\
**Example details:** Numerical Example 4, column 4. Figure 4 gives the section, Figure 11 the infinity aberrations, and Figure 12 the aberrations at an object distance of ten focal lengths.

Example 4 is a strong match for the **December 1978 FD 300mm f/4 L**. Only this example has two positive lenses at νd 81.3 flanking the high-index negative front meniscus. Canon describes the original lens's anomalous-dispersion glass and rear focusing. Marco Cavina identifies its two ultra-low-dispersion (UD) elements at those positions in Figure 4. This breech-lock lens was the first to carry the red ring of Canon's L series. [Canon FD L specification](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd177.html) · [Cavina's glass diagrams](https://www.marcocavina.com/articoli_fotografici/Canon_FD_300_400_500_L/00_pag.htm) · [Canon's L-series history](https://files.canon-europe.com/files/webcontent/rf-lens-world/l-lens/index.html) · [FD mount versions](https://www.nocsensei.com/camera/tecnica/marco-cavina/marcocavina/canon-fd-300mm-156-new/)

The **May 1980 New FD L** replaced the breech-lock ring and weighed 1,060 g at 207 mm long, against 1,235 g and 208 mm for the original. Both are 85 mm in diameter and have a nine-blade iris closing to f/32. The New FD cutaway shows a protective flat, the two highlighted front positives and their negative partner, a fixed rear meniscus, an air-spaced focusing pair and a separate drop-in filter. The protector accounts for the seventh element and group in Canon's count. The patent gives dimensions for the six powered singlets only. The cutaway supports a shared layout, though its resolution cannot establish identical curvatures. [Canon FD L](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd177.html) · [Canon New FD L](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/nfd228.html) · [New FD L cutaway](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/canon/fdresources/fdlenses/300mm.htm) · [Mount change](https://www.nocsensei.com/camera/tecnica/marco-cavina/marcocavina/canon-fd-300mm-156-new/)

Example 1 has a cemented rear pair. Example 2 uses ultra-low-dispersion glass in only the third front element. Example 3 uses νd 70.1 in both front positives and corresponds to the FD 300mm f/4 S.S.C. Canon lists that lens as six elements in six groups with the same 34 mm filter size as the L. ## Optical Architecture

All twelve refracting surfaces are spherical. The positive front group supplies most of the convergence. A 63.03 mm air space separates it from the negative rear group, which extends the focal length while keeping the distance from the first powered surface to the film plane to 242.61 mm. The diaphragm sits between the fixed rear meniscus and the moving pair, 15.06 mm behind surface 8 and 2.19 mm ahead of surface 9.

The patent sets the focal length to one. A uniform scale of ×300 converts its radii, thicknesses, gaps and back focus to millimetres. At infinity the tabulated curvatures and d-line indices give 300.11 mm at f/4, compared with Canon's nominal 300 mm at f/4. The image plane uses the printed back focus of 116.52 mm. Paraxial focus is 0.056 mm farther from the last surface.

The clear radii follow Figure 4, with extra clearance at L2, L3 and L4 for the axial f/4 beam. As in Figure 4, L4's rear face ends short of the element's edge, here at 18.6 mm, and a flat land continues to the 20.4 mm rim. The stop position is printed, while its 16.77 mm radius follows from the paraxial entrance pupil. The 35 mm frame corner falls at 4.1° off axis, near the 4.2° limit of the patent's aberration plots. Some marginal rays at the corner are vignetted.

## Glass Selection and Chromatic Correction

Tsuji's first design condition concerns the difference between the mean Abbe number of the two positive front lenses and that of the negative lens between them. Example 4 gives 40.4. This allows the three elements to balance axial colour with less individual power. The second condition relates the adjacent radii of L1 and L2 to spherical aberration. In column 4 the printed ratio of their magnitudes is 1.0437. [US 4,251,133, design conditions and Numerical Example 4](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/84/1b/72/204175dc61b55d/US4251133.pdf#page=9)

The patent gives refractive indices and Abbe numbers but omits glass trade names and partial dispersions. Cavina identifies L1 and L3 as UD in the 1978 lens. Canon explicitly counts two UD elements in the New FD L. Cavina gives νd 81.6 for the front positives, while the prescription retains the patent's 81.3 for both. The catalog glasses supply dispersion curves. The production suppliers are unknown. [Cavina's FD L glass diagram and caption](https://www.marcocavina.com/articoli_fotografici/Canon_FD_300_400_500_L/00_pag.htm) · [Canon New FD L](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/nfd228.html)

## Focus Mechanism

L5 and L6 move together behind the stationary diaphragm. The front group and L4 stay fixed. Canon describes a vari-pitch focusing system in the New FD L, which moves the pair quickly at close distances and more slowly at long ones. Both versions focus to 3 m, measured from the film plane, and reach 0.11×. The patent's ten-focal-length object distance also scales to 3 m, but Figure 12 gives only aberrations. Column 3 says the pair moves toward the object for closer subjects, but with Example 4's powers only travel toward the film refocuses: about 25 mm, paraxially, for a subject 3 m from the film. The patent leaves the reference plane, travel and magnification at that distance unspecified. The model therefore shows only the printed infinity position.
