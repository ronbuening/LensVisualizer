# CANON FD 300mm f/4 L

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 4,251,133, _Large Aperture Telephoto-Lens_\
**Inventors:** Sadahiko Tsuji\
**Assignee:** Canon Inc. (printed as Canon Kabushiki Kaisha)\
**Filed:** November 8, 1978, with Japanese priority of November 16, 1977\
**Published:** February 17, 1981\
**Example details:** Numerical Example 4, column 4. Figure 4 gives the section, Figure 11 the infinity aberrations, and Figure 12 the aberrations at an object distance of ten focal lengths.

Two ultra-low-dispersion (νd 81.3) positive lenses distinguish Example 4 from the other three versions of Tsuji's 300 mm f/4 design. They flank a high-index negative meniscus in the front group. Canon describes anomalous-dispersion glass in the original FD L, marketed in December 1978. Cavina identifies Figure 4 with that lens and marks both positive elements as low-dispersion glass. Together, their placement and the rear focusing layout make Example 4 a strong match. [Canon's FD 300mm f/4L specification](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd177.html) · [Cavina's FD L glass diagram](https://www.marcocavina.com/articoli_fotografici/Canon_FD_300_400_500_L/04.gif)

The construction diagram available for the **May 1980 New FD L** shows a protective flat ahead of the two highlighted front positives and their negative partner. Behind them are a fixed meniscus, an air-spaced focusing pair and a separate drop-in filter. The front protector accounts for the seventh element and seventh group in Canon's specifications for both versions. The patent describes the six powered singlets. It gives no prescription for either the protector or the removable filter, and both are omitted here. The diagram supports the shared layout, but its resolution cannot establish identical curvatures for the two products. [Canon's New FD 300mm f/4L specification](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/nfd228.html) · [New FD L construction diagram](https://mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/canon/fdresources/fdlenses/300mmf4loptic.jpg)

Example 1 has a cemented rear pair. Example 2 uses ultra-low-dispersion glass in only the third front element. Example 3 uses glass with an Abbe number of 70.1 in both front positives and corresponds to the FD 300mm f/4 S.S.C. Canon lists that lens as six elements in six groups with the same 34 mm filter size. [US 4,251,133, columns 3–4](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/84/1b/72/204175dc61b55d/US4251133.pdf#page=9) · [Canon's S.S.C. specification](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd176.html) · [Cavina's non-L glass diagram](https://www.marcocavina.com/articoli_fotografici/Canon_FD_300_400_500_L/02.gif)

## Optical Architecture

All twelve refracting surfaces are spherical. The positive front group supplies most of the convergence. A 63.03 mm air space separates it from the negative rear group, which extends the focal length while keeping the distance from the first powered surface to the film plane to 242.61 mm. The diaphragm sits between the fixed rear meniscus and the moving pair, 15.06 mm behind surface 8 and 2.19 mm ahead of surface 9.

The patent sets the focal length to one. A uniform scale of ×300 converts its radii, thicknesses, gaps and back focus to millimetres. At infinity the tabulated curvatures and d-line indices give 300.11 mm at f/4, compared with Canon's nominal 300 mm at f/4. The image plane uses the printed back focus of 116.52 mm. Paraxial focus is 0.056 mm farther from the last surface.

Figure 4 supplies the rim proportions. The clear radii allow the on-axis f/4 beam through all six elements. The stop position is printed, while its 16.77 mm radius follows from the paraxial entrance pupil. The full-frame corner falls at about 4.1° off axis, near the 4.2° limit of the patent's aberration plots. Some marginal rays at the corner are vignetted.

## Glass Selection and Chromatic Correction

Tsuji's first design condition concerns the difference between the mean Abbe number of the two positive front lenses and that of the negative lens between them. Example 4 gives 40.4. This allows the three elements to balance axial colour with less individual power. The second condition relates the adjacent radii of L1 and L2 to spherical aberration. In column 4 the printed ratio of their magnitudes is 1.0437. [US 4,251,133, design conditions and Numerical Example 4](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/84/1b/72/204175dc61b55d/US4251133.pdf#page=9)

The patent gives refractive indices and Abbe numbers but omits glass trade names and partial dispersions. The UD identification of L1 and L3 follows the product diagrams and Canon's description of the two UD elements in the New FD L. The catalog glasses supply dispersion curves for the model. The production suppliers are unknown. Cavina's diagram gives 81.6 for the front positives, while the patent prints 81.3 for both. The prescription uses the printed value.

## Focus Mechanism

L5 and L6 move together behind the stationary diaphragm. The front group and L4 stay fixed. Canon specifies rear focusing to 3 m and a maximum magnification of 0.11×. At this scale the patent's ten-focal-length object distance is also 3 m, but Figure 12 gives only aberration curves. It specifies neither the reference plane for that distance nor the axial travel or magnification. The model therefore shows the printed infinity position only.
