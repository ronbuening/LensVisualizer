# CANON FL 85–300mm f/5: mechanically compensated zoom

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 3,377,119 A.  
**Application Number:** US serial 334,386.  
**Filed:** 30 December 1963.  
**Granted:** 9 April 1968; certificate of correction dated 2 December 1969.  
**Inventor:** Eiichi Takano.  
**Assignee:** Canon Camera Co., Inc.  
**Title:** Mechanically Compensated Zoom Lens System.  
**Example details:** Complete detailed-description prescription, PDF page 5 / printed column 6, Figure 3 architecture, at the three printed 87.74, 175.01 and 292.45 mm stations.  
**Priority:** Japan 21 January 1963, 38/1776; 25 January 1963, 38/2691.

The production correlation is probable; the patent does not establish that its numerical prescription is identical to the manufactured lens.

1. Canon's Museum identifies the original FL85–300mm F5 as an April 1965 product, with 15 elements in 9 groups, f/5–22, six blades, 4 m minimum focus and a 72 mm filter. Its 85–300 marketing range is close to, but not numerically identical with, the patent's 87.74–292.45 design range.
2. The period Canon System publication 5334, PDF page 8, lists the same 15/9 product, f/5, automatic aperture with an aperture-release button, f/22 minimum, 4 m distance scale, 72 mm filter, 75 mm cap, and 29°–8° full fields. Its 1840 g weight differs from the Museum's 1850 g. The publication's printing date is not established.
3. Canon FT camera instructions 00705, PDF/printed page 40, visibly name and illustrate the external FL85–300mm F5. This is not an internal optical section or a numerical focus-motion witness. The Museum photograph is likewise an external view.
4. Patent timing, fixed-iris f/5 specification, 24×36 mm format and 15/9 architecture converge. The later FD85–300mm f/4.5 with 15/11 construction is a different version and is excluded.

The detailed description uses wide d5 = 1.984 mm and N8 = 1.62004; the overview prints d5 = 1.934, and the claim prints N8 = 1.62041. The certificate fixes only claim r12 to infinity and r22 to 36.82. The model follows the detailed description; the claim also contains symbolic entries rather than a complete numerical alternative. Figure 2 is an explanatory standalone positive component, not a second complete lens. The related DE1447181A1 publication provides historical context but supplies no prescription values used here.

## Optical Architecture

This is a mechanically compensated five-component zoom with positive–negative–negative–positive–positive net component power. The 15 glass elements form nine air-separated optical groups; the five functional components are distinct from the group count. All refracting faces are spherical or plano. The selected table contains no aspheric, stabilization, sensor-stack or rear-filter prescription.

The source fixes front component I and the two rear positive components IV/V. Negative variator II moves in one direction while negative compensator III reverses once. The reconstructed cam preserves that topology. With printed millimetres unchanged, independently computed component focal lengths are I +249.3703, II −85.0000, III −389.9992, IV +140.8341 and V +154.1415 mm. The rear of IV includes a weak negative meniscus, so positive component power must not be assigned to every constituent.

The diagram brackets use the patent's component notation I–V: r1–r5, r6–r10, r11–r13, r14–r18 and r19–r24. D1–D6 identify the six cemented pairs at r1–r3, r8–r10, r11–r13, r14–r16, r19–r21 and r22–r24. L1–L15 are sequential element identifiers used by the model; the patent instead numbers the refractive-index entries N1–N15 and refracting surfaces r1–r24.

At the wide/middle/long anchors the complete paraxial EFL is 87.735584 / 175.010135 / 292.452654 mm, and BFL is 108.871201 / 108.871706 / 108.871930 mm. The fixed terminal image gap remains the printed 108.87 mm rather than a refitted Gaussian plane. Front-to-last-vertex track is 188.656 / 188.655 / 188.655 mm. The 0.001 mm variation reflects source rounding rather than deliberate rear-group motion. At wide the back focus exceeds focal length; at long the overall track/focal ratio is about 1.017, so the printed rounded telephoto ratio 1.0 is not an exact strict inequality.

The invariant Petzval sum is −0.000169979470506 mm⁻¹ under the explicitly stated sequential surface convention. This small scalar sum is not proof of a flat high-quality image over the published field: finite rays, astigmatism, clipping and MTF require their separate checks.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The following focal lengths describe isolated thick elements in air rather than their power while cemented to their neighbours. Aberration roles are interpretations of position, power and the patent's inequalities; individual aberration contributions have not been calculated.

### L1 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.5927, νd = 35.4. Glass: FF5 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = -283.5276 mm.

Front component I begins with this negative meniscus cemented to L2. Its lower Abbe number than L2 supplies the high-dispersion partner of the front positive collector; the source requires the L2–L1 Abbe difference to exceed 30.

### L2 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70. Glass: S-FSL5 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = +167.5186 mm.

The biconvex, low-dispersion positive member carries most of the front doublet’s positive power. Its νd 70 and L1’s 35.4 give a 34.6 Abbe difference, while the cemented interface avoids an extra airspace.

### L3 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70. Glass: S-FSL5 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = +640.8429 mm.

This air-separated positive meniscus completes component I. Its νd 70 and index below 1.55 satisfy the source palette restrictions; together with the front doublet it provides a fixed positive input component rather than a focusing motion inferred from the product MFD.

### L4 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.713, νd = 53.9. Glass: LAC8 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = -176.4165 mm.

This negative meniscus begins moving variator II. Its high-index, moderately low-dispersion crown coordinate gives negative power without an especially small front radius. The role in changing magnification follows the source’s component-II movement, not a recovered mechanical cam.

### L5 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.713, νd = 53.9. Glass: LAC8 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = -66.0384 mm.

The stronger biconcave member shares a cemented surface with L6 and supplies most of the variator’s negative power. Its νd 53.9 exceeds the partner’s 25.5 by 28.4, satisfying the source’s >25 chromatic separation.

### L6 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.5. Glass: FD60 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = +109.3856 mm.

The positive high-dispersion meniscus offsets part of L5’s negative power and chromatic contribution. The doublet remains part of net-negative component II; its high index and νd below 35 are explicit source constraints, not a supplier identification.

### L7 — Plano-Concave Negative

nd = 1.62041, νd = 60.3. Glass: BACD16 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = -158.6048 mm.

This plano-concave negative member begins compensator III and is cemented to L8 at the planar surface. The source νd difference is 60.3−36.3 = 24.0, above the stated 20. Component III’s weak negative combined power enables the compensating reversal.

### L8 — Plano-Convex Positive

nd = 1.62004, νd = 36.3. Glass: E-F2 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = +270.7503 mm.

This plano-convex positive member moderates L7. Its detailed-description index is 1.62004, while the claim gives 1.62041. The nearly index-matched planar interface has zero first-order surface power but a substantial dispersion difference. The model retains the detailed-description value.

### L9 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.50137, νd = 56.5. Glass: K10 (SCHOTT catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = +63.1972 mm.

The biconvex low-index positive member starts fixed component IV. It is cemented to L10 and provides a positive relay contribution behind the stop. The source requires its index below 1.55.

### L10 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.7552, νd = 27.5. Glass: E-FD4 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = -118.2206 mm.

The high-index negative meniscus is the chromatic partner of L9. Their cemented r15 surface satisfies both source condition-(5) tests: |R|/fmax ≈ 0.162489 and index difference 0.25383. This local success does not clear the corresponding conflict in component V.

### L11 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.60311, νd = 60.7. Glass: BACD14 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = -740.1721 mm.

This weak negative meniscus completes net-positive IV. The isolated power is negative despite both radii being positive; shape alone is not a positive-power label. Its front r17 clear radius is 17 mm, enough for the f/5 axial ray (16.92 mm). The rear r18 stays at 14.2 mm: the r18 and r19 spheres meet at 14.88 mm, below the 15.00 mm f/5 marginal height, so r18 limits the axial beam to about f/5.28. Fig. 3 also draws r18 smaller than r17, with a bevel.

### L12 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.4645, νd = 65.7. Glass: FC3 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = +55.0125 mm.

This low-index biconvex element begins fixed relay V and is cemented to L13. It provides strong positive power; its νd 65.7 with the flint partner is consistent with chromatic balancing, without establishing anomalous partial dispersion.

### L13 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.7552, νd = 27.5. Glass: E-FD4 (HOYA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = -111.0033 mm.

This high-index negative meniscus partly offsets L12. The r20 cemented interface has index difference 0.2907, but |R|/fmax ≈ 0.262028 exceeds the source condition-(5) upper bound 0.2. The model retains the printed prescription despite this inconsistency.

### L14 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.51118, νd = 50.9. Glass: KF8 (SUMITA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = +35.2443 mm.

The biconvex element begins the rear cemented doublet. Its positive isolated power is balanced by the stronger negative L15, making this rear subcomponent net negative despite positive component-V power. The source constrains its index below 1.55.

### L15 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70. Glass: S-FSL5 (OHARA catalog equivalent; production melt unidentified). f = -26.1885 mm.

The rear biconcave element provides strong negative isolated power. Its printed νd is 70; S-FSL5 is a catalog equivalent rather than an identified production melt. The cemented r23 radius falls inside the condition-(5) interval, but its 0.02369 index difference fails the >0.2 clause, so neither interface satisfies both conditions.

The source couples low-index crowns to denser flints in the fixed positive components and uses higher-index moderate-dispersion negative members in the variator. Canon publishes no special-dispersion positions for this lens. Any spectral results based on catalog equivalents describe those substitutes, not identified production glasses.

## Focus Mechanism

The model describes infinity focus only. Canon publishes a 4 m minimum focus distance, but the sources supply no numerical focusing travel. No focusing mechanism or group movement is inferred. Both focus states repeat the infinity spacings at every zoom position, so the focus control produces no optical movement and does not represent the marketed minimum focus distance. Finite-conjugate MTF is outside the model's scope.

## Reconstructed Continuous Zoom and Stop

The three source gap triples (d5, d10, d13), in mm, are (1.984, 113.586, 2.736), (72.172, 21.262, 24.871) and (110.615, 4.954, 2.736). Only those three stations are published. Between them, the variator gap and sum of the three gaps are linearly interpolated; compensator d13 is solved to preserve the linearly interpolated source-derived BFL, with d10 determined by the sum. The compensator equation has one solution with positive gaps at each modelled position; this does not imply a unique chief-ray path.

From wide to tele, component II moves monotonically toward the image by 108.631 mm (70.188 mm to the middle station, then 38.443 mm). Component III moves toward the object by 22.136 mm and then back toward the image by 22.135 mm. I remains fixed; IV/V share only the 0.001 mm source-rounding drift. The variable gap shown as `d13−1` is the spacing from r13 to the inferred stop; adding the fixed 1 mm stop-to-IV space recovers the patent's full d13.

The 257-node path has a sampled maximum target-BFL interpolation error of 0.000459147 mm. The sampled maximum departure from the printed 108.87 mm image gap is 0.002387846 mm, inside the source’s 0.01 mm reporting interval. EFL and variator travel are monotonic; compensator travel reverses once. Positions are continuous, but piecewise-linear velocities need not be smooth. The movement between published stations is reconstructed rather than a factory cam.

The source locates a fixed-diameter iris between III and IV, near IV, without a numerical coordinate. The model places it an inferred 1 mm before IV and splits d13 accordingly. Its fixed wide-open radius, 16.563948530 mm, is derived from nominal f/5 rather than printed in the patent. The exact nominal calibration’s deviation from that constant iris stays below 0.507% throughout zoom. The source’s maximum effective aperture size 60 is not a published physical iris diameter. Read as a front clear diameter it agrees with the 30 mm r1 radius taken from Fig. 3, but it is not used as a per-face specification.

## Source Conditions and Retained Variants

Conditions (1)–(4) are satisfied under the stated component interpretation: the printed n/ν restrictions, IV front focal distance greater than its EFL, rear radius 21.7/292.45 ≈ 0.074201, rear doublet thickness 12.3/292.45 ≈ 0.042059, and rear cement depth 1.94/12.3 ≈ 0.157724 within one quarter. Condition (4) names component V in column 4 but IV in its column-5 explanation; the source's component names disagree.

The printed prescription does not satisfy condition (5). IV’s front cemented interface r15 meets the radius and index requirements. V’s front cemented interface r20 has |R|/fmax = 0.262028, outside [0.065, 0.2], although Δn = 0.2907 meets the index requirement. V’s rear cemented interface r23 has radius ratio 0.109147 but Δn = 0.02369 is too small; it also belongs to the negative rear doublet. No single V interface meets both requirements. The source prose and table disagree, and the model retains the printed radii and glass coordinates.

Using the overview's wide gap gives EFL/BFL 87.716699/108.877389 mm. Using the corrected claim's N8 gives EFL 87.697126/174.961211/292.324459 and BFL 108.813247/108.829162/108.813974 mm. These alternatives differ from the detailed-description prescription used in the model.

## Geometry, Aperture and Model Limitations

Clear radii are estimated from Fig. 3 and compared with axial ray heights at f/5; they are not factory measurements. The drawing is schematic, and its axial spacings match no published zoom station. All printed radii, spacings, indices and Abbe numbers are retained. The clear radii are 30 mm at r1–r2, 29.1 at r3, 28.8 at r4–r5, 17.5 at r6–r10, 16 at r11, 17 at r12–r13, 18 at r14–r16, 17 at r17, 14.2 at r18, 15 at r19–r21, 15.5 at r22–r23 and 10.7 at r24. Where Fig. 3 draws a rim smaller than the f/5 axial beam (the front group and the bevelled r24), the beam height sets the value. The smallest edge thickness is 1.04 mm (L6), the largest rim angle 29.7° (r17) and the largest ratio of gap closure to axial separation is 0.895 (r18–r19), below the model's 0.9 limit.

At the variator, 20 mm rims would make the r7–r8 surfaces overlap; 17.5 mm fits the printed spacings. At r18–r19 the clearance limit is about 14.234 mm, and the printed spheres intersect at 14.88 mm from the axis, below the 15.00 mm height needed by the f/5 marginal ray. No choice of clear radius can transmit that ray through the printed prescription without overlapping glass.

The nominal f/5 beam is clipped at r18. With the current rims the transmitted axial beam is about f/5.28 at the most restricted zoom position; f/5.6 and slower clear on axis. The patent and marketed aperture remain f/5. The paraxial fixed-iris pupil f-number is about f/4.9832; this describes the iris rather than the beam admitted by all lens rims. Axial clearance at smaller apertures does not imply an unvignetted off-axis pupil.

The clear apertures cause off-axis vignetting as well as axial clipping. Sampled ray paths do not establish transmission for every pupil point, field angle or zoom position, or enumerate every possible chief-ray path. Numerical MTF on a clipped pupil does not establish a fully transmitted f/5 beam or measured production performance. Earlier numerical MTF results used different clear radii and do not establish convergence for the current geometry. The source image height of 21.6 mm, geometric half-diagonal of 21.63330765 mm and model format reference of 21.65 mm are distinct.
