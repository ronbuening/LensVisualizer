# CANON EF 50mm f/1.4 USM

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP H06-242370 A, 大口径レンズ (_Large-aperture lens_)\
**Inventors:** Masatake Kato\
**Assignee:** Canon Inc.\
**Filed:** 17 February 1993\
**Published:** 2 September 1994\
**Example details:** Numerical Example 2 (数値実施例2), ¶0023, Figures 2 and 6. Multiplying the printed radii and axial spacings by 50 converts the prescription from unit focal length to millimetres.

Canon introduced the EF 50mm f/1.4 USM in June 1993, four months after filing the patent. Its construction drawing shows the same seven elements in six groups as Example 2. In both drawings, two positive menisci precede the flanged negative meniscus in front of the stop. Behind it, a cemented pair with a nearly flat interface is followed by a meniscus convex to the image and a biconvex final element. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/ef318.html) · [Canon construction drawing](https://global.canon/ja/c-museum/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/ef318-lens-construction.gif)

The best clue for choosing among the four examples is the first air space. Example 2 gives the first two elements 1.015 mm of separation at the 50 mm scale. The other three put them only 0.146 mm apart, and Example 1 also ends with a meniscus. Fitting the 13 axial vertices to Canon's drawing gives an RMS difference of 0.84 pixel for Example 2, compared with 1.39, 1.26 and 1.22 pixels for Examples 1, 3 and 4. Example 2 is the most likely published design for the EF lens. Canon does not identify a numbered example in its product specifications.

## Optical Architecture

Kato divides this spherical double Gauss at the diaphragm. The three air-spaced front elements distribute the converging power between L1 and L2, then reduce it at L3. Behind the stop, the negative L4/L5 doublet and two positive singlets give the rear group greater converging power. This arrangement leaves 37.575 mm between the last vertex and the paraxial image at infinity, about three quarters of the 50 mm focal length. Kato relates that clearance to the space needed behind an SLR lens in ¶0013–0015.

The patent prints 13 radii, 12 axial intervals and seven pairs of glass constants. It locates the stop within the 15.122 mm air space between R6 and R7. The division of that space, the clear radii and the iris opening are estimates. The final image distance is calculated from the prescription. All four example tables print f/1.14, but ¶0001, ¶0020 and Figures 5–8 identify the design aperture as f/1.4. At f/1.14 the axial marginal ray misses R6. The f/1.4 prescription gives 49.9999 mm effective focal length, in agreement with Canon's 50 mm f/1.4 specification.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are calculated for each element in air. The catalog names identify Ohara equivalents for the printed d-line index and Abbe number.

### L1 — Positive meniscus

$n_d = 1.77250$, $\nu_d = 49.6$. S-LAH66 equivalent, $f = +76.09$ mm.

The convex front surface starts the convergence. The much flatter rear face and the 1.015 mm gap behind it distinguish the front outline of Example 2 in Canon's drawing.

### L2 — Positive meniscus

$n_d = 1.83481$, $\nu_d = 42.7$. S-LAH55 equivalent, $f = +72.32$ mm.

L2 shares the front positive power nearly equally with L1. Their focal-length ratio of 0.951 falls within the patent's 0.92–1.42 interval for balancing back focus against barrel distortion.

### L3 — Negative meniscus

$n_d = 1.64769$, $\nu_d = 33.8$. S-TIM22 equivalent, $f = -37.66$ mm.

Its steep rear face forms the first of the two concave surfaces facing the stop. Paragraphs 0019–0020 relate their curvatures to the balance between sagittal flare, spherical aberration and astigmatism.

### L4 and L5 — Cemented doublet

- **L4, biconcave negative.** $n_d = 1.80518$, $\nu_d = 25.4$. S-TIH6 equivalent, $f = -21.68$ mm.
- **L5, biconvex positive.** $n_d = 1.75700$, $\nu_d = 47.8$. S-LAM54 equivalent, $f = +40.74$ mm.

The concave front of L4 faces L3 across the stop. At the common surface R8, the radius is +508.77 mm, which accounts for the almost straight line through the doublet in Canon's section. Positive L5 offsets part of L4's stronger negative power. The cemented pair has a focal length of −59.83 mm in air.

### L6 — Positive meniscus

$n_d = 1.88300$, $\nu_d = 40.8$. S-LAH58 equivalent, $f = +51.83$ mm.

L6 supplies the stronger of the two positive singlets behind the doublet. Its convex rear surface bends the rays toward the image, and its glass has the highest index in the prescription.

### L7 — Biconvex positive

$n_d = 1.80400$, $\nu_d = 46.6$. S-LAH65V equivalent, $f = +77.76$ mm.

The radii are nearly equal in magnitude, +124.391 and −124.393 mm. L7 adds the final convergence after L6. Both curvatures are visible in the production drawing.

## Glass Selection

Kato calls for an average index above 1.79 in the three positive elements behind the stop. Their mean is 1.815 in Example 2. Paragraphs 0016–0017 explain the reason for the lower bound. At lower indices the same positive power would require steeper surfaces, increasing spherical aberration and the Petzval sum.

Across the cemented pair, the Abbe number rises from 25.4 in the negative element to 47.8 in its positive partner. Canon describes two high-refraction elements in the product, without specifying their positions. The prescription is entirely spherical, and neither source identifies an ED element. All seven Ohara equivalents reproduce the printed glass constants at their stated precision. The original suppliers and melts are unknown.

## Focus Mechanism

Canon specifies overall linear extension driven by a micro ultrasonic motor. The entire optical assembly moves toward the subject for close focus, preserving its internal air spaces. Canon gives a minimum focusing distance of 0.45 m and maximum magnification of 0.15×. Example 2 gives spacings only at infinity, so the model ends there. [Canon U.S.A. focusing specifications](https://www.usa.canon.com/support/p/ef-50mm-f-1-4-usm) · [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/ef318.html)

## Field and Illumination

At the patent's 22.7° half-field the chief ray reaches an image height of 20.52 mm, giving −1.87% distortion. Figure 6 shows about −1.9%. The chief ray reaches the 36 × 24 mm frame corner at 23.82°. At f/1.4, all of the sampled axial pupil transmits, compared with 38.5% at 20.9° and 24.3% at the corner. These are geometric pupil fractions for the estimated clear radii. The patent gives no measured illumination curve.
