# Canon EF 28–70 mm f/3.5–4.5 II — Patent Optical Analysis

## Selected source and production qualification

**Patent:** JP H01-189622 A\
**Application Number:** JP S63-15409 (昭63-15409)\
**Filed:** January 26, 1988\
**Published:** July 28, 1989\
**Inventor:** Sadatoshi Takahashi (高橋貞利)\
**Applicant:** Canon Inc. (キヤノン株式会社)\
**Title:** Zoom Lens (ズームレンズ)\
**Example details:** Numerical Example 1, PDF pages 4–5, Figure 1 on page 6

The selected original is JP H01-189622 A, Numerical Example 1, by Sadatoshi Takahashi / Canon Inc. It was filed on
January 26, 1988 as JP63015409A and published July 28, 1989. The supplied original prescription is on PDF page 4,
printed page 192, with group-power/asphere continuation on PDF page 5, printed page 193. Figure 1 on page 6 is the
matching section; Figure 3 on page 7 gives its wide/middle/tele aberrations. This review uses that original only.

The Canon EF 28–70 mm f/3.5–4.5 II is a selected production correlate: ten elements in nine groups, one aspherical
element, three-group zoom architecture, front-group focusing, 0.39 m MFD and approximately 0.22× maximum
magnification. The January 1988 filing and June 1988 introduction are chronologically compatible. These correspondences
do not prove a factory prescription. The current model contains an explicit authored reconstruction of two source
radii, and all quantitative results below refer to that qualified model rather than a literal complete transcription.
No production scaling is applied. Marketing 28–70 mm/f3.5–4.5 remains separate from source and model values.

## Unresolved printed radii and retained authored reconstruction

The original prints R17 = −11.61 mm and R21 = −141.79 mm. The pre-existing data instead uses −36.74 and −37.76 mm.
Those adopted values came from an author fit to the three focal targets and G2 power; no new fit is made in this
review. The source summary and its group-power conditions reject the printed block, but do not uniquely establish
these exact replacements. Structured unresolved source-erratum notes disclose both pairs and their consequences.

Literal G2 has f = 20.140491 mm instead of printed 36.27 mm, outside the claimed 0.48 < f2/FT < 0.59 interval. The
retained model gives 36.262519 mm. Literal G3 is negative, f ≈ −92.618253 mm, whereas the source describes a weak
positive rear group and the authored model gives +719.059284 mm. At the retained inferred apertures, a literal trial
also produces −1.474 mm L8 edge thickness. This supports rejecting that literal trial; an inferred aperture failure
is not proof of the exact original drafting values. The source contradiction remains queued for independent evidence.

Printed condition 7 R3/FT fails both numerical examples. The reciprocal FT/R3 fits the stated interval, but is an
explicit interpretation hypothesis, not an authoritative correction. The literal R3 value remains unchanged.
The older notes’ later-grant reference is not counted as a fresh family-publication check in this review.

## Architecture, source surfaces and controls

G1 is negative (L1–L3); G2 is positive (L4–L8) and carries the stop; G3 is the fixed rear cemented doublet L9/L10.
The adopted group focal lengths are approximately −45.001001, +36.262519 and +719.059284 mm. G2 moves objectward
through zoom while G1 follows a reversing locus and performs front-group focusing. The published flare-cutter R18
is an optically neutral plane with no modeled aperture; it remains omitted, preserving D17 + D18 as 2.675/19.743/34.350 mm.
No glass, filter or rear plate is omitted by that bookkeeping transformation.

The stop coordinate is source-backed. The numerical heading and Figure 3(A/B/C) explicitly print f/3.6, f/4.2 and
f/4.6. These now replace the earlier marketed/inferred 3.5/4.0/4.5 iris schedule. In particular, the middle aperture
is directly printed in Figure 3(B), not inferred. The physical stop diameter is derived; no source clear stop diameter
is claimed. All source gaps, the stop coordinate and the existing finite-focus D6 controls remain unchanged.

## Asphere and native outline

R4 is stored as surface 4A, the rear of L2. Its standard spherical base has K = 0, with A4 = −3.886e−6,
A6 = −1.883e−9 and A8 = −3.390e−11; higher terms are zero. No scaling or coefficient adjustment is made. At the
retained 14.6 mm cap, its sag is 1.993209 mm versus 2.258003 mm for the sphere, a−0.264794 mm departure. Manufacturing
method and any resin chemistry are not established from those coefficients.

Native Figure 1 is a 347 dpi scan. Its first/last vertices span about 960 px for 80.157 mm, approximately 12 px/mm; local
schematic scale varies. Heights are inferred with about±0.5–1 mm drawing uncertainty. The S2 air-facing shoulder is
resolved; sd14.5 mm remains the optical cap while rimExtensionSd draws to 18.8 mm. The front cap becomes 18.8 mm,
matching the relative native front outline. An S2/S3 cap trial at 15.2 mm fails the unchanged gap guard, so their
existing 14.5 mm caps remain. Other revised heights follow the native curves without introducing opaque interface
lands or unsupported blocks. No concave-end cut is inferred.
