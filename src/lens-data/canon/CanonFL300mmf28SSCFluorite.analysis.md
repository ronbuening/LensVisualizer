# CANON FL 300mm f/2.8 S.S.C. FLUORITE — US 3,868,174, Example 1

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 3,868,174 A  
**Application Number:** US 382,284  
**Priority:** 3 August 1972, Japan 47-77308  
**Filed:** 24 July 1973  
**Granted:** 25 February 1975  
**Inventor:** Hideo Yokota (printed "Yakota" on the US cover)  
**Assignee:** Canon Inc. (printed "Canon Kobushiki Kaisha" on the US cover)  
**Title:** Telephoto Type Objective of Large Relative Aperture Ratio  
**Embodiment analyzed:** Example 1 (description and claim 3), f = 100 scaled uniformly ×3 to 300 mm.

The association with the FL 300 mm f/2.8 S.S.C. Fluorite of February 1974 is probable; the patent example is not an authenticated factory prescription.

1. Example 1 is the only example with a fluorspar element (claim 1), and it matches the product's f/2.8 aperture and field: the patent's 2ω = 8.24° agrees with Canon's published 8°15' diagonal. Canon applied in Japan in August 1972, eighteen months before the FL launch. Examples 2 and 3 have six elements but no fluorite (their positive glass is nd 1.61800, νd 63.4).
2. Canon's Camera Museum lists the FL 300 mm f/2.8 S.S.C. Fluorite at February 1974, 6 elements in 5 groups, 12 diaphragm blades, f/32 minimum aperture and 3.5 m minimum focus. The Canon system brochure (PDF page 37) shows the lens only as an external cutaway, which gives no countable section.
3. **Element count.** Canon counts 6 elements in 5 groups; the patent example, and this model, have 5 elements in 4 groups. The most likely sixth element is the mandatory 34 mm rear drop-in filter: the FD-version manual states that the lens "is designed to include the filter" and must always carry one (a clear REGULAR 1× when no filter effect is wanted), and the patent itself reserves space for a filter between the rear group and the image. Canon's published counts for its later super-telephotos also include the drop-in filter (the EF 200 mm f/2L IS USM's 17 elements in 12 groups, for example). Neither source gives the filter's thickness or position, so the model omits it. The alternative reading is a six-element production derivative: Examples 2 and 3 are themselves six elements in five groups, but neither uses fluorite.
4. The later FD 300 mm f/2.8 S.S.C. Fluorite (October 1975) keeps the 6/5 count, 3.5 m minimum focus and 112 × 230 mm size, but its manual attributes improved field curvature and coma to new glass types, so the FD optics need not be identical to the FL.

Description and claim 3 agree in every R, D, N and V value. The aberration-coefficient tables differ in two cells: claim row 2, P prints 0.4078 against the body's 0.4708, and the claim total for II prints +0.1038 against the body's −0.1038. Neither affects the prescription.

## Optical Architecture

The design is a telephoto: a positive front group of three separated singlets (positive–negative–positive) and a negative rear cemented doublet. The patent calls the elements components I to V: I, II and III form the front lens group, and IV and V, cemented together in Example 1, form the rear lens group. The diagram labels the elements I–V and brackets the two groups as FRONT and REAR; L1–L5 below are the same elements in order. The long airspace after L3 supplies the telephoto separation and holds the iris. The element focal lengths below are for each element alone in air; the rear doublet's combined focal length is −212.73 mm at the ×3 scale.

The scaled prescription gives EFL 299.964 mm and a paraxial back focal distance of 71.196 mm. The model's image plane sits at the printed back focus, 23.73 × 3 = 71.19 mm; the 0.006 mm difference is within the patent's printed precision. The total track from the first vertex to the image is 270.592 mm against the printed L = 90.20 × 3 = 270.60 mm, a telephoto ratio of 0.90.

All nine refracting surfaces are spherical, and R8 is the single cemented interface. The patent prints no clear semi-diameters. The model's 55 mm on L1, 50 mm on L2 and L3, and 14.5 mm on the rear doublet follow FIG. 1, which draws L1 tallest, L2 and L3 at about the same height and the rear doublet square-cut on one height. The patent prints no stop coordinate; FIG. 1 draws the diaphragm about three quarters of the way along D6, and the model places it at 75 % (92.551 mm after r6, 30.850 mm before r7).

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 (component I) — Biconvex Positive Fluorspar Singlet

nd = 1.43387, νd = 95.1. Glass: fluorspar (CaF2), named in claim 1. f = +175.81 mm.

The low-index, very-low-dispersion front singlet is the stronger of the two positive elements. The description names fluorite among the positive-element materials that reduce the front group's secondary spectrum, and claim 1 specifies fluorspar for this element. The patent prints only nd and νd; the model's dispersion curve is the catalog CaF2 fit.

### L2 (component II) — Biconcave Negative Singlet

nd = 1.78590, νd = 44.2. Glass: S-LAH51 (OHARA) catalog equivalent; the production melt is unidentified. f = −155.70 mm.

This high-index negative element follows the fluorite collector and balances the front group's primary color against L1 and L3, as the patent's condition (2) on the Abbe-number difference requires. Its first surface, R3, is the surface the patent identifies as strongly affecting spherical aberration and astigmatism.

### L3 (component III) — Biconvex Positive Singlet

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.1. Glass: S-FSL5 (OHARA) catalog equivalent; the production melt is unidentified. f = +194.17 mm.

The second positive element completes the front group. With L1 it gives the patent's positive-element mean νd of 82.6, against 44.2 for L2. Condition (4) limits the ratio of its two radii to weaken the rear surface's contribution to spherical aberration.

### L4 (component IV) — Negative Meniscus, Rear Cemented Component

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Glass: K-BK7 (SUMITA) catalog equivalent; the production melt is unidentified. f = −124.66 mm.

This thin negative meniscus, concave toward the object, begins the rear group and is cemented to L5. Condition (5) holds its front radius between 0.14f and 0.17f (here 0.160f) so that the rear group corrects the spherical aberration left under-corrected by the front group without over-correcting coma.

### L5 (component V) — Positive Meniscus, Rear Cemented Component

nd = 1.72151, νd = 29.2. Glass: S-TIH18 (OHARA) catalog equivalent; the production melt is unidentified. f = +317.11 mm.

The dispersive positive meniscus shares R8 with L4. The pair keeps a net negative power, giving the telephoto rear group, while the flint–crown combination corrects the rear group's color.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent prints nd and νd on the d line. Each of L2–L5 has a present-day catalog glass at the same coordinates: Ohara S-LAH51 (1.785896 / 44.20) for L2, Ohara S-FSL5 (1.48749 / 70.24) for L3, Sumita K-BK7 (1.51633 / 64.10) for L4 and Ohara S-TIH18 (1.721507 / 29.23) for L5. Each agrees with the printed index to the patent's precision and with the printed Abbe number within 0.14. The model uses their Sellmeier curves for dispersion. They are equivalents by coordinates, not the 1970s melts, which remain unidentified; the partial dispersions of the original glasses may differ slightly.

L1's dispersion comes from the catalog CaF2 Sellmeier fit (nd 1.43385 against the printed 1.43387); the d-line trace uses the printed value.

All five of the patent's conditions hold for Example 1: the total length lies between 0.86f and f; the positive-minus-negative Abbe difference in the front group is 38.4 (> 20); the ratio of R2 to R3 lies between 1 and 1.34; the ratio of L3's radii lies between −0.3 and 0.4; and L4's front radius lies between 0.14f and 0.17f. The model's Petzval sum, 0.1325/f, matches the patent's tabulated ΣP of 0.1324.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent describes only the infinity state and prints no close-focus spacing, so the model is infinity-only and its focus control does not move any element. The production lens focuses to 3.5 m (maximum magnification 0.103×, per Canon); the FD version's manual describes a helicoid with a 278° focusing rotation.

## Aspherical Surfaces

None. Every surface is spherical.

## Model Scope and Limitations

- The f/2.8 entrance pupil (53.57 mm radius) is limited by the iris; the axial beam clears every element rim at full aperture.
- At the patent's 4.12° half-field, the chief ray reaches the image at 21.79 mm. At f/2.8 about half of the full-aperture bundle reaches the frame corner in a real-ray trace, the rest being cut by the L1 rim and the rear-doublet rims. Because the rims are estimates, this corner illumination is indicative, not a production measurement.
- The mandatory rear drop-in filter is not modeled; a plate of unknown thickness would lengthen the back focus slightly.
- Only the infinity state is modeled. Dispersion away from the d line follows catalog equivalents for L2–L5, not the original melts.
