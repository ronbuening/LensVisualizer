## Optical Architecture

The five powered elements form a positive–negative–positive front sequence and a net-negative rear cemented doublet. The long airspace after L3 supplies the telephoto separation and room for an iris. The separate thick-element focal lengths below describe isolation in air, not additive in-situ powers. The rear doublet's combined standalone EFL is −212.728871 mm at the conditional ×3 scale.

The unaltered scaled prescription computes EFL 299.964440359 mm, rather than exactly 300 mm. Its Gaussian back focal distance is 71.195558331 mm. The actual primary image interval remains the **printed** BF 23.73 × 3 = 71.19 mm; the +0.005558331 mm Gaussian residual is disclosed and lies within the source's scaled last-place precision. No radius or thickness is adjusted to eliminate it. The first-to-last vertex span is 199.4022 mm; the printed-plane total track is 270.5922 mm, versus printed 90.20 × 3 = 270.60 mm.

All nine refracting faces are spherical. R8 is the one shared L4/L5 cemented interface. The physical clear radii are inferred, not source dimensions: 55 mm on L1, 50 mm on L2 and L3, and 18 mm on each rear-doublet face. The iris is in the depicted D6 airspace at an explicitly reconstructed 75% split, 92.551275 mm after r6 and 30.850425 mm before r7. Neither that percentage nor those clear radii are measured factory data. The source gives no numerical stop coordinate.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Biconvex Positive Fluorspar Singlet

The front low-index, very-low-dispersion positive singlet supplies collecting power. ### L2 — Biconcave Negative Singlet

nd = 1.78590, νd = 44.2. Glass: unidentified historical melt; modern coordinate-compatible substitutes are not adopted. f = −155.702684 mm.

This negative high-index element follows the fluorite collector. The source's front-group dispersion inequality makes its lower Abbe number relevant to primary chromatic balancing against L1 and L3. Its exact separate contribution to every Seidel aberration is not inferred from a simple lens-shape label.

### L3 — Biconvex Positive Singlet

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.1. Glass: unidentified historical melt; modern coordinate-compatible substitutes are not adopted. f = +194.166682 mm.

The third positive element completes the widely separated front power arrangement. Its relatively high Abbe number contributes to the source's positive-element mean νd of 82.6, compared with 44.2 for L2. The lack of partial-dispersion data for this element is a real limit on the runtime's photopic MTF, even though the front fluorite obtains a compatible spectral proxy.

### L4 — Negative Meniscus, Rear Cemented Component

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Glass: unidentified historical melt; modern coordinate-compatible substitutes are not adopted. f = −124.664703 mm.

This thin negative meniscus begins the rear group and is cemented directly to L5. The source constrains the magnitude of its front radius relative to focal length. Its listed focal length is recalculated with air on both sides. An independent audit's earlier optional value had retained the following cemented medium; that historical output is preserved with an additive isolated-element correction, not silently overwritten.

### L5 — Positive Meniscus, Rear Cemented Component

nd = 1.72151, νd = 29.2. Glass: unidentified historical melt; modern coordinate-compatible substitutes are not adopted. f = +317.109759 mm.

The more dispersive positive rear element shares r8 with L4. Together they retain net-negative group power, despite this element's positive isolated power. The doublet supplies the rear telephoto action while redistributing color and field-related aberrations. No unsupported claim of a particular historical glass trade name is made.
