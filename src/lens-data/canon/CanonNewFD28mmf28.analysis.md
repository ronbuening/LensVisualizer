# CANON NEW FD 28mm f/2.8

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 4,176,914, _Compact Retrofocus Wide Angle Objective_\
**Inventors:** Kazuo Fujibayashi\
**Assignee:** Canon Inc. (printed as Canon Kabushiki Kaisha)\
**Priority:** May 26, 1977, Japan\
**Filed:** May 17, 1978\
**Published:** December 4, 1979\
**Example details:** Figure 1 and Table 1 give the patent’s single example. Table 2 gives its aberration coefficients.

Canon introduced the New FD 28mm f/2.8 in June 1979. Canon lists seven elements in seven groups, a five-blade diaphragm, f/22 minimum aperture, 0.3 m closest focus, 0.13× maximum magnification and a 75° angle of view. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/nfd203.html) · [Canon FD Lens Instructions, p. 28](https://flynngraphics.ca/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/FD-Lens-Instructions-for-Web.pdf) MIR lists Super Spectra Coating. [MIR](https://mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/canon/fdresources/fdlenses/28mm.htm) Fujibayashi’s example has seven air-spaced spherical elements, the same f/2.8 aperture and a 76° field. Neither the patent nor Canon identifies an aspherical or extra-low-dispersion element.

The thick biconcave fifth element distinguishes this design from the earlier FD 28mm f/2.8 S.C., which also has seven elements in seven groups. In the New FD cutaway published by MIR, this element occupies much of the space between the two adjacent positive lenses. Its center thickness is 4.42 mm in Fujibayashi’s prescription and 0.98 mm in Example 2 of Kawamura’s earlier US 4,046,459. Aligning the 14 surface vertices to the cutaway at one scale gives an RMS difference of 0.79 pixels for Fujibayashi and 4.20 pixels for Kawamura. This strongly supports the New FD identification. The original publisher of the cutaway is unknown, and Canon’s product entry gives no optical section.

The New FD lens followed the FD 28mm f/2.8 S.C., which Canon marketed from March 1975, with a revised version in April 1977. Canon lists a 55 mm filter thread for that lens and a length of 49 mm, or 47.2 mm for the revised version. The New FD lens takes 52 mm filters and is 40 mm long. [Canon Camera Museum, FD28mm f/2.8 S.C.](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd136.html) · [Canon Camera Museum, New FD28mm f/2.8](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/nfd203.html) Column 1 of the patent starts from the US 4,046,459 lens shortened by 10% in length, with a smaller front member, and corrects the sagittal halo that this introduces. MIR describes the earlier and New FD 28 mm lenses as optically unchanged apart from cosmetic changes. [MIR](https://mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/canon/fdresources/fdlenses/28mm.htm) The shorter barrel, the smaller filter thread and the cutaway comparison above do not support that for the f/2.8.

## Optical Architecture

The front two negative menisci spread the beam. The positive lenses farther back bring it to focus 35.63 mm behind the last surface, leaving more clearance for the reflex mirror than the lens’s 28.01 mm effective focal length. Fujibayashi describes a trade-off in column 1. Bringing the front menisci close together shortens the barrel and reduces the front diameter, but makes halo from sagittal rays harder to correct. The remaining five lenses balance that aberration.

Table 1 uses a focal length of 100. Multiplying every length by 0.28 gives the 28 mm production scale. The distance between the first and last vertices is 39.1776 mm. The printed back focus places the film 35.644 mm behind the last surface, 0.0132 mm beyond the paraxial focus of the rounded prescription.

The patent places the diaphragm between L3 and L4 but prints neither its position nor its diameter, and it gives no clear apertures. The iris plane, 20.72 mm behind the front vertex, is estimated from the MIR cutaway, and its 6.977 mm radius is calculated from the nominal f/2.8. The rims are read from Figure 1. At the four surfaces of L4 and L5 they are widened by 0.05 to 0.23 mm so that the full f/2.8 axial beam passes. The chief ray reaches the corner of the 36 × 24 mm frame, where calculated relative illumination is about 21% of its on-axis value.

## Glass Identification and Selection

Column 2 says the indices are for the sodium D line, but the printed values are helium d-line (587.6 nm) values. Five Ohara catalog glasses reproduce all seven printed indices within 0.000006 and their Abbe numbers within 0.06 at the d line, while at the sodium D wavelength (589.3 nm) the same glasses read 0.00009 to 0.00027 lower. The table’s lowercase νd heading fits the same reading. The model therefore stores d-line values, and the catalog equivalents supply dispersion curves for tracing. The patent gives neither glass trade names nor suppliers, and identifies no extra-low-dispersion element.

## Focus Mechanism

Canon’s FD lens instructions list double-helicoid focusing for this lens. The same table marks the Canon floating system on the New FD 28mm f/2 but not here, indicating that the optical unit moves as a whole. [Canon FD Lens Instructions, pp. 28–29](https://flynngraphics.ca/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/FD-Lens-Instructions-for-Web.pdf) The production lens reaches 0.3 m and 0.13× maximum magnification. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/nfd203.html) The patent gives only the infinity prescription, with no finite object distance, magnification or change of spacing, so the model is focused at infinity. Moving the whole model 3.54 mm forward would focus on an object 0.3 m from the film plane at a calculated 0.126×, which rounds to Canon’s 0.13×. Measured from the front vertex instead, 0.3 m would give 0.094×, so Canon’s closest distance is consistent with a measurement from the film plane. The cutaway locates the iris and the individual elements but does not establish their motion during focusing.
