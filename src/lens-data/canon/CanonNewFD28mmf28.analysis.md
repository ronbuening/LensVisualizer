# CANON NEW FD 28mm f/2.8

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 4,176,914, _Compact Retrofocus Wide Angle Objective_\
**Inventors:** Kazuo Fujibayashi\
**Assignee:** Canon Inc. (printed as Canon Kabushiki Kaisha)\
**Priority:** May 26, 1977, Japan\
**Filed:** May 17, 1978\
**Published:** December 4, 1979\
**Example details:** Figure 1 and Table 1 give the patent’s single example. Table 2 gives its aberration coefficients.

Canon introduced the New FD 28mm f/2.8 in June 1979. Canon lists seven elements in seven groups, a five-blade diaphragm and a 75° diagonal field. Fujibayashi’s example has seven air-spaced spherical elements, the same f/2.8 aperture and a 76° field. Neither the patent nor Canon’s specification identifies an ED element.

The thick biconcave fifth element distinguishes this design from the earlier FD 28mm f/2.8 S.C., which also has seven elements in seven groups. In the New FD cutaway published by MIR, this element occupies much of the space between the two adjacent positive lenses. Its center thickness is 4.42 mm in Fujibayashi’s prescription and 0.98 mm in Example 2 of Kawamura’s earlier US 4,046,459. Aligning the 14 surface vertices to the cutaway at one scale gives an RMS difference of 0.79 pixels for Fujibayashi and 4.20 pixels for Kawamura. This strongly supports the New FD identification. The original publisher of the cutaway is unknown, and Canon’s product entry gives no optical section.

## Optical Architecture

The front two negative menisci spread the beam. The positive lenses farther back bring it to focus 35.63 mm behind the last surface, leaving more clearance for the reflex mirror than the lens’s 28.01 mm effective focal length. Fujibayashi describes a trade-off in column 1. Bringing the front menisci close together shortens the barrel and reduces the front diameter, but makes halo from sagittal rays harder to correct. The remaining five lenses balance that aberration.

Table 1 uses a focal length of 100. Multiplying every length by 0.28 gives the 28 mm production scale. The distance between the first and last vertices is 39.1776 mm. The printed back focus places the film 35.644 mm behind the last surface, 0.0132 mm beyond the paraxial focus of the rounded prescription.

The patent places the diaphragm between L3 and L4. The MIR cutaway suggests an iris plane 20.72 mm behind the front vertex. The nominal f/2.8 aperture gives an iris radius of 6.977 mm there. Figure 1 provides estimates of the refracting rims. At the four surfaces of L4 and L5, the radii allow just enough extra clearance for the nominal axial marginal ray. The chief ray reaches the corner of the 36 × 24 mm frame, where calculated relative illumination is about 21% of its on-axis value.

## Glass Identification and Selection

Column 2 names the sodium D line for the indices, but the values are helium d-line catalog values. Five Ohara catalog glasses reproduce all seven printed indices within 0.000005 and their Abbe numbers within 0.06 at the d line. At the sodium D wavelength the same glasses read 0.00009 to 0.00028 lower. The table header also prints a lowercase νd. The model therefore stores d-line values, and the catalog equivalents supply dispersion curves for tracing. The patent gives neither glass trade names nor suppliers, and identifies no extra-low-dispersion element.

## Focus Mechanism

Canon’s FD lens instructions specify double-helicoid focusing. The production lens reaches 0.3 m and 0.13× maximum magnification. The patent gives only the infinity prescription, with no finite object distance, magnification or change of spacing. The model therefore shows the infinity setting. The cutaway locates the iris and the individual elements but does not establish their motion during focusing.
