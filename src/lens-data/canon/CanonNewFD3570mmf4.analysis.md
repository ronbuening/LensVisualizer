# CANON NEW FD 35-70mm f/4

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 4,299,452, _Zoom Lens Having Two Movable Lens Groups_\
**Inventors:** Keiji Ikemori\
**Assignee:** Canon Inc. (Canon Kabushiki Kaisha on the patent)\
**Priority:** 25 April 1977, Japan\
**Filed:** 24 April 1978\
**Published:** 10 November 1981\
**Example details:** Embodiment 1, Figure 1 and aberration plots 2A–2I.

Canon introduced the manual-focus New FD 35–70mm f/4 in June 1979. Its production cutaway shows three air-spaced elements in front and five behind, with the diaphragm between the fifth and sixth elements. The curvatures and air spaces follow Figure 1, including the nearly flat front of the second element and the narrow gap behind the iris-side positive meniscus. Canon lists eight elements in eight groups. Neither drawing identifies an ED element or an asphere, and all sixteen patent surfaces are spherical. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/nfd243.html) · [Production cutaway, reproduced by MIR](https://mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/canon/fdresources/fdlenses/fdzooms/3570mmf4optic.jpg)

The other two embodiments also use eight elements. Comparing the sixteen axial vertices with the production cutaway gives an RMS difference of 0.70 pixel for Embodiment 1, against 1.68 and 1.76 pixels for Embodiments 2 and 3. Only Embodiment 1 has the production aperture of f/4. The alternatives are f/3.5 and f/4.5. This strongly identifies Embodiment 1 with the production lens. The cutaway does not establish the exact production radii or the clear aperture of each surface. [US 4,299,452](https://patents.justia.com/patent/4299452)

## Optical Architecture

Figure 1 divides the eight singlets into two moving assemblies. Group I has negative power (−58.00 mm) and group II has positive power (+39.26 mm). Their separation sets the focal length. From wide to telephoto the rear assembly advances 22.00 mm toward the subject. The front assembly initially moves toward the film, then reverses direction near 58 mm. This allows the zoom to hold the image plane fixed as the intergroup air space closes from 30.4884 to 0.4752 mm.

All patent lengths use a scale of 0.36 mm per normalized unit. The table gives the intergroup gap at three focal lengths. The rear-surface-to-film distances below are calculated for paraxial infinity focus. Twelve additional zoom positions keep the film within 0.020 mm of that focus between the printed positions.

Canon marketed the lens as a 35–70 mm zoom with a diagonal angle of view of 63°–34°. The patent gives 62°–35.05° and f/4 throughout. Its wide-end first-surface-to-film distance scales to 116.640 mm, 0.113 mm longer than the distance to the calculated paraxial image. ## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are those of the individual elements in air.

### L1 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.65844, νd = 50.9. Ohara S-BSM25 equivalent. f = −79.68 mm.

The concave rear face is the stronger surface. Ikemori credits the bending of this front negative lens with reducing barrel distortion at the wide end and helping control astigmatism.

### L2 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.65844, νd = 50.9. Ohara S-BSM25 equivalent. f = −57.19 mm.

L2 supplies more negative power than L1. Its front surface is nearly plane. Its rear surface and the front of L3 enclose a meniscus-shaped air space that the patent associates with reducing spherical aberration at telephoto.

### L3 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.75520, νd = 27.5. Ohara S-TIH4 equivalent. f = +81.70 mm.

This dense flint offsets part of the power of the two negative crowns. Its higher dispersion helps correct color within the front group as that group moves for zooming or focusing.

### L4 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.60311, νd = 60.7. Ohara S-BSM14 equivalent. f = +58.46 mm.

The front face provides most of L4's power. Only 0.1512 mm of axial air separates its shallow rear face from L5.

### L5 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.60311, νd = 60.7. Ohara S-BSM14 equivalent. f = +60.39 mm.

L5 completes the positive pair ahead of the diaphragm. Together L4 and L5 have a focal length of +29.90 mm. Concentrating power here helps place the rear group's front principal plane toward the subject.

### L6 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.60311, νd = 60.7. Ohara S-BSM14 equivalent. f = +113.43 mm.

L6 supplies weaker positive power immediately behind the iris. Its focal length is 3.79 times that of L4 and L5 together, the ratio tabulated for this embodiment.

### L7 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Ohara S-TIH6 equivalent. f = −20.20 mm.

This dense flint has the strongest negative power in the lens. The patent assigns it the correction of astigmatism from the preceding three positive elements and of spherical aberration that increases toward telephoto.

### L8 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.63636, νd = 35.4. Ohara S-TIM6 equivalent. f = +39.53 mm.

The final flint returns the beam to convergence. Ikemori describes its function as maintaining wide-end back focal clearance and balancing the residual aberrations of group II.

## Glass Identification

The patent's glass table describes L1–L2 as extra-dense crowns, L3 and L7 as dense flints, L4–L6 as dense crowns, and L8 as flint. The five Ohara equivalents match the printed d-line indices within 0.000002 and the Abbe numbers within 0.06. They supply dispersion curves for the model. The supplier and melts used in the production lens are undocumented.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent describes advancing group I toward the subject for closer focus, independently of group II. The production cutaway shows the corresponding three-element front assembly in a single position, so it gives no independent measure of focusing travel. The patent gives no finite object distance, magnification or corresponding group spacing for Embodiment 1, so the model covers infinity focus only. Canon specifies a minimum focus distance of 0.5 m and maximum magnification of 0.15× for the production lens. It has six diaphragm blades and stops down to f/22. ## Clear Aperture at Telephoto

The patent locates the iris between R10 and R11. Its midpoint placement and the opening calculated for f/4 are estimates. Figure 1 supplies the other clear-radius estimates, with allowance for axial rays at R7/R8. Figure 1 draws L1/L2 and L6/L7 touching, so the clear radii at R2 and R12/R13 stop just short of where those spherical surfaces meet.

At telephoto, the axial f/4 marginal ray would reach radii of 8.73 and 8.74 mm at R12 and R13. Those two spheres meet at 8.605 mm. Their 8.51 mm clear radii leave 0.021 mm of air at the edge and would clip a full f/4 beam to about f/4.09. The model still passes a chief ray to the corner of a 36 × 24 mm frame at every zoom position. The production cutaway gives no dimension for this edge.
