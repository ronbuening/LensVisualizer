# CANON NEW FD 28mm f/2.8 — audit

## Source

Kazuo Fujibayashi’s US 4,176,914, _Compact Retrofocus Wide Angle Objective_, was granted to Canon Kabushiki Kaisha on December 4, 1979. Figure 1 shows the layout. Column 1 describes one example, Table 1 gives its prescription and Table 2 gives its third-order aberration coefficients. The claim repeats the prescription. [US 4,176,914](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4176914A/en)

## Transcription

All 14 radii with their signs, 13 axial intervals, seven index/Abbe pairs and the final back focus agree with Table 1. The conversion from the printed focal length of 100 to 28 mm is ×0.28 for every length. In particular, both Table 1 and the claim print R4 as +54.902, giving +15.37256 mm. The nearly flat R6 retains its finite curvature. All fourteen surfaces are spherical, and air separates each of the seven elements. Column 2 names the sodium D line, but all seven index/Abbe pairs are helium d-line catalog values (see Glass below).

Table 2 provides an independent check on the radius signs. The normalized Petzval contributions of the first two surfaces are +0.475556 and −0.844094. The sum over the lens is +0.150653, in agreement with the printed values.

At a 21.633 mm image height the chief ray passes every surface. The rear rims vignette the lower part of the corner beam. Relative illumination at the corner is 21.0% of the axial value.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

- **Stop.** Column 1 places the diaphragm between the third and fourth lenses. The cutaway suggests a position 20.72 mm from the first vertex, dividing the 5.5608 mm air gap into 4.1468 and 1.4140 mm. The paraxial f/2.8 aperture gives a 6.977 mm iris radius. A real axial ray entering at 4.826 mm meets that iris edge. Pupil aberration accounts for the difference from the nominal entrance-pupil radius of 5.003 mm.
- **Clear rims.** The 39.1776 mm vertex span sets the scale of Figure 1 at 32.52 pixels/mm. Each reading is the average distance from the axis to the upper and lower ends of the refracting arc. On R2 the clear radius is 9.84 mm and the flat outer land extends to 12.22 mm. The figure readings and applied values differ at four surfaces, as listed below.
- **Glass.** Every printed pair matches an Ohara catalog row at the helium d line: S-BSM14 for L1 and L2, S-TIM35 for L3, S-LAH66 for L4 and L7, S-TIH6 for L5 and S-LAL8 for L6. Indices agree within 0.000005 and Abbe numbers within 0.06. No pair matches a catalog row at the e line. At the sodium D wavelength the same glasses read 0.00009 to 0.00028 lower, so the text's sodium D label does not fit the printed values. The table header prints a lowercase νd. The model stores d-line values; the catalog rows are dispersion equivalents, and the production glasses and supplier remain unknown.

The resulting rims are clear of that ray and of the corner chief ray. The elements have positive edge thickness and the adjoining surfaces clear one another.

## Product Identification

Canon lists seven elements in seven groups for the New FD 28mm f/2.8, introduced in June 1979. The patent and the cutaway agree on the front negative menisci, the nearly plane back of L3, the iris before L4, the thick biconcave L5 and the two positive rear menisci. After alignment at a single scale, the RMS difference at the 14 vertices is 0.79 pixels, compared with 4.20 pixels for the older S.C. prescription. The product identification is strong. The cutaway’s original attribution is unknown. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/nfd203.html) and [MIR cutaway](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/canon/fdresources/fdlenses/28mmf28optic.gif)

## Corrections

### 2026-11-25

Table 1 is reproduced at the stated scale with its printed back focus. Its radii and spacings give an effective focal length 0.052% longer than nominal. Four undimensioned rims use the axial ray heights above. Canon specifies a 75° diagonal field, 0.3 m closest focus and 0.13× maximum magnification. The patent specifies 76° and gives only the infinity setting. [Canon FD lens guide](https://www.galimbertipaolo.it/CanonFD/FDLensGuide1.pdf) and [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/nfd203.html)

## Remaining Questions

Factory dimensions for the clear rims and diaphragm would settle the four differences from Figure 1 and the exact stop position. The patent supplies no near-focus spacings or diaphragm diameter, and the original source of the retail cutaway remains unidentified.
