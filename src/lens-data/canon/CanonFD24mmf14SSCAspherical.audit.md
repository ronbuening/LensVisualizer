# CANON FD 24mm f/1.4 S.S.C. ASPHERICAL — Audit Log

## Source

Kikuo Momiyama, *Large Aperture Wide Angle Lens System Employing an Aspherical Surface*, US 3,992,085, assigned to Canon Kabushiki Kaisha (Canon Inc.). The Japanese priority application 47-66359 dates to July 4, 1972. The US application of February 21, 1975 continued the June 13, 1973 application. The patent issued November 16, 1976. Fig. 1 shows the optical section and Fig. 2 the aberrations. Example 1 appears on PDF page 8, columns 5–6. Claim 8 repeats the prescription. Canon's museum entry gives the construction diagram, launch date, iris specifications and close-focus performance of the S.S.C. lens. Canon’s FD lens instructions and FD sales guide list its 84° field, Super Spectra coating and automatic diaphragm. ## Transcription Check

All 18 signed radii, glass thicknesses, air spaces, ten refractive-index and Abbe-number pairs and the 36.500 mm back focus agree with the printed page. E3 and E4 share surface 6. E7 and E8 share surface 13. The dimensions are in millimetres at the stated 24.5 mm focal length.

Surface 14 is the rear face of E8. The patent adds fourth-, sixth-, eighth- and tenth-order terms to a spherical base, giving a conic constant of zero. Its sag column gives magnitudes. At the last tabulated height of 14.700 mm, the printed coefficients give 2.21322 mm against the table’s 2.21315 mm.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The table gives a 10.681 mm air space between surfaces 11 and 12. Fig. 1 places the iris 3.42 mm behind surface 11, leaving 7.261 mm to surface 12. The calculated iris radius for f/1.4 is 11.20 mm.

The 69.618 mm vertex span sets the scale of Fig. 1, and the drawn radius of surface 1 agrees with it. The clear radii follow the ends of the drawn optical curves, inside the bevels on E4, E6 and E7. E2, E5, E9 and E10 end square at one height on both faces, as drawn. Surface 2 uses 18.9 mm, compared with about 19.2 mm in the drawing, to keep its sag into the next air space below 90% of the gap. A flat land square to the axis continues from that edge to 20.8 mm, where Fig. 1 and Canon's section both show the step on E1 before the bevel to the front rim.

The glass edges retain positive thickness and adjacent elements clear each other. The iris limits the axial beam at f/1.4. The corner chief ray crosses surface 2 at 18.38 mm and clears every rim. ## Product Identification

Canon's section matches Fig. 1 in the shapes of all ten elements, the eight air-spaced groups, both cemented interfaces and the rear asphere on E8. Both drawings show the step on E1 and the bevels on E4 and E7. Relative diameters agree to within about 2% of the front-element diameter. Example 2 reverses the two singlets ahead of the iris, Example 3 places a cemented pair in group II, and Example 4 opens to f/1.2. This strongly supports Example 1 for the FD 24mm f/1.4 S.S.C. Aspherical introduced in March 1975. The New FD 24mm f/1.4L followed in December 1979. ## Corrections, 2026-11-25

- The prescription as first received was limited to f/8 and a ±5° field. The iris passes the full f/1.4 axial beam and the rims clear the chief ray across the patent’s ±42° field. The prescription keeps the printed 24.5 mm scale and 36.500 mm image distance. The small difference in the last sag entry gives no basis for altering the four printed coefficients.
- The iris moved from the assumed midpoint of the 10.681 mm gap to the position in Fig. 1. At the midpoint, the corner chief ray would cross surface 2 at 19.29 mm and miss its 18.9 mm rim.
- The clear radii now follow Fig. 1. The front rim grew from 24.0 to 25.7 mm and surface 3 from 18.8 to 21.0 mm to pass the corner ray. The 18.9 mm rim at surface 2 preserves clearance to surface 3.
- Group labels I–III follow Fig. 1. The labels C1 and C2 mark the two cemented pairs, and 14A marks the aspherical face that Canon's section also shows. The glass equivalents below supply dispersion for the ten elements while preserving the patent’s index and Abbe-number pairs.
- The production specifications are those of the 1975 Canon FD lens for 24 × 36 mm film. Canon lists 24 mm, f/1.4, ten elements in eight groups, eight blades and 0.30 m close focus. The aperture ring carries f/1.4, 2, 2.8, 4, 5.6, 8, 11 and 16. The model retains the single infinity prescription because the patent gives no floating-group spacings. ## Remaining Questions

- The production clear diameters, exact iris position, blade curvature and glass suppliers remain undocumented.
- Canon's section does not identify the elements that move during close focusing or their travel. The patent gives no finite-focus prescription, and its text does not discuss focusing.
- Fig. 2A ends the d-line spherical aberration 0.03 mm in front of paraxial focus at f/1.4, while the printed prescription and aspherical coefficients give 0.02 mm behind it. Near the axis the printed g-line focus lies about 0.05 mm nearer the lens than the catalog equivalents give. The glasses Canon used and their partial dispersions are not documented.
