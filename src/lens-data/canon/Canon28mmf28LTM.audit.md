# CANON 28mm f/2.8 — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,779,239, Four Component High Aperture Wide Angle Objective Lens](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2779239A/en), Hiroshi Ito of Setagaya-ku, Tokyo, assignor to Canon Camera Company, Inc. The application, Serial No. 530,242, was filed August 24, 1955, with Japanese priority of October 1, 1954. The patent was granted January 29, 1957, with two claims. The drawing sheet (PDF p. 1) carries Figure 1, the section, and Figure 2, the d-line aberration curves. The body table in column 2 (p. 2) gives the sole numerical example at f = 1.0, F = 2.8 and a 72° including angle. Claim 2 in columns 3–4 (p. 3) repeats the table. All printed lengths are multiplied by 28 to give millimetres.

Canon's museum entry gives June 1957, six elements in four groups, six diaphragm blades, f/22, 1 m minimum focus, a 40 mm filter size, 48 × 20 mm and 160 g. Canon's period lens guide shows the optical section and gives six elements, a non-collapsible, non-revolving mount, distance scales from 1 m (3.5 ft) to infinity, a 75° angle of view, a magenta coating and 159 g. Its depth-of-field tables for the 28 mm lenses run from f/2.8 to f/22. A review describes the Leica screw mount, click stops from f/2.8 to f/22 and an infinity lock. ## Transcription Check

The ten spherical radii, nine axial intervals and six index and Abbe-number pairs were reread against the page images. After scaling by 28, every value agrees with the body table. Surfaces 2 and 9 are cemented interfaces. Air spaces of 0.056 and 0.028 mm separate the outer doublets from the inner singlets. The positive members of the front and rear doublets have centre thicknesses of 2.324 and 3.668 mm. These thicknesses constrain their usable edges. The patent gives one optical configuration and no rear plate.

The Claim 2 table differs from the body table in two places. It prints d₂ = 0.063 where the body has 0.083, and r₆ = +0.2090 where the body has −0.2090. Claim 1 refers the indices to the helium D line, and Figure 2 is labelled for line d.

The body values satisfy Ito's stated conditions: n₂ − n₁ = 0.0804, ν₂ − ν₁ = 18.1, ν₃ = 28.2, n₄ = 1.6889, ν₄ = 31.1, n₅ − n₆ = 0.0910 and ν₅ − ν₆ = 14.3. The radii r₂ = 0.2465f, r₅ = 0.2021f, |r₆| = 0.2090f and |r₉| = 0.2460f also fall inside their printed bounds. The one exception is the condition printed as 1.30 > n₃ > 1.64 in the description and Claim 1, which no index can meet. Both tables give n₃ = 1.7400.

Four catalog equivalents match the printed glass coordinates within the printed precision. L4a takes Ohara S-LAL11 as a spectral proxy, within the site's spectral-proxy tolerance; the model keeps its printed index. The differences below are catalog value minus patent value. The production suppliers are unidentified.

At the front doublet, surfaces 2 and 3 meet at a radius just above 5.72 mm. At the rear, r₈ and r₉ meet at 6.35 mm, short of the 6.61 mm edge in Figure 1. Canon's section draws the same edge at about 6.1–6.26 mm, scaled from its front rim. The shared rear edge is set at 6.30 mm, between the two drawings, which leaves L4a 0.12 mm thick at its rim. There r₉ reaches a 66.2° surface slope, so the model allows rim slopes up to 67° (`checks.maxRimAngleDeg`). A 6.19 mm edge, the largest within the usual 64.2° limit, would pass about 4 points less of the pupil at the infinity corner and 3–5 points less between 29° and 35°. Extending both members of either pair to the drawn radii makes the printed surfaces cross. Extending r₂, r₃ or r₈ separately to its drawn radius leaves corner illumination at 5.7%. Figure 1 shows a common edge on each doublet.

The clear radius at surface 5 chiefly affects intermediate fields. With the other radii fixed, 3.68 mm admits 4.4 percentage points less of the pupil near 17° than 3.90 mm. A radius of 3.73 mm is within the drawing uncertainty and limits the largest loss to 2.9 points. The change has no measurable effect at the format corner. At infinity the corner chief ray clears every rim by at least 0.35 mm. The axial pupil transmits fully. At the infinity corner, the entrance rim, cemented interface 2 and surfaces 6–9 intercept off-axis rays. The 16.1% geometric corner transmission and 5.7% relative illumination are calculated at f/2.8. At 1 m the corner transmission is 22.9%.

The patent gives only the infinity state. Both film gaps come from paraxial focus. At infinity the image plane is 0.00004 mm in front of the calculated focus. Moving the entire lens forward 0.8295 mm brings a subject 1 m from the film into focus, with the film plane 0.00003 mm in front of the calculated image. Canon's sources do not say whether the 1 m is measured from the film or from the lens front; the model takes it from the film. The 1 m corner chief ray clears every rim.

## Product Identification

Canon marketed the six-element, four-group 28mm f/2.8 in June 1957. Its labelled section and Figure 1 share the outer cemented positive menisci, the two negative singlets, the central stop and the larger rear rim. A second construction diagram shows the same arrangement. The construction, aperture and dates strongly support identification with the screw-mount rangefinder lens. The patent's 28 mm focal length and f/2.8 aperture match the product. The patent specifies a 72° field, while Canon gives 75°, close to the 75.5° full angle at which the model's chief ray reaches the corner of the 36 × 24 mm frame. Canon specifies a six-blade diaphragm that closes to f/22 and a 1 m minimum focus; the patent prints no near state. ## Corrections, 2026-11-25

- The second element's thickness d₂ is the body table's 0.083 (2.324 mm), replacing Claim 2's 0.063 (1.764 mm). With r₆ negative as drawn, the body thickness gives a focal length of 28.002 mm and the claim thickness 28.097 mm. On the f = 100 scale of Figure 2 the wide-open longitudinal spherical aberration is approximately +0.28 with the body thickness and +1.70 with the claim thickness; the printed curve ends near +0.3. The infinity back focus falls from 20.190 to 19.191 mm and the first-vertex-to-film length from 35.702 to 35.263 mm.
- r₆ stays at the body table's −0.2090 (−5.852 mm). Claim 2 prints +0.2090, but Figure 1 draws that face concave toward the diaphragm. The impossible condition 1.30 > n₃ > 1.64 is left as printed, and n₃ = 1.7400 follows both tables.
- The field extends from a 25° half-field cap to the corner of the 36 × 24 mm frame at 37.76°. The patent specifies 72° across the field and Canon 75°. The full f/2.8 beam now reaches the whole frame instead of a stopped-down beam.
- The diaphragm moves from the middle of the central air space (2.66 + 2.66 mm) to 2.26 mm behind r₅ and 3.06 mm ahead of r₆, where Figure 1 draws it.
- The narrow estimated clear radii, whose rear rims were smaller than the front ones, are replaced by the Figure 1 readings in the table above. The rear doublet's shared edge stays at 6.30 mm, between Figure 1 and Canon's section, with rim slopes up to 67° allowed at r₉. Surface 5 stays at 3.73 mm, 0.05 mm beyond the drawing, to reduce the intermediate-field loss. Surfaces 2 and 3 stop at 5.72 mm, where their faces meet.
- Focus was infinity only, with `focus.closeFocusM` at 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. It now runs by calculated unit focus to Canon's 1 m minimum: the whole lens moves 0.8295 mm forward and the back focus grows from 19.1905 to 20.0200 mm.
- The title drops the mount suffix, and the patent number is written in the form "US 2,779,239". The elements follow Ito's L1–L4 notation, with a and b for the members of each cemented doublet. Both doublets are marked as cemented and each element has its meniscus type. The catalog records Canon's 1 m minimum focus and six-blade diaphragm, and the stop scale f/2.8–f/22 from the period guide. The subtitle names the patent, example, assignee and inventor, and the specification chips give the construction.
- Four elements take catalog dispersion from Sumita F4, Hoya FD3, Ohara PBM28 and Ohara S-TIL26, within the differences tabulated above. L1b and L4a stay unmatched with their printed nd and νd.

The finished model gives 28.002 mm EFL, f/2.80, a 3.506 mm iris radius, 19.191 mm back focus at infinity and a 37.76° half-field to the 21.63 mm frame corner.

## Remaining Questions

- Factory dimensions for the clear apertures, measured focus travel and measured corner illumination remain unknown.
- The patent gives no glass names, and Canon does not identify its rare glasses by position. No catalog glass lies within the site’s spectral-proxy tolerance of L1b’s 1.6970 / 54.7.
- Both patent tables give n₃ = 1.7400, although the description and Claim 1 print the impossible inequality 1.30 > n₃ > 1.64. The intended upper bound remains uncertain.
- Canon's sources do not state whether the 1 m minimum focus is measured from the film plane or from the lens front.
