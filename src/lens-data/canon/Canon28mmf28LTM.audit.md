# CANON 28mm f/2.8 — audit

## Source

Hiroshi Ito, _Four Component High Aperture Wide Angle Objective Lens_, assigned to Canon Camera Co., Inc., published January 29, 1957. Figure 1, Figure 2 and the sole numerical example in column 2 describe the same design. All printed lengths are multiplied by 28 to convert the f = 1 table to millimetres. [US 2,779,239](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2779239A/en)

## Transcription

The ten spherical radii, nine axial intervals and six helium d-line index and Abbe-number pairs follow the body table. Surfaces 2 and 9 are cemented interfaces. Air spaces of 0.056 and 0.028 mm separate the outer doublets from the inner singlets. The positive members of the front and rear doublets have centre thicknesses of 2.324 and 3.668 mm respectively. These thicknesses constrain their usable edges. The patent gives one optical configuration and no rear plate.

Four catalog equivalents match the printed glass coordinates. The differences below are catalog value minus patent value. The production suppliers are unidentified.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The first and last refracting vertices are 16.072 mm apart. Their separation in Figure 1 sets the scale for the two-sided rim readings below, approximately 0.0349 mm per pixel at 300 dpi. Stroke width and bevels limit the readings to about 0.1 mm. The figure places the stop about 42% of the way across the central air space, giving 2.26 mm before the diaphragm and 3.06 mm after it. The entrance pupil for f/2.8 gives a physical iris radius of 3.506 mm.

At the front doublet, surfaces 2 and 3 meet at a radius just above 5.72 mm. At the rear, 6.19 mm keeps the shared edge below a 64.2° surface slope at r₉. Extending both members of either pair to the drawn radii makes the printed surfaces cross. Extending r₂, r₃ or r₈ separately to its drawn radius leaves corner illumination at 4.2%. Figure 1 shows a common edge on each doublet.

The clear radius at surface 5 chiefly affects intermediate fields. With the other radii fixed, 3.68 mm admits 4.4 percentage points less of the pupil near 17° than 3.90 mm. A radius of 3.73 mm is within the drawing uncertainty and limits the largest loss to 2.9 points. The change has no measurable effect at the format corner. At infinity the corner chief ray clears every rim by at least 0.28 mm. The axial pupil transmits fully. At the infinity corner, the entrance rim, cemented interface 2 and surfaces 6–9 intercept off-axis rays. The 12.0% geometric corner transmission and 4.2% relative illumination are calculated at f/2.8.

Both film gaps come from paraxial focus. At infinity the stored plane is 0.00004 mm in front of the calculated focus. Moving the entire lens forward 0.8295 mm brings a subject 1 m from the film into focus, with the film plane 0.00003 mm in front of the calculated image. The finite corner chief clears every rim.

## Product Identification

Canon marketed the six-element, four-group 28mm f/2.8 in June 1957. Its labeled section and Figure 1 share the outer cemented positive menisci, the two negative singlets, the central stop and the larger rear rim. The construction, aperture and dates strongly support identification with the thread-mount rangefinder lens. Canon specifies a 75° field and a six-blade diaphragm that closes to f/22. [Canon lens guide, p. 15](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00331/00331.pdf#page=15) · [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/s36.html) · [Independent construction diagram](https://www.klassik-cameras.de/canon_rf_28mm%202.8des.gif)

## Corrections

### 2026-11-25

- Claim 2 prints d₂ = 0.063 (1.764 mm). The body table gives 0.083 (2.324 mm). With r₆ negative as drawn, the body thickness gives a focal length of 28.0015 mm. The claim thickness gives 28.0969 mm. On the f = 100 scale of Figure 2 the wide-open longitudinal spherical aberration is approximately +0.28 with the body thickness and +1.70 with the claim thickness. The curve ends near +0.3.
- Claim 2 prints r₆ = +0.2090. The body gives −0.2090 (−5.852 mm), as required by the concave face toward the diaphragm in Figure 1.
- The patent specifies 72° across the field and Canon gives 75° for the product. The model follows the printed optics and uses the 36 × 24 mm frame. Canon’s 1 m minimum supplies the endpoint for the inferred unit-focus motion.

## Remaining Questions

The factory clear apertures, near-focus travel and measured corner illumination remain unknown. The patent gives no glass names and Canon does not identify its rare glasses by position. Both patent tables give n₃ = 1.7400, although the description and Claim 1 print the impossible inequality 1.30 > n₃ > 1.64. The intended upper bound remains uncertain.
