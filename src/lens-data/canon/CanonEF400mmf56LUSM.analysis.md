# CANON EF 400mm f/5.6 L USM

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP 2019-070689 A, _Optical system with built-in converter_\
**Inventors:** Shigenobu Sugita\
**Assignee:** Canon Inc.\
**Filed:** October 6, 2017 (JP 2017-195536)\
**Published:** May 9, 2019\
**Example details:** 数値実施例1−A, ¶0035, pp. 6–7 and Fig. 1(A). Both converters are withdrawn.

Canon introduced the EF 400mm f/5.6 L USM in May 1993, 24 years before it filed this application. The 2019 publication gives a later example of the seven-element, six-group layout used in the production lens. The original 1993 design document remains unidentified. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/ef317.html) · [Patent, ¶¶0031–0035 and 0042](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP2019070689A/en)

Three details link the example to the production lens. Both drawings show five separate elements followed by a cemented rear doublet. Canon marks the second element as Super UD and colours the fourth for UD. Those two positions have the highest Abbe numbers in the patent, 95.0 and 81.6. The axial distance from the front of L1 to the front of L4 is about 61% of the distance from L1 to the rear doublet in Canon's drawing and 62% in the prescription. These features strongly identify the base layout. Canon's Museum text calls the UD element fifth, but its drawing places it fourth. [Canon construction drawing](https://global.canon/ja/c-museum/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/ef317-lens-construction.gif) · [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/ef317.html)

Canon describes the base lens in ¶0042 as approximately 400 mm at f/5.6. The table gives 391.89 mm at f/5.80. Examples 1-B and 1-C add converters to reach 560.02 mm and 286.00 mm. The other base lens, Example 2-A, is a 494.84 mm f/4 design with a different construction. The prescription here represents Example 1-A alone. [Patent, ¶¶0035–0038](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP2019070689A/en)

## Optical Architecture

Five air-spaced singlets collect and converge the beam before the diaphragm. The negative rear doublet gives the lens its telephoto proportions. The first reference plane lies 299.30 mm from the image, about 76% of the calculated 391.91 mm focal length. All refracting surfaces are spherical. Surfaces 1 and 12 are flat reference planes in air. The diaphragm is surface 13, 2.50 mm ahead of the doublet. Fig. 1 identifies the whole base lens as MA. [Patent, Fig. 1 and ¶0035](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP2019070689A/en)

The patent gives each effective diameter and the 167.00 mm distance from the last surface to the image. The model uses these dimensions in millimetres. At f/5.80 the paraxial stop radius is 15.501 mm. An exact axial marginal ray reaches 15.648 mm at the iris, consistent with the printed 31.30 mm diameter. The printed half-field of 3.16° covers the 21.64 mm image height. The corner chief ray clears each surface, although the lens rims intercept part of the off-axis bundle.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Focal lengths below are for the individual elements in air. The glass names are Ohara catalog equivalents for the patent's d-line data.

### L1 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.2. Ohara S-FSL5 equivalent. f = +168.15 mm.

The front element accepts the 67.57 mm axial beam at full aperture. Most of its curvature lies on the object side, and both faces contribute converging power before L2.

### L2 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.43875, νd = 95.0. Ohara S-FPL53 equivalent. f = +269.75 mm.

The strong front curve and shallow rear curve add positive power where the beam is still wide. The high Abbe number limits the chromatic spread introduced here. Canon identifies this lens as the first to use Super UD glass. [Canon optical technology](https://www.usa.canon.com/pro/rf-lens-world/advantages/technology)

### L3 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.78590, νd = 44.2. Ohara S-LAH51 equivalent. f = −141.91 mm.

L3 reduces the convergence from L1 and L2. Its higher dispersion gives its negative power a larger variation with wavelength than either front positive element. A 53.38 mm air space separates L3 from L4.

### L4 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6. Ohara S-FPL51 equivalent. f = +155.57 mm.

L4 supplies the strongest positive element power in the lens after the beam has narrowed. Its Abbe number and the coloured position in Canon's drawing identify it as the UD member. It is separated from L5 by only 0.25 mm on axis. [Canon construction drawing](https://global.canon/ja/c-museum/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/ef317-lens-construction.gif)

### L5 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.60311, νd = 60.7. Ohara S-BSM14 equivalent. f = −221.18 mm.

The more steeply curved rear face gives L5 negative power. It offsets part of L4's convergence before the beam reaches the diaphragm.

### L6 and L7 — Cemented Rear Doublet C1

**L6, biconvex positive.** nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Ohara S-TIH6 equivalent. f = +204.28 mm.\
**L7, biconcave negative.** nd = 1.69680, νd = 55.5. Ohara S-LAL14 equivalent. f = −90.65 mm.

The elements share surface 15. L6 supplies positive power in the more dispersive glass. L7's tighter rear curve gives the pair a net focal length of −166.01 mm. It reduces the convergence of the incoming beam and places the rear principal plane well ahead of the last surface.

## Glass Selection

The two low-dispersion positive menisci sit on opposite sides of the long central air space. L3 balances part of their colour contribution with a more dispersive negative element. Behind the diaphragm the dispersion order reverses, with the more dispersive glass in the positive member of a net-negative pair. Canon identifies one Super UD and one UD element in the production lens. The patent prints only refractive indices and Abbe numbers for these glasses. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/ef317.html) · [Patent, ¶¶0032–0035](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP2019070689A/en)

| Position | Ohara equivalent | Optical role                         |
| -------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| L1       | S-FSL5           | Low-dispersion front collector       |
| L2       | S-FPL53          | Super UD position                    |
| L3       | S-LAH51          | Negative member of the front section |
| L4       | S-FPL51          | UD position                          |
| L5       | S-BSM14          | Negative partner to L4               |
| L6       | S-TIH6           | Dispersive positive member of C1     |
| L7       | S-LAL14          | Negative member of C1                |

The Ohara equivalents provide dispersion curves for colour tracing. Neither Canon source identifies the production glass supplier or melt. [Ohara optical glass catalog](https://www.ohara-inc.co.jp/en/product/catalog/)

## Focus Mechanism

Canon describes rear focusing driven by a ring-type ultrasonic motor, with a minimum object-to-image distance of 3.5 m. Canon's specifications give an eight-blade diaphragm closing to f/32. The Museum gives 0.11× maximum magnification. Canon Japan and _EF Lens Work III_ give 0.12×. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/ef317.html) · [Canon Japan specifications](https://faq.canon.jp/app/answers/detail/a_id/95755) · [Canon, _EF Lens Work III_, pp. 51–52](https://flynngraphics.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/EF_Lens_Work_Book_03.pdf)

The master-lens table gives a single set of spacings at infinity. Neither the table nor Fig. 1(A) identifies the moving surfaces or their travel. Consequently the model ends at infinity. The 3.5 m setting and its magnification belong to the production specification. Canon separately lists detachable EF 1.4× and 2× extenders for this lens. [Patent, ¶¶0031–0035](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP2019070689A/en) · [Canon Japan extender specifications](https://faq.canon.jp/app/answers/detail/a_id/95755)
