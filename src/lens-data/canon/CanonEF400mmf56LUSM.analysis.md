# CANON EF 400mm f/5.6 L USM

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP 2019-070689 A, _Optical system with built-in converter_\
**Inventors:** Shigenobu Sugita\
**Assignee:** Canon Inc.\
**Filed:** October 6, 2017 (JP 2017-195536)\
**Published:** May 9, 2019\
**Example details:** 数値実施例1−A, ¶0035, pp. 6–7 and Fig. 1(A). Both converters are withdrawn.

Canon released the EF 400mm f/5.6 L USM in Japan on May 31, 1993. Sugita's application, filed 24 years later, concerns built-in converters. Its 2019 publication gives the base formula of the production lens in Numerical Example 1-A. The original design patent remains unidentified. [Canon Japan product record](https://faq.canon.jp/app/answers/detail/a_id/95755) · [Patent, ¶¶0031–0035 and 0042](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP2019070689A/en)

Fig. 1(A) and Canon's production drawing show five singlets and a cemented rear doublet. Canon colours L2 yellow for Super UD and L4 blue for UD. These elements have the highest Abbe numbers in the patent, 95.0 and 81.6. The axial distance from the front of L1 to L4 is 61% of the distance from L1 to the doublet in Canon's drawing and 62% in the prescription. The Museum text calls the UD element fifth, although its drawing places it fourth. [Canon Camera Museum, block diagram and specifications](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/ef317.html) · [Patent, Fig. 1(A) and ¶0035](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP2019070689A/en)

Canon describes the base lens in ¶0042 as approximately 400 mm at f/5.6. The table gives 391.89 mm at f/5.80. Examples 1-B and 1-C add converters to reach 560.02 mm and 286.00 mm. The other base lens, Example 2-A, is a 494.84 mm f/4 design with a different construction. [Patent, ¶¶0035–0038 and 0042](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP2019070689A/en)

## Optical Architecture

Five air-spaced singlets collect and converge the beam before the diaphragm. The negative rear doublet gives the lens its telephoto proportions. The first reference plane lies 299.30 mm from the image, about 76% of the calculated 391.91 mm focal length. All refracting surfaces are spherical. Surfaces 1 and 12 are flat reference planes in air. The diaphragm is surface 13, 2.50 mm ahead of the doublet. Fig. 1 identifies the whole base lens as MA. [Patent, Fig. 1 and ¶0035](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP2019070689A/en)

Each lens rim has half its tabulated effective diameter. The image plane lies 167.00 mm behind the last surface. At f/5.80 the calculated paraxial iris radius is 15.501 mm. The exact axial marginal ray reaches 15.648 mm at the iris, within 0.002 mm of half the printed diameter. The patent gives a 3.16° half-field (6.32° across) for a 21.64 mm image height. Canon lists a 6°10′ diagonal field for the production lens on 35 mm full frame. The corner chief ray clears every rim, with about 72.9% of the pupil area transmitting at the corner. [Patent, ¶¶0034–0035](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP2019070689A/en) · [Canon EF lens catalogue](https://asia.canon/media/document/2023/08/25/ac24e05e0718479ea233d5c19cb197c6_Canon_EF_Lens_Catalogue_2018.pdf)

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Focal lengths below are for the individual elements in air. The glass names are Ohara catalog equivalents for the patent's d-line data.

### L1 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.2. Ohara S-FSL5 equivalent. f = +168.15 mm.

The front element accepts the 67.57 mm axial beam at full aperture. Most of its curvature lies on the object side, and both faces contribute converging power before L2.

### L2 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.43875, νd = 95.0. Ohara S-FPL53 equivalent. f = +269.75 mm.

The strong front curve and shallow rear curve add positive power where the beam is still wide. The high Abbe number limits the chromatic spread introduced here. Canon identifies this lens as the first to use Super UD glass. [Canon optical technology](https://www.usa.canon.com/pro/rf-lens-world/advantages/technology)

### L3 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.78590, νd = 44.2. Ohara S-LAH51 equivalent. f = −141.91 mm.

L3 reduces the convergence from L1 and L2. Its higher dispersion gives its negative power a larger variation with wavelength than either front positive element. A 53.38 mm air space separates L3 from L4.

### L4 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.6. Ohara S-FPL51 equivalent. f = +155.57 mm.

L4 supplies the strongest positive element power in the lens after the beam has narrowed. Its Abbe number and the blue marking in Canon's drawing identify it as the UD member. It is separated from L5 by only 0.25 mm on axis. [Canon Camera Museum, block diagram](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/ef317.html)

### L5 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.60311, νd = 60.7. Ohara S-BSM14 equivalent. f = −221.18 mm.

The more steeply curved rear face gives L5 negative power. It offsets part of L4's convergence before the beam reaches the diaphragm.

### L6 and L7 — Cemented Rear Doublet C1

**L6, biconvex positive.** nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Ohara S-TIH6 equivalent. f = +204.28 mm.\
**L7, biconcave negative.** nd = 1.69680, νd = 55.5. Ohara S-LAL14 equivalent. f = −90.65 mm.

The elements share surface 15. L6 supplies positive power in the more dispersive glass. L7's tighter rear curve gives the pair a net focal length of −166.01 mm. It reduces the convergence of the incoming beam and places the rear principal plane well ahead of the last surface.

## Glass Selection

The two low-dispersion positive menisci sit on opposite sides of the long central air space. L3 balances part of their colour contribution with a more dispersive negative element. Behind the diaphragm the dispersion order reverses, with the more dispersive glass in the positive member of a net-negative pair. Canon identifies one Super UD and one UD element in the production lens. The patent prints only refractive indices and Abbe numbers for these glasses. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/ef317.html) · [Patent, ¶¶0032–0035](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP2019070689A/en)

## Focus Mechanism

In the production lens, a ring-type ultrasonic motor drives the rear focusing optics and allows full-time manual focus. The range selector sets the near limit at 3.5 m or 8.5 m, measured from the image plane. The eight-blade iris spans f/5.6 to f/32. At 3.5 m the Museum gives 0.11× maximum magnification, while Canon Japan and _EF Lens Work III_ give 0.12×. [Canon, _EF Lens Work III_, pp. 51–52](https://flynngraphics.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/EF_Lens_Work_Book_03.pdf) · [Canon focus-distance specifications](https://en.canon-me.com/store/ae/canon-400mm-lenses/) · [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/ef317.html) · [Canon Japan specifications](https://faq.canon.jp/app/answers/detail/a_id/95755)

The model is focused at infinity. Example 1-A gives no finite-focus spacings or group travel from which to calculate a closer position or its magnification. Canon lists the detachable Extender EF 1.4× II/III at 560 mm f/8 and EF 2× II/III at 800 mm f/11. With the Extender 2× on the model, the combination does not cover the full frame corner. [Patent, ¶¶0031–0035](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP2019070689A/en) · [Canon Japan extender specifications](https://faq.canon.jp/app/answers/detail/a_id/95755)
