# CANON EF 20-35mm f/3.5-4.5 USM

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP H06-230281 A, _Zoom Lens_ (ズームレンズ)\
**Inventors:** Tsunefumi Tanaka\
**Assignee:** Canon Inc.\
**Filed:** February 4, 1993, Japanese application H05-40504\
**Published:** August 19, 1994\
**Example details:** Numerical Example 1, prescription and variable spacings on page 4, wide-angle section in Figure 1 and aberration plots in Figures 4–6.

Canon introduced the EF 20-35mm f/3.5-4.5 USM in March 1993, one month after filing. Its construction drawing shows 12 elements in 11 optical groups. Several features identify Numerical Example 1:

1. A broad positive front meniscus sits just ahead of a stepped negative meniscus. Behind them is a separate three-element focusing unit, consistent with Canon’s description of inner focusing.
2. A thin biconvex element precedes the iris and a much thicker biconvex element follows it. Farther back, the only cemented pair is followed by three air-spaced menisci. The patent specifies spherical surfaces throughout. Canon’s drawing identifies no aspherical or ED element.
3. Example 2 shares this arrangement, but the thin element in Canon’s drawing measures about 2.3 mm on axis. Example 1 gives 2.20 mm and Example 2 gives 3.36 mm. Example 3 separates the cemented pair, giving 12 air-spaced groups.

The shape and thickness of the element ahead of the stop make Example 1 the strongest match. Canon’s public diagram does not identify a patent example or give factory radii. ## Optical Architecture

The three mechanical groups form a negative–negative–positive retrofocus zoom. The fixed front group G1 admits the wide field. G2 has greater negative power and compensates for image displacement as G3 moves 15.31 mm toward the subject from wide to telephoto. G2 first moves toward the image and then returns to its original position. Canon’s description credits this second-group zoom motion with holding distortion down. G3 carries the iris and supplies the final convergence. The three groups have paraxial focal lengths of −70.34, −45.19 and +30.11 mm. At the wide end the 37.75 mm back focal distance is substantially longer than the 20.61 mm effective focal length. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/ef316.html)

Canon markets the example’s 20.60–34.09 mm range as 20–35 mm; the open apertures of f/3.5–4.5 agree with the patent. The published diagonal angle of view runs from 94° to 63°. The patent’s printed angles correspond closely to a rectilinear image height of 21.6 mm. Tracing the refracted chief ray to the same frame corner gives the slightly larger angles above. ## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are for each element in air. The two elements cemented at surface 17 have a combined focal length of −130.52 mm.

### L11 — Positive meniscus

nd = 1.62299, νd = 58.2. S-BSM15 (Ohara equivalent). f = +212.45 mm.

The broad front element bends oblique bundles before they reach the stronger negative element. The patent attributes control of wide-angle distortion and astigmatism to the shapes and index difference of these two menisci (§§0022 and 0036).

### L12 — Negative meniscus

nd = 1.78590, νd = 44.2. S-LAH51 (Ohara equivalent). f = −51.34 mm.

The steep image-side curve gives G1 its negative power. The stepped rim visible in both drawings accommodates a larger clear radius on its front face than on its rear face.

### L21 — Negative meniscus

nd = 1.80610, νd = 41.0. LAH53 (Ohara equivalent). f = −36.46 mm.

L21 provides the larger share of negative power in the moving focus group. Its strongly curved rear surface faces the air gap ahead of L22.

### L22 — Negative meniscus

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.6. S-LAH66 (Ohara equivalent). f = −54.88 mm.

A second negative meniscus divides the diverging power between two elements. Tanaka links this choice to control of field curvature and astigmatism (§0026).

### L23 — Positive meniscus

nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.8. S-TIH53W (Ohara equivalent). f = +46.79 mm.

L23 partly offsets L21 and L22. The patent credits this combination with controlling spherical aberration at the long end and changes in spherical aberration and coma during focusing (§0025).

### L31 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.58313, νd = 59.4. S-BAL42 (Ohara equivalent). f = +67.15 mm.

The first element of G3 starts to converge the beam 1.78 mm ahead of the iris, measured from its rear vertex. Its opposed radii have almost equal magnitudes.

### L32 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.62606, νd = 39.2. H-BaF8 (CDGM equivalent). f = +30.49 mm.

L32 is the stronger of the two biconvex elements flanking the stop. Its 6.90 mm center thickness contrasts with the 2.20 mm thickness of L31.

### L33 and L34 — Cemented negative and positive menisci

- **L33:** nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.8. S-TIH53W (Ohara equivalent). f = −19.58 mm.
- **L34:** nd = 1.78590, νd = 44.2. S-LAH51 (Ohara equivalent). f = +27.32 mm.

The more dispersive negative member and the less dispersive positive member share a shallow interface of radius −417.04 mm. Their powers partly cancel, leaving a weakly negative doublet inside the converging rear group.

### L35 — Negative meniscus

nd = 1.83400, νd = 37.2. S-LAH60V (Ohara equivalent). f = −44.01 mm.

L35 diverges the beam before the final two positive elements. Its rear face and the front face of L36 approach one another toward the rim across a 1.65 mm axial air space.

### L36 — Positive meniscus

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.2. S-BSL7 (Ohara equivalent). f = +63.68 mm.

The first of the two rear crowns supplies the stronger convergence. Most of its bending occurs at its more strongly curved rear surface.

### L37 — Positive meniscus

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.2. S-BSL7 (Ohara equivalent). f = +106.01 mm.

A weaker meniscus of the same index and dispersion completes G3. Its rear vertex is the reference for the back focal distance.

The patent gives refractive indices and Abbe numbers without naming a spectral line. They match d-line catalog values exactly. The catalog equivalents supply dispersion curves for tracing. The original suppliers and melts are unknown.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent focuses by moving G2 while G1 and G3 hold their positions at a given focal length (§0020), and it allows G1 to focus instead (§0024). Canon describes inner focusing driven by an ultrasonic motor, with full-time manual focus, and specifies a minimum distance of 0.34 m and a maximum magnification of 0.13×. Canon measures minimum focusing distance from the focal plane, so the 0.34 m runs from the object to the image plane. The patent’s aberration plots are at −0.02× (§0017) and give no corresponding close-focus spacings. [Canon Camera Museum, Japanese description](https://global.canon/ja/c-museum/product/ef316.html) · [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/ef316.html) · [Canon on focusing distance](https://cam.start.canon/en/C007/manual/html/UG-02_BasicShooting_0110.html)

A paraxial calculation that moves G2 alone, with D4 + D10 held constant, focuses 0.34 m from the image plane after about 3.3 mm of travel toward the subject at each printed setting. The magnification is then −0.081×, −0.093× and −0.133× at wide, middle and telephoto, and the telephoto value matches Canon’s 0.13×. Measured from the front vertex instead, 0.34 m would give only −0.093× at the telephoto end. These positions are calculated, not printed, and the model represents infinity focus throughout the zoom.

The printed zoom spacings place the paraxial images within 0.005 mm of a common film plane 111.3941 mm behind the first vertex. Between the three printed settings, ten calculated positions reproduce the reversal of G2 while G3 advances. Between these positions the paraxial image stays within 0.0169 mm of the fixed film plane. The patent gives neither the continuous zoom motion nor the clear diameters. Figure 1 supplies the rim estimates, with the narrow L35–L36 air space constraining the two facing radii. The aperture-stop position is printed. Its radius follows from the open f-number at each setting. Canon widens the front of the barrel to keep the corners bright and places a flare-cutting diaphragm, shaped to the image format, in the first group. Figure 1 does not draw that diaphragm and its dimensions are unknown, so the illumination estimates account for the iris and glass rims only.
