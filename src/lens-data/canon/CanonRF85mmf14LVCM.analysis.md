# CANON RF 85mm f/1.4 L VCM

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP 2026-094892 A  
**Filed:** 2024-11-29 (JP2024-208503)  
**Published:** 2026-06-10; US family member US 2026/0153708 A1 published 2026-06-04  
**Inventor:** Fumihiro Shinobu (信夫 史弘)  
**Applicant:** Canon Inc.  
**Title:** Lens apparatus and image pickup apparatus  
**Example details:** 数値実施例5, table pp. 24–25; section Fig. 9 and aberration plots Fig. 10 (p. 45); Numerical Embodiment 5 of the US counterpart

The patent describes a large-aperture medium telephoto in three groups, with a positive second group that moves for focus. Example 5 is linked to the Canon RF 85mm F1.4 L VCM by construction rather than by any statement in the patent:

1. **Element and group count.** Canon lists 14 elements in 10 groups; Example 5 has 14 elements in 10 air-separated groups, with four cemented doublets.
2. **Construction diagram.** Canon's published section matches Fig. 9 element by element: two leading positive menisci, a third element cemented to a negative meniscus, a free negative meniscus before the stop, a four-element group behind it and a rear group of two doublets and a single negative meniscus.
3. **Special elements.** Canon marks one UD element (the third) and one aspherical element (the biconvex lens in the focus group). Example 5 has a 1.497 / 81.7 glass in the third position and its only aspherical surfaces, 13 and 14, on biconvex L7.
4. **Focus.** Canon drives a four-element focus group with a voice-coil motor; Example 5 focuses by moving the four-element group U2 toward the object.
5. **Focal length, aperture and near state.** The example states f = 82.50 mm, F/1.46 and a 14.69° half field against the marketed 85 mm, f/1.4 and 28°30′ diagonal angle of view. Its printed near gaps focus at 749.9 mm object to image with β = −0.1265, against Canon's 0.75 m minimum focus and 0.12× maximum magnification.

Example 12 of the same patent fits all five points equally well: it has the same f, F-number, half field and group layout, the same glass in most positions, and its near gaps also focus at 750.0 mm (β = −0.1264). It differs in the glass of L1 (1.79631 / 22.6), L2 and L9 (1.59282 / 68.6) and L11 (1.51742 / 52.4), in total length (118.00 mm against 117.10 mm) and in focus travel (8.21 mm against 8.44 mm). No published datum separates the two, so Example 5 stands as a representative of the design family rather than an identified production prescription.

## Optical Architecture

The lens is a three-group inner-focus design: a positive first group U1 (patent group focal length +189.25 mm), a positive focusing group U2 (+49.83 mm) and a weakly negative rear group U3 (−203.98 mm), with the stop fixed between U1 and U2 (¶0012, ¶0031). The patent's stated aim (¶0016) is to correct chromatic aberration, which is conspicuous in fast medium telephotos, without leaving the field curvature under-corrected, which a purely color-driven layout tends to do.

U1 opens with two positive menisci convex to the object, followed by a cemented UD meniscus doublet and a free negative meniscus whose deep rear face looks at the stop. U2 begins with a negative lens directly behind the stop, then the double-aspherical L7 and a cemented doublet. With the negative meniscus ahead of the stop and the negative lens behind it, the U1–U2 core reads as a modified double-Gauss; U3 adds two cemented doublets and a rear negative meniscus that flatten the field and set lateral color. The total length is 117.10 mm, 1.42 times the focal length, with a 15.09 mm back focus.

Fig. 9 labels only the negative lenses named in the conditions: L1n1 and L1n2 (here L4 and L5), L2n1 and L2n2 (L6 and L8), and L3nF and L3nR (L13 and L14). This page numbers all fourteen elements L1–L14 from the front.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.9. Glass: S-NPH53 (OHARA) equivalent. f = +199.6 mm.

A weak positive meniscus in a high-index, high-dispersion glass. Conditions (11) and (12) (¶0048) set 1.60 < nd < 2.50 and 13.0 < νd < 50.0 for this front lens: the high index keeps its curvature shallow for spherical aberration, and its dispersion is chosen so that axial and lateral color are neither over- nor under-corrected.

### L2 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.59522, νd = 67.7. Glass: S-FPM2 (OHARA) equivalent. f = +95.8 mm.

The second of the two consecutive positive menisci the patent requires (¶0016), and twice as strong as L1. Condition (13) bounds the power ratio of the first two lenses; here fg2/fg1 = 0.48. The low-dispersion crown has positive anomalous partial dispersion (catalog ΔPgF +0.014).

### L3 — Positive Meniscus (UD)

nd = 1.49700, νd = 81.7. Glass: J-FK01A (Hikari) equivalent; UD class. f = +94.2 mm.

The element Canon marks as UD, placed where the axial marginal ray is still about 23 mm high. Its catalog ΔPgF of +0.031 makes it the main secondary-spectrum corrector of the front group. L3, L4 and L5 are all menisci convex to the object, the gentle bending ¶0027 prefers for the third to fifth lenses of a lens with modest overall power.

### L4 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.77047, νd = 29.7, θgF = 0.5951 (printed). Glass: NBFD29 (HOYA) equivalent. f = −68.1 mm.

Cemented to L3, it makes the doublet almost afocal (f ≈ −370 mm), so the pair corrects color with little net power. L4 and L5 are the two first-group negatives that carry the patent's main claim (¶0017–¶0022): nd above 1.50, nd + 0.015νd below 2.35 to hold the Petzval sum, νd between 26 and 60, and θgF below the patent's reference curve, here by 0.0088.

### L5 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.77047, νd = 29.7, θgF = 0.5951 (printed). Glass: NBFD29 (HOYA) equivalent. f = −64.9 mm.

The free negative meniscus that closes U1, with its strongly curved rear face (R = 26.3 mm) concave to the stop. Using the same glass as L4 satisfies condition (22) (¶0067), which asks for the image-side negative to have an equal or higher index so axial color can be corrected without disturbing lateral color.

### L6 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.61340, νd = 44.3, θgF = 0.5633 (printed). Glass: S-NBM51 (OHARA) equivalent. f = −42.5 mm.

The first element of the focus group, directly behind the stop. A negative lens at the front of U2 eases both the Petzval sum and axial color (¶0030). With L8 it is one of the two U2 negatives whose θgF lies below the reference curve, by 0.0058, as condition (25) requires (¶0073).

### L7 — Biconvex Positive (2 aspherical surfaces)

nd = 1.80400, νd = 46.5. Glass: S-LAH65VS (OHARA) equivalent. f = +36.6 mm.

The lens's only aspherical element. The patent places an asphere in the moving group near the middle of the lens to control spherical aberration and coma and their change with focus distance (¶0029).

### L8 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.61340, νd = 44.3, θgF = 0.5633 (printed). Glass: S-NBM51 (OHARA) equivalent. f = −54.4 mm.

The front half of the U2 doublet, the second low-θgF negative of the focus group.

### L9 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.59522, νd = 67.7. Glass: S-FPM2 (OHARA) equivalent. f = +31.8 mm.

The strongest positive element of the focus group; the L8–L9 doublet has f = +68.8 mm. Keeping four lenses in U2 lets the group correct its own aberrations, so spherical aberration and coma vary little with focus (¶0028). Its rear face and the nearly flat front of L10 give the shape factor of condition (14), 1.18 against the patent's range of 0.50 to 2.50.

### L10 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.72342, νd = 38.0. Glass: BAFD8 (HOYA) equivalent. f = −33.5 mm.

Front half of the first U3 doublet (f = −45.7 mm together with L11). Its rear face, R = 25.457 mm, is the tightest radius in U1 and U3, giving |Rmin|/f = 0.31 under condition (19): ¶0061 calls for a strong curvature where the off-axis rays are high, to correct field curvature and coma with few elements.

### L11 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.2. Glass: S-FSL5 (OHARA) equivalent. f = +109.1 mm.

A weak low-index crown cemented to L10.

### L12 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 2.00100, νd = 29.1. Glass: TAFD55 (HOYA) equivalent. f = +23.3 mm.

The strongest single element in the lens. The nd 2.0 glass keeps its radii moderate, and with L13 it forms a positive doublet (f = +42.5 mm) inside the negative rear group.

### L13 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.59270, νd = 35.3, θgF = 0.5933 (printed). Glass: S-FTM16 (OHARA) equivalent. f = −46.0 mm.

The patent's second-from-last negative lens. Conditions (17) and (18) (¶0058) govern its Abbe number and partial dispersion for lateral color and g-line lateral color; its θgF sits 0.0041 above the reference curve, inside the −0.005 to +0.025 band.

### L14 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.59270, νd = 35.3, θgF = 0.5933 (printed). Glass: S-FTM16 (OHARA) equivalent. f = −113.0 mm.

The rear element, concave to the object, where the chief ray is highest (about 17.8 mm at the last surface). Conditions (15) and (16) (¶0055) apply the same lateral-color logic as L13.

The patent prints nd, νd and, for six elements, θgF, defined on d 587.6, F 486.1, C 656.3 and g 435.8 nm (¶0095–¶0096). Every catalog row reproduces the printed nd and lies within 0.05 of the printed νd, and the three printed θgF values agree with the catalog within 0.0001. Those three glasses come from both OHARA and HOYA, so where several vendors tie at the printed precision (L1, L10, L12) the OHARA or HOYA row is used. For L3, J-FK01A is the only row whose νd rounds to the printed 81.7; HOYA FCD1 (81.61) and OHARA S-FPL51 (81.55) are equivalent alternatives. The production suppliers are not known.

The chromatic strategy pairs positive crowns with positive anomalous partial dispersion (L2, L3, L9) against negatives whose θgF lies below the patent's reference curve (L4, L5, L6, L8) in the groups around the stop, which carry the axial color and secondary spectrum. In U3 the two S-FTM16 negatives, with θgF slightly above that curve, trim g-line lateral color. Fig. 10 plots spherical aberration in d and g only, and the two curves start together on the axis. With the catalog dispersion curves the model's paraxial g focus lies 0.002 mm behind the d focus (C 0.021 mm behind, F 0.016 mm in front).

## Focus Mechanism

Focus is internal. U2 (L6–L9, surfaces 11–17) moves 8.44 mm toward the object from infinity to the near state while U1, the stop, U3 and the image plane stay fixed (¶0014). Canon drives this group with a voice-coil motor. Keeping the stop fixed between U1 and U2 limits the change in field curvature and coma with focus (¶0031). The travel gives M2/f = −0.10 under condition (23).

| Gap | Infinity (mm) | Near (mm) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| d10 (stop to L6) | 15.98 | 7.54 |
| d17 (L9 to L10) | 2.10 | 10.54 |

The patent prints both gap states but not the near object distance or magnification. With the image plane held at the printed back focus, the near gaps focus at 632.8 mm from the first surface, 749.9 mm object to image, with β = −0.1265 and the focal length shortened to 74.5 mm. That agrees with Canon's 0.75 m and 0.12×. Positions between the two states are interpolated.

## Aspherical Surfaces

Both faces of L7 are aspherical. The patent's equation (¶0098) is the standard form, $z = ch^2/(1+\sqrt{1-(1+K)c^2h^2}) + A_4h^4 + A_6h^6 + A_8h^8 + A_{10}h^{10} + A_{12}h^{12}$ with $c = 1/R$, and both surfaces have K = 0. No scaling is applied.

| Surface | K | A4 | A6 | A8 | A10 | A12 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 13 | 0.00000e+00 | -4.34414e-06 | 7.63666e-09 | -7.45046e-11 | 3.32786e-13 | -7.64837e-16 |
| 14 | 0.00000e+00 | 2.97381e-06 | 6.88577e-09 | -6.70959e-11 | 3.09798e-13 | -7.23048e-16 |

Both surfaces depart from their spheres in the direction that weakens the element's marginal power. At 15 mm height the polynomial terms subtract 0.23 mm from the 2.23 mm sag of the convex front face and add 0.14 mm to the −1.76 mm sag of the convex rear face. This is the usual pattern for holding down the spherical aberration of a fast positive element. Up to 15 mm height the higher-order terms nearly cancel one another, leaving A4 dominant; they grow toward the 17.2 mm rim. The patent does not state how the element is made.

## Model Scope and Limitations

- The patent prints no clear apertures. The semi-diameters follow Fig. 9 and Canon's construction diagram; the 31.1 mm front rim is about 10 % above the 28.25 mm entrance-pupil radius. The rear face of L4 and the front face of L5 nearly touch at the rim, so S7 and S8 are set at 18.75 mm, just above the 18.70 and 18.64 mm heights of the f/1.46 axial beam.
- Both drawings show L4 and L5 square-cut with flat lands, and the model draws them the same way. L4's rear face ends at L5's rim, and a land carries L4 out to its 25 mm edge. L5 is 18.75 mm high front and rear, and its concave rear face ends at 17.0 mm with a land out to the edge, well ahead of the stop. L6's front face has a short land from 16.1 mm. None of these lands clips any traced beam.
- Fig. 9 and Canon's diagram draw L13 and L14 meeting at the rim, at the height of the L12–L13 doublet. In the prescription their facing surfaces cross at 19.1 mm, so the model sets those faces to 19.0 mm (0.08 mm apart at the rim, with the validator's gap-sag fraction at 0.99) against 20.5 mm for the other faces of the two elements; both elements keep a slight bevel.
- The stop sits at the printed surface 10; its radius follows the F/1.46 rule.
- The last gap keeps the printed back focus of 15.09 mm, an air-equivalent length, without a cover glass. The paraxial back focus computes 15.080 mm and the d-line EFL 82.494 mm; neither difference is applied.
- The printed image height of 21.63 mm equals f·tan 14.69°. A real chief ray at 14.69° lands at 21.86 mm because of about +1.1 % pincushion distortion, which matches the roughly +1 % shown in Fig. 10; in the model the 21.65 mm full-frame corner is reached at 14.6°.
