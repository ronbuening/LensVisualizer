# CANON EF 20-35mm f/3.5-4.5 USM — audit

## Source

Tsunefumi Tanaka, _Zoom Lens_, JP H06-230281 A, Numerical Example 1. Canon Inc. filed application H05-40504 on February 4, 1993. It was published on August 19, 1994. Page 4 gives the prescription and both variable spacings at three zoom settings. Figure 1 shows the wide-angle section, and Figures 4–6 give the aperture and half-field at each setting. [Japanese patent publication](https://patents.google.com/patent/JPH06230281A/ja)

## Transcription

The prescription is in millimetres at the printed scale. The 23 spherical refracting surfaces, the stop at surface 13, 21 fixed thicknesses and air spaces, 12 d-line index and Abbe-number pairs, and six variable-gap values agree with Numerical Example 1. The iris lies 1.78 mm behind surface 12 and 0.92 mm ahead of surface 14. Surface 17 is the single cemented interface, between L33 and L34. The example gives spherical curvature throughout.

The two variable gaps lie after surfaces 4 and 10. The published D4 spacings are 8.29, 10.28 and 8.29 mm, and D10 takes 16.80, 10.50 and 1.49 mm. The table headings are discussed under Corrections.

The largest focal-length difference is 0.012 mm, at the wide end. The printed half-fields are consistent with the rectilinear relation between focal length and image height. A real chief ray reaches the frame corner at each of the 13 zoom settings. At the wide end, direct intersection with the spherical surfaces gives an image height of 21.65 mm at 47.76°. At the three printed settings the geometric pupil fractions visible there are approximately 18%, 64% and 70%. These figures depend on the estimated rims. The axial beam clears the refracting surfaces throughout the zoom.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

- **Clear radii.** Figure 1 spans 73.64 mm from the first to the last vertex at the wide setting. At that scale the front element has a clear radius of 26.8 mm. The stepped second element measures 20.5 mm at its front face and 16.2 mm at its rear. The diagram gives a range around the rear rim of L21, where 11.0 mm clears the next surface. The two facing rims at R20 and R21 use 7.7 mm each to preserve their 1.65 mm axial air space. The table below lists the clear radii for each refracting surface.
- **Aperture stop.** The open f-number determines the iris radius from the paraxial entrance pupil at each zoom setting. The resulting wide, middle and telephoto radii are 6.48, 6.05 and 6.23 mm. The opening drawn in Figure 1 is about 6.5 mm in radius at wide angle.
- **Film position.** The three printed zoom settings put the paraxial image 111.3903, 111.3938 and 111.3982 mm from R1. Their mean fixes the film 111.3941 mm from R1. The changing rear air space follows the axial position of G3.
- **Intermediate zoom motion.** D4 + D10 determines G3 travel relative to fixed G1. Between consecutive printed positions, that sum changes in equal increments. D4 is solved for infinity focus and D10 is the remainder. The ten calculated positions keep the film within 0.00005 mm of paraxial focus at those positions, and linear motion between all 13 settings stays within 0.0169 mm. The intermediate open f-numbers interpolate between the printed values.
- **Glass names.** All 12 elements have catalog equivalents. Eight Ohara glasses cover 11 elements. CDGM H-BaF8 represents L32 at a difference of −0.00002 in index and −0.13 in Abbe number from the printed values. The prescription retains the patent’s indices and Abbe numbers throughout.

## Product Identification

Canon marketed the 20–35 mm EF zoom for 35 mm cameras in March 1993. Its drawing agrees with the example in element order, the three-element inner-focus group, the iris between L31 and L32 and the cemented pair at L33/L34. The drawing and Figure 1 agree in rim height to about 0.5 mm. L31 measures approximately 2.3 mm thick in Canon’s drawing, close to the 2.20 mm of Example 1. Example 2 gives 3.36 mm, and Example 3 has an air space between L33 and L34 that would bring the group count to 12. This makes Example 1 a strong match. [Canon’s construction drawing](https://global.canon/ja/c-museum/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/ef316-lens-construction.gif)

Canon specifies 12 elements in 11 groups, f/3.5–4.5, a five-blade iris closing to f/22–27, a 0.34 m closest focus and 0.13× maximum magnification. The patent describes G2 focusing and shows aberrations at −0.02× without tabulating the focus travel. Only infinity focus is represented. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/ef316.html) · [Canon U.S.A. specifications](https://www.usa.canon.com/support/p/ef-20-35mm-f-3-5-4-5-usm)

## Corrections

### 2026-11-25

The zoom table labels its rows D5 and D11. The surface prescription marks D4 and D10 as variable and explicitly assigns D5 and D11 to glass thicknesses of 1.40 and 2.20 mm. Reading the rows as D4 and D10 reproduces all three stated focal lengths and the group movements in Figure 1. The spacing values are reproduced as printed.

## Remaining Questions

Canon gives no explicit link between this patent example and the production lens. The glass suppliers, manufactured clear apertures, flare-diaphragm dimensions and exact zoom and focus motions remain undocumented in the cited material. In particular, the calculated wide-corner transmission cannot be checked against an absolute production measurement from these sources.
