## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 4,432,615 A  
**Filed:** 17 June 1981  
**Granted:** 21 February 1984  
**Inventor:** Keiji Ikemori  
**Assignee:** Canon Inc.  
**Embodiment analyzed:** Numerical Example 2, Figure 5.

This is a probable patent reconstruction of the Canon New FD 35–105mm f/3.5. The manufacturer's retrospective optical section agrees with the distinctive 4/3 + 4/4 + 1/1 + 6/5 sequence, totaling 15 elements in 13 components. Example 1 has a different 16/12 construction. Exact manufactured radii are not established.

The native focal labels 36.2, 70 and 102 mm and f/3.5 are retained. Literal first-order replay gives approximately 36.1648, 69.9208 and 101.8975 mm; no rescaling to marketing endpoints is applied. R15 is −6974.39 mm, R16 −34.387 mm, R17 −91.235 mm, R22 is plane and R28 +4211.79 mm.

## Optical Architecture

Four optical units have positive, negative, negative and positive power. G1 is the focusing unit but remains fixed in this infinity-only reconstruction. G2 varies magnification, G3 compensates image position, and G4 is the stationary relay. The source infinity iris lies 0.87 mm ahead of R18, leaving positive distances after R17 at all three source zoom positions. The added STO row splits that native gap without changing its total.

## Glass Identification and Selection

All source indices and Abbe numbers are retained. Public catalog glasses with closely matching coordinates supply dispersion where resolvable; they are equivalent modeling choices, not verified manufacturer melts. No partial-dispersion or apochromatism claim is inferred from the values. Every surface is spherical or plane in the selected example.

## Zoom and Focus

The three published infinity gap rows remain unchanged. Forty-one stations include a calculated G2/G3 cam between those rows. It solves paraxial focal length and focus while preserving the interpolated total track and fixed front/rear groups. Maximum intermediate shifts from straight gap interpolation are approximately 2.018 mm for G2 and 1.041 mm for G3. Derived stations are explicitly not source-published.

The source omits back focal distance. The fixed image plane uses the mean literal-source Gaussian BFD, approximately 56.5620 mm. The individual source rows differ from this by less than 0.0048 mm. This derived plane is not a recovered mechanical flange distance.

Finite focus and macro are deliberately outside this model's scope. The source describes G1 advancing 3.3 mm and an iris shift toward R17 at a 1.5 m first-surface-referenced object distance, with mechanical limits depending on zoom. Those statements do not establish a complete continuous finite-focus cam. The focus control is inert; closeFocusM = 1e15 is an infinity sentinel, not a physical minimum focus. No finite-distance MTF conjugate is certified.

## Aperture, Clear Apertures and Validation

The iris is derived from source f/3.5. Estimated clear radii follow Figure 5 and positive-edge/nonoverlap constraints. The normal 0.90 gap-sag rule is retained. Table 2 ray heights are not used as physical clear semi-diameters. The displayed off-axis fan uses 0.3 of the engine's computed field; this central-field choice is not full-pupil illumination certification.

All source radii, material coordinates and fixed axial thicknesses remain unchanged. The image plane, intermediate cam, clear radii and catalog glass equivalences are explicit reconstruction choices. See the package's validation report for measured residuals and integration limits.
