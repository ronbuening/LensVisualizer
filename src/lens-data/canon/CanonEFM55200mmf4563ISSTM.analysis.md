The divisional JP 2017-161933 A (application JP 2017-99432, filed May 19, 2017, published September 14, 2017) reprints the same table as its first numerical example (¶0064) and assigns it to Reference Example 1. All dimensions are in millimetres at the patent's scale.

Example 1 is identified with the Canon EF-M 55–200mm f/4.5–6.3 IS STM. The match rests on construction and specifications; it does not show that the production lens uses every printed number.

1. Canon's Camera Museum lists the July 2014 product as 17 elements in 11 groups. Example 1 has 17 lens elements in 11 air-separated components, excluding its camera-side optical block, and Canon's section shows the same sequence of singlets and cemented pairs.
2. The patent gives 56.60/134.00/195.00 mm, f/4.50/5.70/6.45 and a 13.66 mm image height (¶0015, ¶0068). These are close to the marketed 55–200 mm f/4.5–6.3 and cover the EF-M APS-C format.
3. One double-aspheric element (L10), one low-dispersion element with νd 81.5 (L8) and a small stabilizing doublet (L11/L12) sit where Canon's diagram marks the glass-molded aspherical element, the UD element and the IS unit.
4. The application was filed in May 2013 and published in November 2014, four months after the July 2014 launch; it is the earliest publication of this design.
5. The patent's imageward B4a focusing is consistent with the product's inner focus. Canon's 1 m minimum focus distance and 0.21× maximum magnification are manufacturer figures; the patent prints no near-focus spacings.

Examples 2 and 5 of the same publication share the element count and glass types (Example 2 keeps B2 fixed during zooming), and Canon's static diagram cannot separate them from Example 1. The model shows the stabilizing group centered and is not a measured production prescription.

## Optical Architecture

Example 1 is a four-unit **positive–negative–positive–negative** telephoto zoom. Its rear unit B4 is divided at its widest air space into B4a and B4b (¶0022). The five-unit variant with a fixed positive fifth group is the publication's Example 3.

| Unit | Source surfaces | Elements | Published focal length | Role                                                                |
| ---- | --------------- | -------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| B1   | 1–5             | L1–L3    | +93.52 mm              | Front positive collector, singlet plus cemented doublet             |
| B2   | 6–10            | L4–L6    | −21.90 mm              | Negative zoom variator, doublet plus singlet                        |
| B3   | 11–23           | L7–L13   | +26.02 mm              | Positive relay containing aperture, asphere and centered IS doublet |
| B4a  | 24–26           | L14–L15  | −33.93 mm              | Negative focus doublet                                              |
| B4b  | 27–29           | L16–L17  | −4057.03 mm            | Nearly afocal rear cemented correction pair                         |

At infinity, B1–B4 move objectward toward the telephoto end, and B4a and B4b follow the same path (¶¶0027–0028). The three printed infinity rows keep D26 = 12.62 mm. The aperture stop is surface 16, between the front positive part of B3 and its aspheric element (¶0023). B3b, surfaces 19–21, is the IS doublet (¶0026). The diagram labels the six cemented pairs C1–C6, so that the variable air gaps keep the patent's D labels; C4 is the B3b IS doublet. Figure 1 also brackets B3a (L7–L10, with the stop) and B3c (L13) inside B3.

The final surfaces 30–31 are a camera-side optical block: 1.50 mm, nd 1.54400, νd 60.0, followed by 1.73 mm of air. The model keeps it as a rear plate, not counted among the 17 lenses. The printed BF and total length are air-equivalent (¶0042, ¶0066), so the physical track is about 0.5285 mm longer than the air-equivalent value. Neither is a barrel-length measurement.

## Focus Mechanism and Calculated Zoom Motion

Paragraph 0028 specifies **imageward B4a translation** for near focus. Only D23 and D26 change with focus, and their sum is constant at each zoom position. B4b and all other units keep their infinity zoom positions. Canon's minimum focus distance is 1 m, measured object to image; the patent prints no near-focus spacings.

The nine focus positions at each zoom station are calculated paraxially at equal steps of inverse object distance, from infinity to the 1 m minimum focus distance. The largest B4a travel is 7.29 mm, at 195 mm. At 1 m the calculated magnifications are 0.061×, 0.141× and 0.205× at 56.6, 134 and 195 mm; the last is close to Canon's 0.21×. This agreement does not establish the production focus cam, and the model offers no finite-distance MTF.

The printed infinity rows carry rounding residuals of +0.021, −0.005 and +0.017 mm of paraxial defocus at the wide, middle and telephoto ends; they are kept as printed. The distance readout between focus positions is interpolated and is an estimate: the largest sampled difference from the paraxial object distance is 4.83 %, near infinity at the wide end (16 m shown, 16.77 m paraxial).

Interpolating the three printed rows linearly would leave up to 1.64 mm of paraxial defocus between them. The 30 intermediate stations therefore move B1–B3 linearly between the printed rows and shift B4 as one unit, changing D23 and D29 in opposite directions while D26 stays fixed and the total track follows the linear path. The remaining infinity defocus between stations is below 0.026 mm, comparable to the rounding of the printed rows, and the largest added B4 shift is under 0.4 mm. Only the wide, middle and telephoto stations are printed. The intermediate positions, and their f-numbers interpolated between 4.50, 5.70 and 6.45, are calculated.

## Aspherical Surfaces

Surfaces 17 and 18 on L10 use the conic-plus-even-polynomial form of ¶0065, with the conic term written as (1 + K). K = 0 on both, so the base is spherical, plus A4h⁴ + A6h⁶ + A8h⁸ + A10h¹⁰ + A12h¹². No scaling is applied, and the coefficients keep their printed precision.

## Image Stabilization

The patent describes transverse B3b motion for stabilization (¶0026), and Figure 3 shows the lateral aberrations with a 0.3° correction at the telephoto end (¶0015). The model shows only the centered state; decentering, stabilization range and IS effectiveness are not simulated.

## Semi-Diameters and Model Limitations

The patent supplies no clear semi-diameters. The values are measured from the optical rims in Figure 1, not the barrel or camera block. The parent's Figure 1 is the cleaner scan; it was read first and checked against Figure 1 of the divisional JP 2017-161933 A, which draws the same lens. The 85.68 mm first-to-last-lens vertex span sets the scale, and every drawn vertex falls within about 0.12 mm of the wide-angle infinity spacing. Both drawings show steps at the cemented joints. L5's rear face, L9's rear face and L16's front face sit 0.5–0.8 mm inside their partners. S15 and S27 therefore carry 7.7 and 8.7 mm. The other rims are within about 5 % of both drawings and keep their estimates.

L6 is drawn square at 8.55 mm, just taller than L5's rear face (8.39 mm), the two nearly touching across their 2.01 mm air gap. The model draws L6 square at 8.50 mm and L5's rear face at its drawn 8.4 mm, which keeps that order; the two curved faces then close 94 % of the gap without crossing, so the file allows that fill. The axial ray at the model's iris rim needs at most 7.98 mm at S8 and 8.18 mm at S10, at 195 mm and infinity focus. An exact ray launched at the full paraxial entrance-pupil height would need 8.23 mm at S8. That ray crosses the stop about 3.5 % above the paraxial iris because of the front groups' spherical aberration, so it is not a clip of the stated paraxial f-number. These are inferred apertures, not altered published numbers. The rim changes leave the geometric vignetting unchanged within 0.1 percentage point at every sampled field.

The full 13.66 mm image height is reached at every zoom station, with the ordinary mechanical vignetting of a compact telephoto zoom at the frame corner. Edge contrast at the calculated intermediate zoom positions near a quarter of the range is lower than at the printed stations; axial refocusing does not recover it, and the zoom path was not tuned to improve it. The model is a reconstruction from the patent, not a measured production lens: the clear apertures, the intermediate zoom stations, every near-focus position and the glass dispersions are inferred.
