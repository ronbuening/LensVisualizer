# CANON EF 24-85mm f/3.5-4.5 USM — audit

## Source

Makoto Misaka, Canon Inc., _Zoom Lens_, JP H09-304697 A. The application was filed on 13 May 1996 and published on 28 November 1997. Numerical Example 1 gives the prescription on pp. 4–5. Figure 1 shows the wide and tele sections. Figures 5–7 give the apertures at all three tabulated focal lengths. [Japanese patent publication](https://patents.google.com/patent/JPH09304697A/en)

## Transcription

All 30 surface rows match the printed radii and axial spacings in millimetres at the original scale. These include the main diaphragm SP at surface 14 and the independently moving stop MP at surface 20. The sixteen material entries preserve the printed indices and Abbe numbers, interpreted at the d line. The four coefficients B, C, D and E at surface 6A multiply the fourth, sixth, eighth and tenth powers of ray height. The base curve in ¶0034 is spherical. The table ends at R30 and gives no cover plate or back focal distance.

The calculated focal lengths are 0.04%, 0.11% and 0.16% longer than printed. Relative to the printed 24.84 mm wide focal length, the five group focal-length magnitudes are 3.482, 0.628, 0.889, 1.262 and 1.419. The corresponding values in ¶0039 are 3.5, 0.629, 0.89, 1.265 and 1.420.

The surface geometry has positive clearances. All fifteen zoom positions cover the frame corner, with no blocked chief ray or undersized image circle. The moving stop is the only axial limitation. It exceeds a 3% f-number difference at 45.80 mm (f/3.96 to f/4.11), 50.00 mm (f/4.01 to f/4.20) and 54.08 mm (f/4.12 to f/4.26). At the first four positions, 24.84–32.32 mm, a lower marginal ray cannot be traced at the corner. Calculated corner pupil transmission is 30.5%, 47.3% and 42.9% at the three printed positions.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

- **Clear radii.** Radii are scaled from Figure 1(A) using the 74.15 mm distance between the first and last glass vertices. Radii follow the optical outlines. Surface 3 ends at 27.9 mm to retain edge thickness in the front doublet. Surfaces 8 and 9 share a clear radius of 9.25 mm where their air space closes near 9.69 mm. Surface 23 uses 8.3 mm beside the marginal contact with surface 22. Surfaces 28 and 29 share an 11.0 mm radius where their air space closes near 11.59 mm. These six radii combine drawing measurements with surface clearance.
- **Stop openings.** Figure 1 places MP at a radius of about 6.59 mm at wide and 6.68 mm at tele. A constant 6.6 mm represents the opening. SP is drawn near 7 mm at both ends. Its paraxial radii from the printed f-numbers are 6.36, 7.08 and 6.87 mm. Paragraph 0028 describes a fixed SP opening and an independent moving MP. The two drawn openings cannot reproduce all three printed apertures as fixed radii.
- **Image plane and zoom motion.** The last air space at each printed station equals the paraxial back focal distance. Straight interpolation of all six spaces gives up to 0.829 mm defocus wide to middle and 0.264 mm middle to tele. At the twelve added positions, D13 places the paraxial image on the fixed film plane. The other spaces and f-number interpolate between adjacent printed positions. D19 + D20 + D24 remains 11.46 mm, preserving the equal travel of G3 and G5. The rounded prescription puts nominally fixed G4 within a 0.038 mm range relative to film. Maximum absolute defocus is 0.00010 mm at the fifteen stations and 0.0175 mm between them.
- **Materials.** The fifteen glass elements have Ohara catalog equivalents within 0.000011 in nd and 0.122 in νd. The 0.05 mm layer has nd 1.51640 and νd 52.2. Its thickness, shape and position suggest a bonded resin. The prescription gives its optical constants alone.

## Product Identification

Canon introduced the EF 24–85mm f/3.5–4.5 USM in September 1996. Its construction diagram places the sole asphere on L4 at the front of G2, puts the two stops on opposite sides of G3, and shows the three glass doublets, air-separated G4 pair and nearly flat first face of G5. Canon specifies inner focusing with USM. Claim 3 identifies the moving focus group as G2. The agreement in construction and filing date supports a strong association. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/ef337.html) · [Canon optical section](https://global.canon/ja/c-museum/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/ef337-lens-construction.gif) · [Canon U.S.A. specifications](https://www.usa.canon.com/support/p/ef-24-85mm-f-3-5-4-5-usm)

All four numerical examples share the broad layout. Example 1 alone tabulates a 0.05 mm layer at the asphere and has the fastest wide aperture. The drawing cannot distinguish Examples 2 and 3 from Example 1 with certainty. Example 4 moves G4 and its +130.20 mm front radius on G5 gives a less convincing match.

## Corrections

### 2026-11-25

The printed prescription requires no numerical correction. The model uses the patent's focal lengths and open apertures for the three published positions. Canon's 24–85 mm and f/3.5–4.5 markings describe the production lens. Canon specifies six diaphragm blades, f/22–32 minimum aperture, 0.5 m minimum focus and 0.16× maximum magnification. Only infinity spacings appear in the patent, so the production close-focus figures cannot supply a finite optical position. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/ef337.html)

## Remaining Questions

- The production glass types, resin formulation, finished clear diameters and exact diaphragm openings are undocumented. The middle-aperture discrepancy depends on the inferred MP radius.
- Canon's production diagram does not uniquely identify a numerical example or show the zoom and finite-focus cam paths. The twelve intermediate zoom positions describe one motion consistent with the printed spacings.
