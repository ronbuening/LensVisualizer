# CANON EF 24-85mm f/3.5-4.5 USM — Audit Log

## Source

[JP H09-304697 A, _Zoom Lens_ (ズームレンズ)](https://patents.google.com/patent/JPH09304697A/en), Makoto Misaka (三坂 誠; Google Patents romanizes the name as Mitsusaka), Canon Inc. Japanese application H08-142230 was filed on 13 May 1996 and published on 28 November 1997. The page follows Numerical Example 1, printed on pp. 4–5 with the heading F = 24.84–82.06, FNO = 3.58–4.68 and 2ω = 82.1°–29.5°. Figure 1(A) and 1(B) show the wide and tele sections (PDF p. 9), and Figures 5–7 give the aberration curves and apertures at the three tabulated focal lengths (pp. 9–10).

Canon's museum page gives the September 1996 introduction, 15 elements in 12 groups, six blades, f/22–32 minimum aperture, 0.5 m minimum focus and 0.16× maximum magnification, with a construction drawing marking one aspherical element. Its Japanese page adds a multi-group zoom, a replica aspherical element, ring USM drive, full-time manual focus and a non-rotating filter ring. Imaging Resource lists inner focusing and an 84°–28.5° diagonal field. A Canon camera manual states that minimum focusing distance is measured from the focal plane. ## Transcription Check

All 30 surface rows were reread against the page images, including the main diaphragm SP at surface 14 and the independently moving stop MP at surface 20. The radii and axial spacings match at the printed millimetre scale, and the sixteen material entries keep the printed indices and Abbe numbers at the d line. The four coefficients B, C, D and E at surface 6A multiply the fourth, sixth, eighth and tenth powers of ray height; the base curve in ¶0034 has no conic term, so K = 0. The table ends at R30 and gives no cover plate or back focal distance. No value disagrees with the page.

The surfaces keep positive clearances. At all fifteen zoom positions a chief ray reaches the 21.65 mm frame corner and no surface is undersized for the image circle. The moving stop is the only axial limit: it slows 45.80 mm from f/3.96 to f/4.11, 50.00 mm from f/4.01 to f/4.20 and 54.08 mm from f/4.12 to f/4.26. At the wide end about 5% barrel distortion puts the frame corner at a 42.9° half-field, beyond the 41.05° paraxial half of the printed 2ω.

- **Stop openings.** Figure 1 draws MP at a radius of about 6.59 mm at wide and 6.68 mm at tele; a constant 6.6 mm stands for it. SP is drawn near 7 mm at both ends (6.97 at wide, about 7.07 at tele). Its radius is calculated from the printed f-numbers at each position: 6.36, 7.08 and 6.87 mm at the printed positions. Paragraph 0028 describes a fixed SP opening and an independently moving MP; the two drawn openings cannot reproduce all three printed apertures as fixed radii.
- **Image plane and zoom motion.** The last air space at each printed position is the paraxial back focal distance. Straight interpolation of all six spaces would leave up to 0.829 mm of defocus between wide and middle and 0.264 mm between middle and tele. At twelve calculated positions D13 places the paraxial image on the fixed image plane; D5, D19, D20, D24, the last air space and the f-number vary linearly between adjacent printed positions. D19 + D20 + D24 remains 11.46 mm, so G3 and G5 travel together. The rounded prescription leaves the nominally fixed G4 within a 0.038 mm range relative to the image plane. Defocus is at most 0.00010 mm at the fifteen positions and 0.0175 mm between them.

- **Focus.** The patent prints infinity spacings alone, and claim 3 assigns focusing to G2. `focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity; Canon's 0.5 m, measured from the focal plane, is recorded as the product's minimum focus.
- **Materials.** The fifteen glass elements carry Ohara catalog equivalents for dispersion. The 0.05 mm layer has nd 1.51640 and νd 52.2; its thickness, shape and position, and Canon's description of a replica aspherical element, indicate a bonded resin. The prescription gives its optical constants alone, and no catalog row is assigned.

## Product Identification

Canon introduced the EF 24-85mm f/3.5-4.5 USM in September 1996, four months after the filing. Its construction drawing places the sole asphere on the fourth element, at the front of G2, puts the two stops on opposite sides of G3, and shows the three glass doublets, the air-separated G4 pair and the nearly flat first face of G5. Canon names the asphere a replica aspherical lens, its term for a resin layer that forms the aspherical surface on a spherical glass lens. Claim 3 makes G2 the focusing group, and the product is listed as inner focusing. The agreement in construction and timing supports a strong association. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/ef337.html) · [Canon construction drawing](https://global.canon/ja/c-museum/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/ef337-lens-construction.gif) · [Canon Camera Museum, Japanese page](https://global.canon/ja/c-museum/product/ef337.html) · [Canon, aspherical lens production](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/special/exhibition1.html) · [Imaging Resource specifications](https://imaging-resource.com/lenses/canon/ef-24-85mm-f3.5-4.5-usm/specifications)

All four numerical examples share the broad layout. Example 1 appears on the front page, alone tabulates a 0.05 mm layer at the asphere and has the fastest wide aperture. The drawing cannot separate Examples 2 and 3 from Example 1 with certainty. Example 4 moves G4 during zooming, and its +130.20 mm front radius on G5 matches the drawn flat face less well. Example 1 is the best or joint-best fit. [Patent, Figures 1–4 and Numerical Examples 1–4](https://patents.google.com/patent/JPH09304697A/en)

The production lens is 24–85 mm f/3.5–4.5; the example is 24.84–82.06 mm f/3.58–4.68 and prints no close-focus state.

## Corrections, 2026-11-25

- The prescription needed no numerical correction and the patent has no erratum to resolve; every radius, spacing, index, Abbe number and aspheric coefficient is printed.
- The station apertures became the printed f/3.58, f/4.01 and f/4.68. The first-received model held a fixed 7.0 mm main stop and labelled the stations f/3.25, f/4.06 and f/4.59, values traced through that stop alone. The main-stop radius now follows the printed apertures at each position; the moving stop went from 6.55 mm to the drawn 6.6 mm, which leaves the middle station at f/4.20 through both openings.
- The 41.05° half-field limit was removed. It is the paraxial half of the printed 2ω and stopped short of the frame corner, which barrel distortion places at 42.9° at the wide end; every zoom position now reaches the corner.
- The clear radii were replaced by the Figure 1 readings above. Several earlier estimates were wider than the drawing (surfaces 18–19 at 9.8 mm against 8.2 mm drawn, surface 24 at 10 mm against 8.8 mm, surfaces 15–17 at 8.7 mm against 8.0 mm) and some narrower (surfaces 1–3 at 27 mm against 28 mm). Surfaces 3, 8, 9, 23, 28 and 29 stay inside the drawing for the clearances given above.
- The zoom gained twelve calculated positions between the three printed ones. With the printed positions alone, interpolated gaps put the image up to 0.83 mm from the image plane between wide and middle and 0.26 mm between middle and tele.
- Focus stays at infinity, since the patent prints no finite state. Canon's 0.5 m minimum focus was added to the product data.
- Identity and product data: the lens became visible under the name above, with the patent subtitle and plain specification chips. The example is Numerical Example 1, the inventor Makoto Misaka. The catalog records 24–85 mm, f/3.5, 0.5 m, EF mount, 35 mm full frame, 15 elements in 12 groups, six blades and the stop scale f/3.58, 4, 4.5, 5.6, 8, 11, 16, 22 and 32. The four bonded pairs are tagged C1–C4, the groups G1–G5, and each element carries its shape and role.
- Ohara catalog equivalents were assigned for dispersion, keeping the printed nd and νd. The differences below are catalog minus patent. The resin layer has no catalog match and keeps the printed values.

## Remaining Questions

- The production glass types, resin formulation, finished clear diameters and exact diaphragm openings are undocumented. The middle-aperture difference depends on the 6.6 mm moving-stop radius read from the drawing.
- Canon's drawing does not single out one numerical example or show the zoom and focus cam paths. The twelve calculated zoom positions describe one motion consistent with the printed spacings, and the G2 travel to 0.5 m is not printed.
- Whether the silver and black finishes differ in anything beyond colour is undocumented.
