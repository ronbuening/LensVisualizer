# CANON NEW FD 35-70mm f/4 — audit

## Source

Keiji Ikemori, _Zoom Lens Having Two Movable Lens Groups_, US 4,299,452, assigned to Canon Inc. Japanese priority 25 April 1977, US filing 24 April 1978, publication 10 November 1981. Embodiment 1 gives the prescription in column 6. Figure 1 shows the section, and Figures 2A–2I give the three zoom positions and their aberrations. [US 4,299,452](https://patents.justia.com/patent/4299452)

## Transcription

All sixteen radii, with their signs, fourteen fixed axial thicknesses and air spaces, and eight pairs of d-line refractive indices and Abbe numbers agree with the printed table. All lengths use the same scale of 0.36 mm per patent unit. The intergroup space identified by ℓ in the table measures 30.4884, 9.4032 and 0.4752 mm at the three printed positions. All eight elements are spherical singlets. The prescription ends at R16, with air to the image.

The format-corner chief ray clears all surfaces throughout the fifteen zoom positions. Its image height is 21.63 mm for a 36 × 24 mm frame. ## Values Not Printed in the Patent

The stop lies between R10 and R11. The print gives their total separation of 2.4012 mm. The model places the stop halfway across it, 1.2006 mm from either vertex. Paraxial entrance-pupil geometry at f/4 gives iris radii of 7.088, 8.549 and 9.726 mm. The patent does not dimension the iris.

The endpoints of the refracting curves in Figure 1 give the clear radii. The fixed axial spans correspond to 42.210 mm, which sets the drawing scale independently of the illustrated zoom position. Bevels and label lines are excluded. The ink merges between R12 and R13. The following estimates are in millimetres.

At R2/R3 and R12/R13, Figure 1 draws the elements touching; the radii close to within 2% of the axial air gap, just short of that contact. The edge air is 0.158 mm at R2/R3 and 0.021 mm at R12/R13. At R7 and R8 the exact telephoto marginal ray requires 11.073 and 11.046 mm respectively. Rounding these heights up to 0.01 mm clears the axial ray and remains within about one pixel of the drawn edges.

The patent does not give a rear-surface-to-film spacing. The film lies at calculated paraxial infinity focus. At twelve positions between the three printed ones, the group separation is calculated for an intermediate focal length, then the rear air space is solved for the fixed film plane. At the printed positions the stored rear spacing minus the paraxial back focus is −0.000029, +0.000040 and +0.000024 mm. The absolute residual is under 0.00005 mm at every listed position. Linear interpolation of the two spacings gives a maximum residual of 0.01927 mm between positions. The intermediate motion is an estimate of the two groups' travel.

## Product Identification

Canon lists eight elements in eight groups for the manual-focus New FD 35–70mm f/4 of June 1979. Its cutaway shows the same three-plus-five division, individual lens shapes and internal iris as Figure 1. All three embodiments have eight elements. The root-mean-square axial-vertex differences on the cutaway are 0.70 pixel for Embodiment 1, 1.68 for Embodiment 2 and 1.76 for Embodiment 3. The latter two have maximum apertures of f/3.5 and f/4.5. The evidence strongly favors Embodiment 1. Canon specifies a 35 mm film frame, FD mount, a six-bladed diaphragm down to f/22, 0.5 m minimum focus and 0.15× maximum magnification. ## Corrections

### 2026-11-25

The prescription retains the printed radii, glasses and spacings, including the 0.9648 mm axial air gap between R12 and R13. At telephoto the spheres meet at a radius of 8.605 mm. An axial ray entering at the nominal f/4 pupil edge would reach 8.731 mm at R12 and 8.742 mm at R13, requiring a backwards step of 0.031 mm. At the adopted 8.51 mm clear radius, 0.021 mm of air remains. Those rims pass an axial beam equivalent to about f/4.09 at telephoto. The nominal aperture follows the patent and Canon's f/4 rating.

Using the paraxial image makes the wide-end overall length 0.113 mm shorter than the patent's printed value. The focal-length range calculated from the table is 36.00–68.50 mm, compared with Canon's marked 35–70 mm. Canon's 0.5 m minimum focus and 0.15× maximum magnification describe the production lens. The patent supplies only infinity spacings.

## Remaining Questions

A dimensioned production section would help establish the actual front and rear bevels, the stop position and opening through the zoom, and how the production lens accommodates the outer telephoto rays at R12/R13. Figure 1 and the production cutaway have insufficient detail to decide this. The manufacturer's glass identities and group positions at finite focus also remain unknown.
