# CANON 35mm f/1.8

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,854,890, _High Aperture Wide Angle Lens_\
**Inventors:** Jiro Mukai\
**Assignee:** Canon Camera Co., Inc.\
**Filed:** 4 January 1956, with Japanese priority of 14 January 1955\
**Published:** 7 October 1958\
**Example details:** Figure 1 and the numerical table in column 2. All printed radii and axial spacings are scaled by ×35 from the patent’s unit focal length.

Canon dates the screw-mount 35mm f/1.8 to May 1957. A collector’s history of Canon’s 35 mm lenses places its introduction in April 1956, alongside the Canon VT. The most distinctive evidence for its identification is the rear half of the lens. Both Canon’s product section and Mukai’s Figure 1 show a deeply concave cemented triplet just behind the central air space, followed almost immediately by a biconvex singlet. The front meniscus and cemented doublet also match in shape and order. This arrangement accounts for all seven elements in four groups. All surfaces are spherical. Neither Canon source marks a special glass or a separately moving focus group. The patent contains only one example. Its layout, aperture, assignee and filing date strongly support the identification. [Canon’s product section, p. 17](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00331/00331.pdf#page=17) · [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/s39.html) · [Collector’s history](https://canonrangefinder-org.pages.dev/Canon_35mm.htm)

The scaled prescription gives 35.029 mm at f/1.8, 0.08% longer than the marketed 35 mm. Canon sold the lens as a 35 mm f/1.8 with a 64° diagonal field on 35 mm film, and its 1958 booklet lists an amber coating and Canon’s new Spectra-coating. Mukai specifies a 60° field and plots aberrations to 30° off axis. In the model, the chief ray reaches the 24 × 36 mm frame corner at 32.2°. [Patent, columns 1–2 and Figures 1–4](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2854890A/en) · [Canon’s lens booklet, p. 17](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00331/00331.pdf#page=17)

## Optical Architecture

Positive singlets at the two ends provide most of the convergence. Between them, two cemented meniscus groups face the stop across an 8.05 mm air space. The front doublet has a focal length of −76.98 mm as a unit. The rear triplet has very little net paraxial power, despite the strong curvatures at its front. This leaves its internal surfaces available to correct aberrations. A 0.035 mm axial air gap separates that triplet from the last singlet.

Mukai connects the convex cemented face in the doublet with correction of lateral color. For coma around 20° off axis, he specifies the thickness of the doublet, its spacing from the triplet and the curvature of the first cemented face in the triplet. The normalized Petzval sum is 0.247, close to the patent’s estimate of 0.25. [Patent, column 2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2854890A/en)

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are for each thick element taken separately in air.

### L1 — positive front meniscus

nd = 1.6584, νd = 50.8. Schott N-SSK5 equivalent. f = +44.13 mm. The convex face points toward the subject. The singlet collects the outer rays of the f/1.8 beam and is separated from L2 by just 0.175 mm on axis.

### L2 and L3 — front cemented doublet

- **L2, positive meniscus.** nd = 1.6584, νd = 50.8. Schott N-SSK5 equivalent. f = +47.76 mm. L2 has the same printed glass pair as L1. Its rear face forms the convex cemented surface that Mukai uses to reduce lateral color.
- **L3, negative meniscus.** nd = 1.6987, νd = 30.1. Glass unmatched. f = −26.17 mm. L3 is the most dispersive element in the lens. Its strongly concave rear face borders the aperture space and gives the doublet its net negative power.

### L4, L5 and L6 — rear cemented triplet

- **L4, positive meniscus.** nd = 1.6204, νd = 60.3. Schott N-SK16 equivalent. f = +34.96 mm. Its concave front faces the diaphragm. The steep interface with L5 has a radius of −7.525 mm and leaves L4 thin at its edge.
- **L5, negative meniscus.** nd = 1.5750, νd = 41.3. CDGM QF3 equivalent. f = −14.04 mm. L5 has the strongest individual power. Its front follows the deep curve of L4 and its rear face is comparatively shallow.
- **L6, positive meniscus.** nd = 1.6779, νd = 55.3. Ohara S-LAL12 equivalent. f = +24.74 mm. The index increases by 0.1029 across its cemented face with L5. This exceeds the patent’s 0.08 lower bound for coma correction.

### L7 — biconvex rear singlet

nd = 1.6935, νd = 53.5. CDGM H-LaK6A equivalent. f = +43.00 mm. Its weakly curved front face sits close to L6. The more strongly curved rear face supplies the final convergence toward the film. Claim 2 gives a slightly different front radius and repeats L6’s Abbe number here. The model follows the description table for both quantities.

## Glass Identification

The patent prints d-line refractive indices and Abbe numbers for all seven elements. Six elements have modern catalog equivalents at those coordinates. The closest candidates for L3 differ in refractive index by about 0.00025. Its dispersion is estimated from the printed pair. The original glass suppliers and melts are unknown. A collector’s section hatches L6 and L7 but gives no key to the hatching. [Patent, columns 1–3](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2854890A/en) · [Collector’s section](https://canonrangefinder-org.pages.dev/images/35mm_f1-8.jpg)

## Focus Mechanism

Canon describes a non-collapsible, non-revolving lens head with distance scales from 1 m (3.5 ft) to infinity, and gives 1 m as the closest focusing distance. Neither Canon source says whether that distance is taken from the film plane or the lens front. Mukai gives the infinity prescription only. The model assumes unit focus, with all four groups advancing 1.3161 mm together, and takes Canon’s 1 m from subject to film. The paraxial rear spacing increases from 24.2578 mm at infinity to 25.5739 mm at 1 m, so the model focuses as close as the product. The calculated reproduction ratio there is 0.0376×, about 1:27. Canon Camera Museum gives no maximum reproduction ratio for comparison. The booklet’s “0.7×” is image scale relative to the standard 50 mm lens (its 28 mm and 85 mm entries read 0.56× and 1.7×), not a close-up ratio. [Canon’s lens booklet, p. 17](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00331/00331.pdf#page=17) · [Canon Camera Museum specifications](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/s39.html)

## Aperture and Field Illumination

Canon lists f/22 as the minimum aperture; neither Canon source gives the number of iris blades. The iris position is estimated from Canon’s drawing and the clearance behind L3. Its calculated radius is 6.78 mm at f/1.8. The clear radii follow the optical edges in the drawings, with extra clearance at L4 for the on-axis marginal ray. L4’s two surfaces meet at a radius of about 6.9 mm. The narrow opening passes the axial beam, but clips part of the oblique pupil. The corner chief ray still reaches the film. Calculated corner illumination at infinity is about 10% of the axial value at f/1.8, 24% at f/2.8 and 57% at f/8. The precise values depend on the inferred clear apertures.

At the patent’s 30° half-field, calculated distortion is −1.45%. Figure 4 shows about −1.6%. At f/1.8 the d-line marginal focus is about 0.0078 focal lengths ahead of the paraxial focus, close to the separation in Figure 2.
