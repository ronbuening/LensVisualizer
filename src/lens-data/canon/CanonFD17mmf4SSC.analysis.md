# CANON FD 17mm f/4 S.S.C.

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 3,748,021, _Reverse Telephoto Lens for Near-Distance Photography_\
**Inventors:** Akira Tajima and Kikuo Momiyama\
**Assignee:** Canon Inc. (Canon Kabushiki Kaisha)\
**Priority:** April 11, 1970, Japan\
**Filed:** April 5, 1971\
**Published:** July 24, 1973\
**Example details:** Embodiment 1, columns 3–4 and Figures 1–10

Embodiment 1 is a strong match for the FD 17mm f/4 S.S.C. Both have a 104° field, an f/4 aperture and eleven elements in nine groups. The patent’s only other example has an 84° field, an f/2.8 aperture and nine elements in eight groups, matching the FD 24mm f/2.8. [US patent, Embodiments 1 and 2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3748021A/en) · [Canon FD lens instructions](https://cameramanuals.org/lenses/canon_fd_lenses-lang.pdf)

The production specifications are for the 450 g S.S.C. lens introduced in March 1973 with Canon’s Super Spectra multilayer coating. The 490 g FD 17mm f/4 from March 1971 shares its 11/9 construction, 75 × 56 mm barrel, 72 mm filter thread and 0.25 m minimum focus at 0.10×. Those similarities and the patent’s 1970 priority suggest the design also applies to the 1971 lens. Canon lists the December 1979 New FD separately at 360 g and 76.5 × 56 mm, also with eleven elements in nine groups. [1971 FD](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd129.html) · [1973 FD S.S.C.](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd130.html) · [1979 New FD](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/nfd197.html) · [Canon FD lens instructions](https://cameramanuals.org/lenses/canon_fd_lenses-lang.pdf)

Canon’s S.S.C. cutaway and Figure 1 show two large front menisci, a thick positive element ahead of the diaphragm and a compact rear assembly. The patent resolves the cemented interfaces at R14 and R17 and locates the floating space between E4 and E5. The cutaway alone cannot establish the cemented curvatures or element travel. All patent surfaces are spherical. Neither source identifies an ED element. [Canon lens catalog, p. 16](https://www.360icon.com/PDF/CanonLenses.pdf) · [US patent, Fig. 1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3748021A/en)

## Optical Architecture

The patent sets the focal length to one. Multiplying every length by 17 gives dimensions in millimetres at the marketed focal length. At infinity the prescription gives a 17.00 mm effective focal length, f/4.00 and a 35.214 mm back focal distance. The front group from E1 through E5 has a focal length of −13.69 mm in air. The convergent rear group from E6 through E11 has a focal length of +21.05 mm. Together they leave more than two focal lengths between the last glass surface and the film.

The rear radius of E6 is the one sign error in the US table. Column 3 prints R12 as +0.7380. Figure 1 draws E6 biconvex. With R12 = −0.7380, the calculation reproduces both the printed focal length and back focus. The positive sign would give the complete lens negative power.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

Focal lengths in this section describe individual elements in air. The catalog glasses are equivalents for the printed helium-d indices and Abbe numbers.

### E1 — Negative meniscus

nd = 1.64000, νd = 60.2. Hoya LACL60 equivalent. f = −70.48 mm. The large convex front face receives the oblique bundles. The steeper rear face gives the element its negative power.

### E2 — Negative meniscus

nd = 1.69680, νd = 55.2. Glass unmatched. f = −52.87 mm. The second meniscus continues the divergence before the two positive singlets.

### E3 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.68893, νd = 31.0. Ohara PBM28 equivalent. f = +93.03 mm. The weakly curved front face and more strongly curved rear face begin to reduce the divergence from the first two elements.

### E4 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.64769, νd = 33.8. Ohara PBM22 equivalent. f = +74.75 mm. E4 follows E3 across just 0.0969 mm of air. Its nearly flat rear surface opens onto D8, where the axial rays travel nearly parallel to the axis.

### E5 — Negative meniscus

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.5. Ohara LAH66 equivalent. f = −19.61 mm. This is the strongest negative singlet. Its front surface closes the floating gap and its steep rear surface completes the divergent front group.

### E6 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.50137, νd = 56.2. Hoya C10 (catalog νd 56.30). f = +19.00 mm. The thick central singlet supplies the strongest positive power. The diaphragm sits in the air space immediately behind it.

### E7 and E8 — First cemented doublet

**E7, biconcave negative.** nd = 1.56384, νd = 60.7. Hikari J-SK11 equivalent. f = −20.21 mm.\
**E8, positive meniscus.** nd = 1.72151, νd = 29.2. Ohara S-TIH18 equivalent. f = +41.92 mm.

The two elements meet at R14. E8 offsets part of E7’s negative power. The assembled doublet has a focal length of −38.42 mm.

### E9 and E10 — Second cemented doublet

**E9, biconcave negative.** nd = 1.92286, νd = 20.9. Ohara PBH21 equivalent. f = −22.99 mm.\
**E10, biconvex positive.** nd = 1.48749, νd = 69.8. Glass unmatched. f = +21.74 mm.

The highest- and lowest-dispersion glasses in the system meet at R17. Their powers nearly cancel. The cemented pair has a focal length of +134.03 mm.

### E11 — Positive meniscus

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.5. Ohara LAH66 equivalent. f = +33.37 mm. The final singlet uses the same glass coordinates as E5. Its more strongly curved image-side face adds convergence after the second doublet.

## Focus Mechanism

The S.S.C. lens uses Canon’s floating system to correct the field curvature of close-distance focusing. In Embodiment 1 the entire lens advances, and D8 narrows by 0.5525 mm. The elements behind D8 therefore advance farther than the four in front. Figures 5–7 show the field curvature and astigmatism with D8 held at its infinity spacing. Figures 8–10 show the improved correction when D8 narrows. [MIR, FD 17mm f/4 S.S.C.](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/canon/fdresources/fdlenses/earlyfdlenses/1720mmfd.htm) · [US patent, Figs. 5–10](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3748021A/en)

| Quantity               |   Infinity | Patent near setting |
| ---------------------- | ---------: | ------------------: |
| Object to film         |          ∞ |           293.08 mm |
| D8 between E4 and E5   |  0.9775 mm |           0.4250 mm |
| Rear vertex to film    | 35.2138 mm |          36.5724 mm |
| Effective focal length |  17.000 mm |           17.010 mm |

The patent prints both D8 spacings, the infinity back focus and one finite object distance, 17.24 focal lengths from the film plane. At that distance the calculated rear spacing gives −0.0799× magnification. The model varies both spacings linearly between the two positions and ends at 0.29308 m. The production S.S.C. lens reaches 0.25 m from the film plane at 0.10×. [Canon Camera Museum, FD 17mm f/4 S.S.C.](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd130.html) · [Canon F-1 instructions, distance scale](https://www.cameramanuals.org/canon_pdf/canon_f-1-original.pdf)

## Aperture and Image Field

The S.S.C. diaphragm has six blades. Its full stops are f/4, 5.6, 8, 11, 16 and 22. The patent shows the iris in D12. Placing it at the estimated midpoint gives a calculated radius of 3.878 mm at f/4. The 20 glass clear radii are estimates from the drawn edges. The drawing ends the curved rear faces of E1, E2 and E5 short of their element edges with a step square to the axis, and bevels the front edge of E7. Flat lands carry those four faces out to the drawn edges without enlarging any clear aperture. [Canon S.S.C. specifications](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd130.html) · [Canon lens catalog, p. 16](https://www.360icon.com/PDF/CanonLenses.pdf)

Figures 3–4 put the 52° half-field at an image height of 1.235 focal lengths, or 20.995 mm. The chief ray reaches that height at 51.85° in the model. It also reaches the 21.633 mm corner of a 36 × 24 mm frame at 52.63°. The outer pupil is partly vignetted there, even though the chief ray clears the glass.
