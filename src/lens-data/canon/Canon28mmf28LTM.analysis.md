# CANON 28mm f/2.8

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,779,239, _Four Component High Aperture Wide Angle Objective Lens_\
**Inventors:** Hiroshi Ito\
**Assignee:** Canon Camera Co., Inc.\
**Filed:** August 24, 1955, with Japanese priority of October 1, 1954\
**Published:** January 29, 1957\
**Example details:** Figure 1 and the sole numerical example in column 2. The printed lengths at f = 1 are scaled by 28 to millimetres.

Canon introduced the 28mm f/2.8 for its screw-mount rangefinders in June 1957. Its period guide shows a distinctive construction. Each outer group is a positive cemented meniscus, each inner group is a single negative meniscus, and the rear group extends farther from the axis than the front. Ito draws all three features, including the orientation of both cemented interfaces. The section, the f/2.8 aperture and the timing make the identification strong. The patent contains no second example, and Canon gives no factory prescription. [Canon lens guide, p. 15](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00331/00331.pdf#page=15) · [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/s36.html)

The body table gives the second element a thickness of 2.324 mm at the 28 mm scale. Claim 2 gives 1.764 mm. The body value reproduces both the design focal length and the wide-open spherical aberration plotted in Figure 2. The resulting focal length is 28.0015 mm and the calculated infinity back focal distance is 19.1905 mm. The example specifies a 72° full field. Canon lists 75° for the production lens. [US 2,779,239, column 2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2779239A/en) · [Canon lens guide, p. 15](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00331/00331.pdf#page=15)

## Optical Architecture

The ten spherical surfaces form six elements in four air-spaced groups. Ito labels the groups L1 through L4. Every surface presents its concave side to the central diaphragm, including the two cemented interfaces. The arrangement is nearly symmetrical in shape. In power it is asymmetric, with focal lengths of +28.06 mm for the front doublet and +20.23 mm for the rear doublet. Ito associates the approximate symmetry with distortion control and the steep faces beside the stop with correction of astigmatism and axial spherical aberration. [US 2,779,239, columns 1–2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2779239A/en)

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are for each thick element in air. The letters a and b distinguish the two pieces of glass in each outer group.

### L1 — Front positive doublet

- **L1a, negative meniscus.** nd = 1.6166, νd = 36.6, Sumita F4 equivalent, f = −28.76 mm. The cemented rear face is more strongly curved than the entrance face.
- **L1b, positive meniscus.** nd = 1.6970, νd = 54.7, glass unmatched, f = +13.34 mm. Its higher index and lower dispersion give the doublet a positive member with a contrasting chromatic contribution. An axial air space of just 0.056 mm separates it from L2.

### L2 — Front negative singlet

nd = 1.7400, νd = 28.2, Hoya FD3 equivalent, f = −38.15 mm. This is the most dispersive glass in the example. Its rear face, r₅, forms the front boundary of the large central air space.

### L3 — Rear negative singlet

nd = 1.6889, νd = 31.1, Ohara PBM28 equivalent, f = −45.81 mm. Its front face, r₆, faces r₅ across the diaphragm. The two faces have nearly equal radii of opposite sign.

### L4 — Rear positive doublet

- **L4a, positive meniscus.** nd = 1.6583, νd = 57.1, glass unmatched, f = +12.31 mm. Its weakly curved entrance face lies 0.028 mm behind L3 on axis. Its much steeper rear face forms the cemented interface.
- **L4b, negative meniscus.** nd = 1.5673, νd = 42.8, Ohara S-TIL26 equivalent, f = −35.13 mm. This more dispersive member completes the doublet. Its exit face has the largest clear radius in the lens.

## Glass Identification

The body table gives six refractive indices and Abbe numbers, and Claim 1 identifies the helium d line. The index difference across the front doublet is 0.0804 and its Abbe-number difference is 18.1. The corresponding differences across the rear doublet are 0.0910 and 14.3. Both pairs fall within Ito’s stated bounds. The separate condition on the front singlet, printed as 1.30 > n₃ > 1.64, is internally inconsistent. Both tables give that glass an index of 1.7400. [US 2,779,239, columns 1–4](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2779239A/en)

The four named catalog glasses supply dispersion curves consistent with the printed coordinates. The maker’s guide mentions rare glass without identifying a supplier or assigning a special glass to a particular element. The production melts and the partial dispersions of L1b and L4a remain unknown. [Canon lens guide, p. 15](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00331/00331.pdf#page=15)

## Focus Mechanism

Canon specifies a 1 m minimum focus and describes a non-revolving head. The patent gives only the infinity prescription. The model assumes unit focus across that range. All four groups move 0.8295 mm toward the subject, increasing the rear-vertex-to-film distance from 19.1905 to 20.0200 mm. At 1 m from object to film the calculated magnification is −0.0296×. The period guide lists 0.56× for this lens, proportional to its focal length relative to 50 mm. Canon’s museum leaves the maximum reproduction ratio blank. The patent gives no measured focus travel or finite magnification. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/s36.html) · [Canon lens guide, p. 15](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00331/00331.pdf#page=15)

## Aperture and Corner Illumination

Canon specifies a six-blade diaphragm and a minimum aperture of f/22. Figure 1 places the diaphragm about 2.26 mm behind r₅ in the 5.32 mm central air space. The f/2.8 prescription gives an iris radius of 3.506 mm at that position. The drawing supplies the rim proportions. The curvatures and thicknesses of the cemented members limit their common clear edges. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/s36.html) · [US 2,779,239, Figure 1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2779239A/en)

At infinity the 36° chief ray reaches 20.35 mm from the axis. At 37.76° the chief ray reaches the 21.63 mm corner of the 36 × 24 mm frame and clears every surface. Most of the oblique pupil is already blocked at that angle. The front rim and the rear half of the lens clip opposite sides of the beam. About 12.0% of the pupil transmits at the corner, giving 4.2% illumination relative to the axis. At 1 m the corner chief also clears, with about 18.0% of the pupil transmitting. Actual wide-open corner illumination would require a measurement of the production lens.
