# CANON 28mm f/2.8

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 2,779,239, _Four Component High Aperture Wide Angle Objective Lens_\
**Inventors:** Hiroshi Ito\
**Assignee:** Canon Camera Co., Inc.\
**Filed:** August 24, 1955, with Japanese priority of October 1, 1954\
**Published:** January 29, 1957\
**Example details:** Figure 1 and the sole numerical example in column 2. The printed lengths at f = 1 are scaled by 28 to millimetres.

Canon introduced the 28mm f/2.8 in June 1957 for its rangefinder cameras, which take lenses in the Leica screw mount. Its period guide shows a distinctive construction. Each outer group is a positive cemented meniscus, each inner group is a single negative meniscus, and the rear group extends farther from the axis than the front. Ito draws all three features, including the orientation of both cemented interfaces. The section, the f/2.8 aperture and the timing make the identification strong. The patent contains no second example, and Canon gives no factory prescription. Canon lists a 40 mm filter size, a 48 × 20 mm barrel and a weight of 160 g. The guide gives a magenta coating and a non-collapsible mount. The aperture ring has click stops from f/2.8 to f/22, and the focusing mount has an infinity lock. [Canon lens guide, p. 15](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00331/00331.pdf#page=15) · [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/s36.html) · [35mmc review](https://www.35mmc.com/14/11/2019/a-short-review-of-the-canon-28mm-f2-8-ltm-by-atanas-grozdev/)

The body table gives the second element a thickness of 2.324 mm at the 28 mm scale. Claim 2 gives 1.764 mm. The body value reproduces both the design focal length and the wide-open spherical aberration plotted in Figure 2. The resulting focal length is 28.00 mm and the calculated infinity back focal distance is 19.19 mm. Focal length and maximum aperture agree with Canon’s figures. The example specifies a 72° full field, while Canon lists 75° for the production lens. The patent prints no near-focus state; Canon specifies 1 m. [US 2,779,239, column 2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2779239A/en) · [Canon lens guide, p. 15](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00331/00331.pdf#page=15)

## Optical Architecture

The ten spherical surfaces form six elements in four air-spaced groups. Ito labels the groups L1 through L4. Every surface presents its concave side to the central diaphragm, including the two cemented interfaces. The arrangement is nearly symmetrical in shape. In power it is asymmetric, with focal lengths of +28.06 mm for the front doublet and +20.23 mm for the rear doublet. Ito associates the approximate symmetry with distortion control and the steep faces beside the stop with correction of astigmatism and axial spherical aberration. [US 2,779,239, columns 1–2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2779239A/en)

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are for each thick element in air. The letters a and b distinguish the two pieces of glass in each outer group.

### L1 — Front positive doublet

- **L1a, negative meniscus.** nd = 1.6166, νd = 36.6, Sumita F4 equivalent, f = −28.76 mm. The cemented rear face is more strongly curved than the entrance face.
- **L1b, positive meniscus.** nd = 1.6970, νd = 54.7, glass unmatched, f = +13.34 mm. Its higher index and lower dispersion give the doublet a positive member with a contrasting chromatic contribution. An axial air space of just 0.056 mm separates it from L2.

### L2 — Front negative singlet

nd = 1.7400, νd = 28.2, Hoya FD3 equivalent, f = −38.15 mm. This is the most dispersive glass in the example. Its rear face, r₅, forms the front boundary of the large central air space.

### L3 — Rear negative singlet

nd = 1.6889, νd = 31.1, Ohara PBM28 equivalent, f = −45.81 mm. Its front face, r₆, faces r₅ across the diaphragm. The two faces have nearly equal radii of opposite sign. The curve of r₆ ends at a clear radius of 4.38 mm. Figure 1 shows a flat land extending from there to the 5.04 mm outer radius of L3, level with the edge of r₇.

### L4 — Rear positive doublet

- **L4a, positive meniscus.** nd = 1.6583, νd = 57.1, Ohara S-LAL11 spectral proxy, f = +12.31 mm. Its weakly curved entrance face lies 0.028 mm behind L3 on axis. Its much steeper rear face forms the cemented interface.
- **L4b, negative meniscus.** nd = 1.5673, νd = 42.8, Ohara S-TIL26 equivalent, f = −35.13 mm. This more dispersive member completes the doublet. Its exit face has the largest clear radius in the lens.

## Glass Identification

The body table gives six refractive indices and Abbe numbers, and Claim 1 identifies the helium d line. The index difference across the front doublet is 0.0804 and its Abbe-number difference is 18.1. The corresponding differences across the rear doublet are 0.0910 and 14.3. Both pairs fall within Ito’s stated bounds. The separate condition on the front singlet, printed as 1.30 > n₃ > 1.64, is internally inconsistent. Both tables give that glass an index of 1.7400. [US 2,779,239, columns 1–4](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2779239A/en)

Four named catalog glasses match the printed coordinates within the printed precision and supply their dispersion curves. L4a keeps its printed index and borrows the wavelength dependence of Ohara S-LAL11, a lanthanum crown of the same index whose Abbe number of 57.33 lies within the site’s spectral-proxy tolerance of the printed 57.1. No catalog glass comes that close to L1b, whose nearest lanthanum crowns, such as Schott N-LAK14 at 1.6968 / 55.4, differ by more than 0.7 in Abbe number; its dispersion follows the printed pair. The maker’s guide mentions rare glass without identifying a supplier or assigning a special glass to a particular element. The production melts remain unknown, so the partial dispersions of L1b and L4a are not established. [Canon lens guide, p. 15](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00331/00331.pdf#page=15)

## Focus Mechanism

Canon specifies a 1 m minimum focus. Its period guide gives distance scales from 1 m (3.5 ft) to infinity and describes a non-revolving head. Neither source states whether the 1 m is measured from the film plane or from the lens front; the model takes it from the film plane. The patent gives only the infinity prescription, so the model assumes unit focus across that range. All four groups move 0.83 mm toward the subject, increasing the rear-vertex-to-film distance from 19.19 to 20.02 mm. At 1 m from object to film the calculated magnification is −0.0296×, and the model focuses as close as the production lens. The period guide lists 0.56× for this lens, proportional to its focal length relative to 50 mm. Canon’s museum leaves the maximum reproduction ratio blank. The patent gives no measured focus travel or finite magnification. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/s36.html) · [Canon lens guide, p. 15](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00331/00331.pdf#page=15)

## Aperture and Corner Illumination

Canon specifies a six-blade diaphragm and a minimum aperture of f/22. The guide’s depth-of-field tables for the 28 mm lenses cover f/2.8 to f/22, in full stops from f/4. The patent prints no stop position or clear radii. Figure 1 draws the diaphragm about 2.26 mm behind r₅ in the 5.32 mm central air space, and the iris radius of 3.506 mm at that position is calculated from f/2.8. The clear radii are estimates scaled from Figure 1. Both doublets end in a sharp common edge in Figure 1. At the front, the curvatures and the 2.324 mm thickness of L1b bring its faces together at 5.72 mm. At the rear, Figure 1 draws the edge at about 6.6 mm, beyond the point where the printed L4a surfaces cross, while Canon’s section draws it at about 6.1–6.26 mm. The model sets it at 6.3 mm, between the two drawings. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/s36.html) · [Canon lens guide, p. 31](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00331/00331.pdf#page=31) · [US 2,779,239, Figure 1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2779239A/en) · [Canon lens guide, p. 15](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00331/00331.pdf#page=15)

At infinity the 36° chief ray reaches 20.35 mm from the axis. At 37.76° the chief ray reaches the 21.63 mm corner of the 36 × 24 mm frame and clears every surface. Most of the oblique pupil is already blocked at that angle. The front doublet and the rear half of the lens clip opposite sides of the beam. About 16.1% of the pupil transmits at the corner, giving 5.7% illumination relative to the axis. At 1 m the corner chief ray also clears, with about 22.9% of the pupil transmitting. Actual wide-open corner illumination would require a measurement of the production lens.
