# CANON NEW FD 35mm f/2.8

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 4,257,678, _Wide Angle Photographic Lens_\
**Inventors:** Kikuo Momiyama and Hideo Yokota\
**Assignee:** Canon Inc. (printed as Canon Kabushiki Kaisha)\
**Priority:** September 14, 1977 (Japan)\
**Filed:** September 11, 1978\
**Published:** March 24, 1981\
**Example details:** Example 1, column 4 and Figure 1. Multiplying every length in the normalized f = 1 table by 35 mm gives the prescription at the marketed focal length.

Canon introduced the New FD 35mm f/2.8 in June 1979. Its New FD bayonet barrel measures 63 × 40 mm and weighs 165 g, against 65 × 47 mm and 236 g for the FD 35mm f/3.5 S.C. (III) of July 1977, and MIR lists Super Spectra Coating. The f/3.5 S.C. (III) had six elements in six groups and focused to 0.40 m. The f/2.8 has six elements in five groups. Both have five diaphragm blades and stop down to f/22. [Canon Camera Museum, New FD 35mm f/2.8](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/nfd205.html) · [Canon Camera Museum, FD 35mm f/3.5 S.C. (III)](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd148.html) · [MIR, New FD 35mm f/2.8](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/canon/fdresources/fdlenses/35mm.htm) · [Canon FD Lens Guide, sheet SG2-A13](https://www.galimbertipaolo.it/CanonFD/FDLensGuide1.pdf)

The production cutaway on MIR shows a negative front meniscus, a cemented doublet, the diaphragm and three rear singlets. All three patent examples share that sequence. Example 2 has a thicker positive doublet member than the cutaway, and Example 3 has a shorter diaphragm space. After scaling each drawing, the RMS differences at the eleven axial vertices are 0.63, 1.75 and 1.87 pixels for Examples 1, 2 and 3. The proportions strongly favor Example 1. The cutaway's original publisher is unknown. Canon's museum photograph shows the exterior. ## Optical Architecture

The retrofocus design uses a negative front meniscus separated from a positive cemented doublet by 15.099 mm of air. Behind the diaphragm, a negative singlet is followed by two positive singlets. The distance from the first to the last vertex is 37.261 mm, while the back focal distance is 38.619 mm. This leaves room behind a short focal-length lens for a reflex mirror. The rounded prescription gives 35.007 mm at the 35 mm scale. Example 1 specifies f/2.8 over 64° diagonally. Canon specifies 35 mm, f/2.8 and 63° for the production lens. [Patent, Example 1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4257678A/en) · [Canon New FD lens instructions, p. 28](https://flynngraphics.ca/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/FD-Lens-Instructions-for-Web.pdf) · [MIR, New FD 35mm f/2.8](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/canon/fdresources/fdlenses/35mm.htm) · [Canon FD Lens Guide, sheet SG2-A13](https://www.galimbertipaolo.it/CanonFD/FDLensGuide1.pdf)

All eleven refracting faces are spherical. The patent gives the radii, axial separations, six d-line index and Abbe-number pairs, and the back focus. Figure 1 places the diaphragm between R5 and R6. Its estimated position is 24.50 mm behind the front vertex, where a 7.030 mm iris radius gives paraxial f/2.8. The printed image plane lies 3.44 µm ahead of paraxial infinity focus.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below describe each element in air. L2 and L3 share R4 in the assembled lens.

### L1 — Negative meniscus

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Sumita K-BK7 equivalent. f = −61.75 mm.

The convex face points toward the object. The steeper concave rear face diverges the beam. Condition (3) ties the bending of this meniscus to distortion and field curvature. Its curvature ratio is 0.2630, inside the patent's interval of 0.20 to 0.31.

### L2 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.8061, νd = 40.9. Ohara S-LAH53 equivalent. f = +14.76 mm.

L2 supplies the strongest positive element power. At its rear surface the index drops from 1.8061 to 1.60342. With the negative radius at R4, that index change makes the cemented interface strongly converging.

### L3 — Biconcave negative

nd = 1.60342, νd = 38.0. Schott F5 equivalent. f = −25.29 mm.

L3 completes the doublet, whose combined focal length is +32.59 mm. Its rear face is shallow. The inventors use the power at the shared face to balance spherical aberration, coma and the flare of off-axis sagittal rays introduced by the front meniscus.

### L4 — Biconcave negative

nd = 1.74077, νd = 27.8. Ohara S-TIH13 equivalent. f = −20.42 mm.

L4 supplies negative power immediately behind the diaphragm. Its front face has the stronger curvature. The rear face and the concave front face of L5 bound an air gap that narrows toward the edge.

### L5 — Positive meniscus

nd = 1.6935, νd = 53.3. Hoya LAC13 equivalent. f = +32.12 mm.

The strongly convex image-side face turns the beam back toward the axis. At its rear vertex only 0.147 mm of air separates L5 from the final element. The patent summary reverses the orientation of the stronger convex faces of L5 and L6. Figure 1 and the table place both toward the image. [Patent, col. 1 and Example 1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4257678A/en)

### L6 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.6935, νd = 53.3. Hoya LAC13 equivalent. f = +50.28 mm.

L6 shares L5's glass constants. Its front radius is 225.57 mm and its rear radius is −41.07 mm, placing most of its converging power at the last surface.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The table gives helium d-line indices and Abbe numbers. Figures 2A and 2D identify the d line in the aberration plots. The five catalog equivalents supply dispersion curves for tracing. Their indices and Abbe numbers round to the printed values. The largest Abbe-number difference is 0.043 for L5 and L6. The patent leaves the glass names and suppliers unspecified and identifies no extra-low-dispersion element.

Condition (5) requires the mean index of the positive elements to exceed both 1.7 and the mean for the negative elements. The two means are 1.7310 and 1.6202 respectively. The patent links this choice to the Petzval sum and the shape of the field-curvature plot.

## Focus Mechanism

The production lens uses a manual helicoid. At its closest setting the subject is 0.35 m from the film plane and magnification reaches 0.13×. The patent gives only the infinity spacings. The travel of the individual groups at closer distances is undocumented, so the model represents infinity only. ## Clear Apertures and Field Coverage

Nine clear radii come from the curved portions of the faces in Figure 1. The bevels at R7 and R8 obscure their endpoints. At f/2.8 over the central ±3°, the two faces need clear radii of at least 6.99 and 7.01 mm respectively. Radii of 7.02 and 7.04 mm leave 0.071 mm of air at their common clear edge. Extending both curves to the drawn shoulders would make the glasses intersect.

At f/2.8 the chief ray reaches the 21.65 mm full-frame corner at a field angle of 32.3°. There the clear apertures transmit about 56% of the modeled pupil, giving about 39% relative illumination. These estimates depend on the inferred rim and stop dimensions. The patent gives neither an image height nor a measured corner illumination.
