# CANON NEW FD 35mm f/2.8

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 4,257,678, _Wide Angle Photographic Lens_\
**Inventors:** Kikuo Momiyama and Hideo Yokota\
**Assignee:** Canon Inc. (printed as Canon Kabushiki Kaisha)\
**Priority:** September 14, 1977 (Japan)\
**Filed:** September 11, 1978\
**Published:** March 24, 1981\
**Example details:** Example 1, column 4 and Figure 1. Multiplying every length in the normalized f = 1 table by 35 mm gives the prescription at the marketed focal length.

Canon introduced the New FD 35mm f/2.8 in June 1979. Its five-blade diaphragm stops down to f/22, and Canon lists six elements in five groups. Example 1 gives f/2.8 and a 64° diagonal field. The rounded prescription gives a focal length of 35.007 mm. Canon's production specification is 35 mm and 63°. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/nfd205.html) · [Canon FD Lens Guide, sheet SG2-A13](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/01152/01152.pdf#page=13)

The production cutaway reproduced by MIR shows the same front negative meniscus, cemented second group, diaphragm and three rear singlets as Figure 1. All three examples share that sequence. The thickness of the positive member of the doublet distinguishes Example 1 from Example 2, whose member is thicker than the cutaway shows. Example 3 has a shorter diaphragm space than the cutaway. With one scale for each drawing, the eleven axial vertices agree to 0.63 pixels RMS for Example 1, 1.75 for Example 2 and 1.87 for Example 3. These proportions strongly favor Example 1. The original publisher of the cutaway is unknown, and Canon's museum entry gives an exterior view. ## Optical Architecture

The retrofocus design uses a negative front meniscus separated from a positive cemented doublet by 15.099 mm of air. Behind the diaphragm, a negative singlet is followed by two positive singlets. The distance from the first to the last vertex is 37.261 mm, while the back focal distance is 38.619 mm. This leaves room behind a short focal-length lens for a reflex mirror.

All eleven refracting faces are spherical. The patent gives the radii, axial separations, six d-line index and Abbe-number pairs, and the back focus. Figure 1 places the diaphragm between R5 and R6. Its estimated position is 24.50 mm behind the front vertex, where a 7.030 mm iris radius gives paraxial f/2.8. The printed image plane lies 3.44 µm ahead of paraxial infinity focus.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below describe each element in air. L2 and L3 share R4 in the assembled lens.

### L1 — Negative meniscus

nd = 1.51633, νd = 64.1. Sumita K-BK7 equivalent. f = −61.75 mm.

The convex face points toward the object. The steeper concave rear face diverges the beam. Condition (3) ties the bending of this meniscus to distortion and field curvature. Its curvature ratio is 0.2630, inside the patent's interval of 0.20 to 0.31.

### L2 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.8061, νd = 40.9. Ohara S-LAH53 equivalent. f = +14.76 mm.

L2 supplies the strongest positive element power. At its rear surface the index drops from 1.8061 to 1.60342. With the negative radius at R4, that index change makes the cemented interface strongly converging.

### L3 — Biconcave negative

nd = 1.60342, νd = 38.0. Schott F5 equivalent. f = −25.29 mm.

L3 completes the doublet, whose combined focal length is +32.59 mm. Its rear face is shallow. The inventors use the power at the shared face to balance spherical aberration, coma and the flare of off-axis sagittal rays introduced by the front meniscus.

### L4 — Biconcave negative

nd = 1.74077, νd = 27.8. Ohara S-TIH13 equivalent. f = −20.42 mm.

L4 supplies negative power immediately behind the diaphragm. Its front face has the stronger curvature. The rear face and the concave front face of L5 bound an air gap that narrows toward the edge.

### L5 — Positive meniscus

nd = 1.6935, νd = 53.3. Hoya LAC13 equivalent. f = +32.12 mm.

The strongly convex image-side face turns the beam back toward the axis. At its rear vertex only 0.147 mm of air separates L5 from the final element.

### L6 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.6935, νd = 53.3. Hoya LAC13 equivalent. f = +50.28 mm.

L6 shares L5's glass constants. Its front radius is 225.57 mm and its rear radius is −41.07 mm, placing most of its converging power at the last surface.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The table gives helium d-line indices and Abbe numbers. Figures 2A and 2D identify the d line in the aberration plots. The five catalog equivalents above reproduce every printed index to its stated precision and every Abbe number within 0.04. They supply dispersion curves for tracing. The patent leaves the glass names and suppliers unspecified and identifies no extra-low-dispersion element.

Condition (5) requires the mean index of the positive elements to exceed both 1.7 and the mean for the negative elements. The two means are 1.7310 and 1.6202 respectively. The patent links this choice to the Petzval sum and the shape of the field-curvature plot.

## Focus Mechanism

The production lens uses helicoid focusing. Canon gives 0.35 m as the closest focus and 0.13× as the maximum magnification. The patent prints only the infinity spacings. Neither the single cutaway nor the patent establishes the displacement of any group at 0.35 m, so the model represents infinity only. ## Clear Apertures and Field Coverage

Nine clear radii come from the curved portions of the faces in Figure 1. The bevels at R7 and R8 obscure their endpoints. At f/2.8 over the central ±3°, the two faces need clear radii of at least 6.99 and 7.01 mm respectively. Radii of 7.02 and 7.04 mm leave 0.071 mm of air at their common clear edge. Extending both curves to the drawn shoulders would make the glasses intersect.

At f/2.8 the chief ray reaches the 21.65 mm full-frame corner at a field angle of 32.293°. There the clear apertures transmit about 56% of the modeled pupil, giving about 39% relative illumination. These estimates depend on the inferred rim and stop dimensions. The patent gives neither an image height nor a measured corner illumination.
