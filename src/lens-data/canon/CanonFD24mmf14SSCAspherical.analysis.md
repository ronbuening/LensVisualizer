# CANON FD 24mm f/1.4 S.S.C. ASPHERICAL

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 3,992,085, *Large Aperture Wide Angle Lens System Employing an Aspherical Surface*\
**Inventors:** Kikuo Momiyama\
**Assignee:** Canon Inc. (printed as Canon Kabushiki Kaisha)\
**Priority:** July 4, 1972, Japan 47-66359\
**Filed:** February 21, 1975, as a continuation of the June 13, 1973 application\
**Published:** November 16, 1976\
**Example details:** Example 1, Fig. 1 and the tables in columns 5–6. Claim 8 repeats the prescription.

Canon introduced the FD 24mm f/1.4 S.S.C. Aspherical in March 1975. Its construction diagram strongly identifies Example 1 with this lens. Both show ten elements in eight air-spaced groups, a stepped front meniscus, a cemented pair on each side of the iris, and a biconvex singlet followed by a biconcave singlet just ahead of it. Canon places the polished asphere on the rear of the eighth element, precisely where Fig. 1 marks surface 14. The relative element diameters also agree to about 2% of the front-element diameter. [Canon specifications](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd132.html) · [Canon construction diagram](https://global.canon/ja/c-museum/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/fd132-lens-construction.gif)

Example 2 reverses the two singlets ahead of the iris. Example 3 moves the front cemented pair into group II. Example 4 opens to f/1.2. These differences distinguish Example 1 among the four prescriptions. [US 3,992,085](https://patents.justia.com/patent/3992085)

## Optical Architecture

Momiyama divides the eight air-spaced groups into three powered sections. Group I diverges the beam. Groups II and III converge it, with the iris between them. Their calculated focal lengths are −43.614, +44.701 and +33.576 mm, respectively. This distribution gives the reflex mirror 36.50 mm of back focus with a focal length of only 24.50 mm. The prescription uses the patent's dimensions in millimetres. Canon marketed the lens as 24 mm, and both sources specify f/1.4.

The first and last glass vertices are 69.618 mm apart. The patent places the film 36.500 mm beyond the last vertex, 1.7 µm behind paraxial focus. At the specified 42° half-field the chief ray reaches an image height of 21.61 mm. At 42.05° it reaches 21.65 mm, the corner height used for the 35 mm format. At f/1.4 the visible fraction of the pupil falls from 100% on axis to about 23% at the corner.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are calculated for each element in air. The glass names identify catalog equivalents for the printed optical constants.

### E1 — Negative meniscus

nd = 1.58913, νd = 61.1, Ohara S-BAL35, f = −71.04 mm. The strongly concave rear face diverges the incoming beam. Both drawings show a step between that face and the wider front rim.

### E2 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.58913, νd = 61.1, Ohara S-BAL35, f = +140.81 mm. This weak positive singlet lies between two negative components. Its front surface is nearly flat. The patent relates its rear curvature to distortion correction in group I.

### E3 and E4 — Cemented menisci

E3 has nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4, Ohara S-TIH6, f = +77.33 mm. E4 has nd = 1.58913, νd = 61.1, Ohara S-BAL35, f = −35.43 mm. They meet at surface 6. E4's steep rear face makes the pair divergent overall (−71.14 mm) at the end of group I.

### E5 and E6 — Air-spaced singlets ahead of the iris

E5 has nd = 1.71300, νd = 54.0, Hoya LAC8, f = +24.34 mm. This biconvex lens supplies the positive power of group II. Claim 3 constrains its rear curvature, which the patent connects to correction of coma and distortion. E6 has nd = 1.58921, νd = 41.1, Sumita LF2, f = −46.01 mm. Its biconcave surfaces partly offset E5's convergence across a 0.200 mm air space.

### E7 and E8 — Cemented pair behind the iris

E7 has nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4, Ohara S-TIH6, f = −16.91 mm. Its deeply concave front face looks toward the iris. E8 has nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.6, Hoya TAF1, f = +32.89 mm. This biconvex element shares surface 13 with E7 and carries the asphere on its exposed rear face, 14A. The pair has a focal length of −40.96 mm.

### E9 and E10 — Positive rear menisci

Both elements have nd = 1.77250 and νd = 49.6, equivalent to Hoya TAF1. E9 (f = +40.05 mm) converges the beam leaving the asphere. E10 (f = +52.59 mm) adds the final positive power before the film. Together they give group III its net positive power.

## Glass Identification

The ten elements use five distinct index and Abbe-number pairs. The patent labels them N and V. All five pairs match catalog glasses at the helium d line. E6 has the largest Abbe-number difference, 41.1 in the patent and 41.0 for Sumita LF2. The positive member of the front doublet and the negative member of the rear doublet have the same dense-flint constants. Neither the patent nor Canon identifies a glass supplier or designates an ED element.

## Focus Mechanism

Canon specifies a floating mechanism, 0.30 m minimum focus and 0.117× maximum magnification. Its diagram identifies the elements but gives no movement arrows. None of the four patent examples specifies a moving group, finite conjugate or change in spacing. The model therefore represents infinity focus only. [Canon specifications and diagram](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd132.html)

## Aspherical Surface and Clear Apertures

Surface 14A has a spherical base with vertex radius −33.487 mm and conic constant $K=0$. To the base sag the patent adds even powers of radial height in millimetres, with coefficients $A_4=2.0647\times10^{-5}$, $A_6=3.6845\times10^{-8}$, $A_8=-8.0225\times10^{-11}$ and $A_{10}=5.2622\times10^{-14}$. At the outermost tabulated height of 14.700 mm the calculated sag magnitude is 2.21322 mm. The table gives 2.21315 mm. Canon attributes correction of spherical aberration and astigmatism at full aperture to this ground and polished face. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd132.html)

The prescription gives a 10.681 mm air space between groups II and III. Fig. 1 places the iris 3.42 mm behind surface 11, leaving 7.261 mm ahead of surface 12. Its calculated wide-open radius is 11.20 mm. The clear radii follow the ends of the drawn curves. Surface 2 uses 18.9 mm to preserve clearance to surface 3, compared with about 19.2–19.3 mm in the drawing.
