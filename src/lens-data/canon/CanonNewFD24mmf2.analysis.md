# CANON NEW FD 24mm f/2

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 4,188,092, _Retrofocus Type Wide-Angle Objective Lens_\
**Inventors:** Kikuo Momiyama\
**Assignee:** Canon Inc. (Canon Kabushiki Kaisha on the grant)\
**Priority:** April 5, 1977, Japan\
**Filed:** March 31, 1978\
**Published:** February 12, 1980\
**Example details:** Example 1, Figure 1 and the table in columns 5–6. All lengths use a scale of ×24 because the table sets the focal length to 1.

Canon introduced the New FD 24mm f/2 in June 1979. Its 11 elements in nine air-spaced groups, f/2 aperture and 84° diagonal field fit two examples in Momiyama's patent. The rear cement seam distinguishes them. In the production cutaway reproduced by MIR, the seam lies **inside the biconcave component**. A single positive meniscus follows it. Example 1 shows this sequence. Example 3 has a biconcave singlet and places the seam in the following positive component. The almost flat front of the first lens behind the stop also favors Example 1 (radius 461 mm, compared with 68 mm in Example 3). Examples 2 and 4 are 28 mm designs. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/nfd200.html) · [MIR, angle of view](https://mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/canon/fdresources/fdlenses/24mm.htm) · [Production cutaway](https://mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/canon/fdresources/fdlenses/24mmf2optic.jpg) · [Patent, Figures 1 and 5](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4188092A/en)

These details strongly identify Example 1 with the production lens. The cutaway shows construction at one focus setting. It does not give manufacturing dimensions or show the motion of the focusing parts. MIR identifies the f/2 lens as all-spherical, with Super Spectra Coating, and describes its floating system. Neither the patent nor the production specifications identify an ED element. MIR credits the small barrel to a shorter optical system, smaller front elements and a diaphragm moved forward. The patent states the same aims: a short total length and a small front diameter. Canon gives a 52 mm filter thread, 63 × 50.6 mm and 285 g. ## Optical Architecture

Momiyama divides the nine air-spaced components into three groups by optical function. Group I (L1–L4) diverges even though its first lens is positive. It has a focal length of −20.33 mm. Group II (L5–L6) is a thick cemented doublet of +42.85 mm in front of the diaphragm. Group III (L7–L11) has a focal length of +28.77 mm and brings the beam to the film. This distribution leaves 36.05 mm from the rear vertex to the paraxial image, about one and a half times the focal length.

The patent specifies 24 mm, f/2 and a 42° half-field. It tabulates radii and axial separations with the focal length normalized to one. Multiplying those lengths by 24 gives millimetres. The rounded table then gives a focal length of 23.99 mm and a first-to-last vertex distance of 53.99 mm. The back focal distance is calculated for an object at infinity.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The individual focal lengths below are for each thick element in air. The cemented focal lengths include refraction at the shared glass interface.

### L1 — Positive meniscus

L1 (nd 1.58913, νd 61.1, +180.39 mm) bends oblique rays before they reach the first negative element. Momiyama uses a positive front meniscus to help correct the distortion of the divergent group.

### L2 — Negative meniscus

L2 (nd 1.68893, νd 31.1, −36.69 mm) introduces strong divergence at its steep rear surface. Its front faces the shallow rear surface of L1 across a narrow air space.

### L3 — Positive meniscus

L3 (nd 1.59551, νd 39.2, +96.56 mm) provides a second positive bend between the two negative menisci. The patent places the more dispersive of the two positive front glasses here to balance chromatic coma and chromatic distortion. Its rear surface bounds the floating air space D6.

### L4 — Negative meniscus

L4 (nd 1.58913, νd 61.1, −28.05 mm) gives Group I its strongest negative element. Its convex front faces D6 and its concave rear sends diverging rays toward the central doublet.

### L5 and L6 — Positive cemented doublet

L5 (nd 1.80518, νd 25.4, +34.75 mm) is the biconvex front member. L6 (nd 1.62041, νd 60.3, −210.19 mm) is a thick negative meniscus cemented to L5 at R10. Together they form Group II. The patent relates the axial thickness of this group, 15.62 mm, to the balance of field curvature and astigmatism.

### L7 — Biconvex positive

L7 (nd 1.804, νd 46.6, +25.97 mm) is the strongest positive singlet. Its nearly flat front faces the stop. Its much steeper rear face and the facing concavity of L8 enclose a meniscus-shaped air space that the patent uses to balance spherical aberration and coma.

### L8 and L9 — Negative cemented doublet

L8 (nd 1.6968, νd 55.5, +78.50 mm) is a positive meniscus. L9 (nd 1.80518, νd 25.4, −16.95 mm) is biconcave. Their shared surface R15 lies inside the overall biconcave profile. L9 supplies most of the doublet's negative power, giving the pair a focal length of −20.85 mm.

### L10 — Positive meniscus

L10 (nd 1.6968, νd 55.5, +39.55 mm) follows the negative doublet as a singlet. Its steep rear face restores convergence, and only 0.1464 mm of air separates its rear vertex from L11.

### L11 — Biconvex positive

L11 (nd 1.7725, νd 49.6, +55.11 mm) has a shallow front and a more curved rear face. It supplies the final positive power before the 36.05 mm back focal space.

## Focus Mechanism

For nearer subjects the whole lens moves toward the subject while D6, the air space between L3 and L4, widens. Column 5 and claim 5 describe this floating correction within the divergent front group. The model is focused at infinity, where D6 measures 2.2512 mm. The patent gives no finite spacing or magnification from which to determine intermediate positions. [US 4,188,092, description and claim 5](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4188092A/en)

The production lens uses helicoid focusing with floating correction. It reaches 0.11× at 0.30 m, measured from the film plane to the subject; the model stops at infinity. ## Diaphragm and Field Coverage

Figure 1 places the diaphragm between R11 and R12; the patent prints no separate stop distance. The printed third-order aberration coefficients put it 0.488 mm behind R11, leaving 1.4704 mm to R12. The diaphragm stroke in Figure 1 lies about 0.66 mm behind R11. At this position the paraxial f/2 iris has a radius of 9.10 mm. Pupil spherical aberration makes the real axial entrance radius 5.45 mm at that opening, equivalent to about f/2.20. Canon specifies eight blades and a marked aperture range of f/2 to f/22. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/nfd200.html)

The patent prints no clear radii; they are estimated mainly from Figure 1. Close surface spacing constrains R4 and R16, and the width of the clear opening at the flange on R8 is uncertain. At the 42° half-field the chief ray lands 21.22 mm off axis. Barrel distortion moves the 21.63 mm corner of a 36 × 24 mm frame to 42.49°, where the chief ray still clears every surface. With the adopted rims, geometric pupil transmission falls from 78% at 30° to 27% at 42°. These are estimates for the drawn openings.
