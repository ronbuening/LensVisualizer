## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP S47-8749 B (JP 1972-008749 B)
**Application Number:** JP S43-71246 (1968)
**Filed:** 1 October 1968
**Published:** 13 March 1972
**Inventor:** Michiyoshi Suwa (諏訪道美)
**Assignee:** Canon Inc.
**Title:** Short-total-length high-performance telephoto lens (全長の短い高写力望遠レンズ)
**Example details:** 実施例2; numerical table PDF page 4, section Fig. 4 (PDF page 9), aberration curves Fig. 9 (PDF page 12), third-order coefficients PDF page 6; f1, f2, e, L and Δ defined on PDF pages 1–3. f = 100 scaled uniformly ×3 to 300 mm.

The association with the FL-F 300 mm f/5.6 of May 1969 is probable; the patent example is not an authenticated factory prescription.

1. Example 2 alone among the five examples combines two CaF2 elements with the six-element, five-group layout and the 8.2° full field. Canon identifies two fluorite elements in the FL-F 300 mm, and its section drawing highlights the two elements behind the front glass, which correspond to patent L1 and L2.
2. Canon also cites two rare-earth glasses; the patent's L4 (LaSFo13) and L5 (LaFo10) are lanthanum glasses, which is consistent.
3. **Element count.** Canon lists seven elements in six groups. Its section drawing adds a thin, nearly powerless meniscus ahead of L1 that the patent does not list; its protective function is an inference. The patent example, and this model, have six elements in five groups; no radius, thickness or glass is invented for the front item. The remaining sequence agrees with the factory and 1970 dealer sections.
4. Canon's Camera Museum lists a 3.5 m minimum focus, a 58 mm filter and eight diaphragm blades; the 1970 dealer catalog gives 13 feet. These differences do not affect the prescription.

All lengths are the f = 100 example scaled ×3; the patent prints no 300 mm values. No foreign counterpart of the patent was identified.

## Optical Architecture

The design is a short telephoto. The positive front group has three blocks: two fluorite positive singlets and a negative cemented pair. A long airspace holding the stop follows, then a negative rear group of a negative and a positive singlet. There are six elements in five groups and eleven refracting surfaces, all spherical. The patent's aim is a telephoto ratio near 0.69 at F/5.6, against the 0.8–1.0 of contemporary telephotos; Example 2 prints L/f = 0.6904.

The scaled prescription reproduces the printed first-order values: front-group focal length f1 = 167.07 mm (printed 55.686 × 3 = 167.06), rear-group f2 = −142.22 mm (−47.407 × 3) and principal-point separation e = 104.04 mm (34.681 × 3). The element focal lengths below are for each element alone in air.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.43387, νd = 95.1. Glass: CaF2 (fluorite crystal), labelled CaF2 in the patent table. f = +128.69 mm.

First fluorite positive element. The patent turns the first block's strongly curved face toward the object to remove spherical aberration.

### L2 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.43387, νd = 95.1. Glass: CaF2 (fluorite crystal), labelled CaF2 in the patent table. f = +182.09 mm.

The second block shares the first block's positive power. The patent makes it a meniscus convex to the object to move the front group's principal point forward, which shortens the lens for a given focal length.

### L3 — Positive Meniscus, Cemented

nd = 1.78470, νd = 26.1. Glass: SF56A (Schott) catalog equivalent of the patent's SFS3. f = +345.35 mm.

Dense-flint member of the cemented pair that forms the front group's negative third block (pair focal length −111.21 mm). The patent states that Example 2's cemented surface corrects the chromatic variation of spherical aberration, coma and astigmatism, and also the chromatic difference of distortion, and so lateral colour.

### L4 — Biconcave Negative, Cemented

nd = 1.80440, νd = 39.6. Glass: J-LASF013 (Hikari) catalog equivalent of the patent's LaSFo13. f = −84.20 mm.

The patent explains that the front of the negative third block counters the pincushion distortion typical of telephotos, but that its curvature must stay moderate: a strong curve would over-correct the first two blocks, above all the spherical aberration and coma of short wavelengths. The block's strongly curved air-contact face is therefore turned toward the image.

### L5 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.74320, νd = 49.3. Glass: S-LAM60 (Ohara) catalog equivalent of the patent's LaFo10. f = −41.80 mm.

Strong negative element of the rear group, steep face toward the object. To ease distortion the patent places the rear group's negative lens nearer the stop and its positive lens on the far side.

### L6 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.59270, νd = 35.4. Glass: FF5 (Hoya) catalog equivalent of the patent's F16. f = +71.49 mm.

Positive rear element, steep face toward the image. With L5 it gives the rear group its net negative power.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent prints N and V without a line subscript and names each glass. The CaF2 and SFS3 indices match catalog d-line values, so the table is read as nd/νd. Each label has a present-day catalog glass at the same coordinates, which the model uses for dispersion:

| ------------ | ----------------- | --------------------------------- | -------- |
| CaF2         | 1.43387 / 95.1    | CaF2 crystal, 1.43385 / 95.1      | L1, L2   |
| SFS3         | 1.78470 / 26.1    | Schott SF56A, 1.78470 / 26.08     | L3       |
| LaSFo13      | 1.80440 / 39.6    | Hikari J-LASF013, 1.80440 / 39.61 | L4       |
| LaFo10       | 1.74320 / 49.3    | Ohara S-LAM60, 1.74320 / 49.34    | L5       |
| F16          | 1.59270 / 35.4    | Hoya FF5, 1.59270 / 35.45         | L6       |

They are equivalents by coordinates, not the 1960s melts, whose partial dispersions may differ. The patent prints no partial-dispersion data. The two fluorite elements carry the front group's positive power with very low dispersion; the patent's background names secondary spectrum, which a strong negative rear group magnifies, as a weakness of earlier short telephotos.

Fig. 9 plots spherical aberration in the d, g, F and C lines. Read at the axis and scaled ×3, it puts the C focus about +0.04 mm and the F and g foci about −0.05 mm from d. With the catalog curves the model gives C +0.048 mm, F −0.100 mm and g −0.149 mm, so its F and g foci lie about 0.05 and 0.10 mm shorter than the plot: the catalog curves only approximate the glass data the patent used. No apochromatic performance is claimed.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent prints only the infinity state and no close-focus spacing, so the model is infinity-only and its focus control moves no element. Canon lists a 3.5 m minimum focus; the 1970 dealer catalog gives 13 feet. Neither is a patent value.

## Geometry, Aperture and Image Plane

Every radius and thickness is the f = 100 example scaled uniformly ×3; indices and Abbe numbers are unchanged. The scaled prescription gives EFL 300.041 mm against the printed f = 100 × 3 = 300 mm; the 0.041 mm difference is left as it is.

The image plane is fixed by the printed L = 69.042 (207.126 mm at ×3), measured from the first vertex. The last gap, 83.193 mm, is L minus the axial track; the patent prints no separate back focus. The paraxial back focus is 83.219 mm, 0.026 mm longer, and the model keeps the printed plane without refocusing.

The patent gives no clear apertures. The four front elements have nearly equal outlines in Fig. 4, and their seven surfaces share a 28.9 mm semi-diameter, the 26.79 mm entrance-pupil radius plus 8 %. The rear pair uses 13.3 mm, from the Fig. 4 rear-to-front outline ratio 67/146 = 0.459. The 58 mm filter thread is a mechanical dimension, not an optical radius.

The patent prints no stop position. Fig. 4, which is close to scale, draws the diaphragm about 0.70 of the way along d7 between the r7 and r8 vertices; the model places it at 0.70 (59.825 mm behind surface 7, 25.639 mm ahead of surface 8), keeping the whole printed gap. At f/5.6 the axial beam clears every rim, and the real image-space working f-number is 5.66.

## Model Scope and Limitations

- The semi-diameters and the stop position are estimates. At the patent's 4.1° half-field the chief ray reaches the image at 21.63 mm, the 36 × 24 mm corner; the 28.9 mm front rims cut part of the full-aperture corner bundle, so corner illumination is indicative, not a production measurement.
- The patent's third-order coefficient table (PDF page 6) implies a stop slightly further back. Matching its coma-to-spherical ratios surface by surface puts the stop at 0.717 of d7 (61.278 mm behind surface 7, 24.186 mm ahead of surface 8), 1.45 mm behind the model's figure-based position. The model keeps the Fig. 4 reading.
- The model's Petzval sum, −0.1687/f, matches the patent's tabulated total of −0.16852.
- The production lens's thin front protective meniscus is not modelled; its shape and glass are unpublished.
- Only the infinity state is modelled. Dispersion away from the d line follows catalog equivalents, not the original melts.
