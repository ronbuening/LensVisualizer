# CANON FD 300mm f/4 L — Audit Log

## Source

Sadahiko Tsuji, _Large Aperture Telephoto-Lens_, US 4,251,133, assigned to Canon Kabushiki Kaisha (Canon Inc.). Japanese priority 52-137674 dates to November 16, 1977. Canon filed the US application on November 8, 1978, and the patent was granted on February 17, 1981. Numerical Example 4 is in column 4 (scan p. 9). Figure 4 gives the section (p. 3). Figures 11 and 12 give the aberrations at infinity and at an object distance of ten focal lengths (p. 7). [Patent scan](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/84/1b/72/204175dc61b55d/US4251133.pdf)

Canon lists the original FD L in December 1978 and the New FD L in May 1980. Its specifications give the aperture, diaphragm, close focus, magnification and construction count for each. Cavina compares the original FD L and non-L glass diagrams. The production cutaway on MIR shows the New FD L. ## Transcription Check

All twelve signed radii, eleven axial intervals, six d-line indices and six Abbe numbers agree with the scanned table. All six powered lenses are spherical and air-spaced. The patent sets the focal length to one. Multiplying every length by 300 places the diaphragm 15.06 mm behind surface 8, leaving 2.19 mm to surface 9 within their 17.25 mm air gap. The printed back focus is 0.3884, or 116.52 mm. The front group's Abbe-number difference, radius ratio R2/R3 and index difference reproduce the tabulated 40.4, 1.0437 and 0.3091.

**Iris.** The patent locates the diaphragm axially. Its radius of 16.77 mm follows from the 37.51 mm paraxial entrance-pupil radius at f/4. An exact axial marginal ray at that entrance-pupil radius crosses the iris plane at 17.07 mm. The 16.77 mm physical opening transmits approximately f/4.07 by real-ray tracing.

**Focus and glass.** Infinity is the only tabulated optical state. `focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. Figure 12 supplies no finite spacings, intermediate positions or magnification. The patent also omits glass trade names and partial dispersions. ## Product Identification

Example 4 is a strong match for the powered optics of the December 1978 FD 300mm f/4 L. Only this example places glass with nd 1.49700 and νd 81.3 in both front positives and separates the rear focusing pair by air. Example 1 cements the rear pair, Example 2 has a single UD-class positive, and Example 3 uses νd 70.1 in both front positives. Cavina assigns Example 4 to the original L and Example 3 to the ordinary FD 300mm f/4. Canon describes anomalous-dispersion glass and rear focus in the original L. [Canon FD L](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd177.html) · [Cavina's comparison](https://www.marcocavina.com/articoli_fotografici/Canon_FD_300_400_500_L/00_pag.htm)

The May 1980 New FD L cutaway shows a flat protector ahead of the six powered singlets in the Figure 4 layout. The separate 34 mm filter sits behind them. The protector accounts for the seventh element and group in Canon's figures for both L versions. The non-L S.S.C. has six elements in six groups with the same filter diameter. The later cutaway identifies the shared layout only to the precision of the drawing. [Canon New FD L](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/nfd228.html) · [New FD L cutaway](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/canon/fdresources/fdlenses/300mm.htm) · [Canon FD 300mm f/4 S.S.C.](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd176.html)

## Corrections, 2026-11-25

- The full printed f/4 aperture is retained. The former 4.125° half-field limit and reduced off-axis ray angle gave way to the field allowed by the rims. The frame corner is at 4.1°, within the patent's 4.2° plots. The calculated chief-ray half-field extends to 9.74°.
- The six estimated clear radii now follow Figure 4. The previous front radii of 39.0, 37.6 and 38.2 mm exceeded the drawn rims. L2, L3 and L4 need the extra clearance shown in the table to pass the axial f/4 beam. The other three adopted radii lie within the drawn ranges.
- The ×300 scale ties the unit-focal-length table to the marketed 300 mm. The model retains the printed back focus and every prescription value, including νd 81.3 for L1 and L3. Cavina's 81.6 describes his glass identification. Both patent entries read 81.3. [Numerical Example 4](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/84/1b/72/204175dc61b55d/US4251133.pdf#page=9) · [Cavina's glass comparison](https://www.marcocavina.com/articoli_fotografici/Canon_FD_300_400_500_L/00_pag.htm)
- The original FD L supplies the Canon FD mount, 35 mm format and 300 mm f/4 designation. The published count is seven elements in seven groups including the front protector. The model includes the six singlets for which the patent gives dimensions. The nine-blade iris spans f/4, 5.6, 8, 11, 16, 22 and 32. [Canon FD L specifications](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd177.html) · [Cavina's original FD L description](https://www.marcocavina.com/articoli_fotografici/Canon_FD_300_400_500_L/00_pag.htm)

## Remaining Questions

- The 1978 lens's clear apertures, physical iris diameter and blade curvature are undocumented. The patent gives rim proportions, and the available colour cutaway shows the later New FD L. Scaled by its first-to-last lens span, that small cutaway draws the three front lenses about 1–4 mm larger in radius than Figure 4 and the rear pair about 1 mm larger. The rims follow Figure 4.
- Production glass suppliers and melt data are unknown. Neither the front protector nor the rear filter has a published thickness or glass type in these sources, leaving the factory glass-to-film path uncertain.
- Column 3 describes the rear pair moving toward the object for close focus. With the Example 4 surfaces and a 3 m object-to-film distance, paraxial focus requires about 25 mm of travel toward the image. Figure 12 gives no spacing or displacement to settle the direction or travel.
