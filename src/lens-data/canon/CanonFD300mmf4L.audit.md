# CANON FD 300mm f/4 L — audit

## Source

Sadahiko Tsuji's US 4,251,133, _Large Aperture Telephoto-Lens_, was granted to Canon on February 17, 1981. Numerical Example 4 is in column 4 on page 9 of the scan. Figure 4 is the optical section. Figures 11 and 12 give aberrations at infinity and at an object distance of ten focal lengths. [Patent scan](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/84/1b/72/204175dc61b55d/US4251133.pdf)

## Transcription

All twelve signed radii, eleven axial separations, six d-line refractive indices and six Abbe numbers agree with the printed table. All dimensional values use a uniform ×300 scale from the patent's unit focal length. The iris divides the 17.25 mm air space between surfaces 8 and 9 into 15.06 mm before the iris and 2.19 mm after it. The table gives 0.3884 for back focus, or 116.52 mm. All six lenses are spherical and air-spaced.

The printed plane lies 0.056 mm in front of paraxial focus. Full-frame coverage reaches 100% of the nominal corner radius. At f/4 the axial marginal ray and the corner chief ray clear every lens surface. Off-axis marginal rays meet some rims, giving about 74% geometric pupil transmission at 4.1°.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

**Clear radii.** The 126.09 mm distance from the first to the last powered vertex provides the scale for Figure 4. Half the full height of each element reduces sensitivity to the slight tilt of the scan. Each element uses one clear radius on both faces. Where the drawn rim falls inside the f/4 axial marginal ray, the ray height sets the minimum radius.

**Iris.** Its axial position is printed. At nominal f/4 the paraxial entrance-pupil radius is 37.51 mm and the corresponding iris radius is 16.77 mm. An exact axial marginal ray launched at that entrance-pupil radius crosses the iris plane at 17.07 mm. With the 16.77 mm iris the real-ray aperture is approximately f/4.07.

**Glass.** The five catalog equivalents reproduce the printed indices to within 0.000002 and the Abbe numbers to within 0.051. Sumita K-PFK80 represents L1 and L3. Ohara S-LAH53, S-BAL35, S-FTM16 and S-LAH66 represent L2, L4, L5 and L6 respectively. The patent omits glass trade names and partial dispersions. The UD locations are inferred from the production diagrams.

## Product Identification

Example 4 alone combines two positive front lenses at nd 1.49700 and νd 81.3 with an air-spaced rear focusing pair. Example 1 has a cemented rear pair, Example 2 has one UD-class positive, and Example 3 uses νd 70.1 for both front positives. Cavina assigns Example 4 to the original FD L and Example 3 to the ordinary FD 300mm f/4. Canon's December 1978 FD L specification confirms anomalous-dispersion glass, rear focus and seven elements in seven groups. [FD L specification](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd177.html) · [Cavina's original FD L diagram](https://www.marcocavina.com/articoli_fotografici/Canon_FD_300_400_500_L/04.gif)

The construction diagram is for the May 1980 New FD L. It shows a front protective flat, six powered singlets in the Figure 4 arrangement and a separate rear filter. The protector accounts for the seventh group. Canon also lists the non-L S.S.C. as six elements in six groups with a 34 mm filter. The evidence strongly supports Example 4 as the powered design of the FD L. [New FD L construction diagram](https://mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/canon/fdresources/fdlenses/300mmf4loptic.jpg) · [Canon's New FD L specification](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/nfd228.html) · [Canon's S.S.C. specification](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd176.html)

## Corrections

### 2026-11-25

The prescription uses every value as printed. Its calculated focal length exceeds the marketed 300 mm by 0.11 mm. Cavina labels the two front positives νd 81.6 in his illustration. Both entries in the patent table read 81.3.

Canon counts the front protective flat in its seven elements and seven groups. The six-element optical model follows the patent, which gives dimensions for neither the flat nor the separate drop-in filter. Canon's 3 m minimum focus and 0.11× maximum magnification describe the production lens. The only tabulated spacings are at infinity.

## Remaining Questions

- The clear apertures and physical iris diameter of the 1978 lens are unreported. The section gives rim proportions, and the production diagram available in colour shows the later New FD L.
- The glass suppliers, melt data, and the thicknesses and glass types of the two flats remain unknown. The factory glass-to-film path cannot be determined from the six singlets alone.
- Column 3 describes objectward travel of the rear pair for close focus. A paraxial calculation for a 3 m object-to-image distance instead requires about 25 mm of imageward travel to maintain focus. Figure 12 supplies no displacement or spacing that settles the direction and travel.
