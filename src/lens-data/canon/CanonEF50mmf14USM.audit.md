# CANON EF 50mm f/1.4 USM — Audit Log

## Source

[JP H06-242370 A, 大口径レンズ (Large-aperture lens)](https://patents.google.com/patent/JPH06242370A/en), Masatake Kato, Canon Inc. Application 特願平5-27986 was filed 17 February 1993 with no priority claim, and the application was published 2 September 1994. The conditions are on pp. 2–3, Numerical Example 2 is the table of ¶0023 on p. 6, Figure 2 (its section) is on p. 9 and Figure 6 (its aberrations at infinity) is on p. 10.

Canon's product record gives the June 1993 launch, seven elements in six groups, eight diaphragm blades, f/22 minimum aperture, 0.45 m minimum focus, 0.15× maximum magnification, 58 mm filter, two high-refraction elements, a micro ultrasonic motor with full-time manual focusing, the construction drawing, and optics developed from the FD 50mm f/1.4. Canon's EF lens instructions (CT1-7538-004, 2006) give the 46° diagonal angle of view and a 164 × 249 mm field at 0.15×. DPReview lists unit focusing, and Wikipedia describes the non-rotating front extending by up to about 1 cm. ## Transcription Check

The 13 radii, 12 axial intervals and seven index/Abbe-number pairs of Example 2 were reread against the page image and multiplied by 50 to convert the printed f = 1 prescription to millimetres. All signs, thicknesses, air spaces and glass pairs agree. R8 is the cemented boundary between L4 and L5, and R6 and R7 enclose the diaphragm in D6. All surfaces are spherical. The example has no filter plate, no variable spacing and no printed back focus. The table heading reads f = 1.0, fno = 1.14 and 2ω = 45.4°.

The source has three misprints:

- All four example tables print fno = 1.14. The abstract and ¶0001 give f/1.4, ¶0020 refers to F1.4-class lenses, and Figures 5–8 are labelled fno/1.4.
- Condition (5) is printed as |r₇|/5 in the claim and in ¶0007 and ¶0011. Each example table gives |r₇|/f, which is the intended form.
- Claim 1, the abstract and ¶0006 list a negative meniscus convex to the rear as a "sixth lens" ahead of the positive sixth lens. No such element exists in the tables, and ¶0010 gives that description to the cemented L4/L5 pair.

Over their shared 15.0 mm band R4 and R5 keep about 0.05 mm of axial clearance at the edge, with the combined sag allowed up to 0.96 times the axial gap. Outside that band L2's rear face passes over L3's edge, as Figure 2 draws it. Reducing R4 and R5 to 14.85 mm would make R4 limit the aperture to f/1.416.

D6 totals 15.122 mm. The iris plane is placed 8.959 mm behind R6 and 6.163 mm ahead of R7. Figure 2 suggests about 8.45 mm behind R6, and Canon's section about 8.75 mm. At f/1.4 the entrance pupil radius is 17.857 mm, and its paraxial image at the stop has a radius of 12.354 mm. An exact axial ray launched at that entrance height crosses the stop at 12.220 mm.

The last air space is the calculated 37.575 mm paraxial back focal distance. The patent gives no finite conjugate or focus travel; ¶0021 states that Figures 5–8 are for an object at infinity. `focus.closeFocusM` is 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity. Canon's 0.45 m minimum focus is recorded as product metadata. Moving the whole model forward 7.09 mm focuses an object 0.45 m from the image plane at 0.142×. The same distance from the front vertex would give 0.113×, so Canon's 0.15× fits a distance measured from the image plane.

## Product Identification

Canon's construction drawing agrees with Example 2 in the seven elements and six groups, the stop position, the nearly flat R8 cement line, the L6 meniscus convex to the image and the biconvex L7. The 1.015 mm space between L1 and L2 distinguishes Example 2 from the other three examples, which each give 0.146 mm at the same scale. Fitted to Canon's drawing, the 13 axial vertices of Examples 1–4 differ from it by 1.39, 0.84, 1.26 and 1.22 pixels RMS. The evidence favors Example 2. The drawing gives no dimensions or example number, and the patent was filed four months before the June 1993 launch. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/ef318.html) · [Canon construction drawing](https://global.canon/ja/c-museum/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/ef318-lens-construction.gif)

Canon specifies eight diaphragm blades, f/22 minimum aperture, a 58 mm filter, 0.45 m minimum focus and 0.15× maximum magnification. The 164 × 249 mm field at that distance corresponds to about 0.145×. Canon describes the optics as a development of the FD 50mm f/1.4. It credits two high-refraction elements with reducing flare at full aperture and removing astigmatism, without naming them. The focusing is by unit extension driven by a micro ultrasonic motor with full-time manual focusing. ## Corrections, 2026-11-25

- The design aperture is f/1.4. The prescription as first received set the iris with a calibrated f/1.415 control meant to reproduce an estimated f/1.4 opening, and its catalog recorded the table's f/1.14. That heading is a misprint: the abstract, ¶0001, ¶0020 and the fno/1.4 labels of Figures 5–8 give f/1.4, and at f/1.14 the 21.93 mm entrance radius would send the axial marginal ray past R6. The iris radius is now calculated from f/1.4, the stop scale runs f/1.4, 2, 2.8, 4, 5.6, 8, 11, 16 and 22, and `source.errata` records the printed heading with this evidence. The numerical prescription is used as printed.
- The design focal length is the printed f = 1 at the 50× scale, 50 mm, in place of the computed 49.9999 mm.
- The 23° tracing limit and 46° nominal full field were removed. The chief ray reaches the 36 × 24 mm corner at 23.82°, beyond the patent's 22.7° half-field.
- The estimated clear radii were replaced by Figure 2 readings: R1–R2 18.95 to 19.2 mm, R3 17.8 to 16.2 mm, R4–R5 14.85 to 15.03 and 15.0 mm, R6 13.0 to 12.9 mm, R8 14.8 to 15.6 mm, R10–R11 16.0 to 16.2 mm and R12–R13 15.3 to 15.6 mm. R7 stays at 12.6 mm and R9 at 15.6 mm. R4 and R5 sit just inside the drawn L2/L3 contact, and the allowed combined sag rises from 0.932 to 0.96 of the gap so that R4 does not cut the f/1.4 beam. R7 stays above its drawn 12.3 mm land for axial-beam margin. Because these three depart from the drawing, `source.sdSource` is not set.
- L4 and L5 are marked as the cemented pair C1, as the shared surface R8 and Figure 2 show. The groups are bracketed as the front group (R1–R6) and rear group (R7–R13), ahead of and behind the stop as the patent divides them.
- The example label follows the printed 数値実施例2 as "Numerical Example 2". The subtitle and specs take the patent-and-maker form. Canon's product record and instructions confirm the Canon EF mount, 35 mm full frame, 50 mm, f/1.4 and seven elements in six groups already recorded, and add eight blades and the 0.45 m minimum focus. Each element has a name, type and role.

2026-11-25 rim review:

- The element outlines were compared again with Figure 2, whose scale was confirmed at 12.86 pixels/mm along the axis and 12.9–13.1 pixels/mm on the R3, R6 and R9 arcs, and with Canon's drawing.
- R4: 15.03 → 16.2 mm. Figure 2 draws L2 square-cut, with both faces reaching its 16.1–16.2 mm edge and the rear corner overhanging L3's 15.0 mm edge. R5 stays at 15.0 mm, where the two faces nearly meet, and the allowed combined sag stays at 0.96 of the gap.
- R6: a vertical land from the 12.9 mm end of the curve out to 15.0 mm, where Figure 2 draws L3's rear face meeting the flat edge of the element.
- R7: a vertical land from the 12.6 mm end of the curve out to 15.6 mm, the drawn step at the front of L4 up to the doublet edge. The land lies 1.06 mm behind the stop plane.
- The lands do not change the traced aperture, and the wider R4 is not a limiting surface. Geometric pupil transmission is unchanged at every field: 100% on axis, 38.5% at 20.9° and 24.3% at the 23.82° corner, before and after.
- Canon's drawing shows L3's flange taller than L2 and the lands on L3 and L4 bevelled. Figure 2 draws L3 below L2 with square lands, and the model follows Figure 2.

## Remaining Questions

- Factory dimensions would settle the iris position, the edge clearance between L2 and L3 and the optical edge at R7.
- Canon's drawing and Figure 2 differ on the height of L3's flange and on the shape of the L3 and L4 lands; the production mounting may differ from the patent drawing.
- The published glass constants do not identify the production supplier or melt, or which two elements Canon calls high-refraction.
- The patent gives no close-focus prescription. Unit focus of the model at 0.45 m gives 0.142×, against Canon's 0.15× and the 0.145× implied by its 164 × 249 mm field. Whether the production lens differs or Canon's figures are rounded is undocumented.
