# CANON NEW FD 28mm f/2 — Audit Log

## Source

Kikuo Momiyama, _Retrofocus Type Wide-Angle Objective Lens_, US 4,188,092. Canon Inc., named Canon Kabushiki Kaisha on the grant, filed the US application on March 31, 1978, claiming Japanese priority of April 5, 1977 (52-38690). The patent was granted February 12, 1980. Figure 3 shows Example 2 (PDF p. 4). Column 5 gives its focal length, aperture and half-field, and column 6 gives the prescription (PDF p. 12). Columns 7–8 print the third-order aberration coefficients of each example and the condition values of all four (PDF p. 13). Claim 5, in column 10, names the air space that changes during focusing (PDF p. 14). The patent carries no certificate of correction. [Patent text and drawings](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4188092A/en)

Canon’s museum records distinguish the 1979 New FD lens from the 1975 FD 28mm f/2 S.S.C. Canon’s FD catalogue describes the floating system and lists the diagonal field. Canon’s New FD lens instructions and MIR’s FD reference repeat the floating system and 75° field. The AE-1 instructions give the film format and the reference for the focusing scale. ## Transcription Check

All 19 signed or plane radii, 18 axial spacings and ten helium d-line index and Abbe-number pairs agree with the printed table. All curved surfaces are spherical. R10 joins E5 and E6, and the plane R11 separates E6 from the air in front of the diaphragm. Figure 3 brackets Group I at R1–R8, Group II at R9–R11 and Group III at R12–R19. Group II counts as one of the nine air-spaced components. There is no rear plate.

The condition table gives focal-length magnitudes of 0.9452f for Group I and 2.3889f for Groups I and II together. The prescription gives negative power in both cases. The focal lengths of E1 and E3 are +3.765f and +2.013f, and those of E2 and E4 are −1.195f and −0.992f. Group II is 0.4696f thick. The curved air space between E7 and E8 has a front radius of magnitude 0.7704f and a front-to-rear radius ratio of 0.8246. The mean index of the three positive rear elements is 1.7220. These values agree with the Example 2 column.

The third-order coefficients printed for Example 2 cover all 19 surfaces. With the stop 0.0175f behind R11, the prescription reproduces the coma, astigmatism, Petzval and distortion columns to within 0.0004 and the spherical-aberration column to within 0.0024. The Petzval sum is 0.1288, as printed.

The printed third-order coefficients fit best with the stop 0.0175f behind R11, and the model places the iris there, 0.491 mm behind R11 and 1.6706 mm before R12. ## Product Identification

The June 1979 New FD 28mm f/2 has ten elements in nine groups. The section reproduced by MIR agrees with Figure 3 in the four front menisci of alternating power, the flat-backed cemented doublet immediately ahead of the iris, and the four rear singlets. Their relative diameters agree to about 5%. Example 4, the other 28 mm f/2, has nine singlets. Examples 1 and 3 are 24 mm designs. The earlier FD 28mm f/2 S.S.C. appeared in November 1975, before the 1977 priority date, and has nine elements in eight groups. Together, the geometry, 75° field and chronology strongly favor Example 2 for the New FD lens. The original publication of the MIR cutaway remains unidentified. ## Corrections, 2026-11-25

- The factor of 28 converts all radii and spacings from the patent’s f = 1 table to the 28 mm example. The rounded values give a focal length 0.003 mm short of nominal. The radii, spacings and glasses stand as printed.
- The open iris follows the patent’s f/2 aperture. Its position then followed the diaphragm stroke in Figure 3, 0.70 mm behind R11 and 1.4616 mm before R12, until the 2026-11-26 change below. The earlier midpoint estimate split D11 into two gaps of 1.0808 mm. Every arrangement preserves the printed 2.1616 mm spacing.
- Clear radii from Figure 3 replace the earlier estimates. The former 17 mm front radius clipped the chief ray at the patent’s 37.5° half-field. At the drawn 20.6 mm front radius, the finished rims admit the corner chief ray at 38.16° as well. R4 uses 11.3 mm of the drawn 11.6 mm to keep the steep rim within 90% of its 12.586 mm radius of curvature.
- Only the infinity spacing is known. D6 is 1.9068 mm there and widens as the lens moves toward a nearer subject. The missing close-focus spacing leaves the intermediate positions undetermined.
- The production specifications identify the 28 mm f/2 as a Canon FD lens for 24 × 36 mm film, with ten elements in nine groups and an eight-blade iris. The full stops are f/2, 2.8, 4, 5.6, 8, 11, 16 and 22. Floating correction extends to 0.30 m from the film plane, where Canon gives 0.13×. The three optical groups and the E5/E6 cemented pair are marked as in Figure 3. 2026-11-25 rim review:

- R4 gains a flat land from its 11.3 mm clear edge out to 13.2 mm. Figure 3 draws E2’s concave rear face ending at a vertical step that rises to 13.2 mm, the mean of the upper and lower sides, before the bevel to the 16.6 mm edge. The land adds no aperture, so corner light is unchanged.
- R8 gains a flat land from 8.3 mm out to 9.7 mm. Figure 3 draws E4 with a square top at 9.6 mm and a vertical step down to its concave rear face at 8.1 mm, both means of the two sides. The 8.3 mm clear edge and 9.7 mm top stay within that reading. Corner light is unchanged.
- R15 rises from 8.7 mm to 9.1 mm. At 1,200 dpi, Figure 3 draws E8’s rear face and E9’s front face to the full edge of about 10.3 mm, crossing each other near 8.9 mm. The line earlier read as a bevel ending at 8.5 mm is that crossing. By the prescription the two faces meet at 9.64 mm. At 9.1 mm, R15 sits 0.23 mm clear of E9 and keeps the standard spacing allowance.
- Geometric pupil transmission at the 38.2° format corner rises from 38.0% to 41.4%. No field loses light, the axial pupil stays whole, the aperture stays f/2.000 and the corner chief ray still reaches 21.65 mm.
- The other readings agree with the drawing to within measurement error and stay: E1 20.6 mm, E2 front 16.6 mm, E3 11.2 mm, the cemented pair 10.4 mm, E7 11.1 mm, E9 10.3 mm and E10 11.9 mm. The MIR cutaway shows the same stepped rear edge on E2 and the same square top on E4.

- All ten elements keep their exact Ohara equivalents, so every element carries a full dispersion curve. Figure 4A draws the paraxial g-line focus 0.0023f, about 66 µm, ahead of the d line, and the model puts it 65 µm ahead. Figure 4C reads about −1.9% distortion at 37.5°, against −1.7% in the model.

Element types agree with the signs of the radii, each element's focal length agrees with the prescription, and Groups I–III and the C1 bracket span R1–R8, R9–R11 and R12–R19 as in Figure 3. No element carries a special-dispersion tag; neither the patent nor Canon names one.

## Remaining Questions

- The factory radii, spacings, clear apertures, iris diameter, blade curvature and glass identities remain undocumented.
- Figure 3 draws E8 and E9 overlapping at their edges. How the production mount separates them is unknown.
- The printed coefficients put the stop 0.49 mm behind R11, while Figure 3 draws the diaphragm about 0.2 mm farther back. Which position the production lens uses is unknown.
- Canon gives 0.13× at 0.30 m. The patent gives neither a finite conjugate nor a second value of D6 from which to determine the floating lens travel.
