# CANON NEW FD 28mm f/2 — audit

## Source

Kikuo Momiyama’s US 4,188,092, assigned to Canon Inc. and published February 12, 1980. Figure 3 shows Example 2, column 5 states its focal length, aperture and field angle, and column 6 gives the prescription. All lengths use a scale of ×28 from the table’s normalized f = 1. [Patent text and drawings](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4188092A/en)

## Transcription

All 19 radii, 18 axial spacings and ten helium d-line glass pairs agree with the printed Example 2 table. R10 is the cemented interface between E5 and E6. R11 is a plane glass-to-air surface, followed by the separate diaphragm. All curved surfaces are spherical. Figure 3 brackets Group I at R1–R8, Group II at R9–R11 and Group III at R12–R19. The middle group has two elements and one air-spaced component.

The Example 2 condition table gives the magnitudes of the negative focal lengths of Group I and of Groups I and II together as 0.9452f and 2.3889f. Both follow from the tabulated prescription. The rear air-lens radius ratio of 0.8246 and the mean index of 1.7220 for the positive rear elements also agree.

The infinity image plane is within 0.00005 mm of paraxial focus. The clear radii admit the axial beam at the modeled iris and the chief ray to the format corner. The tightest axial clearance is 0.13 mm at R15. The corner chief ray has at least 1.08 mm clearance, at R4. About 38% of the geometrical pupil passes at that corner.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

Figure 3 gives the relative sizes of the clear faces. The 50.3244 mm interval from the first vertex to the last sets the drawing scale. The radii below are in millimeters, in surface order. R8 ends at the curved edge inside the 9.7 mm outer rim of E4.

| Surfaces | Clear radii            |
| -------- | ---------------------- |
| R1–R4    | 20.6, 20.6, 16.6, 11.3 |
| R5–R8    | 11.2, 11.2, 9.7, 8.3   |
| R9–R11   | 10.4, 10.4, 10.4       |
| R12–R15  | 11.1, 11.1, 10.4, 8.7  |
| R16–R19  | 10.3, 10.3, 11.9, 11.9 |

The drawn R4 radius is about 11.6 mm. At 11.3 mm the clear face stays within 90% of its 12.586 mm radius of curvature. The R15 curve ends near 8.5 mm before a bevel. A clear radius of 8.7 mm passes the 8.57 mm axial marginal ray at the modeled iris.

The diaphragm stroke in Figure 3 places it 0.70 mm behind R11 and 1.4616 mm before R12. These distances sum to the printed D11. The paraxial f/2 aperture gives an 8.9684 mm iris radius. An exact marginal ray reaching that iris enters at a height of 6.443 mm, giving f/2.17 by the entrance diameter. Launching instead at half the paraxial entrance pupil diameter (6.999 mm) would require a 9.99 mm iris radius and 9.49 mm of clearance at R15. The paraxial infinity focus supplies the rear image distance.

The printed nd and νd pairs determine the choice of catalog equivalents. Neither the patent nor the cutaway identifies glass melts or suppliers.

## Product Identification

Canon’s June 1979 New FD lens has ten elements in nine groups, an eight-blade iris through f/22, and floating correction to 0.30 m. The section reproduced by MIR agrees with the four alternating front menisci, the flat-backed cemented pair immediately ahead of the stop, and all four rear singlets. The 28 mm Example 4 lacks the doublet, and Examples 1 and 3 are 24 mm. Taken together, the count, shapes, field angle and chronology strongly favor Example 2. The cutaway’s original publication is unidentified. ## Corrections

### 2026-11-25

The prescription reproduces the table as printed. The 0.003 mm difference from the nominal 28 mm follows from its rounded values. The R4 and R15 clear radii differ slightly from the drawing as described above.

## Remaining Questions

The exact factory clear apertures, iris diameter and glass identities remain unknown. Canon quotes 0.13× at 0.30 m. The patent describes how D6 widens but gives no finite conjugate or corresponding spacing from which to calculate the production lens’s close-focus travel.
