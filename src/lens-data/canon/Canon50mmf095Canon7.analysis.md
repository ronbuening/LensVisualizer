## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** JP S39-10178 B (特許出願公告 昭39-10178).
**Application Number:** 特願 昭35-43650.
**Filed:** 1 November 1960.
**Published:** 10 June 1964.
**Inventor:** Jiro Mukai, romanization of the controlling printed 向井二郎.
**Applicant:** Canon Camera Co., Inc. (キヤノンカメラ株式会社).
**Title:** 高写力写真レンズ, high-aperture photographic lens.
**Example details:** original PDF pages 1–2; Figure 1 on page 3.

This is a qualified probable association with the Canon 50 mm f/0.95 for the Canon 7, marketed in August 1961, not a manufacturer-certified production prescription. Convergent evidence is:

1. The rare f/0.95 aperture and seven-element/five-group construction agree.
2. Two object-convex positive menisci precede opposing positive/negative and negative/positive cemented pairs, followed by a positive singlet. The original maker section supports that ordered architecture.
3. The patent discusses a 50 mm case for a 35 mm camera with a restricted rear opening. The large reduction in group outside diameters agrees qualitatively with the product section.
4. Filing precedes marketing. Neither chronology nor the schematic establishes exact factory radii, glass melts or serial/revision identity.

Canon identifies the clip-on outer three-lug bayonet around the Canon 7’s S screw mount; catalog mount metadata uses `canon-7-bayonet` (Canon 7 bayonet). Canon also describes an asymmetric cutout at the edge of the rearmost lens element. That is not an iris cutout; its shape and optical effects are outside this rotationally symmetric reconstruction.

## Source Prescription and Optical Architecture

The literal spherical prescription is scaled uniformly by 50 mm per normalized source unit. Every signed radius, internal interval and d-line glass pair is preserved. Source S1–S4 are air-gap symbols, not surface labels. The twelve refracting boundaries include each cemented interface once. Missing image space is supplied by the calculated Gaussian back focal distance, rather than a printed zero gap.

The resulting EFL is 50.037203396 mm, BFL from r12 is 19.742885854 mm, first-to-last vertex span is 53.000 mm, and first-vertex-to-Gaussian-image track is 72.742885854 mm. The catalog design spec states the source’s nominal f = 1 × 50 = 50.00 mm; the calculated EFL residual is retained. The architecture is a modified Gauss type, not a demonstrated telephoto or retrofocus layout.

The isolated five-group powers are positive, positive, negative, weak positive and positive: equivalent focal lengths approximately +120.573, +148.959, −74.992, +558.457 and +40.794 mm. These are standalone compound-group calculations in air, not in-situ contributions or an allocation of measured aberration correction.

The source tabulates a 45° full field. Its introduction says 46°, and its plots extend to a 23° half field. The 22.5° table half field and the 23° plot half field are treated separately.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1: Positive Meniscus, Convex Toward the Object

nd = 1.79952, νd = 42.3. Glass: S-LAH52 (OHARA coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed). f = +120.573 mm.

The high-index front meniscus supplies positive power while accepting the large entrance beam. Its source outside group diameter is 60.5 mm; the inferred circular powered cap uses a 30 mm semi-diameter, not the 72 mm filter or 79 mm barrel dimensions. OHARA S-LAH52 supplies a catalog dispersion equivalent without identifying the historical supplier or melt.

### L2: Positive Meniscus, Convex Toward the Object

nd = 1.67003, νd = 47.2. Glass: BAF10 (HOYA coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed). f = +148.959 mm.

This second positive meniscus shares the front positive power and reduces the beam toward the first cemented group. Its inferred 25 mm cap remains below the printed 25.25 mm outside radius. HOYA BAF10 supplies the catalog dispersion equivalent; the original production glass remains unconfirmed.

### L3: Biconvex Positive, Front Component of the First Cemented Pair

nd = 1.69350, νd = 53.4. Glass: LAC13 (HOYA coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed). f = +34.173 mm.

The thick positive component precedes the lower-Abbe negative component. The patent uses their index difference and the facing strong concavity as part of its stated spherical/coma balancing strategy. That design rationale does not establish a unique quantitative aberration contribution for this element.

### L4: Biconcave Negative, Rear Component of the First Cemented Pair

nd = 1.75520, νd = 27.5. Glass: E-FD4 (HOYA coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed). f = −18.043 mm.

The glass-to-glass boundary r6 is shared with L3. The much stronger positive-radius r7 faces the central cavity and is a critical aperture and geometry constraint. Its radius is only 15.1 mm at the adopted scale, so it cannot be extended to the third group's 21.5 mm physical outside radius. The inferred active r7 cap is 12.844194828245 mm, the exact f/0.95 axial marginal-ray height. The resulting L3/L4 group is net negative in isolation, despite L3's strong positive power. HOYA E-FD4 supplies the catalog dispersion equivalent, with the historical production melt unconfirmed.

### L5: Biconcave Negative, Front Component of the Rear Cemented Pair

nd = 1.59270, νd = 35.4. Glass: FF5 (HOYA coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed). f = −28.205 mm.

This opposing concavity bounds the rear side of the central cavity. The source describes the rear cemented pair's action as opposing tendencies from the front pair. The model preserves that asymmetric arrangement without imposing an exactly symmetric Gauss form. Its r8 clear cap is inferred as 12.793310563366 mm from the same marginal ray, while the following cemented boundary remains within the printed 14 mm group outside radius.

### L6: Biconvex Positive, Rear Component of the Rear Cemented Pair

nd = 1.78590, νd = 44.3. Glass: S-LAH51 (OHARA coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed). f = +30.237 mm.

L6 shares r9 with L5 and restores positive power after the cavity. Their isolated net group power is only weakly positive; summing isolated element powers would not reproduce the compound result. OHARA S-LAH51 supplies catalog dispersion with residuals Δnd = −0.000004 and Δνd = −0.097363 (catalog minus printed); the literal source pair remains unchanged.

### L7: Biconvex Positive Rear Singlet

nd = 1.78590, νd = 44.3. Glass: S-LAH51 (OHARA coordinate equivalent used for dispersion; production melt unconfirmed). f = +40.794 mm.

The final positive singlet uses the same printed glass pair as L6, but remains a separate element and group. Its circular modeled 17 mm cap is below the printed 17.25 mm outside radius. The maker's asymmetric edge cutout is deliberately not fabricated. Circular model transmission therefore cannot establish actual rangefinder-roller clearance or cutout-dependent illumination.

## Glass Reference, Petzval and Source Arithmetic

The source explicitly uses d-line refractive indices and Abbe numbers. All seven printed pairs remain authoritative at d. Catalog dispersion uses OHARA S-LAH52 for L1, HOYA BAF10 for L2, HOYA LAC13 for L3, HOYA E-FD4 for L4, HOYA FF5 for L5 and OHARA S-LAH51 for L6/L7. All seven have coordinate equivalents within |Δνd| ≤ 0.1; none remains unmatched. These spectral proxies do not identify the historical supplier or production melt, or establish measured full dispersion. No APO or anomalous-partial-dispersion claim is made.

Surface-by-surface Petzval contributions, including both glass-to-glass boundaries, sum to 0.00588529732967 mm⁻¹, or 0.294264866483 in the source normalization. The printed P7 entry is −0.4247, whereas the unchanged prescription yields −1.424716415027. The literal displayed P rows sum to 1.2944; the printed total is 0.2943, which agrees with the rounded full-precision prescription sum. These are distinct facts, not a silent source correction. The final displayed Seidel coefficient column also sums literally to 2.0255 versus its printed 0.0255 total. The arithmetic is reproduced; a historical cause or complete third-order reproduction is not claimed.

## Stop, Aperture and Focus

No numerical stop coordinate or physical iris diameter is printed. The inferred central iris divides unchanged source S3 into 7.25 + 4.05 mm. A midpoint iris would lie ahead of the outer axial intersection with r7, so the adopted plane is farther toward the image. The iris radius is calculated from nominal f/0.95 and remains 12.827526822 mm. Canon specifies ten blades and an aperture range of f/0.95–16.

The paraxial entrance-pupil calculation gives f/0.940835126, distinct from the exact-ray nominal f/0.95 calibration. The r7 and r8 clear radii are raised from 12.35 mm only to their exact axial marginal-ray heights. Figure 1 shows tapered edges there, so neither adjoining larger face is enlarged. The square-cut r9/r10 pair retains its 14 mm radius, matching the source's 28 mm fourth-group outside diameter. The nominal marginal ray reaches 14.064801959101 mm at r10 and is clipped there. The admitted infinity beam therefore corresponds to approximately f/0.953336, rather than an entirely clear f/0.95 pupil. This is a geometric result, not a measured T-stop or uniform-illumination claim. Before the central-rim changes, r7 limited the admitted entrance radius to approximately 24.964491 mm, or f/1.002168.

Whole-lens helicoid focusing is modeled by changing only the final image-space gap. The patent publishes only infinity geometry; the 1 m endpoint is a paraxial reconstruction using Canon's object-to-film minimum focus distance. For the matrix from the first to last refracting vertex, [A B; C D] = [0.3945641345661052, 46.34263823992748; −0.019985129706097695, 0.187133997704791], the object distance from the first vertex is −(B + Dg)/(A + Cg). Adding the 53 mm lens span and back-focus gap g and setting the total to 1000 mm gives g = 22.478722480428 mm. This extends the lens by 2.735836626367 mm from infinity and gives an object distance of 924.521277519572 mm from the first vertex. It is a calculated endpoint, not a published factory helicoid curve.

## Geometry, Coverage and Model Scope

All five printed group outside diameters remain respected as physical bounds. The minimum sampled common-band edge thickness remains 0.527003 mm, and the maximum actual rim angle is now 58.277962°. Across the shared r7/r8 band, the combined sag occupies 0.968570562 of the 11.3 mm cavity, leaving 0.355153 mm of air; r7 also remains 0.089563 mm ahead of the iris at its own rim. The 0.97 gap-sag allowance is the smallest two-decimal value that avoids hidden drawing trim; 0.96 trims both central faces. Ordinary rim-angle and positive-edge constraints remain satisfied.

Earlier calculations with the smaller 12.35 mm central caps found substantial off-axis vignetting at the default 13.534693° half field, at 20°, and at the source's 22.5° and 23° targets. Some rays missed the entire next finite lens solid after r2, including its caps and cylindrical edge. That behavior is distinct from transmitted rays or ordinary rim clipping. The 25° chief-ray solution had no physically admitted branch. Those results describe the earlier infinity geometry; changed-rim field illumination and finite-focus field coverage have not been established. Neither the source fields nor the product's format guarantee uniform illumination.

## MTF and Model Limitations

Earlier smaller-rim calculations evaluated reference d-line diffraction MTF at the Gaussian plane and a separately optimized axial plane at nominal f/0.95, reference f/8, and an approximate five-line photopic case. The image heights were the center, half format-corner height, the reference-chief heights at 22.5° and 23°, and the broader 21.65 mm format corner. The photopic calculation used the same image heights rather than identical chromatic chief angles.

For that earlier geometry, center, half-height, source-field and plot-field calculations converged in all four configurations. The broader corner remained unconverged at nominal f/0.95 for both reference focus choices and the photopic estimate; the f/8 corner converged. These results concern the earlier clipped pupil and do not establish the performance of the enlarged caps or the reconstructed finite-focus state. That earlier photopic estimate derived normal partial dispersion from d-line index and Abbe number alone; it does not evaluate the catalog dispersion equivalents now used or establish manufacturer spectral MTF. No APO or anomalous-partial-dispersion performance is established.
