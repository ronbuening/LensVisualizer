# CANON 35mm f/1.8 — Audit Log

## Source

[US 2,854,890, High Aperture Wide Angle Lens](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2854890A/en), Jiro Mukai, assigned to Canon Camera Co., Inc. (printed “Canon Camera Company, Inc.”). The U.S. application, Serial No. 557,374, was filed 4 January 1956 with Japanese priority of 14 January 1955, and the patent was granted 7 October 1958 with two claims. Figure 1 shows the sole example, Figure 2 its spherical aberration, Figures 3–4 its astigmatism and distortion to 30° off axis, and the column 2 table its data under the heading f = 1, 2α = 60°, F:1.8. Claim 2 reprints the table with two differences (see Corrections).

Canon’s September 1958 booklet _Canon Lenses_ (Pub. No. 327B) gives the product section and specifications on p. 17: seven elements, a non-collapsible, non-revolving head, f/22 minimum aperture, distance scales from 1 m (3.5 ft) to infinity, a 64° angle of view and amber coating. The Canon Camera Museum gives May 1957, seven elements in four groups, f/22, 1 m closest focus, a 40 mm filter and 125 g, and lists no blade count or maximum magnification. A collector’s history dates the lens to April 1956, with the Canon VT, and shows a second optical section. ## Transcription Check

All eleven radii, ten axial spacings and seven d-line index and Abbe number pairs were re-read against the patent page images, and every value agrees. Each radius and spacing is the printed value multiplied by 35, which takes the nominal focal length from 1 to 35 mm. The L2–L3 doublet shares surface 4, the L4–L5–L6 triplet shares surfaces 7 and 8, and the axial air gap between L6 and L7 is 0.035 mm. The table prints no iris, back focus, aspheric surface or variable spacing.

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

**Iris.** The central air space is 8.05 mm. The iris sits 4.745 mm behind surface 5 and 3.305 mm ahead of surface 6, 12.2 mm behind the front vertex. The diaphragm marks in Canon’s section lie about 0.6 mm farther forward, but the rear rim of L3 reaches 11.65 mm behind the front vertex, which limits how far forward the iris can sit. The f/1.8 entrance pupil gives a paraxial iris radius of 6.781 mm.

**Clear radii.** Canon’s p. 17 section measures about 16.5 pixels/mm from its vertex spacings, and Figure 1 about 16.6 pixels/mm. The two drawings and the full-aperture rays give the following clear radii.

| Surfaces | Clear radius | Evidence                                                                           |
| -------- | ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1, 2     | 11.2 mm      | Front singlet outline                                                              |
| 3, 4     | 8.8 mm       | Doublet edge, 8.6–8.9 mm in the booklet and 8.9 mm in Figure 1                     |
| 5        | 7.8 mm       | Curved rear face of the doublet                                                    |
| 6, 7     | 6.75 mm      | The drawn 6.2–6.5 mm would cut the axial beam. L4’s two surfaces meet near 6.9 mm. |
| 8, 9     | 8.3 mm       | Booklet edge near 8.4 mm, below L6’s knife edge near 8.5 mm                        |
| 10, 11   | 10.6 mm      | Rear singlet, about 10.6 mm in the booklet and 10.8 mm in Figure 1                 |

Every element keeps a positive edge thickness and passes the whole axial f/1.8 beam. The f/1.8 marginal ray reaches 6.60 mm on surface 6 and 6.66 mm on surface 7. The axial ray through the 6.781 mm iris rim meets surface 7 at 6.710 mm at infinity and 6.724 mm at 1 m. The chief ray reaches the 21.63 mm corner image height at both distances. Corner illumination at infinity is 10.3% of the axial value at f/1.8, about 24% at f/2.8 and 57% at f/8; L4, whose surfaces meet near 6.9 mm, sets the oblique limit.

**Film plane and focus.** The last vertex lies 23.695 mm behind the first. Paraxial imaging gives a rear spacing of 24.2578 mm at infinity. Moving the whole lens forward to Canon’s 1 m subject-to-film distance gives 25.5739 mm, an extension of 1.3161 mm and a reproduction ratio of 0.0376×. The image-plane residuals with those rounded spacings are −0.000014 mm and +0.000021 mm. The patent gives no finite spacing or magnification, and neither Canon source says whether its 1 m is measured from the film plane or the lens front.

**Dispersion.** Six elements take their dispersion from the catalog rows in the transcription table; L3 uses the printed index and Abbe number.

## Product Identification

Canon’s p. 17 section and Figure 1 agree element by element: the positive front meniscus, the cemented doublet with its convex face forward, the iris, the cemented triplet with its concave face forward and the closely spaced biconvex rear singlet. The collector’s section shows the same arrangement and hatches L6 and L7, the two highest-index elements behind the iris, without a key to the hatching. The seven elements in four groups, f/1.8, Canon assignment and January 1955 priority all fit the product, and the patent has no second example, so the match is strong. Canon gives a 64° field for the product against the patent’s 60°; the model reaches the 24 × 36 mm corner at 32.2°. Canon’s museum dates the lens to May 1957, while the collector’s history gives April 1956. ## Corrections, 2026-11-25

- The field, previously limited to a 30° half-field with off-axis rays drawn at 5°, now extends to the 24 × 36 mm corner, which the chief ray reaches at 32.2° at infinity. Canon’s 64° angle of view corresponds to the full frame.
- The model keeps the full f/1.8 aperture; the 6.781 mm iris radius is calculated from the f/1.8 entrance pupil at the estimated iris position.
- The estimated clear radii were replaced by readings of Canon’s section and Figure 1. Surfaces 1–2 went from 12 to 11.2 mm, surface 3 from 10.3 mm, wider than both drawings, to 8.8 mm, surface 4 from 8.7 to 8.8 mm, surfaces 8–9 from 8.2 to 8.3 mm and surfaces 10–11 from 10 to 10.6 mm. Surface 5 stays at 7.8 mm. Surfaces 6–7 stay at 6.75 mm, wider than the drawn 6.2–6.5 mm, because the drawn rim would cut the f/1.8 axial beam; L4’s surfaces meet near 6.9 mm, so the rim cannot open further.
- The model was infinity only, with `focus.closeFocusM` at 10¹⁵ m, which stands for infinity, and the derived back focus fixed as the last spacing. It now focuses by moving the whole lens to Canon’s 1 m: the rear spacing runs from 24.2578 mm at infinity to a calculated 25.5739 mm at 1 m. The patent prints no finite focus state.
- The description gives r10 = +4.250 and ν7 = 53.5, and Claim 2 prints +4.259 and 55.3. The description values are kept as printed, giving r10 = +148.750 mm. The description radius gives a normalized focal length of 1.00083 and the claim radius 1.00107, so the focal length cannot decide between them. The claim’s 55.3 repeats ν6 and matches no catalog glass at nd 1.6935, while 53.5 lies within 0.12 of the CDGM H-LaK6A row. The discrepancy is recorded as unresolved.
- The patent number is written US 2,854,890, with the 1958 grant year, the inventor and the assignee added. The name dropped its mount suffix, and the subtitle and spec chips take the patent and maker form.
- The seven elements are named L1–L7 with their shapes, roles and focal lengths, the cemented doublet (L2–L3) and triplet (L4–L6) are tagged, and the patent’s four air-spaced groups are marked I–IV.
- Catalog glass labels were added for six elements, with the differences in the transcription table; L3 stays unmatched.
- The product metadata follow Canon’s booklet and museum page: 35 mm, f/1.8, seven elements in four groups, f/1.8 to f/22, 1 m closest focus, Leica thread mount and 35 mm full frame. The design focal length is the traced 35.03 mm in place of 35 mm. No blade count is given because neither Canon source states one.

The finished model gives 35.029 mm EFL, f/1.800, 24.258 mm back focus, 47.953 mm from front vertex to film, a 6.781 mm iris radius and a 32.2° corner half-field at infinity.

## Remaining Questions

- The factory clear diameters, exact diaphragm position, blade count, original glass melts and measured focus travel are undocumented in the cited sources. Canon’s section draws L4 smaller than the f/1.8 axial ray height; a dimensioned production section could settle the clearance there.
- The patent gives no reason for its two versions of r10 and ν7, and the production lens’s values are unknown.
- L3 (nd 1.6987, νd 30.1) has no catalog row within the printed precision.
- The launch date is uncertain: Canon’s museum gives May 1957 and the collector’s history April 1956.
- Neither Canon source states whether the 1 m closest focus is measured from the film plane or the lens front.
