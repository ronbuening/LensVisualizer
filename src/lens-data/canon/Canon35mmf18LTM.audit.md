# CANON 35mm f/1.8 — audit

## Source

US 2,854,890, _High Aperture Wide Angle Lens_, Jiro Mukai, assigned to Canon Camera Co., Inc. Japanese priority 14 January 1955, U.S. filing 4 January 1956, granted 7 October 1958. Figure 1 and the column 2 table describe the sole example. Claim 2 repeats the numerical prescription. [Patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2854890A/en)

## Values Not Printed in the Patent

**Iris.** The central air space is 8.05 mm. The iris sits 4.745 mm behind surface 5 and 3.305 mm ahead of surface 6, 12.2 mm behind the front vertex. The diaphragm marks in Canon’s section lie about 0.6 mm farther forward. The rear rim of L3 reaches 11.65 mm behind the front vertex, which constrains the estimate. The f/1.8 entrance pupil gives a paraxial stop radius of 6.781 mm.

**Clear radii.** Canon’s product section gives a scale of about 16.5 pixels/mm from vertex spacings. Figure 1 provides a second outline at about 16.6 pixels/mm. The figures and the full-aperture rays give the following clear radii.

| Surfaces | Clear radius | Evidence                                                                           |
| -------- | ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1, 2     | 11.2 mm      | Front singlet outline                                                              |
| 3, 4     | 8.8 mm       | Doublet edge, 8.6–8.9 mm in the brochure                                           |
| 5        | 7.8 mm       | Curved rear face of the doublet                                                    |
| 6, 7     | 6.75 mm      | The drawn 6.2–6.5 mm would cut the axial beam. L4’s two surfaces meet near 6.9 mm. |
| 8, 9     | 8.3 mm       | Brochure edge near 8.4 mm, below L6’s knife edge near 8.5 mm                       |
| 10, 11   | 10.6 mm      | Rear singlet, about 10.6 mm in the brochure and 10.8 mm in Figure 1                |

The surfaces retain positive edge thickness and clear the axial f/1.8 beam. Exact sphere intersections place the axial ray through the 6.781 mm iris rim at a height of 6.710 mm on surface 7 at infinity and 6.724 mm at 1 m. The chief ray reaches the 21.63 mm image height at both distances. At infinity the estimated corner illumination is 10.3% of the axial illumination.

**Film plane and focus.** The last vertex lies 23.695 mm behind the first. Paraxial imaging gives a rear spacing of 24.2578 mm at infinity. Translating the whole lens forward to Canon’s 1 m subject-to-film distance gives a spacing of 25.5739 mm. The signed image-plane residuals with those rounded spacings are −0.000014 mm and +0.000021 mm respectively. The patent gives no finite spacing or magnification.

## Product Identification

Canon’s 35mm f/1.8 section and Figure 1 agree on the front meniscus, the convex-front cemented doublet, the concave-front cemented triplet and the closely spaced biconvex rear singlet. The seven elements in four groups, maximum aperture and filing date support a strong match. There is no second patent example. Canon gives a 64° field for the product and dates it to May 1957. ## Corrections

### 2026-11-25

The description gives r10 = +4.250 and ν7 = 53.5. Claim 2 prints +4.259 and 55.3. The prescription follows the description throughout, giving r10 = +148.750 mm at the stated scale. Its normalized focal length is 1.00083. The claim radius gives 1.00107, so focal length alone cannot settle the discrepancy. The claim’s Abbe number repeats L6’s value.

## Remaining Questions

The factory clear diameters, exact diaphragm position and blade count, original glass melts and measured focus travel remain undocumented in the cited sources. The brochure outline at L4 is smaller than the ray height required at full aperture. A dimensioned production section could establish the clearance there. The patent gives no explanation for the two versions of r10 and ν7.
