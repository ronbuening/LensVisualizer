## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 6,618,197 B1
**Filed:** 1997-12-22
**Published:** 2003-09-09
**Inventor:** Shingo Hayakawa
**Assignee:** Canon Inc.
**Title:** Image Stabilizing System
**Example details:** Second Numerical Example, col. 9 (PDF p. 18); Fig. 4A/4B; Table 1, col. 10. Priority JP 8-358437, 1996-12-27. The certificate of correction leaves Example 2 unchanged.

The patent names no product. Its first two examples are 300 mm F/4.1 stabilized telephotos, filed in Japan about three months before the EF 300mm f/4L IS USM went on sale in March 1997. Canon's construction diagram matches Example 2:

1. Fourteen elements in ten groups: five air-spaced front elements, then four cemented doublets and one singlet. Canon's 15 elements / 11 groups add a flat front protective glass the patent does not tabulate; it has no power and is not modeled.
2. Canon's stabilizer, elements 11 to 13 counting that glass, is E10 to E12 here, the patent's unit L4.
3. The diagram shows about 25.6 mm behind the focusing doublet and 6.5 mm behind the next doublet; Example 2 prints 25.50 and 7.00 mm, Example 1 30.00 and 2.00 mm.

The match is not exact. Canon's two UD elements are the 2nd and 5th counting the protective glass (E1 and E4 here). Example 2 has a fluorite-like crown at E1 but an FK5-class crown at E4; Example 1 has UD-class crowns (1.49700/81.6) at E2 and E4. The production prescription is therefore probably a close relative of Example 2. Examples 3 and 4 (392.9 mm, F/5.7) do not fit.

## Optical Architecture

Five units of positive, negative, positive, negative and positive power: L1 (E1–E5, +145.9 mm), L2 (E6/E7, −156.5 mm), L3 (E8/E9, +133.9 mm), L4 (E10–E12, −32.6 mm) and L5 (E13/E14, +55.9 mm). The model reproduces Table 1 for Example 2: f1/f = 0.486, |f2/f| = 0.522, f3/f = 0.446, |f4/f| = 0.109, f5/f = 0.186, f123/f = 0.50.

L1 is the telephoto front group: three large elements, a 45 mm air space, a positive element and a negative meniscus. L2 moves axially to focus; L4 shifts across the axis to stabilize. With the patent's decentering sensitivity of −2.5, 1 mm of L4 shift moves the image 2.5 mm, enough for about 0.5° of shake. All 24 surfaces are spherical.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### E1 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.43875, νd = 95.0. Glass: S-FPL53 equivalent, fluorite-like low-dispersion crown. f = +206.2 mm.

Front collector of L1 and the only special-dispersion glass, at the position Canon marks as the first UD element.

### E2 — Positive Meniscus

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.2. Glass: S-FSL5 equivalent, FK5-class crown. f = +271.8 mm.

Weak meniscus, convex to the object, with a nearly flat rear face.

### E3 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.80610, νd = 41.0. Glass: S-LAH53 equivalent, lanthanum flint. f = −182.3 mm.

High-index negative that achromatizes E1 and E2.

### E4 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.48749, νd = 70.2. Glass: S-FSL5 equivalent, FK5-class crown. f = +155.7 mm.

Positive element behind the 45 mm air space. Canon marks this position UD, so the production glass probably differs here.

### E5 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.67270, νd = 32.1. Glass: S-TIM25 equivalent, dense flint. f = −431.9 mm.

Weak meniscus, convex to the object, closing L1 just ahead of the iris.

### E6 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: S-TIH6 equivalent, dense flint. f = +211.1 mm.

Positive flint member of the focusing doublet L2, 4.4 mm behind the iris at infinity.

### E7 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.69680, νd = 55.5. Glass: S-LAL14 equivalent, lanthanum crown. f = −89.2 mm.

Gives L2 its net negative power; the flint-positive, crown-negative pair keeps the moving doublet nearly achromatic.

### E8 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.84666, νd = 23.9. Glass: PBH53 equivalent, very dense flint. f = −68.9 mm.

Meniscus, convex to the object, at the front of the fixed doublet L3.

### E9 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.60323, νd = 42.3. Glass: BAM25 equivalent, barium flint. f = +45.3 mm.

Carries the power of L3; the L3 shape factor, condition (9), is −0.48.

### E10 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Glass: S-TIH6 equivalent, dense flint. f = +47.8 mm.

The single positive lens of L4; it sets the unit's color so lateral color stays small when L4 is decentered.

### E11 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.69680, νd = 55.5. Glass: S-LAL14 equivalent, lanthanum crown. f = −30.9 mm.

Strongest negative element. Its deeply concave rear face meets E12 across 5.2 mm, which limits both faces' clear radius.

### E12 — Biconcave Negative

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.6. Glass: S-LAH66 equivalent, lanthanum flint. f = −49.0 mm.

Second negative lens of L4, which gives the unit strong power; the L4 shape factor, condition (8), is −3.21.

### E13 — Biconvex Positive

nd = 1.69680, νd = 55.5. Glass: S-LAL14 equivalent, lanthanum crown. f = +32.7 mm.

Positive front member of the fixed final doublet L5.

### E14 — Negative Meniscus

nd = 1.85026, νd = 32.3. Glass: S-LAH71 equivalent, lanthanum flint. f = −75.7 mm.

Meniscus, convex to the image. The patent leaves spherical aberration and coma in L5 to offset L4's during stabilization; condition (7) is −0.24.

Only E1 is a special-dispersion glass; the FK5-class S-FSL5 at E4, a Canon UD position, is the clearest difference from the production lens. Abbe numbers do not give partial dispersion; the model's secondary spectrum follows the catalog equivalents.

## Focus Mechanism

Inner focus. The patent states that L2, the doublet just behind the iris, moves toward the image, and it plots a near case at about ten times the focal length (Figs. 4B, 5B), but prints no near spacings. The model's close state is derived, not published: L2 moves 11.598 mm to focus an object 3.0 m from the fixed image plane, at β = −0.109.

| Gap | Infinity, mm | 3.0 m (derived), mm |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| D10b (iris to E6) | 4.400 | 15.998 |
| D13 (E7 to E8) | 25.500 | 13.902 |

Canon gives 1.5 m and 0.24×. L2 alone cannot reach that: with D13 closed to 0.1 mm it reaches only about 1.60 m at 0.22×, so the production lens moves more than this unit or differs from Example 2.

## Geometry, Aperture and Image Plane

The model keeps the patent's scale: EFL 300.0225 mm against the printed 300.02301 mm. The patent prints no back focus; the 80.004 mm image distance is the computed paraxial focus. Longitudinal spherical aberration (−0.03 mm at full aperture) and distortion (+1.2% pincushion at 4.15°) agree with Fig. 5A.

The patent shows no stop. The iris sits 17.6 mm behind S10 (0.80 of D10 = 22.00 mm); Canon's diagram puts it at about 0.82. It is sized for F/4.1.

No clear diameters are printed; semi-diameters follow Fig. 4A, including 15.0 mm at the front of the stabilizer doublet and 14.5 mm at the rear of E12. The facing sags of S19 and S20 meet across their 5.2 mm gap near 13.5 mm, so those faces keep a 12.7 mm clear radius, drawn out to the figure's square edges with flat lands. Wide open, geometric transmission at the 4.1° corner is about 70%.

Canon's Extender EF 1.4x and 2x fit this lens; with the 2x the model gives f = 600.8 mm at F/8.2.
