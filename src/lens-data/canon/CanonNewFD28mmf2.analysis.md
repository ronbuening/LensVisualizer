# CANON NEW FD 28mm f/2

## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** US 4,188,092, _Retrofocus Type Wide-Angle Objective Lens_\
**Inventors:** Kikuo Momiyama\
**Assignee:** Canon Inc. (Canon Kabushiki Kaisha on the grant)\
**Priority:** April 5, 1977, Japan\
**Filed:** March 31, 1978\
**Published:** February 12, 1980\
**Example details:** Example 2, Figure 3 and the table in column 6. All lengths use a scale of ×28 because the table sets the focal length to 1.

Example 2 is a strong match for the New FD 28mm f/2 that Canon introduced in June 1979. Canon lists ten elements in nine groups. The cutaway reproduced by MIR shows four alternating positive and negative menisci, a thick cemented pair with a flat back just ahead of the diaphragm, and four rear singlets. Figure 3 shows the same sequence, with the sole cemented interface at R10. The patent uses spherical and plane surfaces. Canon identifies no extra-low-dispersion element. [Canon Camera Museum](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/nfd202.html) · [MIR, production cutaway](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/canon/fdresources/fdlenses/28mm.htm) · [Patent, Figure 3 and Example 2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4188092A/en)

The other 28 mm f/2 prescription in the patent, Example 4, has nine singlets and lacks the cemented middle pair. Examples 1 and 3 are 24 mm designs. The 1977 priority date also precedes the New FD lens. Canon’s specifications and the cutaway support the identification, although neither gives production radii or glass names. [US 4,188,092, Figures 1, 3, 5 and 7](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4188092A/en)

The earlier FD 28mm f/2 S.S.C., introduced in November 1975, had nine elements in eight groups. The New FD lens is shorter (47.2 versus 61 mm), weighs less (265 versus 343 g) and takes a 52 mm filter in place of the 55 mm thread. Both versions have eight diaphragm blades and stop down to f/22. MIR lists high-index glass and Super Spectra Coating for the New FD lens. [Canon, FD 28mm f/2 S.S.C.](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/fd135.html) · [Canon, New FD 28mm f/2](https://global.canon/en/c-museum/product/nfd202.html) · [MIR, New FD specifications](https://www.mir.com.my/rb/photography/companies/canon/fdresources/fdlenses/28mm.htm)

## Optical Architecture

Momiyama divides the nine air-spaced components into three groups by optical function. Group I (E1–E4) diverges the entering beam. Group II (E5–E6) is a positive cemented doublet, yet Groups I and II together still have negative power. The positive rear Group III (E7–E10) brings the rays to the film. At infinity the scaled table gives a 27.997 mm effective focal length and places the paraxial image 36.064 mm behind the last vertex, leaving room for the reflex mirror.

Canon lists a 75° diagonal field for the 28 mm f/2, matching the patent’s 37.5° half-field. At that angle the chief ray lands 21.11 mm from the axis. The 21.65 mm format corner is reached at 38.16°. Figure 3 supplies the clear-radius estimates and places the diaphragm 0.70 mm behind R11. The 8.97 mm iris radius follows from the paraxial f/2 aperture. The rims pass the whole axial pupil and about 38% of the pupil at the corner. This fraction describes geometric vignetting. ## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are for individual elements in air. The two members of the cemented pair share a refracting surface when assembled.

### E1 — Positive meniscus

nd = 1.58913, νd = 61.1. Ohara S-BAL35 equivalent. f = +105.43 mm. Its convex front face starts bending oblique rays toward the axis before they reach the stronger menisci.

### E2 — Negative meniscus

nd = 1.54869, νd = 45.6. Ohara PBL7 equivalent. f = −33.45 mm. Its deeply concave rear face supplies much of the divergence that opens space behind the lens for the reflex mirror.

### E3 — Positive meniscus

nd = 1.80610, νd = 40.9. Ohara S-LAH53 equivalent. f = +56.35 mm. Momiyama places this second positive meniscus where the oblique rays are lower than at E1. Column 4 describes how this helps correct chromatic distortion and chromatic coma. Its rear face is the front boundary of the floating air space.

### E4 — Negative meniscus

nd = 1.59551, νd = 39.2. Ohara S-TIM8 equivalent. f = −27.79 mm. This is the strongest negative meniscus in Group I. Its front face forms the other boundary of the floating air space.

### E5 and E6 — Cemented positive doublet

E5 is biconvex, with nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4, an Ohara S-TIH6 equivalent and f = +33.35 mm. E6 is plano-concave, with nd = 1.54869, νd = 45.6, an Ohara PBL7 equivalent and f = −95.27 mm. The pair converges the beam with a combined focal length of +50.57 mm. Its plane rear face R11 has zero surface power. The iris lies in the air space that follows.

### E7 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.77250, νd = 49.6. Ohara S-LAH66 equivalent. f = +22.20 mm. E7 is the strongest positive singlet. Its rear face and E8’s front face bound a meniscus-shaped air space whose curvatures the patent relates to the balance of spherical aberration and coma.

### E8 — Biconcave negative

nd = 1.80518, νd = 25.4. Ohara S-TIH6 equivalent. f = −20.05 mm. E8 opposes E7’s convergence. Its more deeply curved front face is the second boundary of the corrective air space.

### E9 — Positive meniscus

nd = 1.69680, νd = 55.5. Ohara S-LAL14 equivalent. f = +63.28 mm. Its convex face points toward the image. It restores convergence behind E8 and shares the final positive power with E10.

### E10 — Biconvex positive

nd = 1.69680, νd = 55.5. Ohara S-LAL14 equivalent. f = +42.05 mm. The front surface is shallow. The stronger rear curvature provides most of this element’s power on the way to the film.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The table gives helium d-line indices and Abbe numbers. Seven Ohara catalog glasses reproduce all ten pairs to within 0.0000032 in nd and 0.045 in νd. Their dispersion curves estimate behavior away from the d line. The patent leaves the supplier and melt unspecified.

The strongest contrast is within Group II. The positive member has a higher index and more dispersion than its negative partner. Group III reuses the same dense flint in E8, this time in a negative element. Its three positive elements have a mean nd of 1.7220, the value printed in the patent’s condition table.

## Focus Mechanism

For nearer subjects the whole lens moves toward the subject while D6, the air space between E3 and E4, widens. Column 5 and claim 5 describe this floating correction. The model is focused at infinity, where D6 measures 1.9068 mm. The patent gives no finite spacing or magnification from which to determine intermediate positions. [US 4,188,092, description and claim 5](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4188092A/en)

The production lens uses double-helicoid focusing with floating correction. It reaches 0.13× at 0.30 m, measured from the film plane to the subject.
