## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** DE 3,907,928 C2; the earlier A1 publication was checked as well  
**Filed:** 11 March 1989  
**Published:** A1, 20 September 1990  
**Granted:** C2, 6 November 1997  
**Inventors:** Ernst Rothe; Josef Weiß  
**Applicant:** Optische Werke G. Rodenstock  
**Title:** Objektiv für Großformat-Kameras  
**Embodiment analyzed:** Table 5, with one corrected refractive index (L3)

This prescription is a reconstruction of the patent example, not a verified production prescription. It replaces an
earlier eight-element reconstruction of the A1 table, which had added an ultra-low-dispersion element and a flat rear
surface that the patent does not print, so that the focal length would come out at 148.7 mm. The C2 grant shows that
neither is needed: its table resolves into the seven-element, five-group design described by the claims and by
Rodenstock's own literature.

The production association has the following support and limits:

1. Rodenstock's 1995 film-lens brochure (code 6/95) describes the Apo-Sironar-W family as seven elements in five
   groups with an 80° field. Its section drawing shows the same `1 + 2 + 1 | stop | 1 + 2` arrangement as the patent,
   mirrored left to right.
2. The brochure lists the 150 mm f/5.6 model with a 252 mm image circle at infinity and f/22, a 141 mm optical register
   and a 56 mm overall length. Horseman's lens list, hosted by Kenko, repeats the 252 mm circle and 141 mm register.
   Register is measured from the mounting flange and is not the same as the optical back focus.
3. Table 5 prints a focal length of 148.7 mm, close to the marketed 150 mm. Taken literally, however, it computes
   **300.93 mm**. Neither the A1 publication nor the C2 grant prints the corrected index used here. Photons to Photos
   associates Example 5 with this lens but notes that it changed most index/Abbe pairs; that is secondary evidence,
   not an independent production prescription.
4. The brochure section illustrates the whole W family and sits next to a photograph of the 210 mm lens. It does not
   give exact 150 mm clear apertures. Its statements on ED glass, low distortion and broad field are qualitative; the
   brochure has no numerical MTF curve.

The 141 mm register is consistent with this model. The stop lies 144.2 mm in front of the image plane, so the
mounting flange sits about 3 mm behind the stop, inside the 54.03 mm optical track and within the 56 mm barrel. The
literal table would need its flange about 156 mm behind the last lens surface, which no 56 mm shutter-mounted lens
could provide. This rules out the literal table as the production lens, but on its own it does not single out the
corrected index.

## Optical Architecture

Seven spherical elements form five groups. Group powers are positive, negative, positive, positive and weakly positive.
The cemented groups occupy surfaces 3–5 and 10–12. The stop is central: 8.60 mm after surface 7, followed by 7.45 mm to
surface 8. The first-to-last-vertex track is 54.03 mm.

Surfaces and elements are numbered from the object side, as in the patent. Claim 1 calls surface 2 the image-side face
of the first lens and surface 3 the object-side face of the second, and Figure 1 draws the parallel entrance bundles
arriving at surface 1. The four-lens group therefore faces the object. The brochure's mirrored drawing does not change
this order.

The C2 grant prints each thickness, index and Abbe number between the two radius rows it belongs to. The rear singlet
is therefore surfaces 8–9, the rear doublet is surfaces 10–12, and surface 12 is a curved glass-to-air exit. The claims
agree: the rear group consists of three lenses, and its second and third lenses are cemented.

### The corrected index

L3's printed **nd = 1.69** is replaced by **1.55**. Its printed **νd = 49.6**, every other material pair and all radii
and spacings are unchanged. Three source-internal points support the change:

- The literal table gives 300.93 mm against the printed 148.7 mm.
- At 300.93 mm the example would fail its own claims: claim 3 requires f < R4 < 2f, but R4 is 169.09 mm, and claim 1
  requires L4's focal length (220.6 mm) to be about equal to or longer than f. At 148.04 mm both hold.
- All four sibling tables in the same patent use nd = 1.55 for this element.

The corrected model computes 148.04 mm, 0.45 % below the printed value. It was not optimized toward 148.7 mm.

A systematic search of single changes was made: every index from 1.40 to 1.95, every radius sign flip or one-digit
change, and every thickness and stop-spacing digit. Only this change brings the focal length within 0.5 % of 148.7 mm.
About a dozen others land within 3 %, but each one leaves the third-order aberration balance far from that of the four
sibling examples, and most need glasses that do not exist. The L3 correction alone reproduces the siblings' balance of
spherical aberration, coma, astigmatism, Petzval sum and distortion. It remains an inference: the C2 grant repeats 1.69,
and replacing the Abbe number 49.6 with the sibling value 44.3 cannot be ruled out from the focal length alone.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

The focal lengths below are for each element alone in air, not within its cemented group.

### L1 — Positive meniscus

nd = 1.52, νd = 64.2. Glass: unmatched patent-rounded crown. f = +297.3 mm.

The weak positive object-side meniscus is followed by a 0.10 mm air space.

### L2 — Positive meniscus, front cemented doublet

nd = 1.62, νd = 63.5. Glass: N-PSK53 class (Schott equivalent from the rounded values; N-PSK53 at 1.62014 / 63.48 is closer than N-PSK53A at 1.618 / 63.39). f = +64.2 mm.

The stronger positive member shares surface 4 with L3. A phosphate-crown class fits the rounded values and the
brochure's mention of ED glass, but the exact glass is not established.

### L3 — Negative meniscus, front cemented doublet

nd = 1.55 (corrected), νd = 49.6. Glass: unmatched. f = −41.3 mm.

This is the only material index that differs from Table 5. The front doublet stays net negative, with a focal length
of about −183 mm. The corrected index/Abbe pair is not claimed to match one specific catalog glass.

### L4 — Positive inner front meniscus

nd = 1.50, νd = 56.4. Glass: K10 class (Schott equivalent). f = +220.6 mm.

The last front element faces the 8.60 mm air space in front of the stop.

### L5 — Positive first rear singlet

nd = 1.46, νd = 65.8. Glass: unmatched patent-rounded fluor-crown. f = +167.9 mm.

The C2 grant places the 2.35 mm thickness and this glass between surfaces 8 and 9.

### L6 — Negative meniscus, rear cemented doublet

nd = 1.65, νd = 39.6. Glass: unmatched patent-rounded short flint. f = −50.2 mm.

This element is in front of the shared surface 11. Its glass type is not identified.

### L7 — Positive rear meniscus

nd = 1.62, νd = 53.2. Glass: unmatched patent-rounded dense crown / short flint. f = +55.4 mm.

The 9.55 mm thickness ends at curved surface 12. The rear doublet as a whole is very weakly positive, about +740 mm
in air.

The patent quotes indices at the d line, rounded to two decimals. That does not identify glasses uniquely or show
where the brochure's ED glass sits, so it cannot support an apochromatic-performance claim. L2 and L4 carry Schott
class labels that fit the rounded values; the others remain unmatched. No partial-dispersion data has been invented.

C2 claim 2 asks for a rear Abbe difference of at least 13, while the description says at least 15. Table 5 gives
53.2 − 39.6 = 13.6. The discrepancy is in the patent itself and is left as printed.

## Focus Mechanism

Unit focus is modeled by moving the whole lens on the bellows, changing only the image-side air gap. The patent gives
no finite focus state or minimum focusing distance. The 3.5 m object-to-image endpoint is illustrative, not a
manufacturer specification.

| State | Surface 12 to image | Source |
|---|---:|---|
| Infinity | 120.54 mm | Computed paraxial back focus |
| 3.5 m object-to-image | 127.38 mm | Computed illustrative endpoint |

That is 6.84 mm of bellows travel. The brochure says the family was optimized for about 1:10 reproduction with no
noticeable loss at infinity; the illustrative endpoint does not try to reproduce that design condition.

## Aperture, Field and Geometry Limits

The patent gives no clear semi-diameters. The values used here follow the Photons to Photos aperture estimates for this
example, which look broadly consistent with the brochure section, reduced at surfaces 5/6 to 13.2 mm and at 9/10 to
12.5 mm so that facing sags stay inside the air gaps. They are modeling estimates, not measured 150 mm apertures.
The patent's only lens section, Figure 1, draws Example 2 rather than Example 5; scaled to Example 5's radii, its
rims agree with every value used here to within about 8 %.

The stop radius is derived from the f/5.6 marginal ray (about 10.03 mm). All glass and air clearances stay positive.
The chief ray reaches the 252 mm production image circle (about 40.5° half-field) without being blocked. Marginal
bundles at the edge of the field are clipped by the estimated apertures, so the drawing should not be read as a
calibrated light-falloff prediction.

Chief-ray distortion is about +0.14 % at 20° and −0.34 % at 40°. That fits the brochure's "exceptionally low
distortion", but is not a production match on its own.

## Verification Summary

The transcription was checked line by line against the Table 5 page images in both the A1 publication and the C2
grant; apart from the L3 index, every radius, thickness, index and Abbe number matches. An independent paraxial
calculation agrees with the application on focal length and back focus. Independent exact ray tracing agrees with the
application's ray tracer on every ray tested, across both focus states, three apertures and three field angles.

No manufacturer MTF or distortion curve for the 150 mm lens was found, and none was used to tune the model. The
patent's own Figure 2 plots MTF against relative field angle for Table 5, but it gives neither the test frequencies nor
the aperture. The model's monochromatic curves fall with field in the same way and drop below about 10 % at the 40°
edge, as the patent's do. That is a qualitative check only. Because the
glass dispersions are unresolved, the model cannot confirm the production lens's colour correction or peripheral
performance.
