## Patent Reference and Design Identification

**Patent:** DE 3026282 C2; identical numerical example in DE 3026282 A1  
**Application Number:** P 30 26 282.9-51  
**Filed:** 11 July 1980  
**Published:** A1, 11 February 1982; C2, 30 September 1982  
**Inventor:** Josef Weiß  
**Applicant:** Optische Werke G. Rodenstock  
**Title:** Vierlinsiges, nicht symmetrisches Weitwinkelaufnahmeobjektiv  
**Embodiment analyzed:** Sole numerical example, reference focal length 100, scaled by 0.9 to the 90 mm production focal length.

The association with the 1982 Geronar-WA 90 mm f/8 is **probable, not a confirmed factory prescription**. The principal manufacturer source is the dated 1982 Berkey Marketing Companies / Rodenstock Division sheet, PRC04128, PDF page 11, catalog number 160-202. The following evidence converges:

1. Both sections show four separate menisci in positive–negative–negative–positive order, with the iris between the inner negative elements.
2. The source f/8 aperture and at-least-85° full field agree in broad design purpose with the Geronar-WA. The product's 85° field and 170 mm circle belong specifically to f/22, not a promise of unvignetted wide-open illumination.
3. The advertised 90 mm focal length falls within the patent's preferred 60–150 mm range. The patent emphasizes compact infinity-taking performance and contrast, particularly stopped down.
4. The 1980 filing and 1982 publications precede or coincide with the dated product sheet. The manufacturer distinguishes this four-element wide angle from the ordinary three-element Geronar, which is shown alongside it.
5. The Rodenstock booklet's (PRC00817) 87.1 mm flange focal distance, less its 14.0 mm from shutter seating to the rear of the lens, leaves about 73.1 mm behind the rear cell, close to the model's 72.73 mm paraxial back focus.

PRC00817, PDF pages 15 and 22, independently identifies the four-element Geronar-WA, its 4×5-inch application and aperture range f/8–32. Its 80° / 155 mm specification at f/11 and 85° / 170 mm specification at f/22 are different operating conditions, not contradictory transcriptions. No manufacturer statement found identifies this patent or confirms the numerical factory prescription.

All radii, physical thicknesses, summed airspaces and explicit e-line glass coordinates agree between the original A1 and C2. Both tables repeat the label d3 for the final 10.21 thickness; both drawings label the fourth physical element d4. The value is assigned to that fourth element without changing it or inventing a fifth element.

## Optical Architecture

The design comprises four spherical singlets in four air-separated groups. The outer positive menisci enclose two high-index, high-dispersion negative menisci. Their concave sides face the central iris region. There are no cemented interfaces, aspheres, rear cover plates or movable internal groups in the published example.

The arrangement is approximately balanced around the central region but explicitly not symmetric. The rear positive element is stronger than the front; the two negative elements share the same glass coordinates but differ in curvature and thickness. At the 0.9 scale, their isolated powers are approximately +0.0154055, −0.0128663, −0.0117671 and +0.0182508 mm⁻¹ respectively. These are separated-element powers, not four additive contributions to the complete system power.

The outer airspaces are only 0.099 mm each, whereas the central space is 12.060 mm. The source's printed 6.11 + 7.29 subdivision is interpreted as front-to-back from the diagrams, which place the stop nearer r4 than r5. The implemented split is therefore 5.499 + 6.561 mm. This is a diagram-supported reading of printed distances, not a general rule about the order of addends and not independent proof of the factory iris coordinate.

The optical vertex span is 36.180 mm. The patent prints no back focus; the paraxial values of the scaled prescription are infinity BFD 72.731221 mm and EFL 89.960362 mm. The latter is 0.039638 mm below nominal 90 mm. Uniform scaling retains that residual rather than renormalizing the focal length or adjusting glass values. The manufacturer’s 41 mm overall length is a barrel dimension, not the optical vertex span.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Positive Meniscus, Convex to Object

nₑ = 1.6207, νₑ = 49.47. Glass: SCHOTT N-SSK8 catalog equivalent (dense special crown); production supplier unspecified. Isolated f ≈ +64.9121 mm.

The positive outer front meniscus begins the collecting action. Its weaker rear surface and substantial central thickness differ from those of the rear positive element. Its lower dispersion relative to the inner flints contributes to the refractive power/dispersion balance. Specific aberration cancellation by this element alone is an interpretation, not a separately published Seidel result.

### L2 — Negative Meniscus, Concave Toward the Iris

nₑ = 1.7918, νₑ = 25.87. Glass: SCHOTT SF56A catalog equivalent (dense flint); production supplier unspecified. Isolated f ≈ −77.7227 mm.

The stronger positive curvature on its rear face makes this meniscus divergent. Its high index permits substantial negative power with the source's thin central section. The section drawing shows a smaller clear arc on the iris-facing surface and a stepped edge. The model uses distinct front and rear clear radii joined by a straight rim, without inventing mechanical bevel dimensions.

### L3 — Negative Meniscus, Concave Toward the Iris

nₑ = 1.7918, νₑ = 25.87. Glass: the same printed coordinate as L2, SCHOTT SF56A catalog equivalent; production supplier unspecified. Isolated f ≈ −84.9826 mm.

This rear inner negative meniscus is not a mirror copy of L2. Its radii and central thickness differ, and the source section shows a larger iris-facing clear arc than the corresponding face of L2. The inferred clear apertures keep this asymmetry. Its negative power complements the stronger rear positive member.

### L4 — Positive Meniscus, Convex to Image

nₑ = 1.6251, νₑ = 52.86. Glass: SUMITA SSK2 catalog equivalent (dense special crown); production supplier unspecified. Isolated f ≈ +54.7920 mm.

The final meniscus supplies the strongest isolated positive power. Its rear surface is more strongly curved than its front. The fourth physical thickness is 9.189 mm after uniform scaling; the duplicated source subscript does not alter its position or value. Both drawings show a step on the front face, so its clear arc (16 mm radius) is smaller than the 18 mm rear face. The calculated rear principal plane lies 17.229141 mm in front of its rear vertex.

## Glass Identification and Selection

The patent explicitly uses nₑ and νₑ. These are stored as e-line values (546.07 nm reference), not relabeled as d-line coordinates.

Every printed pair equals a catalog row at the e line to the patent's four-decimal index and within 0.07 in νₑ: SCHOTT N-SSK8 for L1 (nₑ 1.62068, νₑ 49.54), SCHOTT SF56A for L2 and L3 (1.79180, 25.87) and SUMITA SSK2 for L4 (1.62509, 52.85). OHARA S-BSM28, S-BSM22 and PBH23 are equivalent rows within 0.15 in νₑ. The elements are named as catalog equivalents, so the chromatic trace uses those catalog dispersion curves anchored to the printed e-line indices; the actual melts and supplier are not documented, and the printed nₑ/νₑ values are kept unchanged.

The inner pair's greater dispersion and negative power oppose the outer positives' lower dispersion and positive power. That supports a qualitative achromatizing interpretation, not a numerical apochromatic claim. The patent prints no partial dispersions or other line indices, so secondary-spectrum behaviour follows the catalog-equivalent curves rather than source data.

## Focus Mechanism

The patent supplies one infinity prescription and no finite-conjugate data. As a view-camera lens, the Geronar-WA focuses by moving the whole cell relative to the film on the camera's bellows or rail; there are no internal or floating groups.

The model therefore varies only the back-focus gap. The close endpoint is a 1.0 m object-to-image distance, where the paraxial back focus is 82.698 mm against 72.731 mm at infinity: a +9.967 mm extension at about 0.11× magnification. This endpoint is computed for the focus slider; neither the patent nor the Rodenstock literature gives a minimum focus distance, which depends on the camera.

## Stop, Clear Apertures and Field Scope

The patent does not print the iris diameter. The model sets it from the f/8 marginal ray: a 4.263609 mm iris radius for an axial entrance beam of radius 5.622523 mm. The paraxial pupil for that same stop gives f/8.015 rather than exactly f/8, a finite-ray versus paraxial difference rather than a source error.

The entrance pupil is approximately 18.421357 mm behind r1; the exit pupil is 19.674886 mm in front of r8. Their paraxial radii at the wide-open stop are 5.611683 and 5.764247 mm respectively. These calculated pupils do not establish a factory stop size.

Clear apertures are read from the patent section and checked against the spherical geometry; the filter thread and barrel dimensions are not optical clear apertures. The stepped rims of the drawing are simplified to straight joins between faces of different clear radius.

A 170 mm image-circle diameter (85 mm radius) corresponds to about 43.3° half-field in this model, slightly more than half the advertised 85°; the prescription is not altered to make the two product descriptors agree. The viewer's image circle is set to that published 170 mm f/22 value, which bounds the analysis field; it does not certify wide-open illumination there.

## Model Scope and Limitations

The model is the patent example scaled uniformly to 90 mm, not a confirmed factory prescription; clear apertures and the iris diameter are inferred. At the patent's 42.5° half-field the f/8 corner beam is strongly vignetted by the outer element rims, and by f/22 most of it passes, consistent with Rodenstock's recommendation of f/16 and f/22 as working apertures. The manufacturer MTF curves are for 1:30 magnification at f/16 and f/22; the model does not reproduce them numerically. The close-focus state is a paraxial unit-focus extension, not a published conjugate.
