## Optical Architecture

The design comprises four spherical singlets in four air-separated groups. The outer positive menisci enclose two high-index, high-dispersion negative menisci. Their concave sides face the central iris region. There are no cemented interfaces, aspheres, rear cover plates or movable internal groups in the published example.

The arrangement is approximately balanced around the central region but explicitly not symmetric. The rear positive element is stronger than the front; the two negative elements share the same glass coordinates but differ in curvature and thickness. At the conditional 0.9 scale, their isolated powers are approximately +0.0154055, −0.0128663, −0.0117671 and +0.0182508 mm⁻¹ respectively. These are separated-element powers, not four additive contributions to the complete system power.

The outer airspaces are only 0.099 mm each, whereas the central space is 12.060 mm. The source's printed 6.11 + 7.29 subdivision is interpreted as front-to-back from the diagrams, which place the stop nearer r4 than r5. The implemented split is therefore 5.499 + 6.561 mm. This is a diagram-supported reading of printed distances, not a general rule about the order of addends and not independent proof of the factory iris coordinate.

The optical vertex span is 36.180 mm. The unprinted terminal image interval is calculated from the literal prescription: Gaussian infinity BFD 72.731221 mm and EFL 89.960362 mm. The latter is 0.039638 mm below nominal 90 mm. Uniform scaling retains that residual rather than renormalizing the focal length or adjusting glass values. The manufacturer’s 87.1 mm flange distance and 41 mm overall length are mechanical quantities, not substitutes for optical BFD or vertex track.

## Element-by-Element Analysis

### L1 — Positive Meniscus, Convex to Object

nₑ = 1.6207, νₑ = 49.47. Glass: unidentified historical melt; higher-index crown-class coordinate. Isolated f ≈ +64.9121 mm.

The positive outer front meniscus begins the collecting action. Its weaker rear surface and substantial central thickness differ from those of the rear positive element. Its lower dispersion relative to the inner flints contributes to the refractive power/dispersion balance. Specific aberration cancellation by this element alone is an interpretation, not a separately published Seidel result.

### L2 — Negative Meniscus, Concave Toward the Iris

nₑ = 1.7918, νₑ = 25.87. Glass: unidentified historical dense-flint-class coordinate. Isolated f ≈ −77.7227 mm.

The stronger positive curvature on its rear face makes this meniscus divergent. Its high index permits substantial negative power with the source's thin central section. The section drawing shows a smaller clear arc on the iris-facing surface and a stepped edge. The implementation uses distinct front and rear clear radii with the engine's straight rim join, rather than inventing detailed mechanical bevel dimensions.

### L3 — Negative Meniscus, Concave Toward the Iris

nₑ = 1.7918, νₑ = 25.87. Glass: the same printed coordinate as L2; exact melt identity unknown. Isolated f ≈ −84.9826 mm.

This rear inner negative meniscus is not a mirror copy of L2. Its radii and central thickness differ, and the source section shows a larger iris-facing clear arc than the corresponding face of L2. The source-derived asymmetry is retained in the inferred clear apertures. Its negative power complements the stronger rear positive member.

### L4 — Positive Meniscus, Convex to Image

nₑ = 1.6251, νₑ = 52.86. Glass: unidentified historical crown-class coordinate. Isolated f ≈ +54.7920 mm.

The final meniscus supplies the strongest isolated positive power. Its rear surface is more strongly curved than its front. The fourth physical thickness is 9.189 mm after uniform scaling; the duplicated source subscript does not alter its position or value. The calculated rear principal plane lies 17.229141 mm in front of its rear vertex.

## Stop, Clear Apertures and Field Scope

The numerical iris opening is reconstructed by exact marginal-ray calibration. At f/8 its radius is 4.263609 mm for an axial entrance beam of radius 5.622523 mm. The paraxial pupil corresponding to that same physical stop gives f/8.015453 rather than exactly f/8. This finite-marginal/paraxial distinction is disclosed, not treated as a source error. Every stopped-down validation state is independently marginal-calibrated rather than assuming a strictly linear iris-to-entrance-pupil relation.

The entrance pupil is approximately 18.421357 mm behind r1; the exit pupil is 19.674886 mm in front of r8. Their paraxial radii at the calibrated wide-open stop are 5.611683 and 5.764247 mm respectively. These calculated pupils do not independently establish a factory stop size.

Clear apertures are inferred from the original section and checked against actual spherical geometry. The filter thread and barrel dimensions are not optical clear apertures. The stepped source rims are simplified to the native model's straight conical joins. No gap-sag override, hidden trimming or source-cell repair is used. Ordinary blocked rays remain genuine modeled vignetting, not falsely counted as transmitted flux.

The source 42.5° half-field, native numerical heuristic, 4×5 reference corner and aperture-specific maker circles are tested separately. A 170 mm image-circle diameter (85 mm radius) corresponds to a larger angle in this literal conditional model than the advertised 85° field; the two product descriptors are not forcibly made identical by a prescription change. Launch-grid survivor fractions describe the chosen sampling only, not photometric transmission, coating efficiency or an illumination curve.
