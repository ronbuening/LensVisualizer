# Angénieux: The Angénieux Stories

Angénieux maintains a set of six history features under [The Angénieux stories](https://www.angenieux.com/about-us/history/history-stories/), supported by a dated [milestone timeline](https://www.angenieux.com/about-us/history/history-timeline/). Together they cover Pierre Angénieux's two defining inventions — the Retrofocus wide-angle and the cine zoom — and the company's later work for cinema and spaceflight.

The collection is unusual in this index for how far back its subject matter reaches into optical design proper: the Retrofocus story is an account of the inverted-telephoto principle that made wide-angle lenses possible on reflex cameras, and the zoom story describes the cam-driven group movement and aberration balancing that the Angénieux zooms were built on.

The Retrofocus story names both catalog lenses directly: it identifies the R1 as "the very first Angénieux Retrofocus lens," a 35mm f/2.5, and records the 28mm f/3.5 Retrofocus that followed in 1953 with a 75° field.

The last two stories are the thinnest optically. "Angénieux & Hollywood" is largely an awards record and "Angénieux and DPs" is about the profession rather than the lenses, but both are kept here so the collection is listed in full.

## Milestone Timeline

The [history timeline](https://www.angenieux.com/about-us/history/history-timeline/) is a dated list of Angénieux firsts that links back into the six stories. It records the 35mm f/2.5 Retrofocus of 1950, the 4× 17–68mm of 1957, the 10× 12–120mm and the Franscope anamorphic of 1961, the 10× 25–250mm of 1962, the 18× 27.5–500mm of 1967, the 2× 49–90mm for Leicaflex of 1968 and the later Optimo series.

The catalog's [DEM 180mm f/2.3 APO](/lens/angenieux-dem-180mm-f23-apo) is not the subject of any of the six stories and is therefore not cross-linked above.
